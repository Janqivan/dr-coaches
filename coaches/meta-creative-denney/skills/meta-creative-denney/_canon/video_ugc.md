## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Problem-solution script (Cottrell; Denney)
- When they use it: the default for any product with a felt problem — "not a script format" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI] but an umbrella; weave social proof, product proof and objection handling throughout.
- Steps: 1. Hook with the problem or tease the solution (a crazy shot, relatable caption or VO). 2. Break the problem down. 3. Aggravate — "twist the knife" [quote src: YT:KKp3rA0QK9A] — until it needs fixing. 4. Solution + benefit (vary how it solves it). 5. Proof. 6. CTA: where to buy, with social proof. Any order works if problem and solution are both there; "Don't complicate something if it's already working." [quote src: YT:tVXjXJSEjeA]
- After the solution lands: details (bra: seamless knit, wide band, strap thickness) that "are now becoming part of the solution" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI]; a mid-ad re-pitch for warm viewers who forgot what it does.
- Their example: Underbrush tooth powder, Liberare, Dr. Squatch (§4). Sources: [YT:505rIrOKhWI, YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:tVXjXJSEjeA, YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2026-06, 2026-10, 2025-07, 2026-08) · BOTH · DTC/GEN

### Villain vs hero (Cottrell)
- When they use it: there is a clear enemy — the industry, a product, or "more importantly probably your competitor" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ]; a habit works too (the urge to bite your nails).
- Steps (the million dollar villain and hero script framework, 2025-04): 1. Hook. 2. Villain. 3. How bad it is — reasons to hate it "as long as your arm" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ]. 4. Hero reveal fixing each problem, with USPs. 5. Handle an objection. 6. Social proof. 7. CTA. The 2026 five-part, which "always gives me consistent winning results" [quote src: YT:KKp3rA0QK9A]: hook → villain → hero → demo/proof → CTA (where, maybe price, offer).
- Rules: show the USPs so the payoff is worth it; the product can wait ~40 s if the problem talk is relatable; shoot the hero epically (probe-lens) against unappealing villain footage; ~10 s of villain then hero also works.
- Sources: [YT:4o46eGI6XwQ, YT:KKp3rA0QK9A] (2025-04, 2026-01) · BOTH · DTC

### Hook / body / close and the must-haves (Cottrell, Fraggle)
- Steps: 1. Hook — visual + audible, aimed at the right person. 2. Body — an emotional journey that builds rapport, opens loops and resolves the opening objection (A to B): testimonial = USPs/benefits; storytelling UGC = the yapper story; educational VSL = facts and figures. 3. Close — what to do, where, what they get; "arguably the hardest part" [quote src: YT:IX-2-uSJDk8].
- Must-haves: objection handling (why haven't they bought?), social proof from people like them, a strong CTA — "people won't do something unless you tell them what to do" [quote src: YT:tVXjXJSEjeA].
- Layering ("Unskippable"): relatability → education (how it works, why better, why needed) → credibility/tone → CTA. Funnel: story scripts for founder ads and TOF, educational for MOF, product showcase / "why us" lower down.
- Sources: [YT:IX-2-uSJDk8, YT:tVXjXJSEjeA, YT:GCugREdurJk, YT:505rIrOKhWI] (2026-06, 2025-07, 2026-01, 2026-06) · BOTH · GEN

### Mini VSL (Cottrell; Denney's S tier)
- When they use it: TOF and some MOF; anything needing upfront education; it wins "over and over again" [quote src: YT:-q2lqfNysUs].
- Steps: 1. Speaker authority first. 2. Explain the problem. 3. "twist the knife" [quote src: YT:KKp3rA0QK9A]. 4. Educate. 5. Product late, so it's "a very easy sell" [quote src: YT:oZFiSUkJEPE] — "make yourself seem the only solution" [quote src: YT:bNMeFhW6YmA]. 6. "go in for the right hook and sell them" [quote src: YT:bNMeFhW6YmA].
- Production: 1:00–1:30; studio + UGC + archive (maybe AI) footage; a human voice artist for emotion; study documentaries, not ad libraries.
- Their example: Leaf Thorn, Vanish, Portland Leather (§3–4). Sources: [YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:oZFiSUkJEPE, YT:QoGFOGEqijw, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2025-11, 2026-03, 2026-05, 2026-08) · BOTH · GEN/DTC

### Founder ad formula (Denney; Cottrell)
- Steps (Denney): 1. "my name is … and I'm the founder of" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM]. 2. The personal problem that sparked it. 3. Turning point — why you were the one to build it. 4. Solution — "go ham" on product and benefits. 5. Proof — buyers, love, changed lives, guarantee/risk-free. 6. A soft, genuine CTA.
- Cottrell's build: founder on VO or selfie + archive (prototypes, meetings, factory) + cut-in customer testimonials; cut pauses, "short and snappy" [quote src: YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA]; jump cuts zooming in every other shot on long talking stretches; bold or native captions (test); subtle lo-fi music, not "a bumping, you know, bass track" [quote src: YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA]; visible reading is fine — "people don't really care as much as you think they do" [quote src: YT:zpUsN6xQSGQ].
- Comment-reply version (Denney's go-to start): the founder honestly answers a TikTok comment bubble carrying a common objection (price, a recurring FAQ, pre-purchase anxiety); Cottrell cuts the founder reading it and shows a comment graphic to get "straight into the meat and potatoes" [quote src: YT:opWKMc6iavo].
- Beginner ladder (Denney): ugly one-take review or POV ("next to no skill or cash flow" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM]) → founder ad → creator content.
- Sources: [YT:NsHk8o3jVvM, YT:zpUsN6xQSGQ, YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q, YT:opWKMc6iavo] (2025-06, 2026-05, 2025-01, 2025-10) · BOTH · GEN

### "where is this going?" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] story shape (Levinger)
- When she uses it: every story or UGC ad — "it's really only just shock or alignment these days" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY].
- Steps: 1. Open a loop by alignment ("oh, I love Christmas cookies... let's see where this goes" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]) or bizarreness. 2. Long rising action — the setup decides where the viewer lands ("His setup was 51 seconds long" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]). 3. Come "down down down" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] to an emotional resolution, or the brain gets angry. 4. Exit the way they entered: "They came in for education, they need to go out through education." [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]
- Anti-pattern: AIDA-built UGC — "you killed it when you got to interest" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]. Feed test: would it sit naturally between two creator videos ("No commercials")? Can a stranger feel the point in under a second? Does it open on a human moment before any claim? Is the payoff predictably worth staying for?
- Sources: [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY, YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (2025-12) · EVERGREEN · GEN

### Trigger-event AI animation (Cooper)
- Steps: 1. Open on the trigger event. 2. Agitate with repeats. 3. Discovery ("A friend recommended me this" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]). 4. Mechanism — why it beats what she tried. 5. Payoff (the mother-in-law now says she looks great). Easy once you have trigger events; hand it to Claude Code.
- Their example: Quazi (§4). Sources: [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08) · NOW · DTC

### Denney's diagnosis when UGC doesn't work for us
- Steps: check the creators — 1. Profiles don't match personas (not one-to-one; match who the customer feels aspirational toward — a 55+ customer doesn't mean only 55+ creators). 2. Not aspirational enough — "bottom of the barrel" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] creators "just in it for the money" [quote src: YT:4wM-i1cxtAM], "nothing going on behind the eyes" [quote src: YT:4wM-i1cxtAM]. 3. Budget only in big or only in small creators. 4. Weak delivery. 5. Low volume. 6. Not enough personal storytelling.
- Mantra: creators who open up, are vulnerable on camera and tell a personal story of why they use it; Modash AI finds creators already living a story (moving house, pregnant). [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM] (2026-09) · NOW · DTC

### Casting and direction (Cottrell; Hott; Cooper; Levinger)
- Cast for audience match (relatability) or authority, on believability and delivery, not price or speed; miscasting kills trust — a 20-year-old selling anti-aging serum, an 18-year-old in a lab coat claiming 25 years as a doctor (Cottrell). Hott: a creator who hasn't had the problem won't make a good ad unless they quickly feel it; an LA actress was "a gulf in class"; LEADGEN: an affable roofer from the company's own team speaking with authority — "that's really hard to beat" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]. [YT:xB4jykCt_yU, YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-01, 2026-04)
- Segment casting: aspirational ads for younger, athletic guys, separate ones for bigger guys (Cooper, The Perfect Jean); body-type segments lean on relatability (Hott) [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]; several actors for one "you"; creators briefed into rooms in the brand palette (Tumble: black, cream, dusty orange, never the rival's green) (Levinger) [YT:HuI3o3TBI4A, YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY].
- How much to script (Cooper): many creators; a structured hook + talking points + yapper ads as inspiration; the bottom 80–90% get more direction and scripting, the top ~10% go loose — they know a line lands better walking the dog [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w, YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]. Counter-test (guest: Anze Mikac): scripted beat loose — "the more specifics you put in the scripts, the more believable it is" [YT:KSm0pvevV9k].
- Modular shoots: several hooks, lots of B-roll, a couple of CTAs per creator (guest: Danil Saliukov, "Modular UGC"); one creator with one script is "not 2026" (guest: Chris, host) [YT:m8dbcF407H8]; Levinger: "ask them to get you as much b-roll as possible." [YT:DEoj28QXUYE] · BOTH · GEN/LEADGEN

### Partnerships/whitelisting, customer content and rights (Cottrell; Denney; Cooper; Hott)
- Customer-content flow (Cooper): 7 days after purchase, "do you want to make $50 or like $100" (or a discount) to record a video, paid if it runs — "So many winners": imperfect light, no optimized hook, uncut. [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01) · DTC
- Partnership/whitelisting (Cottrell): UGC works because it's "from someone just like them and not the brand"; shiny UGC and AI fake UGC erode that, and partnership/whitelisting helps. [YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:bNMeFhW6YmA] (2025-11, 2025-10)
- Source mix (Denney): Pact's top creatives were 3 partnership ads, 4 external-agency, 6 internal (4 by the in-house creator); use multiple creators and longer partnerships, because the same creator has great and flat days. Duets/stitches need the original creator's rights — she has bought them cheap or free, or faked the original with AI or another creator. [YT:R3Z1VvvgtzU, YT:_VtpfVeB2Kc, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-04, 2026-08)
- Reach over audience (Hott): small creators whose content "scales way beyond their followers"; high-view creators aren't always sellers. [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04)

- C: street interviews (her least favorite, "so painful"; she often fakes them); ASMR (crushes in pet and beauty, needs specialist creators, rarely the "scale the moon" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] unicorn); duet/reaction/stitch (complex, converts well, can hit top five, great for founders); warehouse incl. ### Hott's ugly-ad method, old and new
- Old: ask how a pro would frame and shoot it, then do the opposite. New (2026-03): ask how a creator making content people like to consume (MrBeast, Whistling Diesel) would frame it. - Sequence: "You can make it start ugly and make it prettier along the way" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] — "a Tik Tok is not going to just sell" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g], so add the sales component after the organic opening. ## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree
- **Polished UGC and talking-head-plus-B-roll are out; yapping while using the product is in.** Cottrell (2025-10): "the ones that we spend the most time on" "are the ones that perform the least"; (2026-10) talking head with B-roll laid over "doesn't really work anymore". Denney (2025-01): single-creator testimonials beat compilations in 2024; (2026-08/09) run-of-the-mill UGC is C tier and VO + B-roll problem-solution "just feels very manufactured". [YT:bNMeFhW6YmA, YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]
- **Problem-solution: dated or durable?** Denney (2026-09): the iPhone-creator version now reads as marketing. Cottrell (2026-06/10): still his most effective script, delivered casually. [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM, YT:505rIrOKhWI]
- **How much to script.** Cooper (2026): talking points, then let creators go. (guest: Anze Mikac, 2026-06): scripted won his strict-vs-loose test. Cottrell: ad-libs inside scripts; undecided on podcasts. [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w, YT:KSm0pvevV9k, YT:bNMeFhW6YmA]
- **Delivery is back.** (guest: Harry Delmege): sloppy scripts once printed because "the algorithm is literally rewarding you for doing sloppy script writing"; now delivery matters as much as words. Denney: flat script-readers will soon be noticed, "and they're not going to be down." [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk, YT:_VtpfVeB2Kc]
- **AI arms race.** Hott: as AI fakes authenticity better, do "things that the AI wouldn't easily do or couldn't easily do"; the lavalier-mic look brands copied is already being dropped by creators.
