# Demand Creation vs Demand Capture — The Master Framework

> Source corpus: Chris Walker founder-era Refine Labs material (2019–July 2025).
> This is the single most important distinction in modern B2B marketing.
> Get this wrong and every downstream tactic — content, paid, attribution,
> sales handoff — gets pulled in the wrong direction.

---

## The Core Distinction

There are exactly two jobs marketing can do.

**Demand Capture** — serve buyers who are already in-market, have already
identified the problem, are already evaluating solutions, and are looking
to buy. They typed "best [category] software" into Google. They asked
their peer in a Slack community. They responded to a retargeting ad
because they remembered the brand. You don't create their intent — you
intercept it.

**Demand Creation** — build awareness, preference, and trust with buyers
who are NOT in-market yet — and won't be for 6, 12, 24 months — so that
when they enter the buying window, you are already the obvious choice.
You shape their problem-framing. You teach them what to look for. You
make them remember the company name before they ever consider buying.

These are different jobs. They use different channels. They have
different metrics. They require different content. They typically need
different humans running them.

Most B2B firms collapse them into one function called "demand gen,"
measure both with MQLs, run all the budget through capture channels,
and then wonder why pipeline is flat. That is the bug. It's not a
nuance. It's the entire diagnosis for most B2B companies under $100M ARR.

---

## Why This Matters: The 95/5 Reality

At any given moment in any B2B category, roughly:
- 5% of the total addressable market is actively in-market
- 95% is out-of-market — they have the problem but aren't shopping yet

If 100% of your marketing budget is in demand-capture channels (paid
search, retargeting, review-site sponsorships, gated lead-gen ads,
content syndication, SDR cold outreach), you are competing with every
other vendor for the same 5%. You will pay more per lead every quarter.
Your CAC will rise. Your sales cycles will lengthen. Your win rate will
fall. The 5% you fight over is shrinking relative to your spend.

The companies that win the 95% — the ones who are remembered, trusted,
and preferred BEFORE the buyer enters the market — pay less for the 5%
because the 5% is biased toward them on the way in.

This is why Walker says: "you are running demand capture and calling it
demand gen." If everything you run is intercept-the-in-market, you have
no demand-creation engine. You will plateau.

## The Channel Mapping

**Demand Capture channels** (intercept existing intent):
- Google Search (branded + non-branded)
- Bing / DuckDuckGo search
- Retargeting (Meta, LinkedIn, display)
- Review site sponsorships (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius)
- Affiliate / partner referrals at decision stage
- SDR outbound cold outreach (when targeting in-market accounts)
- Bottom-of-funnel content: pricing pages, comparison pages, demo CTAs,
  ROI calculators

**Demand Creation channels** (build preference upstream):
- Podcasts (owned and guest appearances)
- YouTube long-form video
- LinkedIn organic (executive + employee)
- Paid social distribution of native content (not gated ebooks — actual
  educational video and posts that live on-platform)
- Communities (owned Slack, Discord, peer groups)
- Events (own + sponsored, in-person)
- Newsletter (broad-distribution, not gated)
- Dark social: WhatsApp shares, DMs, peer conversations, Slack
  community recommendations — channels that don't get tracked by your
  attribution software but drive most of your real word-of-mouth

The Walker rule: anywhere people consume content for entertainment,
education, or peer-bonding — not to buy — is a demand-creation channel.
Anywhere people land when they're already shopping is a demand-capture
channel. Mixing them up is the most common GTM mistake.

## Budget Allocation: The Calibrated Recommendation

Walker's data across 200+ B2B SaaS companies (most Series A-D) shows
that 60-80% of total marketing program budget should be allocated to
demand-creation activities (content production, distribution, paid
social as distribution, podcast, community, brand). The remaining
20-40% goes to demand capture (search, retargeting, review sites,
bottom-funnel sales enablement).

The reality at most B2B firms:
- 95% capture / 5% creation (or worse, 100/0)
- Reason: capture is easier to measure, so finance approves it
- Result: pipeline plateaus, CAC rises, sales blames marketing,
  marketing blames sales

The correction is rarely "spend more money." It is "reallocate
existing spend from capture to creation." Cut gated lead-gen ads on
LinkedIn. Cut the content syndication contract. Cut the SDR-as-MQL-
chaser headcount. Put the money into a podcast, a YouTube channel,
LinkedIn paid distribution of native (ungated) educational content,
and the people to produce it weekly.

This will feel like flying blind to a CFO trained on attribution
dashboards. That's the point — see Self-Reported Attribution.

## Why Demand Capture Alone Fails: The CAC Spiral

When everyone in your category is fighting for the same 5%:
- Cost-per-click rises every quarter
- Cost-per-MQL rises
- The MQLs you get are lower quality (the high-intent ones go to
  the brand they already trust, which usually isn't you)
- CAC payback periods extend
- Sales productivity falls

At a Series D SaaS company Walker analyzed: $9MM annual marketing
budget, $520K of which was on LinkedIn gated-content lead-gen
campaigns, result: 1 closed-won deal from those campaigns. That's
not an aberration. That's the architecture.

The fix is not "better LinkedIn lead-gen targeting." The fix is to
stop using LinkedIn for lead-gen and start using it for distribution
of native educational content that builds preference in the 95%.

## Why Demand Creation Alone Also Fails

Creation without capture means you build preference and then have no
way for buyers to raise their hand. The website needs clear paths to
"talk to sales / book a demo / see pricing." The capture infrastructure
matters — it's just the smaller of the two budgets, not the only one.

The B→D→E framework solves this: Brand (creation) → Demand (capture) →
Expand (retention). All three exist. All three are funded. All three
have their own metrics. See `brand_demand_expand_operating_model.md`.

## The Two Metrics

**Demand Capture metric:** Pipeline Created from declared high-intent
sources, with CAC payback measured per channel. Attribution software
works here — the user took an action, you can trace the click.

**Demand Creation metric:** Self-Reported Attribution. Ask every
inbound demo "How did you hear about us?" Open-text or pick-list.
Aggregate. That data tells you what's actually creating preference.
Attribution software will miss most of it (Walker's data: 75%+
undercounted). The gap between what software reports and what buyers
self-report IS your demand-creation footprint.

Hybrid model: attribution software for capture, self-report for
creation. Run both. Trust the gap.

---

## The Common Objection: "But Self-Reported Attribution Is Subjective"

Yes. So is every marketing measurement when you trace it back far
enough. Attribution software is also subjective — it has to choose
which click to credit, which window to use, which channels to model.
The difference is self-report tells you what your customer's brain
remembers as the reason they bought. That's the only ground truth
that matters for buying decisions made by humans.

The CMO who insists on "rigorous" attribution while ignoring the
question "how did you hear about us?" is choosing the dashboard that
flatters their budget over the data that would change it.

## How to Diagnose Where a B2B Firm Sits

Ask:
1. What % of marketing budget is in creation vs capture channels?
   (Run the math from the actual P&L, not the marketing org chart.)
2. Do you have a "How did you hear about us?" field on every inbound
   demo form? Have you run the report? What does it say?
3. What % of pipeline does your attribution software credit to "direct"
   or "organic" or "unknown"? That's mostly dark social you can't see.
4. Is anyone on the team accountable for producing content weekly
   (podcast, video, LinkedIn) that lives on-platform — not gated PDFs?
5. Is the sales team's first question to inbound demos still "what
   piece of content brought you here?" — or "what made you book this?"

If the answers reveal 90%+ capture allocation, no self-report data,
high "direct/unknown" attribution share, no weekly content production,
and a sales team still trained on the old playbook — you have the
diagnosis. The prescription is to rebuild around B→D→E.

---

## The Walker Refrain

"The metric you optimize for is the metric you'll get."

Optimize for MQLs and you'll get more MQLs and less revenue.
Optimize for pipeline and you'll get pipeline.
Optimize for revenue won by source and you'll get revenue.
The dashboard isn't decorative. It's the company's actual strategy
in disguise. Change the scoreboard before you change the playbook —
or the playbook won't take.
