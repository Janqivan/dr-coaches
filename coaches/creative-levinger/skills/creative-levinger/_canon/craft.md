## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 TEAP — Trigger, Exploration, Evaluation, Purchase [GEN] (2026-06/07, NOW)
- **What:** adapted from a Google study of digital purchasing: people loop in and out of exploration and evaluation for a long time, plus a post-purchase experience loop (unboxing, shipping). Each phase = a distinct psychological state + matching format. Tested across ~150 creative strategies. Most brands run creative toward basically just two phases.
- **When:** weekly — count ads per phase to see where you over-index, then decide which ads/formats to make this week. Build a Motion library of ads tagged by phase.
- **Phase jobs (her board labels):** Trigger "Earns attention" · Exploration "Earns understanding" · Evaluation "Earns belief" · Purchase Earns the sale.
- **Format library per phase:**
  - TRIGGER (pre-awareness; don't know a solution exists): relatable micro moments, "this is so me" [quote src: YT:M2ObOuFQSv0]; only job is to name the moment and make people "feel seen, not sold to." [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] Storytelling mechanism: "something just happened." [quote src: YT:vBotjaDGNQI] No required style — parody, text overlays, product focus.
  - EXPLORATION (low commitment, love novelty): short-form video, ASMR, sticky/"gloopy" satisfying content, visual hooks, memes, "car chases", "dogs barking." Rejects anything too clever or too deep too fast.
  - EVALUATION: bring the brand in with direct response — testimonials, before/afters, education, case studies, comparisons, table-style ads.
  - PURCHASE: easiest. Long form works, founder ads work "really, really well" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8], or plain offers (50% off, free gift). Landing page must not over-explain.
- **Her examples:** Ridge rings — trigger data hook "three out of four men misplace their wedding band in their lifetime" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; exploration "He finally wears his ring after 7 years of marriage." [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] / He said I do years ago, but now his ring finally does too; evaluation table-style value ad. AutoBrush — trigger: parent whose kid hates brushing, "I just can't do this anymore" [quote src: YT:vBotjaDGNQI]; exploration: explain U-shaped brush exists and removes plaque; evaluation: why AutoBrush is better; purchase: straight purchase ad. Classifies the AutoBrush winner between exploration and evaluation ("my friend recommended this" [quote src: YT:vBotjaDGNQI] is the crossover); leading with just came back from the dentist would make it trigger.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06), [YT:vBotjaDGNQI] (2026-07), [YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06), [YT:cJlBabiKX9k] (2026-04)

### 2.2 The five "money maker" formats (static set) [DTC] (2026-05, NOW)
- **When:** tested up front with each new brand to see which fits the audience. A format "only becomes a money maker when three things line up" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]: (1) matches the customer's psychological state at that moment; (2) reduces decision friction; (3) carries the right emotional certainty ("Oh, thank god I finally found something..." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]).
- **The five:** (1) Before the problem had a name — mechanism recognition, "Oh my god, that's me." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] (2) Social mirror — promise "one teeny tiny little thing" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] (YOU CAN STOP SWITCHING YOUR SKINCARE NOW). (3) Reframe — belief updating ("HE PUT UP THE TENT, BUT YOU BROUGHT THE FLASK." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]). (4) Specificity — "weirdly almost uncomfortably specific" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] copy (Project Repeat quilt). (5) Earned confidence — for audiences who already like you; show "receipts", third-party validation, real customer footage, "proof that doesn't feel like it's produced" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]; price in a confident bold spot.
- **Audit move:** lay all winners side by side, tag each by format/mechanism, find the gap.
- "not trends… not going to disappear in 6 months" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] — built on "how people actually make decisions." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]
- Sources: [YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] (2026-05)

### 2.3 The five formats she is testing this quarter [GEN/DTC] (2026-07, NOW)
- (1) **Literal POV** — camera at the viewer's eyeline, viewer's hand in frame, result happens to "you"; mechanism mirror neurons / embodied simulation; answers "Is this me?" [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw] not "Is this for me?" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] Most "POV" ads are POV in caption only = "a testimonial with point of view slapped on top." [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw]
- (2) **Multi-series** — same ad TYPE reused by swapping elements (calories stack vs calories stack); mechanism mere exposure: by sighting 3–4 people "stop a little quicker, scroll a little slower, and stay a little longer." [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw] Keep the mechanism, change the surface.
- (3) **Subtle flex** — one iconic image, near-wordless (self-tanner legs, "before"/"after"); contrast effect + fluency.
- (4) **Micro-learning** — teach before you ask; best on carousels; card 1 lays out what you'll learn with simple icons, each card one level deeper; "Format comes secondary to the sequence" [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw]; mechanism reciprocity; carousels qualify (only the interested swipe).
- (5) **Lo-fi yap** — one take, phone, off-center, no cuts, no b-roll, no lighting; ad-filter scan comes up empty.
- Sources: [YT:a5ruZB-Urlw] (2026-07)

### 2.4 Micro movies [GEN] (2025-10 → 2026-07)
- **Definition:** a tiny narrative compressed into a 30–60 s "trailer" (fits 15 s to 60 s+): setup → tension/conflict → emotional payoff. Not about long ads or high production. less novel, more movie trailer. One specific snapshot of a scene where the viewer completes the story (M&M's bumping into each other and spilling Santa's cookies: "could be a movie" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]).
- **Build steps:** (1) define the emotional job (stuck → hopeful, invisible → seen, overwhelmed → calm); (2) pick structure: transformation → before-after-bridge; urgency → AIDA or PAS; identity shift → Hero's Journey or Pixar; (3) write the human story ignoring the product, as if telling a friend, ending in emotional resolution; (4) drop product at the shift point (~40–50% in: ~12–15 s of 30 s, ~30 s of 60 s). Product = bridge, person = hero.
- Predicted as the biggest 2026 trend; campaign-feeling ads "are going to get filtered out pretty early." [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]
- Sources: [YT:8WRS3-y4K98] (2025-12), [YT:UAvSFd3qF8c] (2025-10), [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12), [YT:vBotjaDGNQI] (2026-07)

### 2.5 For You page alignment test [GEN] (2025-12, EVERGREEN)
On top of the core question "where is this going?": (1) Would this fit naturally between two creator videos? ("No commercials"; story first, product second.) (2) Can a stranger feel the point in under one second? (3) Does it start with a human moment before a claim? (4) Can the viewer predict the payoff is worth staying for? [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]

### 2.6 Open loops: "where is this going?" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] [GEN] (2025-12)
The one question viewers should ask. Loops are alignment/identity ("oh, I love Christmas cookies... let's see where this goes") or bizarre ("why is that happening") — "it's really only just shock or alignment these days." Close the loop by making it personal ("This is my bread clip"). Exit the way they entered: "Anything you come in with, you need to go out with." Every story needs an emotional resolution or the brain gets angry ("everyone's dead effect"). [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY], [YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]

### 2.7 Story structure and the 15-framework prompt [GEN] (2025-12 → 2026-06)
- Long rising action, then "down down down" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] to resolution; setup decides where the viewer ends up mentally ("His setup was 51 seconds long" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]).
- Concept development = HOW / WHICH / WHAT. HOW = narrative structure: open on the problem, on the outcome, or in the middle. "the sequence of your story matters almost more than what's in the story." [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] Favorites: hero's journey, Freytag's pyramid, in medias res.
- Story prompt: feed the found subtext/implicit need into a prompt holding 15 narrative frameworks collected over ~2–3 years from comedians, novelists, TED talkers (AIDA, Hero's Journey, three-act, a pyramid); returns 15 script structures (how to build, not what to say). Frameworks differ mainly in tension: low = calm/safe, high = involved.
- Drill: tell the story of your worst food poisoning, set up to make people feel a specific way.
- Sources: [YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (2025-12), [YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06), [YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] (2026-02)

### 2.8 Two-second grab vs full hook [GEN] (2026-02)
Split seconds 0–2 (the grab) from the full hook (typically 3–5 or 5–7 s). The first two seconds must do something interesting or carry open-loop text or nobody reaches second 5. Endorses an attendee scale: celebrity face "a 1", an angle cut "a 1.5" — small grabs, not the whole hook. Group exercise: play ~7 s of an organic video, ask "what millisecond... you decide to keep watching", then separate what happened in 0–2 s from the payoff people credit (they credit the car crash; the text already held them). Review every hook sound on and sound off. [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]

### 2.9 Four 300-millisecond hook elements [GEN] (2026-06, NOW)
(1) Human face mid-emotion (animal faces work, human most). (2) Motion that doesn't match the scroll (something moving down, or unexpected). (3) Visual that breaks format — something in the wrong spot (laptop in a fridge), or a partial view that turns into something else ("Is it real or is it cake?"). (4) Precognitive sound — a familiar pattern like a phone conversation. Every hook is three in one: visual + sound (or silence) + text; spoken hook "can come at that 3 seconds." Build from these, "No more templates." [YT:DEoj28QXUYE]

### 2.10 Predictive hook generation with the feelings wheel [GEN] (2026-03, NOW)
- Before loading hooks, decide the emotion and customer type per hook. Start at the core (happy, surprised, bad, fearful, angry, disgusted, sad), move outward ring by ring (~200 micro-emotions).
- Vocabulary ladder from child development: ages 3–5 core words; 6–8 next ring (confused vs startled, bored vs stressed); teens distinct language (confident vs respected). Outer-ring words "help us build a better hook." [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc]
- Don't go deep: one step past the core, compare two neighbors, use the tool to pick.
- Team exercise (~12 people): play hook + first one or two clips; "How did you respond to this?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc] "Which avatar were you going after?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc] "Which emotion?" Different answers = hook not targeting one emotion. Run it on yourself: if you can't tell, the customer can't.
- Pairs she has ruled on: curious > inquisitive (stronger information gap); pressured > rushed for TOF ("I feel pressure deeper in my body than I feel rushed" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc]).
- Sources: [YT:LxWwRykBtfc] (2026-03)

### 2.11 Distinction (von Restorff) effect, used properly [GEN] (2025-05)
Two parts: blend (look like organic — "This is why I love ugly ads") + inside the ad make one thing the opposite of normal (one red ball among blue; a perfect breakfast with one burnt, smoking pancake). Call out something that "shouldn't be doing what it's doing and make it weird", then say your point. Reading it as "I need a good hook" uses it "very lazily." "Contrast equals memorability." [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU], extended [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02)

### 2.12 Zones and selves (video emotional mapping) [GEN] (2026-03)
- Map an ad by emotional zone (2x2 valence/intensity grid, zones 1–4) and "self" (ought / actual / ideal). Norse winner: mostly zone 3, touches of zone 4, quick zone 1 lift at the end; ought ("should should should" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]) → actual (calm solution) → ideal ("heal by itself" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]).
- Diagnose failure as channels disagreeing: words in one zone, delivery in another = the ad "is pulling itself apart." [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]
- Music by self: ought = somber; ideal = hype ("Eye of the Tiger kind of music" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]); zone 1 = jazz or calming piano; zone 4 = upsetting/sad. Match the track to the dominant self rather than switching per zone.
- Iterating a winner: most swap the hook per self; she prefers reworking the whole video to the chosen self.
- Endorses an attendee's idea to combine zones + self into a static-generating skill: "smash it together and then you have a perfect static." [quote src: YT:cJlBabiKX9k]

## 3. Templates, rules and exact wording

### 3.1 Hook building blocks
- **High-potency words applied to mundane things** [GEN]: "today's victim", "they thought it was safe", "she accidentally did...", "buckle up". Shampoo version: "today's victim" = your 69-year-old mom whose hair you'll help today. Don't make it political or trigger people badly; Meta tags intense words and "will start to look for it and feed it a little bit better." [YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (2025-12)
- **Silent hooks are fine** [GEN]: hooks needn't be intense, loud or questions; even "just a black screen with like a dot in the middle." "Hooks are only meant to get the brain to stop on something." [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02)
- **Visual contrast** [GEN]: make the object "look like it's not supposed to be that way" on a very simple background; red on contrasty background ("we're built to look at things close, especially if it's red"; men especially); show size/color/sound differences vs competitors. [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Odd environment** [GEN]: product where it shouldn't be (shampoo stuffed in a car; computer in the fridge) — brain goes "wrong. Why is that there?" [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **About to fall** [DTC]: product halfway off the table triggers the catch reflex and comments ("it bothers me that it's coming off the table"). "Put things slightly off of what they're supposed to be on." Nerf: guns halfway off the table + "Are you in or are you out?" [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Real-world objects that don't belong on a phone** [GEN]: sticky notes "worked incredibly well for a long long time"; find other such objects. [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05)
- **Animals** [GEN]: "Anytime you put an animal in an ad, it's probably gonna work." [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Arrows and pointing hands** [GEN]: "always put arrows in your ad" (cites studies). [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Pairing**: an overly grabbing visual makes people skip the text — be careful what text you pair with it (agrees with attendee). [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Templates to avoid** [GEN]: "I went from X to Y," "why are we still doing X?," "my main complaints about this industry" — the industry recycles "basically four different hooks"; when they "all sound the same, the brain starts to tune it out." [YT:DEoj28QXUYE] (2026-06)

### 3.2 Static rules
- Headline ≤8 words, 4–7 sweet spot; "The longer your uh core concept, the less you need to stuff into that ad" — then just a color background. Native customer language, no jargon, no clever puns. [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05)
- Image carries the feeling (images processed ~60,000x faster than text); copy backs it up. Statics fail mainly when image and copy "fight against each other." [YT:MAwkpQWEQS8] (2024-10)
- Copy-heavy (Pinterest-like: headline, subheads, bullets, ≥2 buttons) or extremely copy-light, never "copy mid" (tiny headline + five stars + quote). [YT:MAwkpQWEQS8]
- One font if possible, two max; weights add processing; packaging fonts count. [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04)
- Rule of thirds; each element in its own section so the eye runs the F/Z pattern. [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]
- "think before you put statics together": designers bold what the brand says converts (the CTA) and blur headline vs subhead. [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]
- Don't use brand colors, especially blue or white (Meta's colors): please please please don't use your brand colors inside ads. Pink background has done well "in every single industry." [YT:MAwkpQWEQS8] (2024-10)
- Extreme italics (~35°+ slant) on the offer, CTA line upright — forward slant implies forward movement (study below). [YT:kHUSl110eUM] (2025-01)
- Hands holding the product (size perception) and products sequenced in natural color order raise conversion (cited studies): "everybody go sequence your freaking products." [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- Static for bootstrapped teams: "how you say it matters more than almost anything else"; any visual works if it shows the right world (mom in a kitchen = mom world). [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12)
- Competitors in the same feed: angle ads off as many distinct identities as possible. [YT:zDHkSSsjFDw] (2025-02)

### 3.3 Video craft rules
- Speed up cuts; common UGC mistake is one angle "for way too freaking long" (slow only for deliberate calm). "Camera work is your job as a creative strategist." [YT:CwSAZdtqemk]

- Cheap B-roll: screenshots and found images; match visual to the verb ("she went running" → a runner) or the subject. Prove claims with "receipts" and circled numbers (magnitude effect). [YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]

## 6. What changed over time and where she contradicts herself
- **Hook window shrinks: 3 s → 1 s → 300 ms.** 2026-02 still benchmarks hooks at 3–5/5–7 s with a "two second grab" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]; 2025-12 "We don't have 3 seconds anymore. You got one second" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]; 2026-06 "We are off by about 2.7 seconds," [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE] hooks built from 300 ms elements. Reconcile: grab in <1 s, spoken hook can land ~3 s.
- **Statics-only → video-heavy.** 2024-10 she produced no video and said statics alone can scale an account; 2025-12 still "double down" on statics; by 2026-02–07 she films UGC herself (AutoBrush), teaches micro movies, and tests five video/static formats. Statics remain the cheap message-validation layer.
- **Copy-heavy statics → near-wordless.** 2024-10 her statics were deliberately copy-heavy (headline, subheads, bullets, two buttons) with the escape or extremely copy-light; 2025-05 headlines ≤8 words; 2026-07 winners are near-wordless and effort "reads to the brain as doubt." [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw]
- **Ugly/lo-fi vs polished.** "This is why I love ugly ads" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) and "Please don't" be pretty (2026-06), yet the 2026-05 five-format statics all share a polished DTC template with press strips and badges. Polish lives in statics; lo-fi in video.
- **Brand colors.** 2024-10: never use brand colors in ads. 2026-02: brief Tumble UGC sets into the brand palette and avoid the rival's color. Distinction: ad backgrounds vs set dressing/priming.
- **Education.** 2026-04 against over-educating; 2026-03 Norse "didn't overeducate"; but 2025-12 education-in/education-out and 2026-07 micro-learning carousels (teaches before it asks). The line is sequence and dose, not none.
