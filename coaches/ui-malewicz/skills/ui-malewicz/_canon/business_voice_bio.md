## PART 5. Design business and careers, how he talks, his background

### Rules (ranked by how often they say it)
- **Make the case study a visual ride that proves detail, not a term paper** — Top-1% factors: eye-catching visuals, attention to detail showing decisions rest on more than a hunch (hierarchy strips for UI work), dropping the boring factor. Proof sections: a plain-spoken usability section in the first 2-3 of 5 cases (varied each time; sounding junior is fine), accessibility self-critique only in the first case, before/after of a state like the "open only" filter, photo-consistency tweaks mentioned in interviews. Why: ~100,000 people enter UX yearly, a recruiter sees you plus 20-30 others the same day, maybe 1% read a whole case. [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:w2mNSOWiZbU, YT:u01ZGoiLJ70] (2025-11, 2026-09, n.d.) · mentions 8
- **Do not compete on price; charge for what sits outside AI** — AI makes work 10-15x faster, so freelancers, small agencies and medium/large businesses all eventually cut prices (speed cannot differentiate, quality is subjective). Polish what surrounds the AI output (handmade annotations, 20+ years across categories, personality, own processes) instead of cutting prices weekly, which ends in burnout. For juniors AI takes what they call their job: what did learning prompt engineering do for you, extend your agony a few months? [YT:iSC6JgFBZWg] (2025-10) · mentions 6
- **Serious clients still pay skilled people; de-slop requests are rising** — A growing stream of "can you un-AI my app?" emails; three recent talks on slop drew the same report from attendees. Corporate clients (200+ staff, all using AI) laughed at when will AI replace your agency: they want someone else to own the output, can judge outputs only in their niche, and their developers report ~25% horrible or breaking code on long prompts. He admits "de-slop" may be cope, but says it comes from disliking mediocre products. [YT:HQpQhmJi22s, YT:zatFKQqtmuw, YT:vUuOlCjP8VY, YT:lpqTva43Bxo] (2026-05 to 2026-07) · mentions 6
- **"Stop selling a visual. Start selling outcomes."** — Pixels are "a dead end" and prices are dropping. Sell a plan to maximize the client's reach or money: strategy, planning, post-launch ideas, testing, research, design as the cherry on top. Same-looking site: $59 template; about $1,000-$4,000 (intentional copy, near $4,000 only if annotated); $10,000+ pays for what you cannot see. As models get pricier, what you deliver must make sense to the client. [YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI, YT:3BvDDjmzYY8, YT:lpqTva43Bxo] (2025-10, 2026-07) · mentions 5
- **The middle thins out; survivors push above 7/10** — ~80% of bad designers go; survivors go above 7/10, above 8/10 every time, not by repeating templates. "Most people designing right now shouldn't be designers" (no curiosity, drag-and-drop Figma components); design is bloated, especially design systems, and shrinks a little; curious designers who leave the comfort zone thrive. Non-technical teams skipping designers and developers get "comically bad" results. A skilled person with the same AI tools beats someone typing a prompt; he says Anthropic hired designers at $400k+/year right after Claude Design. [YT:HQpQhmJi22s, YT:vUuOlCjP8VY, YT:qEwzCPGt9BQ, YT:MpuMy5yipHM] (2026-04 to 2026-06) · mentions 5
- **Annotate every decision; it lifts you above "just a Figma file"** — A designer who explains reasoning and shows how to modify, test and refine the design later earns the higher price; annotating why a page works shows clients you know what is going on. In the Blueprint he teaches packaging details like leading lines into client-facing value. Since 2026 clients distrust deliverables (they imagine a typed prompt): show process (annotations, paper sketches, explorations) and sell design as a process. [YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI, YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4, YT:wrj4GDcLqJY, YT:_PJLefMZwko] (2025-10 to 2026-09) · mentions 4
- **Own the decision; never downgrade yourself** — Never write "aspiring designer" or "junior designer", just "I'm a designer". Propose as an idea we should test and add how you will test it; clients hire you to take the process off their plate, so do not shower them with questions ("who's the designer here?"). Biggest change in the field: nobody owns work any more (committees, split responsibility, bad design); it does not happen in 2-3 person agencies. [YT:VUY8dOEJSaM, YT:u01ZGoiLJ70] (2023-10, n.d.) · mentions 4
- **Build 3-5 varied case studies** — At least 3, ideally about 5 (a lone project looks lazy; more overwhelms). Vary types: one or two apps, one website, one extraordinary thing (smartwatch app, browser extension, parking meter, ATM, smart fridge). First three research-heavy, the last two to five can lean hi-fi; never repeat the same skill; a small pure-UI case is a welcome breath of fresh air. [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:VUY8dOEJSaM] (2023-10, 2025-11) · mentions 4
- **Tailor every resume; scope beats adjectives** — Research what the employer uses and move it to the top (a member put Sketch up top and got the job at one of ~8% of Sketch firms); niche-match (beer brand = mention the Pilsner project; mobile firm = stress mobile in every job plus the Google cert). Never send one generic resume. Bullets = role plus verifiable scope; soft skills proven by what you did. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08) · mentions 4
- **Small teams are the future** — "Agencies that get it" are 1-10 people; above ten, orgs are less focused and slower to change. Managers demand impossible output because they fear AI more than staff do ("Being afraid of the machine made them into a machine"); his advice: go solo, optimise only when done sanely. [YT:iSC6JgFBZWg, YT:twmPjqYedaw, YT:_PJLefMZwko, YT:u01ZGoiLJ70] (2025-10 to 2026-05, n.d.) · mentions 4
- **Portfolio UI shots: real product screens first, onboarding never alone** — Onboarding alone is "a dead giveaway" of quick artwork and "one of the most lazy designer exercises"; he builds his from the app's own assets. At least six completely different screens for course feedback, many more for a portfolio. Cutouts with custom shadows show "pixel skills": "go that extra mile". [YT:FBDVzr0peO4] (2025-10) · mentions 4
- **Communicate and negotiate; it outlasts every tool** — The most important skill of almost any job is articulating ideas and why you did something: use data, be clear, non-invasive but firm and very confident. "You're going to be negotiating all your life" (PMs and developers too). About 90% of designers do not realize they owe tax on freelance income; for NDA-blocked work, write into the contract that it may be shown, and that removal on request costs more. [YT:c3vssjpwSX4, YT:u01ZGoiLJ70] (2024-10, n.d.) · mentions 3
- **Certificates barely matter; the portfolio gets the job** — "Just a JPEG", "a PNG diploma": one or two help, obscure ones hurt. Test: Google the school, 1-2 results = remove it; keep the real certificate in case they ask. Pick ONE (Google reads best to non-tech people). Coursera Google UX: do segments 1-4 and stop (segment 5 is UI); the certificate is worthless and peer grading pointless. He analyzed hundreds of junior portfolios over two years. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:VUY8dOEJSaM] (2023-10 to 2026-08) · mentions 3
- **Pick your target employer type first** — About 5% are "UX-heavy" (loud on social media, religious about process, footer links to every UX method, which he calls BS plus an SEO trick); 90-95% are "GSD" (get stuff done), skip most UX process and do not fail for it. UX-heavy means a bigger team, mentoring and better pay, but easy mass layoffs, slow pacing and NDA-limited portfolios. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:c3vssjpwSX4] (2024-10, 2026-08) · mentions 3
- **Learn UI and landing pages; visuals get you hired** — Not every business needs a mobile app, every business needs a landing page; companies do not hire juniors for apps but do for landing pages, "and a lot of them". After UX basics learn UI: visual design is easiest to assess. Accept that a lot of UX is boring research and problem solving, not floating glass cards. [YT:aGTqFp7GAKE, YT:VUY8dOEJSaM] (2023-10, 2024-10) · mentions 3
- **Portfolio lives on your own site, and you track it** — firstname-lastname.com (a link beats a PDF download); Wix, Webflow, WordPress, Framer or a friend who codes. Run Hotjar or similar: scrolled past = boring, never clicked = not seen as a button, unclicked case = thumbnail sucks, make it more vibrant. Same projects on Dribbble and Behance ("consistency is key"). Not Figma (a big red flag); PDF is the worst. [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:VUY8dOEJSaM] (2023-10, 2025-11) · mentions 2
- **Network and write publicly** — "People need to know you exist": write your learning story on Medium and LinkedIn (UX Collective contributing author), join communities, comment on others' work, show work everywhere, mentor others. [YT:VUY8dOEJSaM, YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2023-10, 2026-08) · mentions 2
- **Be a generalist; learn how businesses work** — Only about 5% of the biggest companies hire for narrow niches; CSS and 3D sit close to design; the most impactful thing to learn is how businesses work. Endorses Shopify dropping the titles UX and content designer: with AI tools and set patterns, a narrow UX or UI split makes no sense. [YT:u01ZGoiLJ70, YT:f3qjhQG2prU] (2025-10, n.d.) · mentions 2
- **No experience: build it** — Freelance plus unpaid NGO/nonprofit work counts as real design experience; a month or two helping a small nonprofit makes a resume feel experienced (exact layout in Recipe library). [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08) · mentions 2
- **Do not build another SaaS now; no-skill vibe coders ship now** — SaaS economics are breaking: 15-20 years of MRR even with zero recurring cost, lifetime pricing preferred, subscription fatigue, a recession coming, and a cited prediction that ~60% of SaaS gets built into LLMs. In months nobody will care about no-skill vibe-coded apps. [YT:MpuMy5yipHM] (2026-04) · mentions 2
- **Vet employers and clients** — Research the division's people on LinkedIn, Google their names, read past-employee review sites. Freelance: research the client, take a 50% advance, release full files only after full payment; hostility to that means they plan not to pay. Present it as normal practice, never as suspicion. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08) · mentions 1
- **Career path: roadmap, then a fast agency first** — Have a long-term roadmap (e.g., 2 years agency, 2 years freelance, then agency plus a startup or two). First work 1-2 years at a fast 95% agency, even if you want Google; where seniors are, do not go remote that first year, relocate to the office. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08) · mentions 1
- **Do not jump in with a trend; ask, craft the copy, keep testing** — Ask many questions, work out who the site is for and how it benefits them, craft every part of the copy, then keep testing. [YT:Qb7FVefKH3I] (2025-10) · mentions 1

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Resume teardown checklist
- When they use it: building or reviewing a junior/mid designer resume (his 279-minute resume course). Only step 1 is explicitly first.
- Steps: 1. Define the employer type you target (UX-heavy ~5% or GSD 90-95%). 2. Research what that employer uses, move it to the top, tailor the niche. 3. Write bullets as role plus verifiable scope. 4. Keep education short (no primary school; no high school if you have a university; school type, location, degree, years). 5. Keep one or two certificates, Google each school. 6. Prove soft skills with what you did. 7. Interests only if sure the reviewer shares them.
- Sources: [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08)

#### Three-price test (same site at $59, $1K-$4K, $10K+)
- When they use it: explaining why outcomes cost more, and as a client-side check on any quote.
- Steps: 1. Show three identical-looking sites. 2. $59 = pretty template, filler text, no logic, delivered as an image or Figma file. 3. $1,000-$4,000 = same visual plus copy written with intention showing benefits and outcomes (near $4,000 only if the designer annotates it). 4. $10,000+ = strategy, content, hierarchy, planning, research, guidance, post-launch strategy. 5. Client asks what is included; "just the image" is not worth it, and asking brings the price down.
- Their example: car analogy, two identical-looking cars, one maintained and one just washed: "You're paying for what's under the hood". Confidence alone is not the full explanation of higher prices. [quote src: YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI]
- Sources: [YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI] (2025-10)

#### UI sandwich case study
- When they use it: every portfolio case study (tops: 600-wide, white page, 12-column grid, 24 and 48 spacing).
- Steps: 1. Top: a beautiful grid of many screens. 2. Middle: your perspective on how you approached and solved the problem, with photos, quotes and thinking at each stage (storytelling sells, not the longest paper). 3. Prove detail with hierarchy strips (coloured boxes on logical groups, dashed "2x" white-space overlay, contrast ratios, 44x44 touch targets). 4. Bottom: a different set of visuals.
- Their example: a student's skateboard-app case with photos of himself interviewing 14-year-old skateboarders at a skate park, then photographing them with the app. Reverse-engineered cases are fine: ask 3 to 23 people, test with 3 users; how you worked matters more than the project.
- Sources: [YT:VUY8dOEJSaM, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:u01ZGoiLJ70] (2023-10, 2025-11, n.d.)

#### 30-day landing-page header challenge (learn by copying)
- When they use it: beginners on his platform (Pro-only); copying an exact result is "one of the best ways to learn" when starting. [quote src: YT:EaENLPTAt2E]
- Steps: 1. Copy each of the 30 headers one-to-one. 2. After 10 days, try a V2 with changes. 3. Post daily; AI-bot feedback arrives within minutes, plus daily human feedback. 4. Result: 30 unique headers for a portfolio that freelancers can tweak and sell to clients.
- Sources: [YT:aGTqFp7GAKE] (2024-10)

#### 2023 learning path to a first design job
- Steps: 1. Google UX course on Coursera, segments 1-4 only (cheap, scholarships exist, auditing is free but no assignments or certificate). 2. His free "Becoming a Designer" playlist (UX fundamentals, "no-bullshit approach"), then "Learn UI Design for Free"; or his paid courses (red square method, hierarchy strips, type framing). 3. Portfolio site with 2-3 case studies. 4. Network.
- Their example: vet any teacher by real client projects over many years (many pretenders).
- Sources: [YT:VUY8dOEJSaM] (2023-10)

### Fix library (problem → fix → why)
- **Problem:** experience bullets are vague, filler ("I was tasked with designing apps and websites") or fake impact (+17% conversion, $2M extra sales, a 5 billion valuation increase) → **Fix:** role plus verifiable scope: delivered 26 web campaigns for clients like X; visual identity plus a main portal with 200 unique pages; designed and coded nearly 40 websites → **Why:** scope can be checked, invented impact cannot. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08)
- **Problem:** 20 applicants a day blur together (same shared project, two personas, same empathy map, similar paragraph length) → **Fix:** change type, structure, research section and usability method per case; make it personal and quirky → **Why:** recruiters scan, and maybe 1% read a whole case study. [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs] (2025-11)
- **Problem:** clients distrust deliverables because they imagine you only typed a prompt → **Fix:** show process (annotations, paper sketches, explorations, ideas) and sell design as a process → **Why:** one outcome plus a Figma invite is far less profitable. [YT:_PJLefMZwko] (2026-03)
- **Problem:** key visuals and copy feel AI-made, abstract or irrelevant ("un-AI my app") → **Fix:** de-slop: remove the AI feel, abstraction and irrelevance; tie visual plus copy to the client's bottom line → **Why:** designers who do this and repeat the work win over generic generative abstractions. [YT:lpqTva43Bxo, YT:HQpQhmJi22s] (2026-06, 2026-07)
- **Problem:** two years of content marketing at 12 hours per article, mostly wasted because articles and sites were not SEO-optimized → **Fix:** run an SEO audit tool and patch the flagged issues, no change to content strategy → **Why:** traffic +23.5% and unique visitors +25% in just over a month. [YT:eOuOWSmTH2o] (2025-10)

### Coaching and critique moves

- Price probe: ask how much you would pay for the site and "can you tell me why?" [YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI]
