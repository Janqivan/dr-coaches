## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Problem-solution script (Cottrell; Denney's "perfect UGC ad" [quote src: YT:Yi_1Vb4sfoQ])
- When they use it: default script for any product with a real, common problem; where Denney tells brands new to UGC to start; then listicle, tutorial.
- Steps (Cottrell 2026): 1. Hook with the problem or tease the solution. 2. Break the problem down. 3. Agitate it — viewers may suffer but not think it's that bad. 4. Solution + benefit, then add small details (fabric, strap) that "are now becoming part of the solution" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI]. 5. Proof. 6. CTA. Weave social proof, product proof and objection handling throughout. Don't jump to the solution right after the hook (Denney).
- Denney modules: hook → problem agitator → product as solution → specific benefits/features → authentic testimonial → catchy CTA; add-ons: social proof, competitor comparison, how-to, shipping.
- Savannah (2020): under 15s — question/problem open, product as solution, last ~2s CTA.
- Their example: Flakes dandruff recreate — sulfates/parabens → "throw out your big brand" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA] → 2x pyrithione zinc → failed solutions from reviews → "can wear black shirts again" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA] (Cooper).
- Sources: YT:505rIrOKhWI (2026-06), YT:RExPkxyRB6o (2026-10), YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q (2025-01), YT:6OmF0BzBOHQ (2020-05)

### The million dollar villain and hero script (Cottrell)
- When they use it: products with an enemy — an industry, a competitor, a situation, or a habit; recyclable through every video.
- Steps: 1. Hook. 2. Set up the villain. 3. Say how bad it is and twist the knife — list of reasons "as long as your arm" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ]. 4. Reveal the hero (product), show how it fixes the villain's problems, list USPs. 5. Handle an objection. 6. Social proof. 7. CTA. (2026 five-part: hook → villain → hero → demo/proof → CTA.)
- Rules: product can wait to ~40s if relatable; shoot the hero epically (probe lens) against unappealing villain footage; most common mistake: "the payoff just isn't worth it" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ]. Denney (2026-04): David-and-Goliath works more when paired with "the science of why you are better" [quote src: YT:ePcEcT2PH8E], ideally animated.
- Their example: Leaf Shave — "shaving companies got greedy" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ], cartridges "cost more than the razor itself" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ], product at 40s, hero "all metal, zero plastic" [quote src: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ].
- Sources: YT:4o46eGI6XwQ (2025-04), YT:KKp3rA0QK9A (2026-01), YT:SMZyaVvw76c (2025-02)

### Hook – Body – Close (Cottrell / Fraggle)
- When they use it: structure check on every script; body is where scripts fail.
- Steps: 1. Hook — visual + audible, built to target the right person. 2. Body — emotional journey (curious → wanting → craving), opens loops, resolves the objection in the viewer's head (A to B). 3. Close — what to do, where to go, what they get; benchmark TikTok Shop closes.
- Rules: body written like a product brief → hold rate "absolute pants". Every winning script has objection handling, social proof from people just like them, and a strong CTA. Pitch person-to-person ("Listen up, girls..." [quote src: YT:IX-2-uSJDk8]), break usage into simple steps, knock out remaining objections in a list.
- Sources: YT:IX-2-uSJDk8 (2026-06), YT:GCugREdurJk (2026-01), YT:tVXjXJSEjeA (2025-07)

### Mini VSL / VSL (Cottrell; Denney S tier)
- When they use it: TOF and some MOF; health, wellness, fitness, anything needing upfront education or trust.
- Steps: 1. Problem. 2. Twist the knife. 3. Establish speaker authority early. 4. Educate. 5. Delay product until near the end so it's "a very easy sell" [quote src: YT:oZFiSUkJEPE]; sell "as hard as you can" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo], position as the only solution. Length 1:00–1:30 mixing many footage types.
- Rules: "With a bad script, you're going to have a bad video." [quote src: YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q] Take inspiration from documentaries ("mini product documentaries" [quote src: YT:QoGFOGEqijw]), not ad libraries. Prefer a human VO artist (a couple hundred) over AI for emotion; brand-voiced VO reads as authority, worked with older demographics. VSL is the one format still all B-roll. Guests: Mirella — 5–45 min VSLs work on Facebook, often to quiz/long video not PDP; Harry Delmege — AI VO + B-roll with really ridiculous copywriting became top performer.
- Their example: Leaf Thorn "Traditional razors hide behind marketing. The Leaf Thorn exposes the truth." [quote src: YT:bNMeFhW6YmA] — one spaced-caps word per literal visual; top performer.
- Sources: YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q (2025-11), YT:oZFiSUkJEPE (2026-03), YT:QoGFOGEqijw (2026-05), YT:KmbEjy0UBT8 (2026-08)

### Founder ad formula (Denney, from "hundreds" of top founder ads)
- When they use it: trust, price justification, higher-consideration or unusual products.
- Steps: 1. "my name is … and I'm the founder of" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM]. 2. Personal problem/friction. 3. Turning point — why you were the one to build it. 4. Solution — "go ham" on features/benefits. 5. Proof — buyers, lives changed, guarantee/risk-free. 6. Soft, genuine CTA.
- Variants: lo-fi founder answering a TikTok comment bubble raising the top objection (often price) — Denney's recommended starting point. Don't script — ask 10–20 questions (Thesis interviews). Any modern phone, no external mic, fans off; add B-roll of deep emotion. Cottrell: archive footage (prototypes, factory) + founder VO, cut in customer testimonials ("absolute dynamite"); subtle lo-fi music; zoom jump cuts; avoid AI VO for founder ads. Hott: selfie somewhere relevant, passionate, even if self-conscious about accent.
- Sources: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM (2025-06), YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q (2025-01), YT:2cT3ccIzLTU (2025-05), YT:FTotRZL9vAg (2025-04)

### Yapper ad (Denney, Levinger, Hott, Cooper)
- When they use it: 2026 replacement for scripted UGC; Denney A tier — easy-looking but right creators are hard to find; scales once script + creator + setting align.
- Steps: 1. Pick a creator who uses the product or has lived the pain (personal-history yappers open on a true story). 2. Give a structured hook + talking points + reference yappers, not a script. 3. Setting: walk-and-talk, car, bedroom, phone propped on counter. 4. One take, little or no B-roll, maybe captions, probably no overlay. 5. Keep bloopers/interruptions; watch shadows. 6. Soft-advice CTA, never "so buy now" [quote src: YT:mprXXGL42oU].
- Their example: Hott's Flock chips — one uncut 3-min take eating a bag, ran years; Levinger's Lemme gummy in-bed one-take beat creative "probably 40 times more expensive" [quote src: YT:a5ruZB-Urlw].
- Guest (Alicia, Motion) test triad: lo-fi yapper vs traditional problem-solution (clean set, memorized script) vs hi-fi leader creator.
- Sources: YT:ePcEcT2PH8E (2026-04), YT:KmbEjy0UBT8 (2026-08), YT:a5ruZB-Urlw (2026-07), YT:EgnN83jIsj8 (2026-03), YT:mprXXGL42oU (2026-04)

### Micro movie (Levinger)
- When they use it: every video should be a mini movie (30–60s "trailer"); not about length or production.
- Steps: 1. Define the emotional job (stuck → hopeful, invisible → seen, overwhelmed → calm). 2. Pick structure: transformation → before-after-bridge; urgency → AIDA/PAS; identity shift → Hero's Journey or Pixar. 3. Write the human story ignoring the product, as if to a friend, ending in resolution. 4. Drop product at the shift point (peak conflict). 5. Exit the way they entered ("came in for education... go out through education" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0]).
- Rules: "the sequence of your story matters almost more than what's in the story" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]; viewers should ask "where is this going?" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — shock or alignment; every story needs emotional resolution. Product can be a character. Her story prompt holds 15 narrative frameworks returning 15 structures with the same deep need.
- Their example: hair-loss micro movie — mom passes the product at Target, a friend's house, a window until "This is what I need." [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]
- Sources: YT:8WRS3-y4K98 (2025-12), YT:sdEV9rZ70BY (2025-12), YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY (2026-02), YT:pV0tM1gSJiU (2026-06)

### For You page alignment test (Levinger)
- Steps: 1. Would this fit between two creator videos? 2. Can a stranger feel the point in under a second? 3. Does it start with a human moment before a claim? 4. Can the viewer predict the payoff is worth staying for? Loop closes when the creator makes it personal.
- Adapt organic formats by putting the product into the creator's world (bread-clip binder → binder of ~80 shampoo labels).
- Sources: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY (2025-12), YT:sBS1vy-5Be0 (2025-12)

### Trigger-event AI animation (Cooper)
- Steps: 1. Open on the specific trigger event. 2. Agitate with repeat incidents. 3. Discovery ("A friend recommended me this" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]). 4. Mechanism and why it differs. 5. Payoff (the trigger person reverses). Hand the structure to Claude Code once trigger events are listed.
- Example: Quazi — mother-in-law "told me I looked exhausted" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]; product ~2 min in.
- Sources: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ (2026-08)

### Modular content and iteration (Denney; Cooper; guests)
- Steps: 1. Break scripts into swappable modules: hook, problem agitator, solution, features/benefits, social proof, CTA. 2. Shoot modularly: several hooks with 3–4 outfit changes, body as three scripts, several CTAs (guest Chris: one creator, one script, run and hope is "It's 2021"). 3. Iterate a winner with new hook visuals (green screen, split screen, grid), TikTok response bubble with a new headline, or a new face telling the same story. 4. Reshoot winning scripts with a different creator — "very high success rate" [quote src: YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (Cooper). 5. Heath "Frankenstein": splice a high-hook-rate opening onto a high-converting body.
- Guest Viti (Happy Mammoth): "You don't scale the ad, you scale the mechanism" [quote src: YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms] — hook (persona callout), angle, format, emotional driver.
- Sources: YT:yNTlVKvuX0o (2025-03), YT:lWpSmxuwSts (2024-11), YT:m8dbcF407H8 (2026-03), YT:bAo6SICFyIE (2026-03), YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms (2026-06)

### Savannah's app/service formula and QC
- One formula: novel hook → normal UGC sequence → CTA; only the opening changes.
- Formats: self skit (same creator, two outfits, talking to herself — her "go-to"); fake FaceTime; walk-and-talk handling objections (15–30s); photo flip (stills → video); whiteboard for complex/no-product topics; success stories; man-on-the-street.
- Edit QC: length, logical sequence, script alignment, sizes, safe zones, spelling, legible fonts, followable with and without sound, phone-size legibility, CTA; audio — level jumps, background noise, lip sync, headphone check. Watch every ad sound-off 1–2 times.
- Sources: YT:jAc63K9lRIQ (2025-02), YT:j9d6j0B3W6U (2025-02), YT:hS47--F7290 (2024-06), YT:KALGyNhfrVU (2025-07)

### Heath lead-gen video script
- Prompt build (2026-07): "You are a senior performance marketer and UGC ad script writer" [quote src: YT:TpZrPY6jTE8]; top 3 pain points; 5 scripts, one per angle, 18–20s; hook 0–3s, problem 3–8s, solution 8–15s, proof 15–18s, CTA 18–20s; score /10, keep 9.5+; suggest actor age, gender, setting, emotion.
- Service script: confident hook to camera, pause, lean → frustrated agitation ("maybe you get a response in three to five business days" [quote src: YT:M2VReAjHiN8]) → revelation with proof ("after generating over 600 million for our clients" [quote src: YT:M2VReAjHiN8]) → specific pain moment ("When your campaign breaks at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday" [quote src: YT:M2VReAjHiN8]) → offer.
- Video styles to test: UGC, influencer, founder-led, demonstration (strong for portfolio businesses), client testimonial, animation; mix them.
- Sources: YT:TpZrPY6jTE8 (2026-07), YT:M2VReAjHiN8 (2025-10) · LEADGEN

### Editing AI-generated UGC (Cottrell)
- Steps: 1. Trim so the hook starts immediately. 2. Cut AI ad-libbed lines not in the script. 3. Remove rogue frames/weird faces; scale off black bars; 9:16 30fps. 4. Add TikTok-style comment bubble, room tone ("Nowhere is completely silent" [quote src: YT:cv9GbhtGLCc]), over-dramatic music, J/L cuts, subtitles. 5. Cut to B-roll on generic lines so you're not on the AI face too long. 6. Assemble best VO and strongest shots across generations; regenerate bad shots.
- Sources: YT:ahwbstiCVpQ (2026-03), YT:lDtQRWhLXn4 (2025-12), YT:cv9GbhtGLCc (2026-02) · NOW

## 3. Templates and wording (scripts, hooks, direction)
- **Scenario-listing script** (Cottrell, Aloha): It's 11:00 a.m. You're starving. You grab a $5 protein bar. → "300 plus calories" → granola bar "with 15 g of sugar" → "That's why we created Aloha Minis." Each scenario = a sub-persona. [YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2026-08) · DTC
- **Body-timeline script** (Cottrell, General Juice): "What happens to your body when you start drinking General Juice every day?" → "Day one, every meal is a fight" → "Week one, the bloating drops" → week four → "It was never your food, it was your gut." [YT:n_EyIK5eSWY] (2026-08)

### Editing, captions, sound — working rules
- **Pacing**: mundane footage ~0.5s per clip (Denney, citing Claudia Walsh); scene change ~every second, talking head held slightly longer, text frames ≥~3s (Savannah); body scrub ~10 cuts in 6s (Hott); punch-in or slow zoom raises engagement (Denney); J/L cuts lift watch time, rarely discussed (Denney, Cottrell). Retention props: outfit/location changes, funny prop, handwritten list on wall, mixing other creators (Cottrell); rehook every 5–10s (guest: Lucas Hamby). Edit style signals age: fast = younger; slower, fewer clips, bigger fonts = older (Cooper) — but Levinger: 55+ "love information", don't slow speech for them. [YT:R1HQW38EcRA, YT:KALGyNhfrVU, YT:mVb5HKHsC3w, YT:CwSAZdtqemk]
- **Camera**: "Camera work is your job as a creative strategist" — decide POV per moment; overexaggerate the mess if the problem is messy (Levinger; Hott: "How do we visually exaggerate the problem?" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). Hott exports at 480 and upscales to look lower quality; keeps a ~90-item variable list (frame rate, upload resolution). [YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:EgnN83jIsj8]
- **Captions** — disagreement: Cottrell/Denney: native TikTok style, single line, Proxima Nova Semibold (Stories: San Francisco), no brand fonts ("screams ad"), inside safe zone. Levinger: chunk 2–3 words; brands miss the 2026 two-line creator style (static open-loop top line + speech captions). Savannah: don't mirror VO word-for-word; key points only. Cooper (2026-03): organic creators don't caption every word — hook overlay then nothing works. Denney: captions vs no-captions tests inconclusive; fewer captions perform as well. [YT:lDtQRWhLXn4, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w, YT:lWpSmxuwSts]
- **Safe zones/ratio**: keep text mid-frame, never at top/bottom edges; 4:5 and 9:16 (Savannah); start square if one ratio and check Ads Manager crops (Heath); shoot 9x16 (Hott). [YT:mMKLLRTk-7c, YT:KgO_J6pwv9I, YT:fawsUmNeCkg]
- **Sound**: add SFX and room tone, build soundscapes (Cottrell); guest Mella's sound-first method — list before/after emotions, ask what each sounds like; Levinger — music by target "self" (ought = somber, ideal = hype, calm = piano), change it only when doing micro-movies; ASMR has very high watch times (Savannah) but Denney ranks it C; never rip copyrighted trending audio — "don't depend on things you don't own" (Hott). [YT:ePcEcT2PH8E, YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ, YT:lWpSmxuwSts]

## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree
- **UGC went from default to fatigued.** 2020–24: "They want to hear from people who look like them", scripted "three reasons this is a game changer" printed money (guest Harry Delmege: the algorithm was "rewarding you for doing sloppy script writing"). 2025–26: Denney — "the jig is up", viewers know creators are paid; run-of-the-mill UGC is C tier; Cottrell — "Traditional UGC is kind of dead" [quote src: YT:2cT3ccIzLTU], B-roll cutaways no longer work; Cooper — scripted read UGC "does not work on paid social anymore" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w]. Replacement: yappers, story UGC, real creators with niche followings, founder and partnership ads. [YT:yNTlVKvuX0o, YT:4wM-i1cxtAM, YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]
- **Delivery matters again.** Guest Harry Delmege (2026-03): "what you say not how" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] held in the scripted era; now delivery matters as much. Denney (2026-08): vocal variety is the selection test.
- **Short → long.** Savannah/Denney's 2020–21 rules (9–15s, 5–15s) gave way to 2–4 min yappers, VSLs, stitched 4–5 min ads and a 10-min AI animation (Cooper 2026-08). Savannah's age rule still conditions it.
- **Sound-off → sound-on**: Cottrell (2024-11) — sound-off optimisation is ~3 years outdated.
- **Captions shrinking**: Cooper and Denney see fewer/no captions working; Levinger says the creator norm is now two lines and single-line brand captions look like ads. Both sides: Cooper [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03) vs Levinger [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02).
- **Ugly-shot method evolved**: Hott (2026-03) moved from do the opposite of a pro to "how would a creator frame this?" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]; handheld lav-mic aesthetic now adopted by brands and creators are moving away again (cat-and-mouse). Denney (2026): lo-fi is not an excuse to look unpresentable.
- **AI arms race**: Hott — AI can already fake a walk-and-talk, so real creators must do what AI can't (unique human moments, dynamic action); authenticity + authority wins. Cooper — group reaction ads can't be faked by AI. Savannah won't swap real testimonials for AI avatars. Yet Cottrell builds AI UGC mashups and claymation, and Denney rates AI animation/avatars C (fun, net-new pockets, rarely long-term).
- **Creator > script vs script > creator**: Denney/Cooper — "nine times out of 10" [quote src: YT:_VtpfVeB2Kc] it's the creator; Cottrell — "Every single good ad comes from a good script" [quote src: YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (while also blaming bad creators for failed UGC); guests Anze Mikac/Markovic — specific scripts won their tests.
- **Product early vs late**: Levinger/Hott/Cottrell delay the product; Cottrell puts product as reason #1 in listicle UGC and flagged ~6s reveals as TikTok-fine but risky for Meta; Hott also warns 1:22 of agitation is too long.
- **One face vs many**: Savannah/Hott — multiple faces; Denney — single-creator testimonials beat compilations in 2024–25.
- **Look-alike luck**: guest Jason (Flakes) — founder mistaken for Baker Mayfield took ~40% of spend; Denney — a Gwyneth look-alike didn't scale, not replicable.
- **Polish**: Cottrell's big-swing studio Portland Leather ad beat the safe native mashup; Heath — bad video won't beat a good image; Denney — hi-fi founder ads still run 4+ years.
