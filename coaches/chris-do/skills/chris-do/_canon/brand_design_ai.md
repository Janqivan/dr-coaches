## 2. Frameworks (named models and step sequences)

**Part A — Brand strategy**

### A1. Different is better than better → weakness into strength
- When he uses it: positioning against bigger rivals.
- Steps: 1. "How are you different than your competitors? Cuz that's the only thing people remember." 2. Inversion: list your industry's uniform and clichés; do one thing differently. 3. 8 Mile rule: list every attack a rival can make and "embrace those weaknesses as a pillar of strength". 4. Pick a fight: reframe their strength as a weakness. 5. Earn preference too: "different and not preferable is like a lot of political figures". [quote src: YT:uAsAs8MoW9U, YT:sYyj--Yci6M, YT:jd0Ijs0a0ns]
- His example: Mini's "underpowered, small" → "Mighty Small"; Scope vs Listerine: "no more medicine breath", zero to ~33% share.
- Sources: [YT:_WFR0U9rLuc, YT:eS9LtexTMFk, YT:hDlWbmH96hw, YT:TmPHqX2P3vM] (2015–2025)
- ➕ for work you can't show, "we can't show our work" is an advantage, not a disadvantage: there is more intrigue and "mystery around things you can't see." Market yourself by saying "our work is so good that the clients want to keep it for themselves" (that's copywriting); silhouette a distinctive bottle shape and joke "we work with the top 10 spirits companies," want to know which one, "we'd have to kill you first," but slip a thick envelope of cash under the door and maybe "we'll take you to a bar." Prompt: imagine you had yourself as a brand-strategy client — [YT:Wvc8-L6DATY] (n.d.)

### A2. Core (Blind's strategy session)
- When he uses it: client discovery, before any creative.
- Steps: 1. Leadership in one room (CEO, CMO, CRO); the facilitator needs set questions, not domain expertise. 2. Four quadrants: customers, brand (look, feel, tone, voice), goals, the user journey. 3. Ask goals, challenges (if you're achieving them already you wouldn't be calling us), success metrics. 4. Score each goal 1–10 on obtainability and desirability. 5. Deliver tone, voice and value prop in three options, "because it takes three for them to see the pattern". [quote src: YT:OiUOaZJJYzg]
  - ➕ Futur YouTube numbers after switching from one big bet back to many small experiments. The big gamble: they put essentially the whole company's resources against one effort meant to be the hit that changes the game, and it did not produce, so they went back to the model of wanting to "try lots of small failures and see what happens". Surprise: more content at lower quality (less time on edit and refinement), yet views skyrocketed and revenue and sub rate grew dramatically; simple-setup formats like the whiteboard session are the iterative approach. Numbers: toward the third quarter of last year they averaged about 22,000 subs and about $3,500 in AdSense revenue (what YouTube pays for people watching); now about 46,000 subs a month (28-day window) and a little north of $13,000 in revenue. Supporting analogy: the ant queen lays "a thousand gazillion eggs" and a human has one baby, so insects, producing in such large numbers, will outlast us. — [YT:KnLj_I8yzgg] (2020)
- His example: Blind became a client-direct strategy consultancy within months ("we build bridges").
- Sources: [YT:puvKF8gmXUM, YT:ZdUL0sCxiOw, YT:FrQaUoH1riU] (2015–2025)
- ➕ where Core sits: asked if the logo process is in the Core book: "Core is everything before you get here," "a facilitated discovery session"; research, buckets, stylescapes and thumbnails come after; stylescapes are "a very specific formula" his team is trained on (a "revenge of the stylescape" video exists in the Facebook pro group, possible kit later). — [YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017)
- ➕ Walking into a strategy session without assumptions: tells clients up front "I've done little to no reading," hasn't profiled the company or gone past a cursory look at the first page of their site, "because I do not want to walk in here with any assumptions"; the session stays open and goes where it needs to. Reacts against a debate partner using his strategy questionnaire merely as a sales tool to vet clients: go in saying "You are the killer" and you'll conclude you're the killer: "Not a very good detective." Says he is "always surprised" (learning things) and also not surprised (no assumptions). — [YT:vCz4bRcIcpg] (2017)

### A3. Brand framework (keynote, five steps)
- When he uses it: keynotes, workshops.
- Steps: 1. Who you serve. 2. What they want: the value prop is their desired future ("I see this is where you're stuck"). 3. Tone. 4. Look and feel. 5. Hardest: behave consistently across the journey. Word drill: write ~100 words, cross off the first 10, keep two for tone and two for look. Benefit, not feature: "sleep better at night". [quote src: YT:h-eRwbbpfxo]
- His example: Harley — rebellious + passionate; distressed + counterculture.
- Sources: [YT:h-eRwbbpfxo, YT:ivKnj9ffcmE, YT:QZGMbv6Y3Eo] (2019–2026)

### A4. Brand tests
- When he uses it: judging whether a company has a brand at all.
- Steps: 1. Predictability: can't predict them? "I'm going to say they have no brand." 2. Transpose: could Nike make a hotel, Hilton a shoe? 3. Value lift: the name on a blank adds nothing = "neutral or negative, you have no brand". 4. Premium over the cheapest alternative. 5. "When you're competing on price you have no brand unless your brand is we're cheap". [quote src: YT:JFwpMR3Nj8Q, YT:SABhSGYADUE, YT:Ed9OqOQAfBs]
- His example: a ~$10 Costco tee vs the Nike or Y-3 version: "that little mark tells us a story". [quote src: YT:JFwpMR3Nj8Q]
- Sources: [YT:h-eRwbbpfxo, YT:QZGMbv6Y3Eo, YT:0zmIAhEI09A, YT:g5a3LlHodsM] (2019–2026)

### A5. Positioning: claim → proof; repositioning
- When he uses it: generalists, price-competitors.
- Steps: 1. Pick a focus. 2. Claim it ("like us planting a flag in the ground"). 3. Prove it: "we don't have to be smarter or more creative than our competition only more focus". 4. Allow 6–12 months to grow into it. Reposition: "who are they considering other than you?" → list their top three traits → "be anti whatever they are, be the opposite". [quote src: YT:5a488th9fkM, YT:h-eRwbbpfxo]
- Sources: [YT:HbKnXeZAXoU, YT:5a488th9fkM, YT:9xF0kcs9lm4] (2015–2025)
- ➕ claim → proof; repositioning: the "magic sentence" to give people: "I did something special for a certain type of client" and "we got a remarkable result," then "let me tell you about it"; make it easy for clients to see if you're a good fit, "don't make them work so hard" or they go elsewhere — [YT:HF__2yrb6y8] (n.d.)

### A6. Messaging kit
- When he uses it: value props, taglines, names.
- Steps: Pitch (Book Yourself Solid): who (+ adjective) → frustration → "You know how..." (pause for yes) → result → the craft only if asked; "one Avatar one problem one result". Two-word brand: first word "starts with the negative" ("loud introvert"). Flip a category cliché; We are X, not Y. Run dull facts through brand filters (Pinkberry → "swirling daily"). Names: hard K, threes, rhyme. [quote src: YT:sLe5iNSyejk]
- His example: The Futur Pro Group helps creative entrepreneurs scale their business.
- Sources: [YT:sLe5iNSyejk, YT:d7bgouAH3ns, YT:NfoocYAuZN0, YT:h-eRwbbpfxo] (2016–2026)

### A7. Customer profile → avatar test → TGO
- When he uses it: before messaging, built live with the room.
- Steps: 1. Buyer → hobbies → brand affinities → content sources → name + nickname → external need → internal want ("What's the internal emotional want? What's the feeling?") → business needs → hopes → fears. 2. Fill-in: "I'm a [adjective] [job title]" → "I struggle with [professional challenge]" → "I want [solution/feature/benefit] because..." → "or I risk [what's at stake]". 3. Test: "Can you pick them out from a crowd? That's always the test" (fails: ranges, composites, an industry instead of a person). 4. TGO: tasks "from morning to night", obstacles, opportunities. [quote src: YT:mgNTZRpZmEk, YT:tswMCGiLCYo]
- Sources: [YT:mgNTZRpZmEk, YT:-JAPZs9QQUM, YT:rl69VQbs3gA, YT:h-eRwbbpfxo] (2015–2026)

### A8. Niche and dream-client tests
- When he uses it: someone cannot say who they serve.
- Steps: 1. Market × passion; Dib: "if it doesn't pass the first test forget the rest". 2. Size: 15–25 competitors (David C. Baker). 3. Ladder down: "I do design things" → logos → logos for food-delivery apps. 4. Trifecta on your last 10 clients: joy, money, mission; check hunger and ability to pay. 5. 2026 forced binary: each dimension A or B (gender, stage, age, business type, revenue, region, daily pain). [quote src: YT:QH6b8mMEP2g]
- His example: blue-collar trades in Texas doing $2–5M; his own (2025): "creative entrepreneur", under 10 people, ~$200K–$2.5M.
- Sources: [YT:_1z-_VuImVg, YT:HafqJ_pB9NI, YT:KSGzJiK9Uz8, YT:FFPaVFBEIhI] (2015–2026)
- ➕ over-niching trap example: "only five people in the world who can hire me" is fine if those five spend a billion dollars a year on marketing and design, one of them sustains you — [YT:z9VhsEyT7cI] (n.d.)

### A9. Attribute sliders
