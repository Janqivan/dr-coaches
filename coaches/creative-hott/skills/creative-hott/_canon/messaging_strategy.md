## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 The relevance chain (2026, NOW)
- **When:** auditing any ad plus its post-click experience.
- **Steps:** (1) What is literally the first frame — the top pixels entering the feed? (2) What do the first 3 seconds do? (3) What does the rest of the ad do? (4) Does relevance carry through the click to the landing page/website? If links don't connect, you lose.
- **Ad-to-page match rule:** need not be one-to-one. Option A: hook problem, rest of video and post-click all about one tiny thing — "If you can scale being super niche on one thing, do it." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] Option B: open on one specific thing and broaden through the ad, as long as the landing-page headline and image still feel relevant to someone arriving from that ad.
- **Homepage extension:** check who is in the homepage hero, what they look like, person or product, does it change, or has the same person sat there for 6 months. People landing on a product page often "escapes to the homepage because that is what almost always happens" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI]; others leak to Google reviews, competitors, or type your .com.
- Sources: [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05, NOW)

### 2.2 The belonging questions (2026, NOW)
- Ask of every ad and landing page: "How will someone feel like this is for them?" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] / "How will someone feel like they belong?" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] / "This is what I want my life to be." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI]
- Check age, way of speaking, language, interests. His example: an ad leading to a page showing a 65-year-old woman (or his dad), or an 18-year-old kid, makes him think "I'm not in the right place" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI], depending on what the ad promised.
- Sources: [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05, NOW)

### 2.3 The three empathies (core of his course, 2025)
1. Empathy for the consumer consuming your ad.
2. Empathy for the creators who make the content your audience likes to consume.
3. Empathy for the algorithm/system: Meta makes money by showing more relevant ads, which keeps people on platform, "so rewarding those ads more" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU].
- Meld all three; structure media buying to work with the system's motivations. Related: ugly ads rest on deep empathy for the viewer, including people who *could* be customers, not only existing ones — post-purchase surveys perpetuate themselves (AT&T small business and golf example).
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05), [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12)

### 2.4 "Aspir-relatable" (2025)
- He jokes it's his trademark (captioned "aspir relatable").
- **Hook decision:** aspirational moment or relatable problem — they are not the same. Can open on a relatable problem that is "more visual and icky" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] and move to an aspirational solution.
- **Setting rule:** when he or a creator talks about the product and its results, the setting is aspirational but relatable: about "30% nicer" than what the audience has (nicer home, apartment, view).
- Sources: [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01)

### 2.5 Relevance is multi-dimensional (2025)
- Slide: a get-ready-with-me video, a bass fisherman, a chess player. Almost no one finds all three relevant, yet one product can be sold to all three with a different piece of content for each group. Viewers decide "within the first frame" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA].
- Sources: [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05)

### 2.6 Concentric audience circles (2023)
- **Example (Lone River Ranch Water):** ranch water lovers → hard seltzer lovers → drinkers → non-drinkers.
- **Step:** write for an outer circle (craft beer / Coors Light drinkers). An ad that changes their minds also works for the inner circles.
- Sources: [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12)

### 2.7 Threading the needle between segments (2025)
- **Steps:** (1) make the ads for segment A (bigger guys), iterate — each gives scale in its own space; (2) make ads for segment B (fitness guys), iterate; (3) hunt for the ad that works for both — that's where "mega scale" comes from.
- **His idea, The Perfect Jean:** stretchy jeans, "the perfect Gene for every guy" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] [caption: jean], split frame — tiny skinny guy on one side, "chunky old me" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] on the other.
- **His own critique:** people in the middle may not relate; serving both risks speaking to no one. Fix: pull back from the specifics to what makes sense to "all guys with legs" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g].
- Related (2025-01): geo-limited $2,000 product at 3x ROAS in Canada — use breakdowns to see who the working creative works for, then make creative for those it doesn't reach yet.
- Sources: [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:_lAjkqWSDsA] (2025-01)

### 2.8 Organic research through alt accounts (2023 → 2025, 9 mentions)
- **Why:** your feed is skewed and personal — "what you consume is not what I consume" [quote src: YT:4BOtccKnC2Q].
- **Steps:**
  1. Make a fresh Instagram/TikTok account per brand, category or broad topic near your product (his: cars, beer, ads; examples: men's styling, hair care, nostalgia-punk candy, chess, trains).
  2. Only search, stop on and engage with that interest; the algorithm quickly serves more.
  3. Also look at what gets the most views overall — "often the uglier, more authentic stuff" [quote src: YT:kD_0ENSXdZw]; take inspiration from "broad broad reach" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] and "massive massive views" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA], not pretty publishers ("Just because Vogue makes pretty content…" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]).
  4. Break down massive-view posts: how it's shot, where the camera is (handheld, held by someone else, stationary, doorbell camera, drone), the story arc.
  5. Note "vibes", not trending hook lines: camera angle low vs high, light source, clutter vs clean background, speaking pace, volume, whispering/intimate delivery. (He mentions Savannah Sanchez's hook lists respectfully but calls copied lines short-lived.)
  6. Bookmark organic content, share in a team group chat.
  7. Use the organic piece as the beginning of an ad and tack the sales component on after.
- **Second prong (2023):** ChatGPT — describe product and what's great, ask for pain points, how to make a user care, how to create emotional connection, "make them feel anxious" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU], "insufficient". Combine organic aesthetic with pain-point stories.
- Guest note: (guest: Dara Denney) does the same for YouTube, from Paddy Galloway's method.
- Sources: [YT:XIn0vQdErsM] (2023-08), [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12), [YT:kD_0ENSXdZw] (2024-06), [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05)

### 2.9 Review mining (2023)
- Study reviews of your product and competitors'. Most are useless; 1 in 5 or 1 in 10 says I was having this problem.
- Don't assume a positive review works. Pull potent, relatable, emotional excerpts — especially past-tense negative I-statements ("I was...", "I had...", "I didn't...").
- Language level: big, hyper-relatable problems in third-to-fifth-grade language.
- Use them without closing the loop; the viewer infers the product is the answer.
- Sources: [YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06), [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12)

### 2.10 Comment mining (2023 → 2025)
- **Reviews vs comments:** reviews show why people crossed the finish line; comments (questions, criticisms) show why they didn't. A commenter is "a proxy for other people" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] with the same doubt — be thankful.
- **Fast method:** open your top-spending ads, read the biggest, most repeated comments.
- **Low spend:** sort the account by comments, not spend; open the most-commented ad — "it's not necessarily a good or bad signal it's just a signal" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs].
- **Use:** tack the answer onto the next batch of ads, but not as the opener — an objection like setup difficulty goes later in the ad.
- Read negative comments: hide them but read them.
- Sources: [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12), [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01)

- Example: Obvi's phrase (caption "foa killer", likely "FUPA killer") at the top of an ad — "really relevant and thumb stopping" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]; he imagines other executions, like shaving the words into hair with a trimmer. ### 2.12 The stubborn-friend frame (2025)
- Starting question: how would I talk to a friend I love who is stubborn and can't see the bigger picture? How do I convince them as a friend while making them feel inadequate, like they're missing out, or anxious?
- Adjust for what the average consumer already understands about the problem and the solution.
- (guest: Levinger) labels it "ought self": inadequacy ("you should have already done this" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]) and anxiety.
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05)

### 2.13 Full-funnel / infomercial structure (2024 → 2025)
- For new-category products: (1) a reason to pay attention, (2) a reason to keep paying attention, (3) more info, (4) a reason to buy.
- Study As Seen On TV and late-night infomercials (ShamWow, Ronco "set it and forget it" [quote src: YT:in1l2TPFc1c]), not QVC: they hold people who weren't there to shop, with a relevant problem and "but wait there's more" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] storytelling.
- Once attention is earned: "you've earned the right to spew science at people and keep their attention" [quote src: YT:FTotRZL9vAg].
- MrBeast model: get attention, keep it, then do something with it — attention alone is attention bait.
- Sources: [YT:xyvIAmfuZUo] (2022-04), [YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2024-12), [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04), [YT:FTotRZL9vAg] (2025-04)

### 2.14 Learning from winners and losers (2024 → 2025)
- In any account you can access, sort all-time by most spent; learn from top spenders. His team agreed to hunt down and share all top spenders across accounts. Wants a systematic way to learn from losers too (harder — you can't sort for them).
- Lowering CPA: what scaled most → check survivorship bias → ask what the winner is missing (e.g. 55+ performs: lean in, or ask what would get younger people's attention).
- Break results down by audience segment to find which ads get sales from the coldest audience.
- First diagnostic when a concept fails: "what was the first moment" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] — did it fail the so-what test?
- If you can't understand why something is a winner, then you can't make more better ads [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw].
- Sources: [YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2024-12), [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04), [YT:u4B0GGBBDXY] (2025-05)

### 2.15 Studying a competitor (when he does)
- Ask why they're doing it and what they're looking at; some teams chase misleading metrics (low CPC, high hook rate) or just copy another brand, so he limits which brands he studies. Repetition is "often a good bet" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] unless he doesn't trust that team.
- Don't copy big-brand clean ads blindly (Caraway, Allbirds): weigh their scale, goals, funding, retail distribution; marketers cherry-pick the prettiest ads they see.
- Cited trick (Zack Stuck, Hollow Socks as captioned): keep worst ads alive on ~$1/day so spying competitors copy them.
- Sources: [YT:Fg3kHHRdhdk] (2023-01), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05)

### 2.16 Selling lo-fi creative to a resistant client (2023 → 2026)
1. Show organic content that is not fully polished, branded or styled but is relevant and "actually a little bit polished" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8], so they don't hate it as much.
2. Show other experts saying the same, so it isn't just him.
3. Pull up brands already doing it.
4. Ask for a small test: "Give me $500. Give me $1,000 to spend on this." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] — "If this doesn't work, you know, I'm the idiot." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] / "If this does work and we make money from it, hooray." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]
5. The question underneath: "do you want to be rich or do you want this to look good" [quote src: YT:MbqiexQiyGs].
- If they still refuse: "I don't know why they're working with me." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] No one has a metric proving an ad "hurts the brand" [quote src: YT:DGTwYyI-81s].
- Sources: [YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06), [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05), [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03, NOW)

### 2.17 Luxury-objection fix (2023)
- Find the most expensive component of your production flow and make an ad without it: RED camera → iPhone; studio → real bathroom/kitchen. A Rolex can be shot on an iPhone in a premium, relatable setting.
- Interrogate every choice — camera, lighting, color, text overlay: what in my marketer background led here? Ask "what would a real creator do" [quote src: YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (100Ks–millions of views). Lighting only matters if your audience's content has it.
- Sources: [YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07), [YT:SU5Tv9cRONg] (2025-02)

### 2.18 Founder ads (2026, NOW)
- Team does the setup: prompt the founder, ask the right questions, put them in the right setting, set up the camera so they think about none of it.
- Founder talks to camera — they understand the problem better than anyone.
- Founder stays out of the edit: "I don't really want a founder editing." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]
- Hard to imitate with AI, "certainly hard to do legally with AI or ethically." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]
- Sources: [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03), [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03)

### 2.19 Creative sourcing: streams, creators, customers (2023 → 2026)
- **Streams:** at least one internal and one external creative resource; 4–5 only when spend and conversion data afford it. One internal team = "one locked perspective... one set of logic or rules" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]; an agency in many accounts is "failing way more than you in way different ways" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. Only one agency, no team? Add the other, and don't force both into the same aesthetics — "let them cook" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04).
- **Creators over influencers:** an influencer has followers; a creator makes content that scales beyond them. Pick creators whose views consistently exceed follower count (hundreds of thousands to millions of views on five- or six-digit followings) in the style your audience consumes (2024-12). Prefer actors or experienced storytellers with a perspective over the cheapest UGC (2023-05).
- **Direction vs "let the creator cook" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04):** a bit of both. He trusts his own direction ("billions of dollars worth of ads" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]), but brands copying Alex said give a lot of direction without understanding modern aesthetics give out-of-date direction. A creator can miss with a perfect beat-by-beat brief or with total freedom (especially the hook) — weigh your expertise against how good the creator is.
- **Your own customers:** pay happy buyers cash, Amazon gift cards or product (cash for bidets — how many bidets can you have); they're more authentic than creators who need spoon-feeding.
- **In-house talent:** brands now act as their own talent agency; likes in-house creators but dislikes when brands won't let them do what they're good at. MKBHD with Ridge as a smart partnership (2026-04).

## 3. Templates and exact wording

**Openers and copy patterns**
- Past-tense negative I-statement customer quote as opener: I never felt like a brand really understood my love for climbing (2023-08). Pattern: "I was...", "I had...", "I didn't..." — don't close the loop.
- "You" as the first word instead of "this" (2024-03).
- First three words of primary text matter most — put something potent there, e.g. a review; long primary text is "frequently underutilized" (2023-08).
- Insider-wink rewrite of a context-dependent tagline: for the people who know what 'when the mountain is out' means... if you know, you know [quote src: YT:kYSxNySgmKc] — belonging for insiders, FOMO/curiosity for outsiders (2023-01).
- Competitor switch line: I love Trollis until I found these... everything I want with 60% less sugar (2024-03).
- Benefit with a vivid consequence: 60% less sugar so your guts don't turn into goo — team reused it in DMs to objecting commenters (2023-12).
- Fix for a vague supplement static: ask a question, or "reduces bloating in under one hour" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (if compliant) (2023-12).
- Category-in-three-words headline: "silicone wedding rings" [quote src: YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] (2024-03).
- Over-the-top claim that creates an itch (best cup in ages → "now I gotta try it to find out" [quote src: YT:9YKsHMJXiIQ]) is OK; unprovable superlatives like best detergent I've ever tried are "a nothing statement" [quote src: YT:P3IpafQunzU] (2023-02, 2023-03).
- Cheesy unscripted lines (the bomb.com) can read as authentic from the right person (2024-03).

**Closer**
- "I don't know what you're waiting for." [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] — at the end of every ad; "trying to make people feel like they're idiots" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-04, 2025-05).

**Prompts**
- Customer/AI prompt: What specific frustration do people have with regular [blank] that your product solves effortlessly? (2024-12). Jeans version — what frustration did you have with your rigid jeans → stiff on the knees, couldn't bend over.
- ChatGPT research asks: pain points; how to make a user care; how to create an emotional connection; "make them feel anxious" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU], "insufficient" (2023-08).

**Customer-video briefs**
- Email customers: $50 to make us a video, with a chance at $1,000 more if their ad does well; or tell us the most crazy/believable/painful story with $100–$500 for the best (2024-12).

**Client lines**
- "do you want to be rich or do you want this to look good" [quote src: YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06).
- "Give me $500. Give me $1,000 to spend on this." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] … "If this doesn't work, you know, I'm the idiot." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05).

**Editor/designer brief line**
- don't remove the crease or damaged box, keep it native and believable — and explain the performance reason (2023-07).

**Do-not list**
- Don't start with "we" meaning the company. Don't lead with the holiday ("new year, new you" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). Don't use insider taglines that need context. Don't accuse the viewer of an illness in second person on Facebook. Don't stack happy-holidays greetings with hard selling in one founder email (2023-01). Don't steal content — get usage rights; "it's like speeding" [quote src: YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07).
