> Scope: Barry Hott's evidence on Meta ad creative, drawn only from his three verified canon files: C = craft.md (ugly ads, formats, hooks, teardowns), M = messaging_strategy.md (relevance, angles, copy, production), T = testing_ai_voice.md (spend, testing, delivery, AI). ## 0. Read this first
- These are creator-reported results, mostly from DTC e-commerce client accounts (Tushy, Tails, Cheers, Rotten, Perfect Jean, Lone River). Lead-gen evidence is thin and tagged LEADGEN wherever it appears.
- Dates matter. He changed his mind on attribution, hook rate, faces, long-form, puns and AI, so check the date on any claim before you act on it (§5).
- His numbers are unaudited (Cheers +$5M equity, Ad Crate $130–140K in 7 days). He also showed ChatGPT-invented stats as a demo of fake data. Never cite those (T §5).
- Ivan's own spend data outranks anything here. Use this playbook to generate hypotheses, then let spend decide, which is Hott's own rule.

## 1. Working now (2026) — ranked plays

**1. Build the ad from the first frame outward and spend your effort on the top of the frame.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: a first frame in which something happens (motion or a reveal) and that triggers a feeling (curiosity, discomfort, insecurity or laughter). Put the relevance cue, meaning the setting plus one relevant text line, in the top third, because that part scrolls in first. Front-load the words in the VO, the overlay and the primary text.
- Why, per Hott: "you got to obsess over the first frame" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. "so many ads just die in the first frame" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. In 2026 Meta learns from the first frame "very fast" that "this video means nothing" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] and stops serving it. Diagnose a failing ad with a consumer-eye read: is it irrelevant to me, boring, confusing, or unclear in the first second?
- Who: every ad, every account.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) · [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09) → C §1.7, §2.4, §2.5 · T §1.6

**2. Run the relevance chain from the first pixel to the landing page and the homepage hero.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: an ad whose hook, body, click and landing page all stay relevant to the same person. Either go niche on one tiny thing end to end, or open specific and broaden, as long as the page headline and image still feel right to someone arriving from that ad. Then audit the homepage hero (who is in it, and has it been the same person for 6 months?), because people "escapes to the homepage because that is what almost always happens" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI].
- Why, per Hott: if one link breaks, you lose. "If you can scale being super niche on one thing, do it." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI]
- Who: any account with a dedicated landing page.
- Sources: [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → M §1.1, §2.1

**3. Run the belonging test on every ad and every page.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: cast, write and set each ad so that a viewer can tell it is for them. Check age, way of speaking, language and interests. Make sure the page the ad leads to doesn't show someone who makes the viewer think "I'm not in the right place" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI].
- Why, per Hott: the failure he describes is the viewer thinking "Nah, I'm good. This isn't for me." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] "Relevance... is everything" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA], and audiences keep fragmenting into pockets.
- Who: GEN. It matters most where the buyer's age and identity vary.
- Sources: [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → M §2.2

**4. Make at least one founder-led ad, with the founder speaking as the person who had the problem.** NOW · DTC/GEN
- What to make: the team handles the setup (questions, setting, camera) and the founder talks to camera, ideally as the customer who had the problem, not in a "why we started" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] story. Keep the founder out of the edit: "I don't really want a founder editing." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]
- Why, per Hott: it is the one content type every brand should test at least once. Authority plus authenticity is the "killer" pairing. It is hard to fake with AI, "certainly hard to do legally with AI or ethically." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] Proof cases: the Cheers founder wine bath became the top spender, and the Fulton founder bunion story.
- Who: founder-run brands and service businesses where the principal has authority.
- Sources: [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:FTotRZL9vAg] (2025-04) · [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05) → C §1.12, §1.13 · M §2.18

**5. Shoot it the way a creator would frame it, not the way a marketer would.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: ask how someone who makes content people actually like would frame and shoot this, then do that. Shoot on a phone, in one take, somewhere relevant. Change at least one lever toward uglier: talent, lighting, camera type, angle, script, story or setting.
- Why, per Hott: "It's really how would a creator frame this?" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] Production cost has no positive correlation with performance. In 2026 he argues the relationship is inverse: "you do not get extra credit points for using a fancy camera" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs], and "in fact you get penalized" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs].
- Who: GEN, luxury included (shoot on a phone, put luxury in the frame).
- Sources: [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] (2026-04) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) → C §1.4, §2.2, §2.3

**6. Use the setting as targeting: shoot one script in several settings and with several casts.** NOW · GEN/DTC
- What to make: pick the background for the person you want (kitchen, garage, bathroom, business-class cabin). Shoot the same message with creators aged 20–60 or with different accents, sometimes "10 different videos with 10 different settings" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] for 10 types of people.
- Why, per Hott: setting is "like the most important thing" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4], and you are "always targeting with your creative" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE]. The setting "beams the understanding of relevance into the consumer's eyes" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] before any words land. Relevance is a "multi-dimensional spectrum" [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] that Meta's ML can read and you can't.
- Who: GEN. Match the setting to price point (aspir-relatable, about 30% nicer than the audience's own life).
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) · [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) → C §1.8, §2.6 · M §2.4

**7. Make long talking or yapper ads, and make them uninterrupted and interesting.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: single-take phone selfies or question-led story ads of 1–3 minutes, with a mid-roll CTA and an end card.
- Why, per Hott: there is no such thing as an ad that is too long, only one that is too boring [YT:frk28Y6nOHg]. His Flock chips yapper (about 3:16, uncut) ran for about two years, "I promise you that ad is still evergreen to this day." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] Yapper ads aren't new to him (2018–19).
- Who: GEN. Watch placements, because 3 minutes is too long for Reels.
- Sources: [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → C §1.11, §4a, §6

**8. Judge ads by spend, and never turn off the top spender.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: more ads like the big spender, or ads that complement it, rather than ads chosen because of a cheap CPA on low spend.
- Why, per Hott: "Spend is a meritocracy" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw]. Meta optimizes at ad-set or campaign level, not per ad. Turning off a top spender is "an atrocity" that starts a race to the bottom. In 2026 he says he rarely sees the system get it wrong, provided the account has enough conversion data, solid media buying, and an optimization event that tracks real business results.
- Who: accounts with enough conversion volume.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] (2026-04) · [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01) → T §1.1, §1.2, §2.1, §2.2

**9. Replace hit rate with guided volume of cheap bets.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: many cheap concepts (phone, Canva, CapCut, Descript, AI components), each one guided by what is already spending and by "words that matter" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]. Do not pump out random volume.
- Why, per Hott: hit rate is "a binary metric in a nonbinary game" [quote src: YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg], and "if your hit rate is really good, that means you're not trying enough things." [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] "Blind volume is useless. You need guided volume." [quote src: YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] He would trade a hit rate falling from 20% to 0.5% for a shot at a massive winner. Judge creative sources by the spend their ads carry.
- Who: GEN. How many ads you can test is set by conversion volume (T §1.14).
- Sources: [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] (2026-04) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → T §1.3, §1.10, §2.13

**10. Run at least two creative streams that don't share an aesthetic.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: ads from one internal source and one external source (4–5 sources only when spend and data afford it). Let each cook in its own style. Prefer creators whose views exceed their follower count over influencers with big audiences.
- Why, per Hott: one internal team is "one locked perspective... one set of logic or rules" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. An agency in many accounts is "failing way more than you in way different ways" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. Small creators make content that scales beyond their followers.
- Who: brands that spend enough to feed two sources.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12) → M §2.19 · C §6

**11. Stay ugly, but treat ugly as the entry ticket and not the whole ad.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: native-looking ads that also tell a story, are worth consuming, are relevant, and then sell.
- Why, per Hott: "make ugly ads is here to stay. Like it's not going away." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] Ads "are not going to be getting prettier" [quote src: YT:zDHkSSsjFDw]. The cat-and-mouse game keeps going: creators adopted the lav-mic-in-hand look, brands copied it, and creators are now moving away from it. What changes is what authentic looks like. "What looks native today may feel stale in six months." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) → C §1.3, §1.5, §6 · T §6

**12. Use AI for components and for volume, never to fake a human experience.** NOW · GEN/LEADGEN
- What to make: AI images (he says he no longer needs a designer for image ads), AI hooks you couldn't shoot, B-roll, and AI pre-shoots of a storyboard. AI hook plus real human footage is his usual pattern. AI characters may voice problems or tell a story, but never give testimonials.
- Why, per Hott: images are "pretty much toast" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (solved), and for real-looking humans it's "Game over". A flood of polished AI ads makes authenticity and trust worth more: "that doesn't decrease the human need, it just increases the output need" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4].
- Who: GEN. He tested an insurance lead-gen script in Veo 3 (LEADGEN).
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05) · [YT:4BOtccKnC2Q] (2025-07) → T §1.12, §2.16 · C §2.14

**13. When results swing, check the site before you touch the ads.** NOW · GEN
- What to do: before you change any creative, check landing page, homepage and product page changes, Google results, Reddit review threads, and external events. Screenshot landing pages daily.
- Why, per Hott: "It's probably not Meta's fault. It's probably your fault, to be honest." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] "it's almost always a website change that just someone didn't know happened" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]. If the top spender's CPA doubles, ask "Is there something wrong with that ad?" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw]
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → T §1.7, §2.9

**14. Strip click-bait elements now that only link clicks count.** NOW · GEN
- What to do: compare your top spenders before and after late March 2026. Expect comment-heavy ads, fake buttons, and statics with text small enough that people tap to enlarge to lose spend. Make ads that earn link clicks.
- Why, per Hott: attribution now counts only link clicks, which sharpens optimization. In-platform metrics may look worse, "and you should figure out how to be okay with that" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4].
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) → T §2.11, §6

**15. Feed Andromeda the way the best advertisers already did: with diverse concepts.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: genuinely different concepts (different settings, people, formats and stories) rather than 3-second hook swaps.
- Why, per Hott: the Andromeda panic is overblown. Making more diverse creative is what the best advertisers have done "literally since 2024 or if not earlier." [quote src: YT:s4mLWmECoKI] "iterating to death" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] means only swapping hooks or headlines.
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05) · [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) → T §6, §2.7

**16. Sell lo-fi to a resistant client with a small paid test.** NOW · GEN
- What to do: show relevant organic examples that are "actually a little bit polished" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8], show other experts saying the same, pull up brands already doing it, then ask for "Give me $500. Give me $1,000 to spend on this." [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]
- Why, per Hott: no metric proves that an ad "hurts the brand" [quote src: YT:DGTwYyI-81s]. In 2026 most of his clients arrive already bought in.
- Who: agencies and in-house teams facing brand resistance.
- Sources: [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05) → C §2.13 · M §2.16

**17. Build ads meant to run for years.** NOW · GEN
- What to make: evergreen concepts built on a durable problem rather than a short-lived trend, then keep them running.
- Why, per Hott: Flock is "evergreen to this day" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]. Cadbury has run the same ad for about 20 years. He wants ads that last 2–5 years: "Make ugly heads. Make banger ads. Make ads that last for years" [quote src: YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg]. Don't spend $100,000 producing a weak idea.
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] (2026-04) · [YT:nUVl_zpeipY] (2023-05) → C §1.19 · T §3

## 4. Hooks and watch time

**Hook types his evidence backs most**
- **Past-tense I-statements** taken from reviews ("I was...", "I had...", "I didn't..."). "almost every ad I run with I gets crushed compared to" [quote src: YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] the same line written with "it"/"this". Don't close the loop, and let the viewer infer that the product is the answer. Example: I used to be very self-conscious of my bikini line at the beach (10/10). → M §1.6, §2.9 · C §3
- **Insecurity / "ought self"**, meaning inadequacy, anxiety and FOMO: "I'll never put body wash on my balls again" [quote src: YT:YuS18wnD_bc]. The Tushy strategy is to make every American feel insecure about not having a bidet. Line: never accuse the viewer of an illness in the second person on Facebook. → M §1.7 · C §4b
- **Call-out openers**: "hey [blank]", "people with this problem" [quote src: YT:frk28Y6nOHg], "[blank] lovers", "hate [blank]?". Example: "watch this if you struggle with focus and discipline" (Commit Action, LEADGEN). → C §3
- **Exaggerated problem or stunt that makes sense instantly**: the Cheers wine bath, a fake parking ticket on a dirty car, a toilet seat around the neck. His prompt: what could I pour on my head that would make sense to my audience immediately? → C §2.11
- **Question-first cold open** (Tails), with outrageous moments pulled forward ("yelled out the window at the fuzz" [quote src: YT:cpnVXGQAWHg]). → C §3
- **Number or proof-first**: for a property-investing lead-gen ad, lead with the 22,000 downloaded this book line, not "make money simple again" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE]. LEADGEN. → C §4c
- **Category in three words**: "silicone wedding rings" [quote src: YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc]. Clear over clever: puns and insider taglines that need context fail (the Busch "bush guide" pun, "The mountain is out" [quote src: YT:kYSxNySgmKc]). → M §1.8

**Retention moves**
- Front-load the VO, the overlay and the first 3 words of the primary text, and cut filler (say "knives", not "when I go to the kitchen to use a knife" [quote src: YT:fawsUmNeCkg]). → C §2.4 · M §3
- Use the native in-app overlay (white box, black text), at most two lines ("keep it a two" [quote src: YT:9YKsHMJXiIQ]), placed in the top third. A % off bar in the hook "kills an ad" [quote src: YT:M2ObOuFQSv0]. → C §3
- Use relevant, native audio in the first moment: birds, a crinkling bag, ASMR. Use no stock music in talking ads. → C §1.20, §2.4
- Use story structure: problem hook → angle → re-hook. Tell many problems through different people, use "but wait there's more" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw], and make the curiosity line "if you want to hear the rest of the story stick around" [quote src: YT:cpnVXGQAWHg]. Reach the solution by about 0:45, not 1:22 (Earth Breeze). → C §2.10
- Keep the story moving: the strawberry-legs ad has about 10 cuts in 6 seconds. Once attention is earned, "you've earned the right to spew science at people and keep their attention" [quote src: YT:FTotRZL9vAg]. → C §4b · M §2.13
- Use a mid-roll CTA plus an end card; he reversed his old dismissal of end cards. Closer: "I don't know what you're waiting for." [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] → C §3, §6
- If retention is strong, fix the hook to bring in more relevant people. If a short loop gets attention, "live in that a little bit more" [quote src: YT:HNnBBOYUlN0]. → C §2.10 · T §4
- Metric caution: I have made plenty of ads with insanely high hook rates that don't actually generate any sales [YT:RVRRGf746Bw]. A 2% hook rate means no one is watching. Track hook-to-hold, cost per 3-second view and cost per share. → C §6 · T §3, §6

## 5. What stopped working or changed (dated)

- **Ugly alone → ugly plus story plus relevance.** About 2018–21 anything ugly beat pretty, because feeds were full of friends. By 2023 "Ugly ads can be bad ads too" [quote src: YT:YuS18wnD_bc]. In 2024 feeds were "TikTok-ified", so novelty matters. In 2026 the authentic look keeps moving (the lav-mic-in-hand look got copied, and creators are abandoning it). → C §6 · T §6
- **Thumb-stopping → "relevance building" (2025).** "Ad blocker" became "subconscious content blocker" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA], because personalisation means fewer skips. → C §6
- **Shooting method (2026-03).** He moved from "And then I'll not do that. I'll just do the opposite." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] (inverting what a pro photographer would do) to "It's really how would a creator frame this?" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] → C §2.3
- **Unknown faces: cover them (2023–24) → softened (2026).** Feeds went from friends to celebrities to influencers to "now it's just anything" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. Still cover a face if it isn't relevant. → C §6
- **Post-its: signature trick → "barely even level one" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2024-12).** He calls it a "gateway drug" toward recreating what the audience consumes. He also takes blame for the MS Paint / CEO-is-out-for-a-week nostalgia trend, which worked only briefly. → C §2.7, §6
- **Long-form: skeptic (2022) → "Big long ad guy" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2023 on).** Contradiction he owns: he hates it when 3–6 second loops win, but has seen enough of them win. → C §6
- **End cards: dismissed → used** to close the back half for people who watch 30–90 seconds (2024-09). → C §6
- **Puns: "let's try it" [quote src: YT:9YKsHMJXiIQ] → "party pooper" (2025-05).** "Clear over clever" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]. → M §6
- **Hook rate: compared across ads → "don't compare" (2025).** He keeps hook-to-hold and cost per 3-second view only as diagnostics. → T §6
- **Attribution: 1-day click (2022) → 7-day click (2025) → incremental attribution recommended (2026-04)** once Meta enabled cost caps with it. He is "fundamentally rethinking" account structure. → T §6
- **Click attribution (late March 2026):** only link clicks count now. Ads that won spend on cheap clicks (comment bait, fake buttons, tiny-text statics) lose it. → T §2.11
- **AI: skeptic (2022, AI raises "the floor" but not "the ceiling") → daily copy and research tool (2023–25) → images "Game over", no designer needed for image ads (2026).** Video is close but not there yet. → T §6
- **AI images: worry about oversupply (2025) → mocks "a thousand of those. One of those might hit." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026).** Yet he says cheap AI volume is fine if relevance and authenticity hold. → T §6 · C §6
- **Organic: vanity (about 2021) → invest (2024) → small creators whose content scales beyond their followers (2026).** → C §6 · M §2.19
- **Creator direction (2026-04):** a mix of his direction and letting the creator cook. Heavy direction copied from others is often out of date. → M §6
- **Flexible ad format:** he no longer recommends it because it has no reporting (2025-04). → C §6
- **Andromeda (2026-05):** the panic is overblown, because diverse creative was already best practice. → T §6
- **His own teardown misses (2023-01):** he picked the loser in the Flude, ISSA and VO tests by trusting story and feel over clarity of message. Keep this in mind when you read his gut verdicts. → C §6

## 7. Lead-gen transfer

**What Hott says directly (LEADGEN evidence)**
- He runs insurance lead gen and high-ticket lead capture with the same infrastructure and concepts as a supplement brand. B2B is full of "really dumb legacy mindsets" [quote src: YT:FTotRZL9vAg], and "businesses don't buy anything people buy things" [quote src: YT:frk28Y6nOHg]. → C §1.15 · M §1.13
- His first ads, at about 17–18, recruited summer-camp counselors by targeting teachers and education students. That was lead gen. → T §8
- For long sales cycles or high-ticket offers: set a difficult conversion goal (a downstream action, not just a lead), optimize to an earlier tracked action (request info, watch a site video) if needed, and judge ads by a proxy that is hard to do, not a click. For his $2,000 course, a lead or nurture touchpoint matters more than an immediate sale. → M §1.13 · T §1.15
- High-AOV testing exception: test on a cheaper, earlier event only if the funnel has enough barriers that the event shows real intent. → T §2.12
- Roofing, painting, windows and doors: relevant attention scales everything. For roofing, use an affable roofer from the team (authority plus authenticity). → C §1.13 · M §1.13
- B2B teardowns: staged and stock looks fail (monday.com, ClickUp, Pipedrive, TikTok for Business). Phone-shot creator footage and one bold caption bar draw praise. For an agency ad, show the people behind it, a real dashboard screenshot or a Loom. → C §4c
- His own course ads (lead-gen-like, high-ticket) used Post-it plus Ads Manager screenshot statics ("your ad account needs therapy" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]) and absurd-prop end cards. They became an account's #1, 2, 3, 7 and 12 top spenders (creator-reported). → C §4a, §5
- The insurance AI test script (Veo 3, Texas): opens on "You're overpaying for home and auto insurance. Do you even know how much you're paying?" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]. Its $1,700 figure was a demo, not verified data. → T §3
- Brooklyn vs Austin walk-and-talk: the same concept, shot in two cities for a Texas-only brand, performed differently. Local setting matters. → C §4a
- Compliance line relevant to regulated verticals: don't accuse the viewer of a condition in the second person, and AI characters may voice problems but never give "I had an experience" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA] testimonials. → M §1.6 · T §1.12

**Transferable plays (principle-level, not tied to e-commerce)**
- First-frame and top-of-frame discipline, setting and casting as targeting, belonging test, relevance chain to the landing page (§1.1–1.3, §1.6).
- Founder or principal talking to camera as the person who understands the problem best (§1.4).
- Past-tense I-statements and the stubborn-friend / insecurity frame, within compliance limits (§4).
- Post-it copy tests to find the "words that matter" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] before you produce anything (C §2.7 · T §2.8).
- Spend as verdict, guided volume, don't kill top spenders, check the site first (§1.8, §1.9, §1.13). All of these need enough conversion volume. With one conversion a day "you can't be running 50 ads" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (T §1.14).
- Long story ads with mid-roll CTA and end card (§1.7). Evergreen concepts (§1.17).

**DTC-only or weak transfer**
- DPA and catalog wins for unambiguous products (C §1.17). There is no catalog in lead gen.
- ASC as the harvest campaign, plus the ASC/incrementality audit built on existing-customer and engaged-audience segments (T §2.3–2.5). This is sales-campaign machinery, and its lead-gen use is not evidenced in the canon.
- The ATC/IC proxy metrics and the Shopify/Triple Whale multiplier (T §2.5, §2.12). Only the principle (a proxy that shows real intent) transfers.
- Product-in-hand, unboxing, POV product-filling-frame and packaging-as-creative (C §3, §4b · M §4). A service has no physical product, so the analogue is the problem, the place or the person.
- Sale and deal-feeling tactics such as LTOs and findable discounts (M §2.22).
- Caution: nearly all of his numbers come from DTC e-com accounts (T scope note). Treat every benchmark as untested for lead gen until Ivan's own spend data confirms it.
