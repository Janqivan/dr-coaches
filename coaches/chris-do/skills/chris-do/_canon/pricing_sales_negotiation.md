# Pricing, Sales & Negotiation — how Chris Do sells and prices
> Scope: how Chris prices, opens the money talk, anchors, handles objections, runs discovery, closes and walks away. Sources: [YT:<id>] = YouTube video id (see voice_bio_sources.md for corpus stats). (year) = upload year. "mentions" = how many separate items in the corpus support it.

## 1. Core positions (ranked by how often he says it)
- **Budget follows the size of the client's problem; context decides high or low** — "Small problems small budget big problems big budget"; it is relative to the client, not to you. A tiny budget against a "big" problem means the problem isn't real. [YT:GoX94-uZhb4, YT:-C01FC27Jg0, YT:SABhSGYADUE, YT:RFk8ZmIDrFM] (2014-2026) · mentions 107 [quote src: YT:B-NxAZiDkKo]
  - ➕ ask for the "desired future state"; if it's a small delta, small improvement, small budget, small pain, walk away and find another problem. Common under $250K annual revenue: small clients with small pain points. If they insist "this is the only problem I have," say "I'm not the right person for you," get off the call and move on — [YT:G1aSl_gcNZQ] (n.d.)
- **Charge more to get better clients** — payers value their time and they're not going to micromanage you; "Pricing is positioning"; "Think like Gucci": set the price, then find buyers who can afford it. [YT:h-eRwbbpfxo, YT:fJSLXqiBVDQ, YT:fIZD4KHxcow, YT:qfA0RkYube8] (2016-2026) · mentions 106
- **Selling is serving and asking, not pitching** — S.A.L.E.S. = Serve, Ask, Listen, Empathize, Summarize ("notice there's no selling in this at all"); "you don't tell you ask"; question quality, not portfolio, proves expertise. [YT:KXXfXtey9Jc, YT:B-NxAZiDkKo, YT:tfeFSjrZ_Aw, YT:KlnOS_Blig4] (2016-2026) · mentions 97 [quote src: YT:-JAPZs9QQUM]

- **Whoever carries the risk earns the profit** — "All profit comes from risk": hourly leaves the risk with the client, "that's why your rates hourly are so low"; fixed fees and guarantees move it to you and cost more. ## 2. Frameworks (named models and step sequences)
### Pricing-model ladder: inputs, outputs, outcomes
- When he uses it: asked how to price. First: "what are you really selling, what units will we use to measure effectiveness" (2024). [quote src: YT:tfeFSjrZ_Aw]
- Steps: 1. Hourly/day rate prices inputs ("a temporary employee"). 2. Fixed fee prices outputs, padded because "whoever takes on the risk makes more money"; fuzzy scope gets "a fixed fee for the finite scope and a variable fee for the undefined scope". 3. Outcomes = value pricing, the "Holy Grail". 4. Royalty/commission: "I would not build an entire agency around this fourth model". A retainer is "still selling time"; with a subscription "you don't sell time at all". [quote src: YT:b5OtiUpNvqs, YT:tfeFSjrZ_Aw, YT:2dY6MsSedKg]
- Sources: [YT:8vwgUwgH2Js, YT:b5OtiUpNvqs, YT:YjWOxhMD9Ho, YT:Ju4Hz6tZUXg] (2021-2025)

### Price the client: the value conversation in order
- When he uses it: before any number, replacing scope → time x rate → price.
- Steps (2019): 1. "Where would you like to be a year from now, 3 years from now?" 2. "How will we measure success?" 3. "How valuable is that to you?" 4. Price. 5. Only then the solution. 2025 adds: a fair price "discounted for uncertainty", confirmed in neutral words, then scope to that number with a 50% deposit. [quote src: YT:ivKnj9ffcmE]
- His example: 6 leads/month x 50% close x $10K, minus 30% = $21K, x 10 = A$210,000: "would that make you happy?" (2021).
- Sources: [YT:b0fQyaBSSI0, YT:6rZQPhXGOlk, YT:ZIF7aRNr8BA, YT:qZ1EYMgQsKE] (2019-2025)

### The Two B's: Baseline and Benchmark
- When he uses it: any goal or "make the logo better" request (his coinage).
- Steps: 1. Baseline: where things are now. 2. Benchmark: what result, measured how, by when. 3. Need: "how long have you been stuck at 30 customers a month". 4. Value: "what is one new customer worth to you". [quote src: YT:GoX94-uZhb4]
- Rule: people "pay you for the difference between where they are and where they want to go". [quote src: YT:u0laTWsoNvs]
- Sources: [YT:Osoz1cVM-mw, YT:-JAPZs9QQUM, YT:B-NxAZiDkKo] (2019-2026)
- ➕ Baseline and Benchmark: for an employee seeking a raise: set the benchmark of where things are now and have the supervisor set the goal of where they want you; once the goal is set and you think it's realistic, ask "would that warrant a pay raise or a title change?" if you achieve it — [YT:RvFTHr_NE88] (n.d.)

### Fair share of value (the insurance percentage)
- When he uses it: once the problem has a dollar size.
- Steps: 1. "You wouldn't want to spend a million dollars to fix a million dollar problem". 2. "if you could buy an insurance policy to stop this from happening," what percentage? 3. Reference ~10%, "on the low end". 4. If they say 30%: "Sounds very generous... I don't think we need to spend that much." 5. "Here's the great news, I can help you for a lot less than that". [quote src: YT:-C01FC27Jg0]
- His example (2024): a logo last bought for $2,000; "Are we solving a $30,000 problem?"; rollout ≈ $2M; in what universe is 10% of the printing cost a lot of money? [quote src: YT:6rZQPhXGOlk]
- Sources: [YT:6rZQPhXGOlk, YT:pc66141WYEI, YT:-C01FC27Jg0] (2018-2025)

### Bid like an entrepreneur (cost-plus build-up)
- When he uses it: fixed bids for freelancers who just multiply hours by rate.
- Steps: 1. Price what you'd pay someone else, and pay yourself. 2. Mark freelancers up ($500 to $750/day "because artists always underestimate"). 3. Add a producer line even if it's you. 4. Your art direction at your day rate. 5. Rentals. 6. Profit on its own line, 10-30%: "it's markup it's our profit".
- His example: naive $50/hr x 10 days = $5,000; built up = $17K + 20% = $20,400 (2016-2021). Later he calls cost-plus "a very self-centered approach to pricing". [quote src: YT:SABhSGYADUE]
- Sources: [YT:OiUOaZJJYzg, YT:6BKWq9VJd8w, YT:I48unL6m0eU, YT:TB54_6bEP-A] (2016-2025)
#### Red-flag rule (from Emily Cohen, endorsed)
- When he uses it: pricing a client who shows warning signs.
- Steps: 1. Add 10% to the estimate for every red flag. 2. The three flags: a slow or overly verbose client who chews up your time; not talking to the decision-maker; too many stakeholders, "too many cooks in the kitchen". 3. All three: a 30% tax. [quote src: YT:9lq8gs3CYfk]
- Sources: [YT:Ejvr-yzCjXw] (2020)

### Three options and price brackets
- When he uses it: presenting price without inviting a shop-around.
- Steps: 1. Core offer in the middle, cheaper left, pricier right; your highest expected budget becomes the lowest option. 2. Lead with the premium and pause: "let the price anchor do its work for you". 3. "80% of the time they pick the one in the middle". 4. Ranges: two round numbers far apart, high first ("25,000 [pause] and 5,000"); never narrow ("the only number between four and six that I know is five"). [quote src: YT:fVLK9fJoM1g, YT:sHut1B9gWWY, YT:Pii_T2wIPwI]
- His example (2020): "the White Glove experience" at "$45,000"; "our most popular package it's $20,000"; a quick logo, "we can do that for 10k". [quote src: YT:fVLK9fJoM1g]
- Sources: [YT:fVLK9fJoM1g, YT:87CPZdh6FIc, YT:o8IdDEJym1U] (2015-2024)

### Orders-of-magnitude budget finder
- When he uses it: unknown scope, six-to-seven-figure jobs.
- Steps: 1. "Is it 10,000, 100,000, or is it a million dollars?" 2. "just be quiet let the client say something." 3. "Are you closer to the million or to the hundred thousand?" 4. "How shy of a million are you, a hundred thousand or two hundred thousand?" 5. Bid just under their approval limit; don't echo their exact number ("460, 480, you're okay?"). 6. Paid discovery, then a fixed bid: "we don't do wags". [quote src: YT:sHut1B9gWWY, YT:o8IdDEJym1U]
- Sources: [YT:o8IdDEJym1U, YT:OuOL8xQGpyE, YT:h1voCMAT9Qc] (2021-2025)

### Raise-your-rate rules and the ceiling game
- When he uses it: too many yeses, overbooked, or right after praise.
- Steps: 1. "clients say yes to your price three times in a row you're charging too little". 2. "just double your rate" ($50/hr to $60-75, not $52). 3. Rangefind like Battleship: too far, too short, then close. 4. Raise per new client: "I want to hear a no so I know what it sounds like". 5. Too many no's: don't cut; get another client, level up. [quote src: YT:Bm0nYSJikwo, YT:Ju4Hz6tZUXg]
- Sources: [YT:Bm0nYSJikwo, YT:Dr4Ux8_mfU8, YT:MTqiV6robGg, YT:OREd4PPWCyY] (2017-2026)
#### Four icons: why sales work (plus the reverse sale)
- When he uses it: explaining why a price move lands, or raising a price too cheap to be believed.
- Steps: 1. Anchor: $299 beside $149 makes it feel like $150 off. 2. Urgency: a deadline. 3. Scarcity: "only three left", true or not (none when it's digital). 4. The train: customers learn your prices only go up. Reverse sale: he told Ben Burns "this class is too cheap" and announced $149 to $299 in 30 days.
- Sources: [YT:UNrd-XtaC28] (2020)

### Symmetry of logic
- When he uses it: a belief that only works one way.
- Steps: 1. "if it works one way then it should work in most other ways". 2. Find the mirror: a $7 IPA gets no free refills, so why repour a design 14 times? 3. Ask where else you buy something when you can't find the specs or the price? [quote src: YT:Abi8kwkfZbA]
- Sources: [YT:pc66141WYEI, YT:Abi8kwkfZbA, YT:DeriW0XyR_k] (2017-2025)

### Discovery question sequences
- When he uses it: every first call; "the first problem that you hear is not the correct problem". [quote src: YT:my_p1fDOz00]
- Universal: "what outcome or result are you looking for" → what is the challenge for you what's getting in your way → "what's the real problem for you then" → "what Solutions have you considered" → "is there anything else" → "so where would you like to go from here". [quote src: YT:zX5jrOdoCe8]
- Coaching Habit call (2021): "what's on your mind?" → "what's the real challenge here for you in getting new clients?" → "if we do this together, what would success look like?" → his add-on: "what's one client worth to you?" → "how much money makes sense to spend against that?" [quote src: YT:TqczYbFPWnk]
- Socratic Selling (2025): now story → change story → obstacle → desired future → quantified impact; past first: "last time you did this how did it turn out". [quote src: YT:-JAPZs9QQUM]
- Sources: [YT:ZSnGe9bUIuc, YT:B-NxAZiDkKo, YT:6Thuc_W_dDU, YT:VIJdAhWu7QU] (2017-2025)

### Why this, why now, why me
- When he uses it: early, so buyers sell themselves (from Jonathan Stark).
- Steps: "why this"; "why now" ("why not do nothing"); "why me" ("of all the options you have why would you want to work with me"). No good answer: "this is probably not the right time to move forward". [quote src: YT:4kVo2YEzPqQ, YT:Zl2NEKUCZSg]
- Sources: [YT:9xF0kcs9lm4, YT:51t6-c9Y0y0, YT:6rZQPhXGOlk] (2018-2025)

### Embrace and pivot
- When he uses it: any objection.
- Steps: 1. Embrace ("I just own it you're right we are too expensive"). 2. Pivot: make that option less desirable. 3. Show why you're different, with stories. Skip step 1 and "you're slippery you're distrustful". [quote src: YT:4kmc253GZWs]
- Sources: [YT:dKIyObgkBVI, YT:LMvb32KyXP8, YT:ivKnj9ffcmE] (2016-2025)
- ➕ when he hits a barrier he pivots rather than hitting it head on; he won't say a $3,500 budget is "way too low," "ridiculous" or an insult to his artistic integrity; instead he says "let me just table that" and "I want to get into what the real problem is" — [YT:XlfaPZQEp0M] (n.d.)

### Retreat and follow
- When he uses it: new opportunities (Blair Enns).
- Steps: 1. Step back; see if they follow. 2. Try to kill it three times: "this might not be a good fit for me", "the budget might not be right", "I'm not sure my schedule can accommodate". 3. If they pursue, "they're pursuing you now"; if not, you saved heartache. [quote src: YT:cHveCGDZfhQ]
- Sources: [YT:LMvb32KyXP8, YT:7MB_nwvmB-E, YT:4kVo2YEzPqQ] (2016-2026)

### Hypothetical close
- When he uses it: problem, goals, pain and number agreed.
- Steps: 1. Summarize. 2.
