Source note: the input has no dates (n.d.); an era is written inline only where an item states one. "(Fast Hours)" items are podcast voices, not Jamey's own opinion. Jamey: 24 at recording, New York brand designer and creative director ("TechBimbo" on X), co-owns the sleep supplement Portal, teaches the Maven course The AI Creative Director. ### Rules (ranked by how often they say it)
- **Use reference images, not long prompts.** Once references (and a profile) carry the look, the whole prompt can be one word (`astronaut`, `cat`, `dog`); mood, lighting, color and vibe already live in the image, and an LLM describes an image better than you will. About 80% of the time she works from reference photos and moodboards because it is far more repeatable; she is publicly "very anti-prompt". [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:61_srA-xmts, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.) · mentions 17
- **Build taste daily, mostly from outside design.** Save constantly into a few broad boards (Pinterest, Cosmos, X), watch films, read, follow fashion and cooking content; taste is "a muscle trained on exposure", and scrolling design inspiration or copying trends does not build it. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:ig2qp8-a0Ic, YT:61_srA-xmts] (n.d.) · mentions 15
- **Pick the model by the job.** Diffusion models (Midjourney, Flux) are aesthetic and weak at adherence; multimodal models (Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2) think and keep subject, text and logos, so mock-ups, text and likeness go there. Do not force Midjourney into realistic product photography. Test models yourself; choose a wrapper only for the layer it adds. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:61_srA-xmts] (n.d.) · mentions 13
- **AI is a hard skill, not one click.** Nothing good arrives finished: about a 50% one-shot rate, nearer 80% with a clear aim plus proper references; stacking or swapping things will break, and fixing in Photoshop is fine. Giving up on a tool "comes back to like skill issue a lot". [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU, YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 11
- **Work in steps, one change at a time.** Many asks in one prompt lower adherence and add variables: remove the background first, then insert; two or three changes only when each is simple. Number-one fix in her course critiques; it can be broken depending on model, task and how particular you are. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 9
- **Be visible: invisibility is worse than incompetence.** Post work, takes, WIPs and reactions; clients say they always see her posting and come to her first; credibility came from X, not cold outreach. Content is career insurance. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:ig2qp8-a0Ic, YT:aP3wFAoohZI, YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU] (n.d.) · mentions 8
- **Append a "prompt booster" to anything with text, logos or product consistency.** A standard directive paragraph that basically says do not change anything; she claims it makes generations about 50% more accurate and keeps a similar one for faces. Models still do not treat this as common sense. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 8
- **Right tool for the right job; Photoshop the small stuff.** Tiny logos (serifs, indentations), lost fingernails and fine details get fixed in Photoshop. She uses Nano Banana the way she used Photoshop (edit, upscale, change a detail) and generates from scratch only when the image must be super realistic. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc, YT:61_srA-xmts, YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU] (n.d.) · mentions 6
- **One loaded word replaces a paragraph.** `candid` loads a casual imperfect angle, `quaint` a believable not-too-new apartment, `luxury` tall windows and a skyline, `editorial` or `Dazed editorial photo shoot` a gritty cover feel, `product photography shot` a high-quality, photoreal, straight-on orientation. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 6
- **Judge output against the board and the business goal with brutal honesty.** People call it a win once hands stop having six fingers; two images can look great alone and miss the board. While generating, keep asking whether the path still maps to the original goal. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:61_srA-xmts] (n.d.) · mentions 5
- **Consistency with a through line is the mark of skill.** Viral fantasy art falls apart for a brand that speaks to several audiences, so keep swappable elements; but a set in exactly one style turns flat or too pink, so weave in a few off-style images as long as the through line holds. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.) · mentions 4
- **Define the look up front, then hand the client a master prompt.** She does the defining work (space, codes, reference images) and the client generates new images alone; she is "kind of allergic to retainers". Per product, write a master prompt listing what the model gets wrong every time. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 4
- **Match the stand-in or reference object to the product's shape and visual weight.** Swaps work when shape matches (her jar vs a similar cylinder); an Ozempic-pen product failed because the shape differed. The closer references are to the final result, the easier the model listens. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.) · mentions 3
- **Prompt positively.** For Portal she says what she wants (metallic, matte); if you need heavy negative prompting, something is wrong with your starting conditions, so change the photo or approach. The `no bubble gum` style parameter works sometimes, mostly not. [YT:_r7NUcM41Kc, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.) · mentions 2
- **No JSON prompts.** In her view they make images flatter and more "AI" and lose textural depth; "the laziest way ever to do prompting". [YT:61_srA-xmts, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.) · mentions 2
- **Use AI in a sprint only where it is faster or does what is hard or expensive (mainly the latter).** Client latitude depends on the job: branding backgrounds, stock-style photos and illustration can go wild; ecom product photography, especially apparel, is by the book with a planned shot list and exact product look. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU] (n.d.) · mentions 2
- **Run brand work as time-boxed sprints.** Founder or key decision-makers, lean team, no endless deliverables; pre-seed startups change within 1-18 months, so a 4-6 week deep brand project is wasted. If a startup wants branding in 2 weeks for $10K, take it. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs] (n.d.) · mentions 1
- **Never feed a Nano Banana image of a real person back into Midjourney.** It degrades the face and changes details (gloves); making it a video and pulling frames also drifts from the likeness. [YT:61_srA-xmts] (n.d.) · mentions 1

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### The AI Creative Director framework (five principles, plus starting conditions)
- When they use it: any image, text or vibe-coding task; when a run fails, find which principle was mishandled (rarely just you or just the model). She distilled it over ~6 months from what trips people up; she says most people are bad at AI for three reasons: no direction, over-prompting, wrong tools or models.
- Steps: 1. Aim before you shoot: know what you are making and roughly how it looks, then set the starting conditions: (a) model, (b) reference material for subject (person or product) and style (illustrative vs photoreal, film vs flash; SREFs), (c) prompt, vibey (one word in Midjourney) or specific and instructional for editing. 2. An image is worth a thousand words. 3. Nothing good arrives finished. 4. Work in steps. 5. Use the right tool for the job.
- Sources: [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc, YT:EWmtRROWEE8] (n.d.)

#### SWORD prompt structure
- When they use it: prompts that need a specific output; e-com shot lists and briefs that non-AI or non-creative teammates can read. More upfront work with the client means fewer headaches.
- Steps: S = subject (who or what is in the image). W = where (environment, setting, placement). O = orientation (pose, angle, framing, product position). R = rendering (camera, lighting, color, texture, realistic vs illustrative). D = directive (rules, constraints, common-sense checks such as `do not change anything`; her prompt booster). Changing SWORD elements retroactively, or several at once, is where things break.
- Their example: product prompt = subject `a matte black supplement jar with metallic text`; where `a white photo studio environment`; orientation `product photography shot`; rendering ultra high quality, strong directional natural light, camera type, image style; directive = booster. The UGC version is in the Recipe library.
- Sources: [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc] (n.d.)

#### Brand-look flow in Midjourney: direction, create, iterate, reinforce, scale
- When they use it: building a consistent look for a client or a stock set; her opening claim is that it all comes down to a "very tight and manicured process". Demoed live on a pink, internet-coded look.
- Steps: 1. Direction: a Pinterest or Cosmos mood board of the vibe (camera style, attitude, model type or scene). 2. Create phase, fast and not precious, ~10-15 minutes: throwaway test prompts only to learn what board, refs and prompt tell the AI (`beautiful female model`, `astronaut`, `ethereal female model`, cats, runner). 3. Paste the board into Midjourney mood boards for a style code and compare honestly: it averages out (missed high contrast, darkness, neon-with-pastel). 4. Retry with the same images as SREFs in the prompt bar. 5. Remove the image causing a cast, regenerate. 6. Iterate: layer a personalization profile (`late 2025 aesthetic`, built by ranking 2025 iPhone-style images) and extra boards over the SREFs; it cut over-stylization, kept the pink, added depth and crisper skin. 7. Reinforce: ~30 liked images into a new board, test on a new subject (turtle), optionally beside a `real skin` board with a one-word prompt like `model`. 8. Scale: same SREF/profile setup, swap only the subject; Vary Subtle or Strong for hand problems; edit in Nano Banana; deliver as a package.
- Their example: demo brief was very pink and cute but not super girly, internet-coded, built on juxtapositions ("things where they shouldn't be"). With her refs and profile codes almost any prompt returns high contrast, black backgrounds and rainbow accents; without them, a generic look.
- Sources: [YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:EWmtRROWEE8] (n.d.)

#### Mood-board layers ("style layering")
- When they use it: stacking boards to steer Midjourney; she says it feels like making potions or alchemy.
- Steps: 1. Style board = blanket layer for color, saturation, vibe; kept in almost everything (SREFs sometimes inject style better; V7 and V8 behave differently). 2. Element boards inject specific traits that bleed through: a board of AI versions of her makes women come out "sort of like me"; a `real skin` board (skin texture, wrinkles, freckles) pushes toward profile shots and skin focus; same trick for makeup and fashion. 3. Stack them and learn what each does; add the `real skin` board on top of an edgy blurry style to keep the style. 4. Board size varies: 50 images, 3, or only aerial-view images so only the angle comes through (Fast Hours).
- Sources: [YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU, YT:EWmtRROWEE8] (n.d.)

#### Client brand pipeline: intake, board, SREFs, handoff
- When they use it: branding clients (CXO case, Magic Path launch assets, client questionnaires).
- Steps: 1. Questionnaire in advance plus a FigJam board: half functional (what you do, goals, business model, competitors), half silly (what movie, which brands would be its friends, what car, a playlist); clients answer differently alone than on a call, so offer both. 2. Shared Pinterest-sourced FigJam board with thumbs-up/down stickers: builds shared language and fixes "I want to look like Stripe and Apple". 3. Design-focused brand boards (Figma, Pinterest, Cosmos); client picks one; decide the actual images behind it; build a secondary board to feed Midjourney as SREFs or mood board. 4. Color from the board, tuned in HSB (she cannot guess hex), Coolors with locks, tied to a meaning the client cares about. 5. Handoff in Figma: final prompt, profiles used, stylization, reference photos, a set of images (90-100% of the showcase set came from that one setup); client types one word. Midjourney has no sharing, so big projects get a dedicated account. Keep the Figma file organized so Figma MCP with Claude works. [quote src: YT:N-6LCrvxHIs]
- Sources: [YT:ovhFdZ_6uB8, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU, YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.)

#### Midjourney to Nano Banana Pro handoff
- When they use it: Midjourney gives composition, model and expression but misses hands, devices and sometimes clothing; she skips inpainting in Midjourney and does the finish in Flora.
- Steps: 1. Build the look in Midjourney (accept its slop and low realism for vibe). 2. Take winners into Flora and use Nano Banana Pro as Photoshop; prompt like talking to an intern. 3. Name the swap, then guardrails: do not change anything else, keep position and size exactly the same, state exactly what is visible. 4. Upscale for texture while the style stays nearly unchanged. 5. For founders and likeness, place the person with the Midjourney images as scene and mood reference.
- Sources: [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:61_srA-xmts] (n.d.)

### Fix library (problem → fix → why)
- **Problem:** `a black jar` gives a different jar every time → **Fix:** prompt `a matte black candle`, then `replace the candle on the coffee table` with the real product → **Why:** models know a candle's shape and visual weight; for odd bottles find a well-understood abstraction. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** label pastes flat, reads as screen-printed or sits dead center → **Fix:** say it is metallic, give the colors, a matte-vs-jar texture difference and a small margin → **Why:** the Illustrator file shows no texture; a bare place-the-label prompt pastes like a flat image. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** label mockup is bad and cannot be prompted out → **Fix:** remove the old text first, then place the label, as two steps → **Why:** colors and text bleed in from the reference and hallucinate. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** two runners in perfect unison with matching socks → **Fix:** (1) change the angle to straight-on, (2) `change the man's shoes and socks to black`; for a no-logo client remove the shoe logo, funnel it back with `replace the man's shoes` plus `keep everything else the same`, repeat with gray shorts → **Why:** one thing at a time; over-prompting kills generations. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** Midjourney mood board averages out the vibe → **Fix:** paste the same images as SREFs → **Why:** SREFs fixed style and camera treatment; tight boards (five fuzzy 3D cats) transfer, general ones average. [YT:2RD3FP5iWJY, YT:EWmtRROWEE8] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** every output has a cast (green eyeshadow image, bubble gum) → **Fix:** delete that SREF or crop out the dominant part and re-drag; do not prompt a removal → **Why:** one overwhelming feature drags every generation. [YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** a reference pulls Vogue-like results when you want a thinking photo → **Fix:** zoom and crop the reference inside the Midjourney UI, drag the crop back in, rerun → **Why:** composition largely is style, so image and style references behave the same. [YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** dither or pixel style will not hold → **Fix:** generate clean, then layer a shader or filter afterward → **Why:** AI is bad at holding it; (Fast Hours) exact hex, posterization and vibe in one pass is too many variables. [YT:_Ldhm8qB_JU] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** swap fails on an odd-shaped product (Ozempic pen, lip pencil) → **Fix:** start from a similar-shaped image or even a sketch (a frame with two red squares) → **Why:** the model then need not rescale or change shadows, so effort goes to packaging and aesthetics. [YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y] (n.d.)

### Coaching and critique moves
- Stuck generation: "Firstly, take a break. Always." Return with a new SREF, a different prompt, a reference or a camera; then ask what is actually the problem, read the board and decide which elements truly matter (too busy, "too Midjourney", 18 SREFs). [YT:2RD3FP5iWJY] (n.d.)
- Description exercise: describe an image in extreme detail in 10 seconds in your head; humans give two sentences, art directors add film camera and lime-wash wall, an LLM given the image writes three paragraphs. Lesson: let the model extract the latent information. [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs] (n.d.)
- Triage a struggling student: no direction? over-prompting? wrong tool or model? Then name which of the five principles was mishandled (usually many asks in one prompt). [YT:EWmtRROWEE8, YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:wLWaHlVPA-Y] (n.d.)
- Make clients react instead of describe: thumbs-up/down on a shared board shows ideas they would not name. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs] (n.d.)
- Learning path: copy tutorials (~25 Illustrator ones), one small task a couple of hours a week, an AI sabbatical or AI Fridays, and tell Claude your goal and ask what you do not know you need to know. [YT:N-6LCrvxHIs, YT:_r7NUcM41Kc, YT:A-fjtCssmt8] (n.d.)
