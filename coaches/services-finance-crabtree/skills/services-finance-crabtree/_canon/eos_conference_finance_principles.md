## Framework 1 — What Belongs on the EOS Scorecard (Crabtree's view)

**What it is:** the EOS Scorecard is 5-15 weekly leading-indicator numbers. Most EOS firms fill it with revenue, pipeline, and activity metrics. Crabtree's argument — at least three of those slots should be financial leading indicators, not lagging GAAP numbers.

**The Crabtree-EOS Scorecard finance slots:**
1. **Trailing-13-week LER** — labor productivity, rolling. Updated weekly off the rolling P&L. Goal: above 2:1 floor, trending toward 2.5:1+.
2. **Trailing-13-week GP per direct labor dollar** — the delivery-team productivity number. Catches problems before they show up in pretax profit.
3. **Cash position vs. Core Capital Target** — cash today divided by 2-months-opex target. Goal: >100% always.
4. **AR aging > 60 days** — leading indicator on working-capital squeeze. Goal: under 10% of total AR.
5. **Pipeline GP (not pipeline revenue)** — pipeline weighted by GP margin, not gross revenue. Forces the sales team to price right at proposal stage.

**Why weekly and rolling, not monthly and YTD:** the EOS Scorecard runs weekly at the L10. YTD or monthly closes too slowly to be a leading indicator. Rolling 13 weeks gives you a slope every week without seasonal noise from a single bad week.

**Failure mode:** EOS Scorecard with 12 activity metrics, 0 financial leading indicators. The leadership team optimizes for activity. Six months later the P&L is broken and nobody saw it on the Scorecard.

## Framework 2 — Financial Rocks (Quarterly Priorities the Crabtree Way)

**What it is:** EOS Rocks are 3-7 quarterly priorities per seat. Crabtree's overlay — at least one Rock per quarter at the company level must be a Financial Rock tied to a Simple Numbers metric.

**Examples of well-formed Financial Rocks:**
- "Raise Direct LER from 2.1:1 to 2.4:1 by end of Q3 — by re-pricing top 5 underpriced engagements and exiting 2 below-floor accounts."
- "Build Core Capital from 1.2 months of opex to 2.0 months by end of Q4 — via $X retained from monthly profit and zero owner distributions until target hit."
- "Cut Management labor by $Y to bring Management LER from 1.4:1 to 2:1 — via the org redesign already drafted."
- "Re-cast last 24 months of P&L on the SN chart of accounts (labor out of COGS) before annual planning session."

**The form (SMART for EOS):** specific number, specific timeframe, specific seat owning it. "Improve profitability" is not a Rock. "Move Direct LER from 2.1 to 2.4 by Q3 end, owned by Integrator" is a Rock.

**Why every quarter:** without a Financial Rock the leadership team can have a great quarter on revenue, sales, hiring, and ops — and lose ground on profitability. A Financial Rock guarantees one eye stays on the economic engine.

**Failure mode:** "growth Rocks" only. Three quarters of growth Rocks land you in the Black Hole at the same broken LER, only bigger.

## Framework 3 — Simple Numbers + V/TO (Vision Traction Organizer)

**What it is:** the V/TO has 10-Year Target, 3-Year Picture, 1-Year Plan, Quarterly Rocks. Crabtree's overlay — each layer needs specific financial targets, not just revenue.

**The financial overlay on a V/TO:**
- **10-Year Target:** revenue + ROIC target (e.g., $50M revenue at 30% ROIC). Revenue alone produces "big and broken" firms; ROIC keeps the target honest.
- **3-Year Picture:** revenue + pretax % + Core Capital months + LER target. A real picture of the financial shape of the firm in 3 years.
- **1-Year Plan:** revenue + GP + pretax + Core Capital position + the 3 LER buckets (Direct / Management / Sales). The full operating model for the year.
- **Quarterly Rocks:** at least one Financial Rock per quarter (Framework 2).

**Why ROIC at the 10-Year:** firms that set 10-year targets purely on revenue grow into 12% ROIC monsters that don't pay the owner more. Setting ROIC at the same level forces all the operational choices (capacity, hiring, working capital, pricing) to compound toward enterprise value.

**Failure mode:** V/TO with revenue-only financial targets. The leadership team grows the firm to a number that's bigger and worse than where they started.

## Framework 4 — The L10 Meeting Finance Section

**What it is:** the Level-10 Meeting runs every week, 90 minutes, fixed agenda — Segue, Scorecard, Rock Review, Customer/Employee Headlines, To-Do List, IDS. Crabtree's view — the Scorecard portion of every L10 must include a 5-minute look at the financial leading indicators, and the IDS section gets one finance issue per month minimum.

**The L10 finance protocol:**
1. **Scorecard (every L10, 5 minutes):** review the 5 financial leading indicators from Framework 1. Anything off-track gets dropped to the Issues List.
2. **Monthly L10 (once a month, 15 minutes):** rolling-twelve P&L review. Slope of LER, slope of GP, slope of pretax. Working capital position. Cash position vs. Core Capital Target.
3. **Quarterly L10 (the State of the Company session):** full Simple Numbers review — LER by bucket, ROIC trend, Core Capital, owner comp at market check, four forces of cash flow on the trailing year.

**Why the finance section can't be skipped:** every operator knows the L10 has a tendency to drift toward customer/employee fire-fighting in the IDS section. Without a fixed finance slot in the Scorecard portion, finance gets visited only when something breaks.

**Failure mode:** L10 where finance only comes up in IDS when the bookkeeper raises a red flag. By then the slope has been wrong for a quarter.

## Framework 5 — Accountability Chart Meets the Three LER Buckets

**What it is:** EOS Accountability Chart names seats and the 5 roles each seat owns. Crabtree's three LER buckets (Direct / Management / Sales) map cleanly onto the Accountability Chart and force a question most EOS firms duck — who owns the LER target for each bucket?

**The mapping:**
- **Integrator seat** owns total LER (blended) and the company pretax target.
- **Head of Delivery / Ops seat** owns Direct LER.
- **Head of Sales seat** owns Sales LER.
- **Integrator (or COO if seated) also owns Management LER** by default, because Management LER is about how the leadership layer pays for itself.
- **CFO / Controller seat** owns Core Capital, working capital, tax reserve, and rolling-twelve discipline. (This seat is missing from most sub-$10M EOS firms — Crabtree argues it should be filled, even fractionally.)

**Why this matters:** EOS without LER ownership produces leadership teams where "profitability" is a shared abstraction. Nobody owns it, so nobody fixes it. Naming the bucket owners makes the number a person's problem.

**Failure mode:** Accountability Chart that names roles like "Marketing" and "Operations" without naming the financial KPI each role owns. Leaders run their function without a profit KPI; finance is "the CFO's job" — but the CFO doesn't control hiring or pricing decisions inside the functions.

## Framework 6 — Profit Allocation Through the EOS Lens (Crabtree vs. Profit First inside EOS)

**What it is:** EOS firms commonly run Profit First (Michalowicz) for cash allocation. Crabtree's view — Profit First's allocation buckets work as a cash discipline, but the underlying profit number must be calculated the Simple Numbers way first, or the buckets are filling from a wrong number.

**The two-step sequence inside an EOS firm:**
1. **Step 1 (Crabtree):** calculate true pretax profit. Labor out of COGS. Owner comp at market. True GP. True LER. The pretax number on the SN chart is the number you work from.
2. **Step 2 (Michalowicz):** allocate that true pretax number across Profit First buckets — Profit, Owner Comp top-up, Tax, Opex — per the firm's chosen target allocation percentages.

**Why both, not one:** Profit First alone fills buckets from whatever profit the firm currently sees, which on a contaminated chart of accounts is wrong. Simple Numbers alone produces the right number but doesn't give the owner the cash discipline to actually preserve it. Used together — Crabtree calculates the truth, Michalowicz preserves it — they answer different questions and don't conflict.

**Crabtree's actual endorsement of Profit First:** the JamesClear-flagged endorsement on the Profit First book — paraphrased — says Michalowicz makes profit allocation simple enough that there's no excuse not to have profit AND cash flow. Crabtree explicitly recommends running both.

**Failure mode:** EOS firms running Profit First on a contaminated chart of accounts (labor in COGS, owner under-paid). The buckets fill, the firm looks healthy, the underlying business is broken.

## Framework 7 — The Quarterly + Annual EOS Session, Finance Edition

**What it is:** the EOS Quarterly Session (1 day) and Annual Session (2 days) are where the leadership team works on the business. Crabtree's overlay — every Quarterly Session opens with a 60-minute Simple Numbers review, and every Annual Session opens with a 3-hour one.

**The Quarterly Session finance block (60 min):**
1. Rolling-twelve P&L on the SN chart (15 min).
2. LER by bucket — trend over last 4 quarters (10 min).
3. Core Capital position vs. target + working capital trend (10 min).
4. Top 3 financial issues to drop into the Issues List for the session (15 min).
5. Confirm at least one Financial Rock for the coming quarter (10 min).

**The Annual Session finance block (3 hours):**
1. Re-cast trailing 24 months on the SN chart.
2. Industry benchmark comparison (the 100-company dashboard if accessible, or industry data).
3. Full four-forces-of-cash-flow review on the trailing year.
4. ROIC trend over the last 3 years.
5. Set the 1-Year Plan financial targets (revenue, GP, pretax, LER by bucket, Core Capital position).
6. Set the 3-Year Picture financial overlay (Framework 3).

**Why this much time:** the financial truth of the business is what every other choice in the V/TO depends on. An Annual Session that skips the deep finance review sets a 1-Year Plan on assumptions instead of numbers.

**Failure mode:** Annual Session that spends 8 hours on Core Values and Vision and 30 minutes on finance. The team leaves inspired and broke.

## Framework 8 — The Integrator's Finance Discipline (Wickman + Crabtree synthesis)

**What it is:** the Integrator role in EOS owns execution of the V/TO. Crabtree's overlay — the Integrator's job description, financially, is to hold the line on five specific disciplines week after week, regardless of leadership team mood.

**The Integrator's five disciplines (Crabtree-EOS edition):**
1. **Hold market-based owner comp.** The Visionary will want to under-pay themselves to celebrate growth or look profitable. The Integrator runs payroll at market and reports the real pretax.
2. **Hold Core Capital Target.** When the Visionary wants to pull a distribution before Core Capital is full, the Integrator says no — until the bucket is full, distributions are below the threshold.
3. **Hold the LER floor on every hire.** The Visionary will want to hire ahead. The Integrator runs the seat-level GP math and only approves hires that clear the 2:1 floor at trailing-twelve capacity.
4. **Hold rolling-twelve as the operating dashboard.** YTD numbers get used for taxes. Operating decisions run on rolling-twelve. The Integrator reports rolling-twelve every L10.
5. **Hold the Quarterly + Annual finance block.** The Visionary will want to skip the finance review to spend more time on Vision. The Integrator protects the session block.

**Why the Integrator and not the Visionary:** Visionaries are wired for growth, optimism, and revenue. Crabtree's frameworks require somebody who will hold a line when the line is unpopular. That's the Integrator's seat.

**Failure mode:** Integrator who defers to the Visionary on finance discipline. The frameworks collapse the moment a Vision-driven growth bet violates the LER floor or Core Capital threshold.
