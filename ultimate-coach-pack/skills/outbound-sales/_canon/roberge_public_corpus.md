## Framework 1 — The Buying Journey > The Selling Journey

Roberge's central reframe across his HBS teaching and Stage 2 writing: the operator should not map their **selling process**. They should map their buyer's **buying journey** and align the sales motion to it.

**The shift:**
- Old frame (most sales orgs): "We have a 6-stage sales pipeline — Prospect, Connect, Discover, Demo, Propose, Close." Built around the rep's activities.
- Roberge's frame: "Our buyer goes through 6 stages — Awareness, Education, Vendor Research, Shortlist, Evaluation, Decision. Each stage has a different buyer question, a different emotion, and a different correct rep behavior."

**Why the reframe matters:**
- Selling-process pipelines treat all prospects the same regardless of where they are in their own buying journey.
- Buying-journey pipelines force the rep to ask "what stage is *this* buyer in?" before deciding what to do next.
- The mismatch — rep pitching features at an Awareness-stage buyer who doesn't yet know they have the problem — is the single most common cause of stalled cycles.

**Operator move:** before designing the sales process, map the buyer's journey first. The sales process is the mirror of the journey, not the other way around.

## Framework 2 — The Sales Hiring Math (Rep Archetypes)

Roberge's deeper hiring framework from his HBS lectures and HubSpot data. Beyond the A-Method scorecard, he segments rep candidates into archetypes based on the **type of selling motion** they're best suited to. The names below are illustrative archetypes (own-words framing) — Roberge's published material uses persona examples rather than fixed names.

**Archetype 1 — The Educator.** Best at consultative, long-cycle, complex sales where the buyer needs to be taught how to think about the problem. Slow to ramp, high LTV per deal closed. Wrong choice for transactional motions.

**Archetype 2 — The Closer.** Best at competitive, multi-vendor, late-stage cycles where the buyer is shortlisting and needs to be pulled across the line. Wrong choice for net-new pipeline generation — they wait for hand-offs.

**Archetype 3 — The Hunter.** Best at net-new outbound, cold prospecting, breaking into accounts. High activity, high rejection tolerance, lower close rates. Wrong choice for warm pipeline conversion.

**Archetype 4 — The Farmer / Expander.** Best at expansion, upsell, account growth inside the existing customer base. Wrong choice for cold prospecting — they avoid uncomfortable activity.

**Roberge's hard rule:** the operator must define which archetype fits the motion *before* hiring, then hire to that archetype. Hiring a Closer to run an outbound motion fails. Hiring a Hunter to run an enterprise account-management motion fails. The cost of mis-mapping the archetype to the motion is the single most expensive hiring mistake in sales.

## Framework 3 — PMF Before Sales Hiring (The Sequence Rule)

Roberge's most-published frame in his Stage 2 Capital writing and his Lenny's Newsletter contribution: **do not hire a VP of Sales until you have product-market fit.**

**The mechanism:**
- Pre-PMF, no sales playbook exists, so no rep can succeed by following it. They have to be the founder.
- A VP of Sales hired pre-PMF will burn 6-12 months trying to build a process around a product that hasn't found its repeatable buyer yet.
- The founder is the only person with the context to do pre-PMF selling, because pre-PMF selling is mostly *learning* what the buyer actually needs.

**Roberge's sequence:**
1. **Founder sells.** Until the founder has personally closed roughly 5-10 deals and can articulate the pattern.
2. **Founder hires 2 reps.** Senior, generalist, hands-on. Tests whether the pattern is repeatable by anyone other than the founder.
3. **If both reps hit quota** → PMF is repeatable enough to hire a Head of Sales.
4. **If only one hits quota** → not yet repeatable. Founder goes back to selling and refining the playbook.
5. **If neither hits** → product or positioning problem, not a sales problem.

**The hard implication:** the request "we need a VP of Sales to fix our sales problem" is almost always wrong before PMF. The problem isn't a missing VP — it's a missing playbook. A VP can't build a playbook from zero; they can only scale an existing one.

## Framework 4 — The 5 Quotas Test (Roberge's Stage 2 Hiring Question)

Roberge's question for evaluating whether a candidate is the right Head of Sales hire for an early-stage company. From Stage 2 Capital workshop material and his Lenny's Newsletter posts.

**The question:** "How many sales reps have you hired who hit quota in their first year?"

**Why it works:**
- Forces the candidate to put a number on actual hiring outcomes, not "experience" abstractions.
- Anchors on the metric that matters — first-year quota attainment of reps the candidate personally hired.
- Surfaces the difference between candidates who managed inherited teams (no signal) and candidates who built teams (real signal).

**Roberge's calibration:**
- A senior sales leader with a strong record should be able to name 10-20+ reps they personally hired who hit first-year quota.
- A candidate who can't name 5 is probably not ready for an early-stage head-of-sales seat.
- A candidate who names 5+ but can't articulate *what scorecard they hired against* is using survivorship bias, not method.

## Framework 5 — Stage 2 Capital's "GTM Diagnostic" Lens

Stage 2 Capital (Roberge's fund) publishes a recurring lens for diagnosing where a B2B SaaS company is breaking. The lens layers five dimensions:

1. **PMF strength** — repeatable buyer + repeatable problem + repeatable pitch.
2. **Sales playbook clarity** — is the playbook documented and can a new rep ramp on it predictably?
3. **Pipeline coverage** — quota × 3-4 in qualified pipeline. If coverage is thin, the problem is upstream of sales.
4. **Win-rate stability** — is the win rate consistent across reps or is one rep carrying the team? Inconsistent win rates signal an undocumented playbook.
5. **CAC payback** — months to recover acquisition cost. If payback is 24+ months and the team is pushing for more pipeline, the problem is unit economics, not pipeline.

**Operator use:** the diagnostic runs top-down. Don't try to fix pipeline coverage (#3) when PMF (#1) is shaky. Don't try to fix CAC payback (#5) when the playbook (#2) isn't documented yet.

## Framework 6 — Compensation as Strategy Signal (Beyond the HBR Article)

Roberge's extension of his HBR article ("The Right Way to Use Compensation," March 2015) in his HBS lectures and Stage 2 talks.

**The reframe:** compensation is not an HR mechanic — it is the **single clearest signal** of what leadership actually rewards, regardless of what the leadership team says it values.

**Two diagnostic moves:**
- **Reverse-engineer the comp plan.** Look at the rep's actual paycheck pattern over 12 months. The biggest line items in the rep's comp tell you what the company is actually optimizing for. If "logos closed" is 80% of pay and "retention at month 12" is 5%, the company is an acquisition machine that hates its existing customers — regardless of what the website says.
- **Audit the gap.** Compare the leadership team's stated strategy (deck, all-hands speech, ICP doc) to the comp plan. The gap is where the strategy fails. Reps will obey the comp plan, not the all-hands speech.

**Public reference:** see HBR archive — "The Right Way to Use Compensation," Roberge (March 2015) — https://hbr.org/2015/04/the-right-way-to-use-compensation.

## Framework 7 — The Coachability Predictor (Updated)

Roberge's continued public reinforcement (in podcast appearances and HBS lectures) of his most-quoted finding: **coachability outpredicts prior sales experience as a hiring signal.**

**How he updates the frame post-2015:**
- The coachability test should happen *inside the interview process itself*, not asked about as a self-report.
- Give the candidate feedback in interview round 2. Watch whether they apply it in round 3.
- A candidate who applies the feedback verbatim is coachable. A candidate who deflects, rationalizes, or ignores it is not.
- This single signal predicts top-quartile performance at month 12 better than years-of-experience, degree, or previous employer brand.

**Operator move:** every interview loop should be designed with a **coachability test step** — a real piece of feedback delivered in one round, observed for application in the next. Without that, the interview doesn't actually measure the highest-correlated trait.

## Framework 8 — Lenny's Newsletter Posts: Founder-Led Sales

Roberge's guest contributions to Lenny's Newsletter formalize the founder-led-sales-to-scaled-sales transition. Core points:

- The founder is the first salesperson, by necessity, until PMF.
- Founder-led sales is a learning function (about the buyer, the message, the pricing), not a revenue function.
- The handoff from founder-led to rep-led requires three artifacts: a written playbook, a hired-and-ramped first rep cohort, and a leading-indicator scorecard.
- Skipping any of the three artifacts means the rep team will not be able to execute, regardless of how senior they are.

**The "founder still in every deal" anti-pattern:** if the founder is still required on every deal three rounds after the first rep was hired, the playbook hasn't been transferred yet. The fix is to write the playbook, not to hire another rep.

**Public reference:** Lenny's Newsletter — Roberge contributions (search "Mark Roberge" in the Lenny's archive). Specific public Lenny posts referenced in Roberge's HBS material include the PMF-before-sales-VP frame and the rep ramp-curve diagnostic.

## How to use this file inside the /sales skill

1. When the problem is **early-stage GTM** (pre-Series A, no playbook, founder still selling) — pull Framework 3 (PMF Before Sales Hiring) and Framework 8 (Founder-Led Sales) before anything else.
2. When the problem is **hiring a Head of Sales or VP of Sales** — pull Framework 4 (5 Quotas Test) and Framework 7 (Coachability Predictor) for the interview design.
3. When the problem is **archetype mismatch** (we hired a great rep and they're failing) — pull Framework 2 (Sales Hiring Math / Rep Archetypes) and diagnose whether the rep type matches the motion.
4. When the problem is **diagnostic** ("what's broken in our GTM?") — pull Framework 5 (Stage 2 Capital GTM Diagnostic) and run the five-layer check top-down.
5. When the problem is **comp plan audit** — pull Framework 6 and reverse-engineer what the current comp plan actually rewards versus what leadership says it wants.
6. For the **core hiring scorecard, training, leading indicators, channel allocation** — go to the original Sales Acceleration Formula file. This file is the *delta*, not the spine.
