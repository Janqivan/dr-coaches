## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 Valence × intensity map (four zones). GEN
- **What:** from James A. Russell (1980), applied to ads; she teaches it as a course. Valence = "an emotional thumbs up or thumbs down" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (positive: joy, pride, relief; negative: fear, shame, frustration). Intensity = how strongly felt (high: rage, euphoria; low: contentment, boredom). Intensity on the horizontal axis, valence vertical.
- **Zones:** 1 = positive/low (cozy, calm, supportive, reassuring). 2 = positive/high (joy, pride, thrill, confidence; "hurry, hype"). 3 = negative/low (annoyed, frustrated, fatigued, irritated). 4 = negative/high (panic, sadness, loss, anxiety) — where marketers love to live and where it's expensive.
- **How she uses it:** place the emotion to choose headlines, hooks, stories. Test across zones, e.g. intense language with calm delivery, or "kind of a more boring language" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] with intense delivery. Same message at two intensities: "Barry, your beard is fantastic" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (boring) vs "god level beard" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] / "since literal Zeus era" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU].
- **Rules:** words, music and delivery must sit in the same zone (mismatch causes "this like I don't believe you kind of reaction" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]). Don't jump from zone 1/2 hype into fear-urgency (her pet peeve). "There is no wrong" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A] in zone reads: the same ad is zone 3 for most, zone 4 for some. Beginners work one zone at a time, then combine. Getting copy into the right zone puts it in the customer's voice automatically. You can read a team's conversion beliefs from how intense they go.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06) · [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) · [YT:HuI3o3TBI4A] (2026-03)

### 2.2 The three selves (self-discrepancy theory). GEN
- **Actual** = who I am right now, today only ("I wish I could lose 30 pounds, but I also love Snickers bars" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). **Ideal** = future, who I want to be ("Be the runner you were always meant to be" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); all marketers talk to it but it's rarely switched on. **Ought** = past-focused guilt, "should" ("I should be a better marketer" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); often very sensitive.
- Selves switch with situation, company, environment. "great ads don't just sell the products. They actually validate which self is switched on." [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] Self is "where I personally believe iteration lives" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A].
- Every industry activates a different self: Nike = ideal; dentistry has no ideal self, so it talks to ought ("you should care about your teeth" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). She held that line when Hott (guest) argued dental scare messaging is future self.
- Sun-oil example, one ad per self: actual = the tan "that like is you" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]; ideal = "the tan that you wish you had" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]; ought = the one "everyone expects you to use" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU].
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) · [YT:HuI3o3TBI4A] (2026-03)

### 2.3 The two pre-production questions + five heuristics. GEN
- Before any production: (1) which emotion, how intense? (2) which self is the customer protecting or proving? Then layer her five heuristics.
- Advertisers' usual five: social proof, authority, urgency, scarcity, loss/gain. **Her five** (used without people knowing): fluency, distinction (von Restorff), loss aversion done properly, temporal landmarks, identity.
  - Fluency: compare hard ideas to something known ("apples to oranges" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); characters (Allstate Mayhem, Geico, Flo) are a fluency mechanism. Strange-but-clear beats "gorgeous but average" [quote src: YT:_iMPT2SsjeU].
  - Loss aversion: put the loss in the message, "protection"; the word "stuck" communicates loss.
  - Temporal landmarks: fresh starts (Monday, New Year, quarter, "before the holidays" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); bring the future closer — "Weeks or months is probably as far as I'd go." [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]
  - Identity: we buy what reflects who we are; the ad's job is to remind people an identity exists and to act now.
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) · [YT:_iMPT2SsjeU] (2026-07)

### 2.4 Lego blocks (angle on top, three changing blocks below). GEN
- The angle/hypothesis sits on top and never changes; it is built from a "when" (Norse: "my 13-year-old has acne" and feels bad looking in the mirror). Underneath only three blocks change: valence, intensity, self. "This is just how we're communicating the message." Keeps authority/curiosity angles from exploding into hundreds of combinations. ### 2.5 Social Judgment Theory headline ladder. GEN
- **Steps:** (1) Write the customer's anchor belief right before purchase ("what do they believe the most that might block this behavior?" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]). (2) Diagnose each headline: indifferent ("that's true," move on), acceptance ("I didn't think about that before" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]) or rejection ("I don't believe anything you're saying" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]) — this replaces the unanswerable "is this headline good?" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]. (3) Rewrite at intensities 1–5 and ask "does this ad move them just one step closer" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]. (4) Pick the middle of the range, test one at a time, push harder only if needed. (5) Run several rewrites at once if each differs "just differently enough" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo].
- **Rejection causes:** a ridiculous claim ("drive bananas" to work) or one outside their experience (sleep training for the childless). Most readers are beginner level.
- **Placing the belief:** find only what the belief is, not where it came from or how many hold it; she agrees it connects to market sophistication.
- **Purchase target:** someone "educated but not ready to buy" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]; a #1 bestseller scar serum headline only confirms she needs a serum.
- Origin: a Motion-event attendee asked which model gets people to persuade themselves; found the name with Claude. Examples in section 4. [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04)

### 2.6 TEAP — four mental stages. GEN
- **Trigger:** need becomes conscious. Four trigger types: life event (moved, dog, new job); social comparison; internal frustration hitting a tipping point; external disruption. "Trigger creative is not about your product, it's about the moment they're experiencing." [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]
- **Exploration:** know the need, "not looking at products yet" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]; exploring the category, doing "identity work", forming what's normal. Speak to the category. Exploration is where we tell them about the industry. A dermatologist talking about the category still counts as exploration.
- **Evaluation:** short list, seeking "permission to choose" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y], "a logical reason to make an emotional decision" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y], scanning for risk. Social proof works here. "Evaluation is where they tell them about us." [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] Fastest CAC lever: evaluation buyers "know all they need to know" [quote src: YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY], so "I can tip them over the fence pretty quick" [quote src: YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY]; cheaper evaluation CAC funds losing money up top.
- **Purchase:** "the psychological decision has already been made" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]; remove friction, reinforce, bust objections against inertia ("If I don't have to move, I won't" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]).
- **Apply in 3 steps:** (1) audit every running ad by "What mental state am I speaking to?" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y], not cold/warm; (2) find gaps — most brands over-index on evaluation/purchase, absent in trigger/exploration ("not a targeting problem, it's a messaging problem" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]); (3) re-brief by state "ridiculously specifically".
- Runs all four all year, each with its own CAC. For 2026 keep the structure but "All of your te ads now need to be wrapped around story" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]; untested whether that means a movie per stage. Worked for her 18 months as of 2025-12.

### 2.7 The 12-question creative strategy system (4 phases × 3 questions). GEN
- She runs it for clients weekly; nailing the four phases gets ~80% of what you need. It's a 12-step program because humans skip phases when things get tight.
- **Phase 1 Research = WHEN / WHY / WHO.** WHEN: the micro-when and "moments of newness" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]; understanding the when "almost better than anything else" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] yields hooks. WHY: the emotion under the problem sets the tone; get it wrong and ads "feel off". WHO: identity layer only — what every avatar has in common, not differences.
- **Phase 2 Ideation = WHICH / WHAT / WHERE.** WHICH problem is most relevant and underserved "right now in this particular period of history" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] ("direct the movie, not build the app" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]). WHAT format delivers the message best, never because it trends. WHERE in TEAP.
- **Phase 3 Concept development** (questions not detailed in this input).
- **Phase 4 Production = WHO / HOW / WHICH.** WHO produces falls out of format + platform. HOW long, and does it fit the test's urgency — brands "bleed momentum"; fix parameters (UGC and b-roll back "in exactly 4 days" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]). If you can't ship, fix the ad factory, not the strategy.
- **Cadence:** one "creative strategy source of truth" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] doc; nothing goes to production until every cell is filled; update ~monthly. Stuck? List the questions you can't answer and spend 1–2 hours on them this week.
- Sources: [YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06)

### 2.8 Three realities research system + customer-reality cake. GEN
- **Customer reality** (how they experience the problem), **brand reality** (what the business needs now; under-researched), **presentation reality** (which format the brain responds to, not trends).
- **Cake, bottom→top:** generational insights → micro moments → emotional mapping.
- **Four generational questions:** childhood information environment (three authoritative TV channels vs internet where "everything is negotiable" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]); relationship with money ('90s boom vs 2008 grads with loans); actual life now (career pivot, sandwiched, newly retired); how they judge risk and whom they trust.
- **Emotional mapping per micro moment:** strongest emotion? private or social? acute or chronic?
- **Brand reality:** Health (account position, capacity, cost, time, who) + Alignment (this month's priority, what we're not aiming for, how we'll know).
- **Cadence:** customer "big dump" first Monday monthly → updated profile per avatar; brand research first Monday quarterly and monthly → brand optimization statement; presentation research every Monday 30–60 min → TEAP inspiration library. "Having a system is not the same as using it." [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]
- **Small business:** customer then presentation research; skip the generational layer ("this is going to break my heart" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]), keep micro moments + emotions.
- **Rule:** before opening any tool ask "What decision will this research help me make?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; if none, close the browser.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06)

### 2.9 Behavior-based avatar (4 steps + working card). GEN
- (1) Stop building the person, find the when (category entry points: when noticeable, when researching, when annoying enough to act). (2) Feed real evidence to AI, not opinions. (3) QA the AI with the three-categories rule (rule not detailed in input). (4) Behavioral working card: trigger and category entry point; current behavior vs desired state; emotional need and gaps; supporting evidence; what you're testing now.
- Readiness checklist: no fictional person? behavior and triggers over demographics? every claim traceable to evidence? does the team know what ad to write next? An avatar is a hypothesis you keep updating.

### 2.11 Subtext and hidden audience. GEN
- Subtext = "how humans communicate between each other in the unspoken space" [quote src: YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY]. ### 2.12 Identity matching (2025). GEN
- Platforms serve content mirroring identities; ads that "mirror" the viewer get rewarded.
