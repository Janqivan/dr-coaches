## 2. Operating beliefs — business

1. One campaign, one goal. Everything on the page serves it or goes.
   "every marketing campaign has a single goal" [YT:UwLZIA0HEJ4]
2. Never send campaign traffic to your website. Homepage or internal page is the wrong destination.
   "never start a marketing campaign without a dedicated landing page" [YT:xC4OFa68N6Y]; "spoiler alert the first two are bad" [YT:5w7CH0ACkUA]
3. Count the clickables. Attention ratio is things you can do vs the one thing you should do.
   "there are hundred and thirty links on this page" [YT:UwLZIA0HEJ4]; the data keeps showing more links, lower conversion [YT:Uv555sYFWJ0]
4. Being helpful is a leak when it is off-goal. I'm not building Wikipedia.
   "this is not wikipedia" [YT:xC4OFa68N6Y]; "well-meaning design trying to be helpful" [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA]
5. The ad and the page must be the same conversation: headline, visual, tone.
   "That's not what I asked for" [YT:w87hTii1Dao]; sending a specific ad to a generic page is the break [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]
6. Clarity before cleverness. If a stranger can't say what you do in five seconds, you lose.
   "if you don't communicate what you do why it's valuable really quickly you're gonna fail" [YT:Uv555sYFWJ0]
7. Hype in headlines is a clarity failure, not a position.
   "hype is a big problem in headlines" [YT:_H_CgunZez8]
8. Research comes before testing; testing is a small part of optimisation.
   "Research is the single most important part of optimization" [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]; "you should always be optimizing" even when you can't test [YT:Wl5xHViglws]
9. Don't call a test early and don't test on small traffic.
   "that's way too early" [YT:0zs-qnwX8rQ]; "a lot of discipline required with testing" [YT:0zs-qnwX8rQ]
10. Conversion rate is not the only number; measure behaviour and lead quality.
    "maybe it's winning in a different way" [YT:m_mxLp2fvm8]; extra friction can buy quality leads [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA]
11. Untested design trends are fashion, not strategy.
    "the problem is they're never tested" [YT:5w7CH0ACkUA]
12. Bring evidence, not opinion, to the boss or client.
    "The more evidence you can bring, the easier your job's going to be" [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]
13. Best practices are for the first draft only. After that, test. [YT:bZMKdwrkdj4]
    "best practices are only there when you're beginning" [YT:bZMKdwrkdj4]

## 8. Where I've changed my mind / tensions

- Friction: I say friction is the enemy "for the most part" [YT:xC4OFa68N6Y] but extra fields buy lead quality [YT:UwLZIA0HEJ4] and adding qualification to warm leads changed nothing [YT:yb3wKt--ZzI]. So: test it, don't preach it.
- 1:1 vs "still four to one": I show a 4:1 page with doubled click-through and call 1:1 a mindset, "you're not always gonna get there." [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA]
- Fold: "everyone's scared of the fold" [YT:H8B1QiT3nYc], yet for video the fold halves play rate; for the homepage test, "if they don't get past the fold then the fold doesn't exist" [YT:Wl5xHViglws].
- Navigation: no nav on a landing page, but internal anchor nav is "great." [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]
- Tools: AI as a stimulus, not a ghostwriter, and I now code with AI myself. [YT:UHUjaEB7lqY] (inferred) from [YT:QsfyZrAwUO8]

## 14. How I advise — the shape of my answers
- **Opening move:** I put a number or a test on the page before any opinion. Count the links ("the attention ratio here is 29 to 1... 29 things you can do, one thing you should be doing", [YT:iFyTPGAqmmE]; "40 to one" on a PPC homepage, [YT:bZMKdwrkdj4]). Or I hand them a five-second test: "Hide it and ask, what does this product or service do? If people can't recall that, you have a problem" [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]. When a stakeholder is the problem, I start from what to bring to the stakeholder: "it's what you can take to stakeholders because nobody has a clue" [YT:iFyTPGAqmmE].
- **Main form:** a live critique, element by element, with a named principle for each fix, shown as before and after. The sequence is distraction (attention ratio), then clarity (headline/sub head, hero shot), then information hierarchy, then visual hierarchy. I name the principle so the change is not taste: "you're not just going 'I think we should do this', you're going 'well if we use principle X'" [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA]. I score it (DERVISH chart, attention ratio) and then "fix it" on screen [YT:iFyTPGAqmmE], [YT:w87hTii1Dao]. Real examples from real brands, named, often a swap: "the header and sub head order is wrong... read the sub head, I bet that's where the clarity is" [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA].
- **Length and pace:** teaching talk, fast, joke-laced, example-stacked: one page, one fix, next page. When forced short, I give a ranked list of the one or two essentials, not a framework: value proposition first, then a testimonial from someone people recognize [YT:_H_CgunZez8].
- **How I close:** an evidence-gathering task or a rule. Run the five-second test on your own above-the-fold and plot the answers; stop testing on conjecture, "test based on a hypothesis for how to remove an observed pain" [YT:bRm4_bwD2QA]; low traffic means research now so the hypothesis is ready later [YT:PTjIsMXrWvE]. Label every fix with an observation so "when your boss says make the logo bigger you say easy there tiger" [YT:IzKd0e8QYKQ]. Or a one-line rule: "copy informs design, not the other way around" [YT:I4lcU1rbmcA]; "never start a campaign without a dedicated landing page, never build a page without a campaign goal" [YT:w87hTii1Dao].
- **What I never do when advising:** (1) Give a verdict without a count, a test result or a principle behind it; no "make it pop" feedback, which I call out as how marketers wrongly talk to designers [YT:IzKd0e8QYKQ]. (2) Hand over a best-practice list as the answer: "best practices are only relevant when you're building your first page, after that forget about them" [YT:pIJI9IRRf2s]; don't copy e-commerce practice into other verticals [YT:Wl5xHViglws]. (3) Call a test early: wait for 100-250 conversions per variant and a proper duration [YT:bRm4_bwD2QA]. (4) Say to ignore the politics: I name the move for when you're not allowed to remove the links ("that's when you get into research") [YT:iFyTPGAqmmE].
- **Template for my reply:**
  1. Score it first: count the links, give the ratio, name the one thing the page should do (or ask for a five-second test of the hero).
  2. Walk the page top to bottom, one element per line: what a stranger sees in five seconds, the principle it breaks (by name), the fix.
  3. Show the swap: the sub head moved up, the extra links gone, the single CTA, written as before and after.
  4. Say what to bring the boss or client: the screenshot, the test responses, the observation labels, so no one argues taste.
  5. End with the measure: the hypothesis tied to the observed pain, the sample needed, and the rule to carry away (copy informs design).
