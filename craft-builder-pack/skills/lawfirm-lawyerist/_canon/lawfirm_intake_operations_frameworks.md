# Law Firm Intake, Pricing, Hiring, and Operations — Lawyerist Frameworks

The "modern lawyer business operating system" as Lawyerist teaches it across
the Lawyerist Lab cohort, the Small Firm Operating System, the Healthy Firm
content series, and 600+ podcast episodes.

This canon covers four operational pillars that show up in nearly every
diagnostic: intake, pricing, hiring/team, and operations/systems.

---

## 1. INTAKE AS MARKETING

The single most under-respected truth in small law firm operations.

**The premise:** marketing spend that arrives at a broken intake is wasted.
A 3x improvement in intake conversion will outperform a 3x increase in ad
spend almost every time — and it costs less to fix.

The intake process IS the marketing. The lead's decision to hire you happens
across the intake experience, not on the ad they clicked. By the time they
land on your phone or your form, they have already decided you might be the
one. The intake's job is to not lose them.

### 1a. The Intake Funnel (the diagnostic frame)

Walk this funnel every time:

```
Inbound lead (call / form / chat / referral)
    ↓
First human response (target: minutes, not hours)
    ↓
Qualified vs. not qualified (do they have a matter you handle?)
    ↓
Consultation booked
    ↓
Consultation attended (no-show rate matters)
    ↓
Engagement letter sent
    ↓
Engagement letter signed
    ↓
Retainer / fee collected
    ↓
Matter opened, work begins
    ↓
Closed matter
    ↓
Review + referral asked for
```

Every stage is a leak. The strategy is: find the biggest leak, fix it, watch
the metric for 30 days, find the next biggest leak.

### 1b. Speed-to-Lead — the single biggest lever

Industry guidance for law firms in 2025–2026:
- Paid leads (Meta, Google PPC): respond within 2 minutes
- Organic web form leads: respond within 5–15 minutes during business hours
- Worst-case: same business hour
- Conversion probability drops sharply with every hour of delay
- After 24 hours, you are unlikely to win the business

Most small firms respond in 4–48 hours. This is the gap.

**Prescription:**
- Define a written response-time target by lead source
- Assign explicit accountability: who responds, in what time, on what channel
- Use SMS + email + phone for the first touch
- Build a 5-to-7 touch follow-up sequence over 14 days for non-responders
- Track average days-to-respond as a weekly KPI

### 1c. The Written Intake Script

Most small firms have no written script. The intake call quality depends on
who happens to answer. This is the second-biggest fixable leak.

Components of a healthy intake script:
- Warm opener (acknowledge what they're going through — being a legal client
  is unnerving)
- Quick qualification (matter type, jurisdiction, urgency)
- Brief firm intro (what we do, what we don't do — proves the niche)
- Clear next step (book the consultation, send the intake form, gather
  documents)
- Confirmed appointment with explicit time, attendee, and what they need
  to bring
- Confirmation email and SMS sent before the call ends

### 1d. The Consultation

The consultation is not a sales pitch. It is a structured conversation that
ends with either: (a) the prospect understanding their situation and how
you'd help, plus a clear engagement letter offered; or (b) a clear no — they
don't have a matter, you're not the right firm, here's who is.

The healthy consultation:
- Has a defined length (30 / 45 / 60 minutes — pick one)
- Has a written agenda
- Ends with a recommendation (engage / don't engage / refer)
- Sends the engagement letter within 24 hours, ideally same day
- Has a follow-up cadence for non-signers (3 touches over 7 days)

### 1e. Engagement Letter → Signed → Paid

This is where the longest leak typically lives. The prospect said yes on
the call. The engagement letter goes out. Then nothing happens for two
weeks. By the time you follow up, they've signed with someone else.

**Prescription:**
- Send the engagement letter same day
- Use e-signature (DocuSign, AdobeSign, the practice management software's
  built-in tool)
- Take the retainer at the same time, in the same flow
- Follow up at 24 hours, 72 hours, 7 days if unsigned
- Have a written cadence and a person responsible

### 1f. The Conversion Metric

Track these monthly at a minimum:
- Inbound leads (by source)
- Consultations booked / inbound (booking rate)
- Consultations attended / booked (show rate)
- Engagement letters signed / consultations attended (close rate)
- Retainers collected / engagement letters signed (collection rate)
- Average days from inbound to retainer collected
- Cost per signed matter (not cost per lead — cost per signed matter)

The "marketing problem" is almost always a stage in this funnel.

## 2. PRICING — Moving Off the Billable Hour

Lawyerist's position is unambiguous: the billable hour is the default form,
not the right form for most matters. Alternative pricing wins on speed of
payment, client experience, and firm margin when the scope is predictable.

### 2a. Why the Billable Hour Loses

- Clients hate the uncertainty (industry surveys consistently show 70%+ of
  clients prefer flat fee for entire-matter pricing)
- Hourly bills are negotiated after the fact (every bill is a fight)
- Hourly creates the wrong incentives (the firm earns more by being slower)
- Hourly punishes efficiency (templates, automation, expertise all reduce
  billable hours and therefore revenue)
- Hourly billing collects slower (industry data: flat-fee firms collect
  almost twice as fast as hourly billers)
- Hourly matters close 2.6x slower than flat-fee matters on average

### 2b. The Pricing Ladder

Design pricing as a ladder of options, not a single number.

For most matter types, you can build:
- **Essential / Standard tier** — predictable scope, flat fee
- **Plus / Premium tier** — adds white-glove service, faster response,
  more inclusions
- **Custom / Hourly tier** — for matters that genuinely require hourly
  (litigation with unpredictable scope, novel issues)

The client picks the tier. The conversation moves from "what's your
hourly rate" to "which level of service do you want." This is a
fundamentally different sales dynamic.

### 2c. Subscription / Recurring Revenue

For matter types where the client has ongoing needs (small-business
general counsel, estate planning maintenance, ongoing compliance):
- Monthly subscription
- Annual retainer
- Membership / access model

Lawyerist has covered this extensively on the podcast — multiple
episodes on subscription law firms, recurring revenue models, and
the math behind monthly recurring revenue (MRR) for small firms.

### 2d. Value-Based Pricing

The most mature form. Price is set by value delivered, not hours spent.
This requires:
- A discovery conversation that identifies value
- Confidence to charge what the outcome is worth
- The ability to walk away from matters where the math doesn't work

Most Stage 2-3 firms are not ready for full value-based pricing. They
should start with flat fees on predictable matters, then evolve.

### 2e. Owner Pay First

Lawyerist's finance position (consistent with Crabtree/Michalowicz):
- Pay the owner a real market-rate salary
- Pay it first, on a schedule, like an employee
- Profit is what's left after market-rate owner pay, not the owner pay
- "I'll pay myself when there's money" is the path to burnout

## 3. HIRING + TEAM

Lawyerist's hiring frame is closer to ghSMART's Topgrading / Smart's "Who"
methodology than to traditional law-firm hiring. The defaults differ
substantially.

### 3a. Hire on Purpose, Not in Panic

The dominant small-firm pattern: owner drowns → owner hires the first
warm body → hire doesn't work out → owner hires another body → firm
ends up with a team that doesn't fit.

The healthy pattern:
- Define the role and outcomes BEFORE posting the job (scorecard)
- Hire when you have 90+ days of forecasted need, not when you're
  already drowning
- Hire for core values fit first, skills second
- Use structured interviews, not "let's just chat"
- Reference-check seriously

### 3b. Core Values — The Hiring Filter

Lawyerist's own core values include "Grab the Marker" (be confident,
bold, brave, not perfect, ready to step up) and "Experiment Like a
Lobster" (try new things, shed old shells). The exact values matter
less than the discipline of having them, writing them down, and using
them in hiring and firing.

Misalignment of core values is one of the biggest reasons hires fail.
Skill mismatches are recoverable. Values mismatches almost never are.

### 3c. The Accountability Chart

Different from the org chart. The accountability chart says: for every
function in the firm, ONE person owns it.

For a Stage 2 firm:
- Owner: strategy, finance, primary rainmaker, senior legal work
- Office manager / paralegal: intake response, calendar, client
  communication, day-of-matter ops
- Bookkeeper (often outsourced): AR, trust accounting, payroll

Every function on the list has a name next to it. No function is owned
by "we" or "the team."

### 3d. The First Real Hires

Order of operations for most small firms:
1. **Admin / intake person** — first hire is almost always not another
   lawyer. The intake/admin lever frees the owner from $20/hour work.
2. **Paralegal** — second hire. Production capacity at lower cost than
   another attorney.
3. **Associate attorney** — third or fourth hire. Only after the
   matter pipeline is consistent enough to keep them billable.
4. **Operations lead / firm administrator** — Stage 2-3 transition.
   This is the person who lets the owner become CEO.

### 3e. Scorecards

Every role gets a scorecard with:
- Mission of the role (one sentence)
- Outcomes (3-5 measurable results the role is accountable for)
- Competencies (the behaviors that produce the outcomes)
- Core values alignment (yes/no, evaluated continuously)

Used in hiring, used in performance reviews, used in firing decisions.

## 4. SYSTEMS + OPERATIONS

The infrastructure that makes the firm run when the owner isn't watching.

### 4a. The Documented Process

The default small-firm state: every process lives in someone's head,
usually the owner's. The firm cannot scale past that.

The healthy state:
- Top 10 processes documented (intake, consultation, engagement,
  retainer collection, matter open, billing, payment, closing, review
  request, referral request)
- Documents live in a shared knowledge base (Notion, Loom video walk-
  throughs, Google Docs, practice management software)
- Updated quarterly
- Used in onboarding new hires (target: new hires productive in 30
  days, not 6 months)

### 4b. The Tech Stack

Lawyerist is software-agnostic in principle, but the common stack is:
- Practice management: Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther, Smokeball,
  Rocket Matter
- Document automation: Documate, Lawyaw, native PM tool features
- Client communication: client portal (in PM tool), e-signature,
  scheduling (Calendly / native), SMS
- Bookkeeping: QuickBooks Online + Clio Accounting / TrustBooks
- CRM / intake: Lawmatics, Lead Docket, Captorra, or native PM tool
- AI: matter summarization, document review, intake screening

### 4c. The Meeting Cadence

Healthy firms have a written meeting cadence:
- **Daily** — 15-minute team huddle (optional but common in Stage 2-3)
- **Weekly** — 60-minute team meeting with structured agenda (KPIs,
  issues, to-dos)
- **Monthly** — finance review, leadership team review
- **Quarterly** — quarterly planning, set top 3-5 priorities, review
  scorecards
- **Annual** — annual offsite, vision review, next-year priorities

Lawyerist Lab structures member coaching on the quarterly cadence —
that's not accidental. Quarterly is the natural rhythm for small-firm
operations.

### 4d. KPIs and Dashboards

Track these at a minimum, weekly:
- New leads by source
- Consultations booked
- Engagement letters signed
- Revenue collected (this week, MTD, YTD vs. target)
- AR aging
- Trust account balances
- Matters opened, matters closed
- Owner hours by category (technician / manager / entrepreneur)

The dashboard is the management tool. The dashboard makes the issues
visible. Issues stay invisible without a dashboard, and the firm
manages by anecdote.

## 5. MARKETING-TO-INTAKE HANDOFF

This is where most agencies (including DR) get implicated.

The agency drives leads. The firm receives leads. Between the two is
a handoff. The handoff is almost always broken.

**Common breakages:**
- Lead form goes to an email no one monitors
- Lead form fields don't match what intake needs
- Lead source is not tracked through the funnel — firm can't tell
  which channel produces signed matters vs. tire-kickers
- Speed-to-lead target is not defined or enforced
- No closed-loop reporting from the firm back to the agency

**Healthy handoff:**
- Leads route to a system the intake team actually uses (PM tool, CRM)
- Speed-to-lead target written and tracked
- Lead source flows through every stage of the funnel
- Weekly closed-loop report: agency sees signed matters by source,
  not just leads
- Cost per signed matter is the metric, not cost per lead

This is the conversation an honest agency has with a small firm.
Lawyerist's framing supports it: marketing isn't separate from intake.
You can't sell more marketing to fix what is structurally an intake
problem — and the responsible move is to fix the intake first.

## 6. HOW THESE FRAMEWORKS COMPOSE

When the user shows up with an operational question, route to these
frameworks in this order:

1. **Intake question** → walk the intake funnel, find the leak
2. **Pricing question** → name the matter type, choose ladder vs.
   value-based vs. hourly, set owner pay first
3. **Hiring question** → scorecard before posting, core values fit,
   first-hire-order
4. **Systems question** → document top 10 processes, weekly meeting
   cadence, KPI dashboard
5. **Marketing problem that's actually an intake problem** → say so
   out loud and route to the intake funnel

Every prescription should be stage-appropriate (see the methodology
canon) and tied to one of the six focus areas.
