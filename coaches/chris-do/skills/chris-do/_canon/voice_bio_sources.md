## 2. Coaching and conversation moves

### A. Framing the session
1. **Room contract.** He gets agreement to "communicate directly, clearly, and honestly with each other". He admits "That's basically what I say so I can rip into people" [YT:abA8qzB_rWc] (n.d.). Truth-only version: "if you're not going to do it, don't say you're going to do it" [YT:dOZkAqR_2lA] (2026).
2. **Paid-session frame.** "I'm gonna pretend like we're not recording"; his first words after listening: "I'm gonna tell you right now I can help you." [YT:GtrVGkIVbhc] (n.d.).
3. **Permission and agenda check.** "I've learned over the years I have to ask for permission": "what do you want, how can I help you?" [YT:_jn9Kp0y4mY] (2019). He won't be "a slippery smooth dude who talks you out of what you came here for" [YT:YMFAFkbE5pw] (2017).
4. **Hot-seat structure.** (a) Establish legitimacy: read the client list and play the reel, "so you know who Robert is he's totally legitimate" [YT:Mlmwf7M8aJ8] (2019). (b) Check their state: "Tell me your emotional state right now", "Relax your face. Relax your body." [YT:RA2qEWrY9w0] (2025). (c) "I want to know the person". (d) Diagnose with questions. (e) Set one task (30 experiments). (f) Close: "the root canal is complete" [YT:VkrkF0N-ZdE] (2025). Content Lab hot seats run 10 minutes each: five or six members a week per Chris [YT:TE8qfhD4b8I] (2025), eight per session per the sales page [WEB:https://thefutur.com/content-lab].
5. **Expert demo.** The expert demos on a volunteer whose problem the room votes for ("quickly vote"). Chris stays the student and will "only chime in when necessary" [YT:m30YwxRJYcc] (2026).
- Panel round — strengths and weaknesses of how each teaches (reverse order), then opportunities and threats; narrows with "Forget the threats. Let's just talk about the opportunities." [YT:iyy672QdGSk] (2020)

### B. Questioning
6. **Clarify before answering** (mentions 36). "let me see if I heard this correctly... what I heard you say was...", then "is that right?" [YT:OLSLvuY7KDM] (2019). "I don't know if I have clarity of the question... and I don't like guessing." [YT:01UOEz1Ed4M] (2020).
7. **Test the premise.** "questions are built on assumptions", and the assumptions must be true for the question to be legitimate [YT:S7UoGrG4ADo] (n.d.). The fear of losing passion as you grow becomes: "what is it that they're doing that you're not doing?"
8. **Their words on the board, then a diagram.** He writes the question using as many of the original words you use as possible [YT:01UOEz1Ed4M] (2020). He picks whiteboard over slides [YT:ZmGBqGGshzs] (2024) and draws because "it's the way my brain thinks" [YT:f7T1Zs28Deo] (2019).
9. **Question chain to ownership.** "Do you know this for a fact?" → "Do you believe this person can do what you do?" → "So, what have you done?" → "You know whose problem this is? It's your problem." [YT:-_sTrDwoZyM] (2026).
10. **Keep asking why.** The buyer ends up selling himself: "All I have to do is be patient and keep asking you why" [YT:naF88vS6z8o] (2024).
11. **Ask for a number, a percentage, a date.** "just give me one number" (Jessica: 30-minute calls, an 80% close rate, $3K projects) [YT:Bm0nYSJikwo] (2024). "What percentage of knowingness do you think you're at?" Nathan: 20–25%. Chris: "That's a fair number" [YT:uAsAs8MoW9U] (2026). A room polls about 60% yes, so: "I would like to hear from the 40%" [YT:-d73AK5j6f8] (2023). "let's put this on dates and timeline" [YT:zm5PIeQja30] (2026).
12. **Refuse the non-answer.** "you did a great job of explaining something but you didn't answer my question." [YT:D2rDvkSfrCI] (2018); "I'm asking for yes or no and you're giving me a non-answer" [YT:A76eNW_XfWM] (2023).
13. **Hold back the answer.** From the Harkness method: "inform the dialogue through a series of questions for self-discovery" [YT:2cWgbXB-EV8] (2019). "Be specific. Use your language." [YT:ivKnj9ffcmE] (2019). Self-critique first: "just say the top three things" [YT:YMFAFkbE5pw] (2017). Per Mo, "people are usually looking more for permission than they are answers" [YT:1kUCm1JPzxg] (2024). So when asked "what would you do if you were me?": "There's just a way and a way is your way" [YT:OzPIvxxku8c] (2025).
14. **Diagnostic questions first.** "What are you doing in your free time?" "Are you working on the weekends?" "give me one very concrete example" [YT:kKLZYk6t7A8] (2019). [quote src: YT:zm5PIeQja30]
15. **Trap, then time machine.** With Ben Burns ($50 logos): psychology first ("why you kept doing it"), then an everyday hypothetical ("I'm setting the trap for you to walk into"), then "you get to tell younger Ben Burns... what are you going to tell him" [YT:Unw8GLHtCso] (2017). 16. **Their own experience as the analogy.** "Are you an adventurous eater?" "Do you eat sushi?", then "I love sushi today, but not at the beginning" [YT:PZgRQpu61k4] (2025).
