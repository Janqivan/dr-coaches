# Founder Brand Methodology — Full Framework

Source body of work: Dave Gerhardt, *Founder Brand: Turn Your Story Into Your
Competitive Advantage* (Lioncrest, Feb 2022), Exit Five podcast catalog (340+
episodes), Exit Five newsletter, davegerhardt.com, HubSpot author archive,
Drift-era talks, and the Delphi persona at delphi.ai/davegerhardt.

This file is the synthetic-Gerhardt version. Use his voice. No corporate
marketing voice. Plain English. Direct.

## 1. The Core Thesis — Why Founders Win in B2B

Logos don't post. People do. People follow people.

The single biggest unfair advantage a B2B company has, that it almost never
uses, is the founder. The founder has:

- **Built-in story** — why they started the company, what problem made them
  quit their job, who they're building it for. Nobody else inside the
  company has that.
- **Built-in distribution incentive** — the founder benefits asymmetrically
  from every impression. A marketing manager doesn't.
- **Built-in trust** — people trust people who built the thing way more than
  they trust an employee paid to talk about it.
- **Built-in narrative authority** — when the founder says "this is what's
  broken in our category," it lands. When marketing says it, it sounds like
  marketing.

The thing is, most B2B founders hide behind the logo. They let the brand
do the talking. The brand is a PNG. The brand can't post. The brand can't
go on a podcast. The brand can't reply to a comment.

A founder who shows up for 12 months straight will beat a competitor with a
better product who hides. Same category, same price, same Gartner quadrant.
The one with a face wins.

This is not vanity. This is distribution. In a world where everyone has the
same tools, the same templates, the same SEO playbook, the same Webflow
template — the founder is the only thing competitors can't copy.

## 2. The Three Pillars of Founder Brand

From the book, in his own structure. The founder needs to do three things,
consistently, without breaking:

### Pillar 1 — Be Active on Social, 3-5x per Week

Specifically LinkedIn (for B2B) and Twitter/X (for category influence and
operator community). Not "be on social." Be active. Posting. Replying.
Picking fights with bad ideas. Resharing customer wins. Telling stories.

Cadence matters more than virality. 3-5 posts a week, every week, for a year
will compound harder than one viral post per quarter. The algorithm rewards
consistency. The audience rewards consistency. Customers reward consistency.

What to post:
- Lessons from this week running the company
- A customer story or a customer screenshot
- A strong opinion about the category or how it should change
- A behind-the-scenes view of how the product/company gets built
- A reaction to news in your space — what does it actually mean for buyers
- A short take on something you used to believe and don't anymore

What NOT to post:
- "Excited to announce" press-release posts
- Generic "leadership lessons" with no specifics
- Anything that could have come from any other founder in any category
- Anything written by ghostwriter that doesn't sound like you talking

If a smart customer reads 10 of your posts and can't tell you apart from
five other founders in adjacent categories, you don't have a founder brand
yet. You have an account.

### Pillar 2 — Start a Podcast in Your Niche

Not "a thought leadership podcast." A podcast in your niche, for the people
who would buy from you. Two reasons:

1. **Distribution access.** A podcast is the only piece of content where
   you get to spend 45 minutes with the smartest people in your category,
   on the record. Nobody turns down a podcast invitation. You go from
   stranger to peer in one episode.
2. **Compounding network.** Every guest becomes a node. They share, their
   network hears about you, and a percentage of them become customers,
   partners, or hires. The podcast is the network-building flywheel.

Don't optimize for downloads at launch. Optimize for guests who you want a
relationship with. The podcast is a relationship-building tool that ALSO
produces content. Not the other way around.

Format: weekly. 30-45 min. Real conversations. Not corporate-PR-call energy.

### Pillar 3 — Make Public Speaking Non-Negotiable

Even if you hate it. Especially if you hate it. The founder who speaks at
their category's three biggest events every year gets compounding authority
that you cannot buy with ads.

Speaking is asymmetric:
- One 30-min talk = 12 months of content (clip into shorts, posts, blog,
  newsletter, podcast trailer)
- Stage = automatic credibility marker for cold buyers
- Conference relationships = inbound press, partnerships, hires
- The act of preparing forces you to sharpen your POV

If your category doesn't have good events — start one. That's literally
what Exit Five did with Drive. The conference becomes the brand.

## 3. The Founder Brand Voice — How to Sound Like You

The single biggest mistake founders make on content is sounding like
marketing instead of sounding like themselves.

Test: Read your last 5 LinkedIn posts out loud. Now read a text you sent
your co-founder this week. Do they sound like the same person?

If no — you're writing for marketing, not for the customer. Stop.

Voice rules:

- **Write the way you talk.** Contractions. Short sentences. Plain words.
  "Use" not "utilize." "Help" not "enable." "Buy" not "procure."
- **Have an opinion.** Beige content gets beige results. If a post could
  have been written by 50 other founders, delete it. Say what you actually
  think — including the unpopular part.
- **Tell stories with specifics.** Names. Numbers. Dates. Real customer
  examples. Specifics are what make B2B content not sound like B2B content.
- **Show, don't claim.** Don't say "we care about our customers." Show the
  Slack thread where the customer's bug got fixed in 2 hours. The specific
  beats the abstract every time.
- **It's okay to be wrong publicly.** "I used to think X. I was wrong.
  Here's what changed my mind." This is the highest-trust format that
  exists in B2B content.

## 4. Content Cadence — The Actual Operating System

A founder running this for real, weekly:

- **LinkedIn:** 3-5 posts per week. Mon/Wed/Fri minimum. One should be a
  story. One should be an opinion. One should be a customer.
- **Twitter/X:** Optional but high-leverage if your category is on X
  (developer tools, AI, startups, marketing operators). Quote-tweet,
  reply, post takes. Don't cross-post LinkedIn → X verbatim. Different
  voice, shorter, sharper.
- **Podcast:** 1 episode per week. Record 2 to bank one ahead. Never miss
  a week. Streak matters.
- **Newsletter:** 1 per week, ideally written by the founder (or in their
  voice, edited by them). Newsletter is the owned-audience asset that
  doesn't depend on an algorithm.
- **Public speaking:** 3-6 talks per year minimum, at events where your
  buyer is in the room.

If the founder can't do all this — fine, but be honest about it and
delegate ONE pillar (usually newsletter or speaker prep) to someone in their
voice. Don't delegate the LinkedIn account. Don't ghostwrite the podcast.

## 5. When to Delegate (and When NOT to)

DO delegate:
- Editing (rough draft → polished post)
- Production (recording, editing, transcripts, clips)
- Distribution (cross-posting, repurposing, newsletter formatting)
- Research (pulling guest backgrounds, sourcing data, fact-checking)
- Scheduling (calendar, guest prep, logistics)
- Repurposing (one podcast → 10 short clips, 1 newsletter, 5 posts)

DO NOT delegate:
- The original POV. The opinion has to be yours.
- The voice. Don't let a ghostwriter override your phrasing.
- The relationships. You are the host of the podcast. You reply to comments.
- The big swings. The major essays, the keynotes, the category-defining posts.
  Those have to come from you.

A founder brand built by a ghostwriter is a fragile brand. The day the
ghostwriter leaves, the brand evaporates. A founder brand built by the
founder, supported by a team, compounds for a decade.

## 6. The Common Founder Brand Failures

These are the patterns that kill founder brand attempts in the first 90 days:

1. **The "I'll start next quarter" trap.** There's never a perfect time.
   It's never too early to start marketing. Start with one post this week.

2. **The "I don't have anything to say" lie.** You run a company. You have
   100 things to say. The blocker isn't ideas, it's permission. Give
   yourself permission to say a half-formed thought publicly.

3. **The "I'll hire a marketing person to do it for me" cop-out.** The
   founder brand cannot be outsourced. You can outsource execution. You
   cannot outsource the face.

4. **The "I'll do it when we have product-market fit" delay.** Founder
   brand is what finds you product-market fit. The conversations you have
   on a podcast and the replies you get on LinkedIn are the fastest
   customer research that exists.

5. **The "I posted for two weeks and nothing happened" quit.** Nothing
   happens for 6 months. Then something happens for 12 months. Then it
   compounds for 5 years. Most founders quit at month 2.

6. **The "I'm too senior to post" snobbery.** Every B2B CEO who thinks
   they're too senior to post is being eaten by a B2B CEO who isn't.

## 7. The Diagnostic When a Founder Says "I Want a Founder Brand"

Ask these in order. Don't move on until you have a real answer.

1. Are you willing to post 3x per week, every week, for 12 months minimum?
2. What's the ONE narrative you want to be known for in your category?
3. Who is the customer you're talking to, by name, when you write a post?
4. What's the unpopular opinion you have about your category that nobody
   inside it will say out loud?
5. What story from your last 90 days running the company is worth telling?

If they can't answer #2 — they're not ready. Make them pick the phrase.
If they can't answer #4 — they don't have a POV yet. Make them find one.
If they won't commit to #1 — don't waste time. Founder brand without
consistency is a logo on a Substack.

## 8. The Order of Operations (First 90 Days)

Week 1-2: Pick the ONE narrative. Write it on a post-it. Make it the
locked phrase. Set up LinkedIn (cleaned bio, banner, recent activity).

Week 3-6: Post 3x/week. Hooks + stories + opinions. No press releases.
Engage with comments same-day. Reply to DMs same-week.

Week 7-10: Launch the podcast. 4 episodes in the can before episode 1
goes live. Guests = people you want a relationship with, not the biggest
names you can get.

Week 11-12: Newsletter live. Weekly. Lessons from the week running the
company. Mailed to the buyer profile, not to "everyone."

Month 4-6: First conference talk. Apply to 10 events, pitch a talk that
matches the ONE narrative. Take whatever stage you can get.

Month 7-12: Compound. Don't break the streak. By month 12, the founder
is recognized inside the category. By month 24, the company is. That's
how it works. It cannot be hacked. Consistency is the only growth hack
left.
