## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 "What's spending": the two-sided empathy read. GEN
- **When:** every time he reviews an account, and before touching a high-spend, low-return ad.
- **Steps:** (1) Give the system lots of ads plus "the rules of the game" [quote src: YT:M-zOOiG5GLw], often a cost cap and sometimes a bid cap. (2) "the game really is a game of spend" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]: look at which ads are spending. (3) Empathize with the consumer: why do they pay attention, and why do they act? (4) Empathize with the system as if it were a human media buyer flipping switches and allocating budget: what data drove that decision? (5) Ask why it spends $40K on one ad and $20K on another. Is the audience warmer or colder, older or younger? What interests does it reach, and what problem does it solve or raise? (6) Make more ads like the big spender, or ads that complement it.
- **Kill test:** watch the ad and ask "would I want this out there spending more changing more minds" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]. If yes, leave it on. If it is not the message you want spending, turn it off. A frequent cause of a bad big spender is "it's not the right type of attention" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs].
- Sources: [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01) · [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04) · [YT:XIn0vQdErsM] (2023-08) "empathize with customers and also empathize with machines"

### 2.2 The breakdown effect, his two-part TL;DR. GEN
1. The ad someone clicks right before buying is often not the ad that brought them into the funnel.
2. Meta predicts where to spend at the level where you set budget (the ad set, or the campaign under CBO), not per ad. Say an ad set has a 5-ROAS ad on low spend and a 2-ROAS ad on high spend. Meta is predicting that more spend on the 5-ROAS ad would not drive more incremental sales. It could even put 90% of budget on an ad with a terrible CPA if that lets the other 10% "slam dunk" conversions.
- You can't see whether an ad works at the top, middle or bottom of the funnel, or on warm vs cold users. Meta can. He credits Dara Denney's video from about 4 years ago and plans a definitive 2026 version. On almost every consult call he spends about 10 minutes on it.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12)

### 2.3 His account structure (2025 version, consistent since 2023). DTC, principles GEN
1. **One testing campaign** (ABO or CBO, he doesn't care). Each new batch of concepts gets its own ad set with **one variable**: only the creative changes, with the same targeting and the same bid or cost cap. Alternatively, the same creative with custom placements as the variable. Use **4–6 ads per ad set**, broad targeting, **exclude all customers**, and exclude recent visitors and engagers (1/7/30/180 days depending on the product, kept consistent; probably not 10-day) so creative is learned from cold audiences.
2. **One Advantage+ Shopping (ASC) campaign** handles all retargeting and other prospecting. you don't need granular video-view or engager retargeting. Graduate proven winners into it.
3. Elsewhere he describes the main campaign as a prospecting–retargeting hybrid, really "a retargeting campaign that prospects a little bit" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs], plus ASC, plus a separate test-and-learn campaign.
- In 2023 and 2024 he leaned ABO in testing (the same budget per new ad set, roughly $200 per ad against a benchmark). He accepts that this performs slightly worse than CBO in exchange for an even, confident read. Launch your best-known ads first as the baseline "goal cost to beat" [quote src: YT:XIn0vQdErsM]. CBO is OK with a minimum spend set to force budget.
- Hybrid cap: a high cost cap of about 2x expected CPA (a $100 cap for a $50 CPA) only to prevent runaway tests. Caps that are too low keep the system stuck on warm, low-incrementality pockets.
- Ad counts: no more than about 6 ads in a normal ad set, but 50+ in ASC "with zero problems" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw]. "with ASC, the rules are different" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw]. Put as many ads in ASC as your conversion volume supports.
- Keep the structure consistent over time so changes in results can be read. the game is getting out of the way so you can read ad tests, landing page tests and site changes.
- Sources: [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) · [YT:nUVl_zpeipY] (2023-05) · [YT:XIn0vQdErsM] (2023-08) · [YT:R0k0rVzv_yQ] (2024-08) · [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04) · [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01)

### 2.4 The ASC / incrementality audit. DTC
1. At ad-account level, define **existing customers** and **engaged audience**.
2. Open the **audience segments breakdown** (new / engaged / existing), which is now in all sales campaigns, not only ASC. He calls it "the most valuable thing" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] Meta has offered for looking into the black box.
3. Use **compare attribution settings** side by side: 1-day view, 1-day click, 7-day click, 28-day click and incremental.
4. Red flags: heavy spend on already-engaged users converting on 1-day view means cheap, non-incremental impressions. More view-attributed than click-attributed sales on an audience "should not be possible" [quote src: YT:u4B0GGBBDXY].
5. Use the attribution-setting breakdown to check whether a "winning" ad's conversions were mostly 1-day view.
- Sources: [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04) · [YT:u4B0GGBBDXY] (2025-05) · [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01) · [YT:PSMit4vB8n8] (2022-12)

### 2.5 The attribution multiplier. DTC/GEN
- Optimize on 7-day click (or 1-day click), then build a multiplier on top by comparing days or weeks of in-platform CPA against real business results (e.g. reported $104K ≈ actual $150K). Matching Shopify in-platform is not the goal. If it does match, you're criminally under-leveraging, because shares and off-platform sends aren't tracked.
- Triangulate platform data, third-party tools (Triple Whale, Northbeam) and post-purchase surveys. There is no single source of truth. Never judge ads at ad level in third-party tools: an ad can drive few tracked sales but many views and shares that feed other ads.
- Sources: [YT:u4B0GGBBDXY] (2025-05) · [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04) · [YT:nUVl_zpeipY] (2023-05) · [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01)

### 2.6 "Think like a scientist" [quote src: YT:PSMit4vB8n8], not "be a scientist" [quote src: YT:PSMit4vB8n8]. GEN
- Ask questions, observe in detail, hypothesize, test, and question your answers. Ad data is too tainted to be scientific: on optimized platforms, A/B differences come from delivery, "it's quicksand". He dares people to run an **A/A test** of the same ad: if a "winner" appears, the tool is invalid. He tests in **concepts**, not single variables, and says he taught Denney this.
- Hypotheses must be sharp and actually testable: most people's are terrible. The problem is usually observation, e.g. crediting the headline at the top for a win.
- Sources: [YT:PSMit4vB8n8] (2022-12) · [YT:M2ObOuFQSv0] (2022-12) · [YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2024-12)

### 2.7 Iteration ladder for a winner. GEN
1. Before declaring a former winner dead, ask why it died: was it built on a trend that is no longer relevant? Check the comments (a top negative comment is "vandalism on your ad" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]) and check site changes.
2. **Step one:** recreate it with new ad copy and a new thumbnail, nothing major changed. This gets a fresh ad without the negative comments.
3. **Bigger iterations:** reshoot it rather than re-editing it. Change the audio and the first second, the words we hear and what we see, the background visuals, and add or remove music. Have another creator do the same script. Use a new voiceover or the CapCut voice changer. Have a creator green-screen over the ad (Denney's method). Use a different editing style or stock clips. Recast it with someone from a different subsection of the demographic.
4. **Creative diversity, "level three":** break the winner into hook, pain point and visuals, then redo it with the same person in a new location, or have creators rewrite it. Don't redo it word for word.
5. **Statics:** turn the static into a video, and break down its psychological components so you can recreate them in different ways.
6. If that fails, "almost always" find more relevant content your audience is consuming and remake the ad that way.
- Anti-pattern: "iterating to death" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] means only swapping 3-second hooks or headlines. The better question is how to make the winning angle relevant to bigger audiences.
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) · [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) · [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12)

### 2.8 Variant spectrum and the Post-it messaging test. GEN
- From the base idea, make one variant that "overdoes" it and goes way harder, and one that reels it back in. A winner then shows where on the spectrum it falls. — [YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] (2024-03)
- Post-its: handwrite, shoot, run many, find the winning messaging, then double-click into it in other styles. Diversifying is a gradient: the more dialed in you are on a story, a message or even just words, the more you diversify. He starts with Post-its to nail the "words that matter". — [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) · [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01)

### 2.9 Diagnosing a performance swing (knowing when not to act). GEN
1. First question: what changed in the account or on the site in that period? Sudden changes rarely come from media buying unless someone changed the media buying ("almost always what happens" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]).
2. Check landing page, homepage and product page changes, organic and paid Google results, and Reddit threads ranking for "[brand] reviews". Check external events (e.g. a war).
3. If the top ad (spending 30–50% of budget) suddenly doubles its CPA, ask "Is there something wrong with that ad?" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] or did the website break? The top spender looks worst first because it carries the most spend.
4. Under pressure, admit the numbers are bad but explain that you can't make changes because this isn't the problem.
- His test: "if you can't tell me what your site looked like on February 13th" [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4], you're missing something.
- Sources: [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04) · [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03)

### 2.10 Meta refund playbook, "the three P's" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]. GEN
- Persuasion, persistence, patience. (1) Confirm the outage on metastatus.com. (2) Don't expect reps to know about it. (3) Push chat support until the case moves to email, and document everything. (4) Set daily or weekly follow-up reminders. (5) Frame it as expected vs observed behavior, and say it is not okay ethically, legally or as a business: "it erodes trust" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]. (6) Ask only for what was reasonably lost, calculated from normal vs observed spend, or normal vs observed cost per purchase multiplied by the overspend. "Don't be a jerk. Don't be unreasonable." [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8]
- Sources: [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03) · [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03)

### 2.11 Click-attribution change audit (2026). GEN
- From late March 2026, click attribution counts only link clicks. Compare your top spenders before and after. Expect ads that won spend through cheap clicks on cheap placements to lose it: comment-heavy ads, click-baity elements such as a fake button, and images whose text is so small people click to enlarge it. Expect better real performance but possibly worse in-platform metrics, "and you should figure out how to be okay with that". — [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04)

### 2.12 High-AOV / high-volume testing exception. DTC, relevant to LEADGEN
- For very large creative volume, or high-AOV (~$1,000 CPA) products: test at lower budget optimized to initiate checkout (or add to cart), because the best cost per checkout almost always correlates with the best purchase ad. He calls it "very bad" from a pure DR purchase view. It only works if the funnel has enough barriers that those actions show real intent. ATC is "wonkier" because of window shoppers.
- Custom early-indicator metric: raw ATC + initiate checkout + purchases, then cost per that combined count.
- Caveat: if the first button on the landing page adds to cart, cost per ATC is useless.
- Sources: [YT:s1oyWDqCV9w] (2023-03) · [YT:nUVl_zpeipY] (2023-05) · [YT:PSMit4vB8n8] (2022-12) · [YT:R0k0rVzv_yQ] (2024-08)

### 2.13 Evaluating people and agencies (anti-hit-rate). GEN
- Compare team members by cumulative spend on the ads each person made. Evaluate agencies, editors and creative sources on the amount of spend their ads can carry.
- His pop quiz: source A (2 wins out of 10) or source B (3 wins out of 100)? "It's not a real question" [quote src: YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg]: it leaves out what each source cost and how much the winners spent.
- Hit rate tolerance depends on cost per ad. Spend $100,000 to produce an ad and "you better have a hundred% hit rate" [quote src: YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg]. At $0 per ad he doesn't care about 0.001%. Count your own time, editor hours, strategist review and copywriter in that cost.
- If you must use hit rate, base it on a spend threshold, never on CPA or ROAS.
- Team questions: if we got rid of hit rate, would we make more ads? Where do our best winners actually come from?
- Sources: [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg] (2026-04) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04)

### 2.14 AI copy process, "meat first". GEN
1. Throw an idea, thought, question or problem loosely at ChatGPT ("I'm bad with a blank sheet" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]).
2. He is "a great editor" [quote src: YT:DPvFnflNEYQ] and likes it when the output is "not it", because then he can give feedback and better examples.
3. Write the main body first (often drafted by ChatGPT and rewritten by him), then ask for "five headlines for this" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU].
4. Pick the one that resonates and ask for "five more like that" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU].
- He uses two-sentence prompts because he likes to see the model fail before he trusts it. Prompt AI the way you manage an employee: give the problem and context, not the solution.
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05)

### 2.15 AI research workflow. GEN
1. Build the "ecosystem" first: PDP/landing-page info and a transcribed long-form launch video script. Most advertisers jump straight to create ad copy for this brand.
2. what are the 10 pain points of a consumer of this product, sorted by priority. This yields 2–3 he wouldn't think of. He treats it as good-enough market research, then double-checks.
3. Ask for statements about those pain points. Pick the ones you can visualize and tell stories around.
4. Upload reviews and ask find me past tense I statements. Use them to empathize and as copy you can paste directly. Positive reviews become content that illustrates them. Negative reviews and comments become content that combats them.
5. Work backwards: list the points you want in the ad, then ask ChatGPT or Claude what questions to ask a customer to get those points said.
6. Use AI to empathize with audiences whose problems you don't have (e.g. his women's vaginal supplement client), but never just trust it, because it hallucinates "garbage answers".
- Sources: [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) · [YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] (2024-03) · [YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12) · [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05)

### 2.16 AI video production method (Veo 3 era). GEN/LEADGEN
1. Design the person first in ChatGPT or an image generator. "90% of the prompt was just describing what she looks like" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]: age, heritage, hair, eyes, type of smile, lighting, a specific location (a part of Texas).
2. Direct the line reading with adverbs and speed notes: "incredulously", "say this faster" [quote src: YT:4BOtccKnC2Q], "be more incredulous" [quote src: YT:4BOtccKnC2Q]. He got the read he wanted in about the 4th generation.
3. Add background ambience (birds etc.) afterwards from Descript sound effects, because silence is a giveaway.
4. Use the best AI take as a reference: send the winner to a real creator to recreate, which avoids reshoots when a creator misses the intonation.
5. Make an AI "pre-shoot" of the storyboard for a few bucks before bigger shoots.
6. His usual pattern: an AI-generated hook followed by real human footage. A few ads are fully AI, and some have scaled.
7. Entry level: animate existing static product photos (push-in, pull-out, a model turning the product) and play with Veo and Kling.
