## 1. Core positions

1. **"Make ugly ads" means uglier, not ugliest.** Take the uglier route than your marketing brain wants: "it doesn't have to be ugly it just needs to be uglier". It is a sliding scale: any step away from the most perfect, aligned, branded ad (imperfect lighting, no hand model, off-centre framing, phone not studio). Not garbage ads people dislike. ## 2. Frameworks and methods

**2.1 The feed "range" model** — use: explaining why polished ads fail. Organic feed sits in a band from not-pretty to decent amateur photography; anything more professional falls outside the range and raises ad red flags before the hook lands. Viewers don't understand lens physics but instantly recognise professional vs creator vs doorbell-camera footage (background blur from a 200mm f/2.8 lens alone can lose attention). [YT:PSMit4vB8n8] (2022-12) · [YT:DGTwYyI-81s] (2022-08) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12)

**2.2 Ugliness levers checklist** — use: uglifying any brief. Levers: talent, lighting, camera type, camera angle, script, story, setting. Change at least one to be uglier (not the prettiest talent, imperfect lighting). Target "aspa-relatable" (aspirational + relatable). Polish signals to strip: logo at the start, perfect fades, sweeping zooms, ring light visible in the eyes, branded backdrop. [YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07) · [YT:DGTwYyI-81s] (2022-08) · [YT:kYSxNySgmKc] (2023-01)

**2.3 "How would a creator frame this?" (shooting method, dated)** — v1: ask how a professional (he was a professional photographer) would frame it, "And then I'll not do that. I'll just do the opposite." v2 (2026): ask how a creator — not a marketer, someone who makes content people like — would frame and shoot it, then shoot it that way. Supporting moves: ask what the snobbiest brand would never allow (he wears a hat, keeps an untrimmed beard); export at 480 and upscale so it looks lower quality; keep a "90 items long" list of small variables that could explain a winner (lighting, camera style, frame rate, upload resolution 720/1080/480). [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09) · [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) · [YT:FTotRZL9vAg] (2025-04) · [YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-03)

**2.4 First-moment discipline (be precious about the first moment)** — use: every ad.
1. First frame: make something happen (motion or a reveal in shot one); make a feeling happen (curiosity, discomfort, insecurity, laughter).
2. Top of the frame: it scrolls in first and video starts playing about a third into view, so the thumbnail and top line count; put a relevant text line and the setting cue there.
3. Front-load words in VO, overlay and primary text; cut filler (say "knives", not "when I go to the kitchen to use a knife" [quote src: YT:fawsUmNeCkg]).
4. First-moment audio: birds, crinkling bags, ASMR — relevant and native.
5. Faces: cover a face viewers don't know unless relevant (gesture, motion, product, captions) — softened in 2026.
6. Run the "so what" test on first pixels, first sound, first words.
[YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09) · [YT:SU5Tv9cRONg] (2025-02) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04) · [YT:s4mLWmECoKI] (2026-05)

**2.5 The "so what" test** — reframe of the "boring test": I don't think it's about boring I think it's what is the point where someone says so what. Easiest to apply post-hoc on ads that failed to scale; creators, editors, strategists and media buyers all apply it. In 2026 he diagnoses failing ads (can't get spend) by consumer-eye read: not relevant to me, boring, doesn't make sense, or unclear in the first frame/second. [YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2024-12) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12) · [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04)

**2.6 Targeting with creative / spectrum of relevance** — use: casting, setting, words. Relevance is a "multi-dimensional spectrum" (accent, skin colour, age, interests, gender, attractiveness, language, cadence) that "our puny little brains can't compute" but Meta's ML handles. Steps: pick setting for who it's relevant to (economy vs business class read differently); pick talent who speak like, share the problem with, or have authority over the viewer ("I don't need to see a thick Brooklyn bearded guy in every ad" [quote src: YT:fawsUmNeCkg]); cast variety (five creators aged 20–60 saying the same thing; Southern vs Boston accent version); sometimes "10 different videos with 10 different settings" for 10 types of people. Trust the unexplained "it just doesn't feel relevant to me" reaction from a reviewer. **2.7 Post-it note ads** — origin: a founder (brown guy in his 30s) asked what to shoot to sell to women in their 40s; Barry said Post-it ad; it "popped off" on Twitter. He claims he popularised it; still "crushing" because it doesn't look like an ad. Steps:
1. Buy a Sharpie; handwrite a relevant problem line.
2. Stick it on the product, the competitor's product, the problem itself, or something emblematic of the problem (write a direct message on a relevant thing).
3. Put it in a believable organic setting (dashboard for car care, beach for bikini line); off-centre, imperfect, text near the top of frame.
4. Photograph (static) or film (reveal video). No line yet? Stick a blank Post-it, shoot, add text later in Photoshop/iPad (ask ChatGPT for lines).
5. Run many: do 20 and you're copy testing for basically zero dollars; test opposite claims as separate ads ("parents hate this" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] vs "parents love this" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE]).
Caveats: Post-its are "barely even level one" / "just a gateway drug"; next step is recreating what the audience consumes. Fails when photoshopped/pretty, centred, generic sale copy, or when note-to-product pairing confuses. **2.8 Viral-page / Pubity-style publication static** — copy big Instagram viral pages (monetised, so optimised for easy consumption): full-colour photo fading to black at the bottom with white (and yellow) caps text, often a circle inset (zoom/detail or problem vs solution), or a white bar on top with black text. Write problem copy "as if it's news" (not TV-news style). Twitter-screenshot meme style is a sibling format. "make an ad that isn't what your brand would do". [YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07) · [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) · [YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] (2024-03)

**2.9 Self-shot talking ad ("just do it" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw])** — use: founders, media buyers, anyone.
1. Phone selfie somewhere relevant to the product/problem (kitchen, bathroom, car, walk, subway, where the problem happens).
2. Write bullets or ask ChatGPT/Claude how you'd explain to a friend why someone would buy.
3. Have someone off-camera ask questions — people perform best answering questions; talk to a friend, not "the Instagram audience" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE].
4. Let it roll: one take or 20, 20 minutes; selfie while walking.
5. Can't be on camera: POV hands-only, b-roll plus VO, whiteboard or write a note while talking. Behind-camera VO can be re-recorded any time for fixes or 5 versions.
6. Watch back and keep what's relevant; "slash and burn anything that does not move the story forward" [quote src: YT:cpnVXGQAWHg].
Even if it never runs, shooting teaches what to say and what a camera can do. [YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06) · [YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07) · [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12) · [YT:SU5Tv9cRONg] (2025-02)

**2.10 Long-form story ad structure** — from Harmon Brothers and old infomercials (ShamWow, Flex Tape): strong problem hook → angle → re-hook; many problems told by different people; "but wait there's more" [quote src: YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] = built-in diversity. Frame it as a storytelling video rather than an ad (a story with a five second ad spot in it); trailer-style front without revealing the payoff; curiosity line borrowed from YouTubers ("if you want to hear the rest of the story stick around"); mid-roll CTA plus end card. Don't over-agitate: Earth Breeze took 1:22 to reach the solution, get there ~0:45. When retention is strong, fix the hook to get more relevant people in. ## 6. What changed over time and contradictions
- **Ugly alone → ugly + story + relevance.** ~2018–21 anything ugly/UGC beat pretty because feeds were friends; by 2023 "Ugly ads can be bad ads too"; 2024 feeds are "TikTok-ified" strangers' content so novelty matters. [YT:YuS18wnD_bc] (2023-07) · [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12)
- **"Ad blocker" → "subconscious content blocker" (2025)**, and thumb-stopping → "relevance building" as personalisation means fewer skips. [YT:6jvIXIjieyA] (2025-05) · [YT:4BOtccKnC2Q] (2025-07)
- **Shooting method: do the opposite of a pro → "how would a creator frame this?"** (2026-03) [YT:EgnN83jIsj8]
