## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 Research

**Brainstorming process / audience one sheet** (ALLTIME, DTC; done at every client onboarding)
- When: start of every account; for endless ads.
- Steps: (1) research the product: problem it solves, benefits, why someone would buy, core USPs; (2) review mining + social comments; (3) find competitors (Amazon, their sites, their ads); (4) search the problem on YouTube/TikTok/Shorts for problem-aware language; (5) ad inspiration in Foreplay (long-running ads); (6) study others in and outside the niche; (7) compile into an "audience one sheet" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA].
- One sheet headings: Problems product solves / Benefits / Reasons to buy (with review quotes) / Core USPs. Images allowed; no fixed template (organize however your brain works); one per brand and per product.
- Example: Yumy probiotic gummies — landing page ("tastiest way to improve digestion" [quote src: YT:NNg9-TDiFDA]) → core benefit say goodbye to bloating → USPs (gluten free, palm oil free, non-GMO, 100% vegan) → FAQ listing problems (bloating, constipation, low mood, poor sleep): "they've done the work for us" [quote src: YT:NNg9-TDiFDA].
- Sources: [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03)

**Research source order** (EVERGREEN, GEN): (1) Reddit, (2) reviews (site, Amazon, Trustpilot), (3) your own ad comments, (4) competitor ads (Meta Ad Library, Foreplay), (5) AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini). Store everything in one Google Drive folder; it feeds AI context later and lets you compare today's customer with three years ago. — [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2025-11)
- Extra sources (2026-05): email past non-buyers/customers a few questions or a survey; always ask clients for a review export and post-purchase surveys ("absolute gold"). — [YT:QoGFOGEqijw]

**Review mining** ("where absolute gold is discovered" [quote src: YT:RSa4wUuw_2k]) (NOW, DTC)
- Manual first: read site/Trustpilot reviews "with our marketing cap on" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]; for each line ask: is it a messaging angle to build out, or a direct quote for the top of a static? Note recurring themes.
- Then AI: export reviews to Claude: "Identify me the common themes and reasons why people purchase this product" [quote src: YT:RSa4wUuw_2k]; or a spreadsheet with "ingest these" to pull everything usable; keep the export in a Claude project.
- Persona extraction: upload reviews and ask Claude for who buys, why, and their problems.
- Rules: discard generic praise ("very tasty"); hunt for specific outcomes and use cases; watch for imported reviews from other products; don't discard negatives — address them in ads; mine competitor and similar-product reviews too; look for comparisons customers already made (what they replaced) → swap hooks; print/highlight use cases; patterns you hadn't thought of (pregnant users → "there you go. That's an ad angle." [quote src: YT:gP-68LmgAQ4]).
- Sources: [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:PuQ2TvXrWg4] (2026-04), [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03), [YT:Mqj05tYuDI0] (2026-09), [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2025-11), [YT:VCnx3v_gZQI] (2024-11)

**Reddit / social mining with Reddit Answers** (EVERGREEN→NOW)
- Steps: (1) skip subreddit scrolling; ask Reddit Answers a question (why do people not like <brand>?, "why do people like leaf shave?" [quote src: YT:HXox1I6QQvQ], "Why do men love natural soaps over something artificial?" [quote src: YT:RSa4wUuw_2k]); (2) open AI summary themes → underlying posts and comments; (3) follow suggested related questions and ask your own follow-ups (e.g. are Aloha bars gluten-free?); (4) repeat for competitors if your brand isn't mentioned organically; (5) aggregate nuggets in one doc; (6) feed to the client's Claude project to write scripts/statics/hooks.
- Why Reddit: most honest, unfiltered; people go there to moan, so it surfaces objections, pain points, competitor complaints, snippets for statics, unserved features.
- Natural-soap query returned skin health/hydration, less dryness, fewer irritants, avoiding harmful chemicals, natural lasting scents: "a gold mine" [quote src: YT:6OmF0BzBOHQ] in 2 minutes.
- Sources: [YT:RpNcnKS2WYc] (2025-08), [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] (2025-08), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07)

**Comment mining** (GEN): comments on the brand's own ads are the best place (organic Instagram weak; TikTok good for comments and customer content). Comments show missing angles, use cases, friend-tagging, real demographic, reasons for not buying, and language. He reads comments whenever an agency ad appears in his feed. — [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03), [YT:svW3EyoYAiw] (2024-08), [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2025-11), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

**Problem search** (ALLTIME): for problem-aware audiences search the problem ("how to fix bloating stomach") on YouTube, Shorts, TikTok (Gen Z searches TikTok); read videos and comments for language (learned peppermint tea, period cramps → bloating). — [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03)

**Persona burner accounts** (NOW, cited from someone on X): set up burner social accounts per brand and persona, scroll as that persona, note formats, language and hooks that appear; choose formats from it (feed full of female podcast clips → test fake podcast clips; car yapper videos → next format). "The answers are normally always already there." — [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

**The "brand bubble"** (GEN): teams working on a brand daily get tunnel vision; talk to or read customers for the real reasons to buy — "that's where you unlock ridiculous growth." Also: "sometimes the brand is wrong with who they believe is buying the product". — [YT:tVXjXJSEjeA] (2025-07), [YT:-q2lqfNysUs] (2025-11)

### 2.2 Competitor research

**Meta Ad Library method** (ALLTIME): location = all, category = all ads, search a brand (BlendJet, 93 active ads); review static vs video mix and offers; click the CTA to see the landing page. Whitelisting trick: creator-page ads don't appear under the brand page, so search the brand name as an exact phrase to surface creator and publisher ads. — [YT:Ak6I-EIbBd4] (2023-11)

**Amazon competitor reviews** (ALLTIME): search the category ("gut health gummies"), sort by customer reviews, read competitor reviews for new reasons to buy ("more energy", "well formulated poos", stomach ache after hospital) — real use cases vs "propaganda that brands put out". — [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03)

**Foreplay** (ALLTIME→EVERGREEN): save ads to swipe boards so links don't die; Discovery AI keyword search ("bloating", "gut health"); discovery feed → filter niche → format = statics → sort longest running; keyword search if too broad. Caveat (2025-08): longest running isn't always best performing (people leave ads on spending pence a day). Foreplay API pulls Meta Ad Library ads into his AI workspace (2026-03). — [YT:Ak6I-EIbBd4], [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA], [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ], [YT:wXe0B2XZTGI]

A brand scaling hard over Q4 = heavy gifting product. Via MCP from Claude: "find me the top five ads in the male grooming space" then "break down why". "inspiration with data", better than "off vibes". ### 2.3 Strategy structures

**3-step basic creative strategy** (ALLTIME→EVERGREEN, DTC/GEN)
- (1) Audit the account for what's missing: media mix (carousels, images, video, UGC) and angles; (2) read comments (ads, competitors, organic, Twitter, Reddit) for what people want to know; (3) idea = gap from step 1 + insight from step 2, filled with a true story from reviews, then script and film.
- 2025 version: cover as many missing angles as possible because that adds the creative diversity Meta likes; reflect the customer using their actual words.
- Audit payoff story: one messaging angle performed really well but had only one ad; more ads around it "led to a couple of winners". — [YT:svW3EyoYAiw] (2024-08), [YT:tVXjXJSEjeA] (2025-07), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07)

**Standard angles every account needs** (ALLTIME): problem-solution (acne → acne-free via product); testimonial (UGC to camera, customers or paid actors, or review static); us-vs-them; objection handling (his favourite). — [YT:svW3EyoYAiw] (2024-08)

**Idea matrix** (EVERGREEN, GEN): Angle ("angles, concepts… it's all the same thing") → idea → Hook (first ~3 seconds, purely attention) → Format. Order is flexible: start from a hook ("that will make a great UGC video") or an account gap ("we need more founder ads"); you need all three. — [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2025-11)

**Four levers of creative diversity** (NOW, GEN): (1) persona — same product, different buyer; (2) messaging — different reason to buy; (3) hook — visual, audible, copy hooks, bold claims, visual demos in the first 3 seconds; (4) format — UGC, founder video, static, carousel, demo: "the wrapper". Building an ad unit: strategist fixes persona + messaging, picks format from historic account data; no history → "throw a load of stuff at the wall and see what sticks". Example pair: Humantra — athletes/runners at peak performance; office workers who forget water and get headaches. — [YT:CPWoW09WCCo] (2026-02)

**Layers of every ad** (NOW, 2026-10): (1) angle/concept (what are you trying to say?) + (2) message, (3) persona, (4) format. Grun's: message "why suffer downing green juices when you can have this one simple gummy"; persona AG1 convert; format us-vs-them UGC or VSL. Launch 4–5 a week varying all. — [YT:RExPkxyRB6o]

**Angle vs messaging** (NOW): angle = "a lens" for presenting the product, different angles resonate with different audience buckets (solution-first for the problem-aware); messaging = how best to tell the story. — [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07)

**Persona + hidden sub-persona** (NOW, DTC): pick a specific persona plus a messaging point that connects; dig for sub-personas (mucky-work soap: builders obvious, dirt bikers hidden). Phrase call-outs to keep the core buyer: "perfect for natural soap users who happen to work on a construction site". Question to ask: "how can I make better ads that connect to personas". — [YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2026-08)

**Products with no direct problem** (NOW): target a demographic who wants something different (True Classic: bigger men/dad bods — "They basically made up a problem"), or sell lifestyle/feel. "If you dig deep enough, there will be something there". — [YT:505rIrOKhWI] (2026-06), [YT:Lkx7nOGIslw] (2026-07)

**Funnel placement by format** (ALLTIME, DTC)
- TOF educate: UGC educational, product demos, UGC testimonials, studio product showcase, callout statics.
- MOF dial in/sell: us-vs-them statics, review statics, carousels ("so underused"), high-prod lifestyle video, UGC product videos.
- BOF make them want it: review statics, offer statics (20% off, BOGO), carousels, UGC testimonials, studio product in a lovely environment. — [YT:qpJqASEvYuc] (2024-02)

**Awareness-stage rules** (EVERGREEN→NOW, DTC)
- Solution-aware is the stage most skip; us-vs-them statics fit here; educate on the brand, what it does, how it works.
- Product-aware: blockers are clarity, social proof, trust, and "One of the biggest hurdles here is price" [quote src: YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q] → mini VSL + founder video on why it costs what it does, mixed with review statics.
- Most-aware: "about to fall off the cliff and the cliff is buying your product" [quote src: YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q] → offer so good they can't say no; conversion creative and offer statics; don't re-educate.
- MOF: mostly sold, one niggle (usually price).
- Spec details (non-toxic coating, third-party tested) don't belong TOF; introduce them further down, building on earlier ads.
- Ask where the ad sits and what questions the viewer is asking; facing objections helps tell Meta where to place the ad. Layer awareness levels from "awareness level zero" to diehard fans. — [YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q] (2025-11), [YT:qZhOnITrN0Q] (2026-05), [YT:505rIrOKhWI] (2026-06), [YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2026-08), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

**Sequence for small/new brands** (NOW, DTC): start with very simple statics to find messaging that gets clicks → once you know why people buy, build those signals into video (UGC or mashup; with AI sometimes no creator) → keep both formats "forever", statics testing new messaging. For $70K+/month clients with data, Fraggle goes straight to video with supporting statics. Statics-to-video: release video versions with each static set, or animate whichever statics win. — [YT:_BuCO3Jf-K0] (2026-02), [YT:xIpMOTNFvkY] (2026-08)

**Spend-stage guidance** (NOW, DTC): 10–20k/month: lean — AI creatives, image ads, a couple of self-shot UGC; brands make their own. 50–70k: look at agencies/in-house; "don't skimp out"; alignment is "a make or break part". ~70k: start testing offers but keep one evergreen. — [YT:DNI5NI2-lSk] (2026-08)

**Fraggle end-to-end process** (NOW): (1) audit the ad account → (2) research (AI deep research, review mining, social mining, competitor research) → (3) messaging and angles → (4) formats for diversity → (5) find, cast, brief creators → (6) launch with forced spend → (7) read data and iterate, back to step 1. Earlier (2025-11) version: research → ideation → execution (pre-production → production → editing) → testing (hook, hold, click-through rates; decide learnings; iterate). — [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:-q2lqfNysUs] (2025-11)

**Weekly flywheel** (NOW, GEN): write hooks → brief creators → launch and test → reconcile data → start again, so "every single week you know what's going in". Losing brands launch when they feel like it and review data only when performance tanks. — [YT:qZhOnITrN0Q] (2026-05)

**Volume terminology** (NOW): "net new" = new assets/ideas/angles (what volume targets count); "variation" = a variation on a net new (new hook); "iteration" = changing something on an ad already live. — [YT:DNI5NI2-lSk] (2026-08)

**Five decisions, $1M vs $10M brand** (NOW, DTC; "nothing to do with the product"): (1) invest in creative before ROI is obvious; (2) treat every asset as a compounding investment; (3) play the long game in the account; (4) trust the people you hire; (5) make the mindset shift before you're forced to. $1M brands want proof and guarantees at signing; $10M brands commit and build proof. — [YT:QdNIajKiC2s] (2026-04)

**10 most common creative mistakes** (NOW): (1) not knowing why an ad wins, (2) too many ads, (3) ads that don't start conversations, (4) too much AI slop, (5) one format, (6) not reusing footage, (7) too many offers, (8) not understanding the customer/personas, (9) playing it safe, (10) creative that doesn't match the landing page. — [YT:PuQ2TvXrWg4] (2026-04)

**Strategist role** (EVERGREEN): strategist ideates, writes the script, picks the cast, then oversees pre-production, production and editing so the final matches "what was in their head". "The number one mistake" is stopping at ideation; strategists are "basically the director" and need to understand camera movement (focus), lighting (mood) and static design (thumb stop) — but not be used as editor or creator. Execution is often the most expensive, slowest part. — [YT:-q2lqfNysUs] (2025-11)

### 2.4 Copy and script frameworks

**Psychology triggers** (EVERGREEN, mostly DTC): contrast (Mac vs PC: side by side, no direct attack); FOMO ("only three left"; Supreme queues; Ridge "once a year anniversary sale"); scarcity (QVC callers and stock); social proof ("10,000 happy customers", simple testimonial; bottom of funnel "is social proof"; people fear "that we might be wrong"); price anchoring (smallest unit); authority (dermatologist for acne; Pet Lab Co vets); empowerment (Nike, Apple; easier in services: "I can change your ad account"); transformation (Hims sells "the vacation"); reciprocity (free gifts in bundles; in B2B, free value content such as his YouTube is a main lead driver for Fraggle); novelty ("new" triggers dopamine, links to FOMO); guarantees (main reason statics work: make them feel safe). — [YT:oVpT0GnGY8Q] (2025-05), [YT:tVXjXJSEjeA] (2025-07), [YT:GCugREdurJk] (2026-01), [YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (2025-06)

**Us-vs-them / hero vs villain** (NOW)
- Structure: first portion all "them" (inferior option, "rubbish"); final half sells the solution with social proof and product proof.
- Villain options: an industry, a competitor, the "traditional" version, even "the internet" (viral hacks); stack a bonus villain.
