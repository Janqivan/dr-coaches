# THE PAIN OF DISCIPLINE vs THE PAIN OF REGRET — standards work

Sources: [S60]/[S70] official collections; [7S] Ch. 1; [LAIL] Ch. 1; official discipline articles [JR]/[SU]; [5MP]; seminars [YT].

## The canonical line — exact wording matters

VERIFIED official wording:
> "We must all suffer one of two things: the pain of discipline or the pain of regret." [S60 #2]
> Variant, also official: "We must all suffer one of two things: the pain of discipline or the pain of regret or disappointment." [S70 #48]

NOT verified: the popular "one of two **pains**" phrasing, and the "discipline weighs ounces; regret weighs tons" addendum — commonly attributed to *Take Charge of Your Life* era audio, found in no primary source this pull. Usable only as: "Rohn is credited with adding that discipline weighs ounces while regret weighs tons." [ATTR]

Both options are pain. Choose the lighter one early. That's the whole frame.

## STANDARDS > GOALS (application frame)

A goal set without raising the standard is a goal you won't keep. The standard is the floor you refuse to go below; the goal is the ceiling you're reaching for. Rohn's material anchors the floor:
> "Your life, my life, the life of each one of us is going to serve as either a warning or an example." [SU]
> "Life is not a practice session." [SU]
> "One of the best places to start to turn your life around is by doing whatever appears on your mental 'I should' list." [S60]
> "Don't say 'If I could, I would.' Say 'If I can, I will.'" [JR/S60]
> "The best way to live: hot or cold — pick a direction and go with everything you got." [paraphrase of YT:0sG31mTbHJU indifference segment; his words: "pick a direction and go with everything you got. Just pick one and go."]

## COACHING PROTOCOL — the two-pains decision frame

For any decision or slipped standard:
1. "Which pain are you choosing right now — discipline today, or regret in three years?" Make both pains concrete: name what the discipline costs this week; name what the regret costs at the 3-year mark.
2. "What's the standard here — the floor you won't go below? Is that the standard of the person you said you're becoming?"
3. "What's the discipline that, if you started today, removes the future regret?" One discipline. Small. Daily. ("Start with the little disciplines.")
4. Date the review. Regret compounds silently; the review is the interruption.
