## Framework 1 — RMBC Method (the master workflow)

**What it is:** A four-stage workflow that takes a piece of direct response copy from blank page to finished sales asset. Each stage gates the next — you do not advance until the prior stage is complete.

**The four stages:**

**R — Research.** Avatar excavation. Before you write anything:
- Read reviews of competing products (Amazon, Trustpilot, niche forums).
- Read sales-call recordings and customer support tickets.
- Read refund-reason data, churn-exit surveys, and unsubscribe replies.
- Read the comment sections under viral content in the niche.
- Read what the avatar says to their *friends* about this problem, not what they say to *vendors*.
- Capture the language verbatim. The avatar's exact phrases beat anything you can invent.

The output of R is a research dossier: who they are, what they've tried, what they believe, what they fear, what they secretly want, and what specific words they use about it all.

**M — Mechanism.** The specific reason YOUR offer works when others didn't. Not "we're better". *Why*. A new ingredient, a new sequence, a new combination, a new piece of science, a new system. Without a mechanism, the copy is just claims, and claims without mechanism trigger skepticism.

The output of M is a one-sentence mechanism statement: *"This works because of [specific reason], unlike everything else that fails because of [specific reason]."*

**B — Brief.** The document that contains everything the copy needs. 1-2 pages. The lead promise, the mechanism statement, the proof stack, the offer, the bonuses, the guarantee, the avatar profile, the voice notes, the forbidden words, the P.S. line. The brief is the work — if the brief is right, the copy almost writes itself.

The output of B is a finished brief that any competent copywriter could execute from.

**C — Copy.** Only now do you write. Lead first, P.S. second, body last. If you find yourself staring at a blank page or rewriting the lead seven times, the problem is upstream — the brief isn't done.

**Georgi's rule:** never skip a stage. The temptation is always to skip R or B and "just start writing". That's how you produce slow, weak, generic copy.

## Framework 2 — Mechanism-First Copywriting

**What it is:** The discipline of leading with the *mechanism* — the specific reason your offer works — rather than the claim alone.

**The contrast:**
- **Claim only:** "Lose 30 lbs in 30 days." → Triggers skepticism. Sounds like every other ad.
- **Claim + mechanism:** "Lose 30 lbs in 30 days because of this overlooked cellular process discovered at Johns Hopkins." → Triggers curiosity. The mechanism is the proof the claim demands.

**Why it works:**
- Modern audiences are trained to filter out claims. Decades of advertising have made every promise feel hollow.
- A mechanism reframes the claim from a *promise* into an *explanation*. Explanations are easier to believe than promises.
- A specific mechanism filters competitors out of the conversation — they can copy your claim, but they can't copy your mechanism without sounding like a knockoff.

**Where to put the mechanism in the copy:**
- Foreshadowed in the lead ("there's a specific reason this works that most marketers don't know about")
- Named in the body, mid-letter (the reveal)
- Re-stated in the offer ("you're getting the [mechanism name] system")
- Anchored in the P.S. ("remember — this works because of [mechanism], not because of [generic competitor approach]")

**Where DR uses it:**
- The Case Flow sales letter — the Case Flow installation IS the mechanism. Name it, explain it, defend it.
- Cold email — the mechanism is the curiosity hook in the subject line and first paragraph.
- VSL scripts — the mechanism is the "big reveal" 30-40% of the way through the video.

## Framework 3 — The Brief as the Work

**Georgi's claim:** 90% of the copy's quality is decided in the brief, not in the writing.

**Why:**
- A weak brief produces weak copy no matter how good the writer.
- A strong brief produces strong copy from an *average* writer.
- The brief is where you make every important decision: what you're promising, who you're promising it to, what mechanism backs the promise, what proof you'll use, what objections you'll preempt, what offer you'll stack, what guarantee you'll attach, what voice you'll use, what words you'll avoid.
- Once those decisions are made and written down, the actual writing becomes execution — much faster, much higher quality, much less staring at the blank page.

**What a Georgi-style brief contains (1-2 pages):**

1. **Lead promise** — the single most important benefit, in the avatar's own language.
2. **Mechanism statement** — one sentence. Why YOUR thing works.
3. **Proof stack** — 3-5 specific pieces of proof (case studies, data, third-party verification, demos).
4. **Avatar profile** — who they are, what they've tried, what they fear, what they secretly want, verbatim language samples.
5. **Offer** — what they get, exactly. Components, prices, total.
6. **Bonuses** — named, priced, justified.
7. **Guarantee** — risk reversal language.
8. **P.S. line** — restated offer + deadline + biggest objection answered.
9. **Voice notes** — tone, formality, sentence length, energy.
10. **Forbidden words** — words and phrases the avatar mistrusts or the niche has burned out.

**Operator rule:** if you cannot complete every section of the brief, you have not done enough research. Go back to R. Do not advance to C with an incomplete brief.
