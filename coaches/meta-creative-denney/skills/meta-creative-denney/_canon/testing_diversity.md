## 2. Frameworks and methods (named models, step sequences)

### Andromeda → GEM → Lattice: who picks the ad (Denney)
- When they use it: explaining post-2024 delivery (2025-12).
- Steps: 1. Andromeda (launched December 2024) only retrieves — it narrows millions of ads to a smaller pool per user, every session. 2. GEM, the generative ads recommendation model, narrows further — per a Meta employee a "super brain" that may know what customers want next week. 3. Lattice, the library of all the ad models (GEM lives in it), matches the right ad to the right user and makes the final pick; the models used to be separate.
- Their example: competitors who doubled down on creative operations get picked over you — "the age of creative strategy after Andromeda" [quote src: YT:ZydGeio1DGQ]. By 2026-06 she says Andromeda itself picks per individual; Levinger (2026-07): under the "lattice structure" Meta "is going to get real cranky" [quote src: YT:OiKpEOXY4zc] about how creative is generated.
- Sources: [YT:x1b4h42HTG8] (2025-12), [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06), [YT:OiKpEOXY4zc] (2026-07)

### "Two doors, not one" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (Levinger) and the AI pre-screen (Cottrell)
- When they use it: explaining why a solid ad can fail before it ever competes (2025-12; 2026-10).
- Steps: 1. Door one, the FYP prediction layer: "Will this piece of content hit Sarah at the right moment" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — judged on concept, execution, content value. 2. Door two: the auction against competitors. 3. So ask "does it behave like content people respond to?" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — content mechanism first, ad mechanism second; the FYP reads "the big four" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (hover, swipe speed, rewatch, hesitation) plus day and hour.
- Their example: "you're trying to market a 2018 ad in a for you page ecosystem" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — it goes viral to the wrong audience. Cottrell (2026-10): the AI pre-screen (§1) means loading a range of formats, angles, messages, personas.
- Sources: [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

### Levels of creative diversity (Denney) and diversity audits
- When they use it: auditing a brand's library; her in-progress diversity analyzer ranks brands against it (2025-12 → 2026-09).
- Steps (titled "5 levels", numbered 0–5): L0 statics vs video → L1 format expansion → L2 awareness expansion → L3 desire and motivation expansion → L4 audience segment and use-case expansion (personas) → L5 true creative diversity. Then a content-gap check: monthly spend share on images, video, carousel and partnership ads ("partnership ads is really really that big one" [quote src: YT:4wM-i1cxtAM]), and whether diversity spans the messaging wins.
- Their example: she can't yet put a KPI on diversity; Facebook blocks LLMs from the Ad Library, so her analyzer reads primary text and thumbnails. Cooper (2025-04): a 90-day chart across all sources by format (static, low- and high-prod video, carousel, GIF, motion graphics), style, angle, net new vs iteration.
- Sources: [YT:ZydGeio1DGQ] (2025-12), [YT:Uh0TE6nZs5A] (2026-06), [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM] (2026-09), [YT:EOZp34uDIc4] (2025-04)

### Making a variation count after the similarity score (Cottrell, Levinger)
- When they use it: re-running a proven message without Meta collapsing it into the original (2026-07 → 2026-09).
- Steps: 1. Keep the proven message or storytelling mechanism. 2. Change the surface completely — several differently designed statics (e.g. a call-out ad) around one key message such as the group-chat idea, not one static with new headlines; or a video version of a static (different media type, low risk of the same entity ID). 3. Make the difference obvious (Levinger: different calorie counts, different foods from the shop) so Meta sends it to a different person. 4. Never copy hooks word for word.
- Their example: static variations are now "a bit more of a dance" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (Cottrell 2026-09); "we can't just make the same asset and call it a variation anymore" [quote src: YT:xIpMOTNFvkY] (2026-08).
- Sources: [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:xIpMOTNFvkY] (2026-08), [YT:a5ruZB-Urlw] (2026-07), [YT:cO1wlDtG5TY] (2026-06)

### Three testing setups by budget (Denney, e-commerce)
- When they use it: choosing structure; testing at $3M/month is "vastly different" from starting out (2025-03).
- Steps: 1. Lean (~$5K–15K/month or less): one Advantage+ Shopping campaign that is all testing; ~10 creatives, then one or two new a week. 2. Growth (~$1,000/day, $30K–50K/month): ASC stays the scaler; add a manual testing campaign, one ad set per concept, 3–6 creatives (3 on lower budgets, 6–7 with more) at $100–$200 per ad set per day — "widely accepted as a best practice" [quote src: YT:7knQyPYLmfo], credited to Barry Hott's course. 3. Scale (>$1M/month): still one testing campaign (she has seen $5M/month run one); 10–20+ tests a week at $200/day for ~7 days or ~$1,000; often cost caps to surface "the best of the best" [quote src: YT:7knQyPYLmfo]; nearly all ABO. 4. Duplicate winners into the ASC (horizontal plus vertical scaling).
- Their example: onboarding surfaces 20–30 (up to 50) low-hanging iterations, launched together in one net-new ASC (Hott accepts it only because they iterate proven winners); when results slide, relaunch an ASC with last year's top performers.
- Sources: [YT:7knQyPYLmfo] (2025-03), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05), [YT:R3Z1VvvgtzU] (2026-04) · EVERGREEN · DTC

### Simple account structure (Hott, 2025)
- When they use it: default setup; the key mistake is "doing too much" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g].
- Steps: 1. One testing campaign (ABO or CBO); each new batch of concepts gets its own ad set where only the creative varies (same targeting, same bid/cost cap). 2. 4–6 ads per ad set. 3. Exclude visitors and engagers so creative is learned cold. 4. One Advantage+ Shopping campaign for retargeting and all other prospecting. 5. No manual retargeting or targeting tests ("Shut off your retargeting campaign right now" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg]); make ads that work warm and cold.
- Their example: in his at-scale tests, manual retargeting and mid/bottom-funnel targeting weren't incremental; a 1% lookalike may beat broad at small scale, but broad contains it.
- Sources: [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05), [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05) · EVERGREEN · DTC/GEN

### Structure by funnel phase (TEAP) and budget thresholds (Levinger)
- When they use it: structure and the month's budget focus (2026-02 → 2026-06).
- Steps: 1. One campaign, four ad sets: trigger (link-click/traffic optimized), exploration, evaluation, purchase (conversion). 2. Pick the month's goal — TOF eyeballs: weight trigger formats (short-form, pattern interrupts, novelty); sales: evaluation/purchase formats with depth, proof, specificity, trust ("not really a place for short hooks" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]); lower costs: formats your team makes at very high quality ("Quality is a cost reducer" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]). 3. Thresholds: "anything under 30k a month, don't break it up yet" [quote src: YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] into avatars; hook splits by generation or gender need at least $50k–$100k/month.
- Their example: CAC problems come from "chunking money" at purchase or trigger while ignoring the middle. By 2026-05 "trigger and exploration should be outside of the ad account" [quote src: YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0] — moved to organic, "because the ad account is meant to convert" [quote src: YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0].
- Sources: [YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] (2026-02), [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06), [YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0] (2026-05) · NOW · GEN

### Net-new volume ladder by monthly spend (Cottrell, 2026-08)
- When they use it: setting monthly output (DTC).
- Steps: 1. $10–20k: 3–4 net new a month — "one net new every week is probably going to be enough" [quote src: YT:DNI5NI2-lSk]. 2. $20–50k: ~two a week (5–6 a month): "The focus here is quality. It is never volume" [quote src: YT:DNI5NI2-lSk]; start educational TOF (VSLs, maybe founder ads). 3. $50–70k: "a pivotal moment" [quote src: YT:DNI5NI2-lSk]; winners may last four to six weeks; upper end 10–15 net-new concepts a month plus variations. 4. $70–100k (Fraggle's minimum client spend): "Creative is your main lever here to scale" [quote src: YT:DNI5NI2-lSk]; up to 20 concepts a month plus variations across formats and personas. 5. $100–250k: add EGC and an agency or two; "Volume does actually compound here" [quote src: YT:DNI5NI2-lSk]; ~30 net new a month as a very minimum. 6. $250–500k+: 50+ a month (minimum high 40s), with rapid hook/creator iterations on winners.
- Their example: guardrail (2026-02) — 50 new ads in a $100/day account is no test; at ~$50/day launch one or two a week.
- Sources: [YT:DNI5NI2-lSk] (2026-08), [YT:_BuCO3Jf-K0] (2026-02) · NOW · DTC

### Volume "at quality" (Levinger)
- When they use it: setting weekly output (2026-06).
- Steps: 1. Set a weekly target where every ad passes QA (e.g. 25). 2. Hit it → add five a week (30, 35). 3. Aim for 25 and hit 7 → 7 is the benchmark until skills or, usually, systems improve.
- Their example: ~80 ads a week makes it "really hard for me to decide what to make" [quote src: YT:OiKpEOXY4zc]; Denney uses no calculator — budget, creative ops and a "spidey sense".
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06), [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06) · NOW · GEN

### Primary vs storytelling metrics and the column view (Denney; Cottrell, Hott)
- When they use it: every read; data answers what works (spend and purchases only) and, harder, why (2025-01).
- Steps: 1. Far left: amount spent, purchases (leads/CPL for lead gen), cost per purchase, ROAS — only these call winners; profitable CPP plus purchase volume = raise budget. 2. Reach: frequency, reach, impressions, cost per reach, CPM. 3. Storytelling: unique outbound CTR, cost per link click, hook rate, hold rate, average play time, post shares. 4. Sort by spend; keep cost per result visible for stakeholders. 5. Analyze first, then tweak.

[quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] and adds cost per 3-second view, cost per ThruPlay and cost per share.

### Reading delivery: "what's spending" and the breakdown effect (Hott)
- When they use it: before pausing anything, or when a big spender shows bad CPA.
- Steps: 1. Give the system many ads plus "the rules of the game" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (often a cost cap, sometimes a bid cap). 2. For each ad, ask why the system spends on it — empathize with the consumer and with the system as a human buyer. 3. Check data depth: conversions, not dollars ($1,000 for 1,000 conversions is a lot; $1,000 for one is not); on thin data it backs attention, so "make sure it's relevant attention" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]. 4. Judge at budget level: Meta may put 90% on a weak-CPA ad so the other 10% can "slam dunk". 5. Before killing a top spender ask "would I want this out there spending more changing more minds" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]. 6. Read the audience-segments breakdown (cold vs warm) — "the most valuable thing" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] Meta has given him.
- Their example: with a $100 target CAC and a top spender at $220, the low-spend, better-CAC ads almost definitely won't scale to its level. Denney tested throttling spend toward better-CPP ads several times; they typically did worse.
- Sources: [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04), [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01) · NOW+EVERGREEN · GEN

### Four-part creative analysis (Denney)
- When they use it: a big swing or "a unicorn banger" [quote src: YT:CCsty8R0UaA] scaling — not every test (2025-01).
- Steps: 1. Metrics. 2. Content: format, creator, messaging, imagery (production quality, setting). 3. Comparison: vs your top performers, competitors, industry and platform trends — the step "a lot of people miss out on" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]. 4. Feedback: far more than ad comments. Then breakdowns: age (if 50+ responds, make more for them), gender, placement.
- Their example: Version B won on 1.2 ROAS, 1.64 CTR, 21% hook, 7.8 hold, so make more variants of B — "this is not a real analysis" [quote src: YT:CCsty8R0UaA].
- Sources: [YT:CCsty8R0UaA] (2025-01), [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01) · EVERGREEN · GEN

### Account audit before any brief (Levinger; Cottrell)
- When they use it: every new client, before research or briefing (2026-05/07).
