## T4. Sales pitch, deck, discovery-call and proposal narrative (build or teardown)

Evidence type: the deck/script itself, slide by slide or line by line. Cite slide or line numbers.

- ★ **Opens with an insight, not the company.** First beat is a claim about the buyer's world, not "Hi, we're DR, we do performance marketing for...". Logo, history or mission first = FAIL. A good pitch starts with an insight unique to you, a conversation no competitor can have. [YT:Z1X6XlwzQS4] [YT:RjI2UBQLp6U] [YT:b-EfETdb_zs]
- ★ **The insight is debatable but defensible, and not a feature complaint.** Three tests: a smart buyer nods and says "huh, that's true"; it reframes success in a way that favors DR's attributes; a competitor couldn't claim it as is. Matches one of: market shift, hidden cost, false trade-off. Universally agreed = table stakes = FAIL. Her example: "data is rapidly exploding" is something customers and every competitor already say, so nothing differentiates. [YT:Z1X6XlwzQS4] Help Scout's insight (support as growth driver, not cost center) is a model. [YT:jUuNIDZqqBI] [canon: SKILL Core Framework 6] (three tests and three types not found in transcripts)
- ★ **Setup precedes product.** Insight, honest alternatives, perfect-world setup all appear before DR or its offer is named. Product shown inside the Setup = FAIL. Setup is insight, pluses and minuses of alternatives, and perfect world. [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:b-EfETdb_zs] [YT:wa5jr1Il788]
- **Alternatives walked fairly, including do-nothing and in-house.** Each gets a fair pro and con. A strawman or a trash-talk slide = FAIL. If the buyer could name an alternative that isn't there, FAIL. She says to talk about the alternatives' approaches, "what's good and bad", not necessarily naming competitors. [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:b-EfETdb_zs] [YT:vM_1G1LCotU]
- **Perfect world is stated in outcomes, not capabilities.** The buyer hears results ("close cases in days, not weeks" style), and the description follows logically from the insight and the gaps in the alternatives. A bullet list of capabilities = FAIL. Her wording: "can we agree really good solutions should tick these boxes"; the demo then follows those agreed points. [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:EwCtAVhQU2o]
- **Category line is one or two sentences.** "[Product] is a [category] that [differentiated promise primed by the perfect world]." Category is one the buyer recognises. Her model intro: "we're Help Scout, customer service for digital businesses". [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [canon: SP Framework 6] (exact formula not found in transcripts)
- **1-3 value themes, each with its own proof.** Proof is a step after value: case study or independent verification; local proof where the buyer cares about region. Strongest proof type available is used first: named client + real number (e.g., Ashoori $5.19M / 5.91x ROAS is in SKILL context; verify before using). (Count rewritten from 2-4.) [YT:NRWCAYfuoYU] [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:hieEsVhCt4A] [canon: SKILL Context] (logo-wall point not found in transcripts)
- **Known objections are inside the pitch.** The 3-5 objections that always appear (price, "we'll hire in-house", "tried an agency before", contract length, attribution trust) are answered as pre-emptive beats, not left for Q&A. She makes this an optional step for big unspoken objections (hard to deploy, buy everything at once, pricing structure); the pricing model can be discussed even without an exact price. [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:eydpgUJJWWo] [YT:i1r4nChWelA]
- **The ask matches the stage and follows the proof.** Specific, named, time-bound. Over-asking early or "let's keep in touch" late = FAIL. The ask is the next step in your sales process (proof of concept, send a quote). [YT:ZQGFw-FXnQU] [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w]
- **Setup is a conversation and is not bloated.** Setup is partly discovery: ask "is this what you're seeing?" rather than talk at the buyer. Marketing-built pitches often carry "way too much setup" (29 slides of trends) and sales wants the demo; flag a setup the buyer would not sit through. (Rewritten: the canon said flag a short setup; she warns about a long one.) [YT:-VqmFI9vY7w] [YT:3W_uaqN7tpE]
- **Right tool for the deal.** Full 8-step is used only for a considered B2B sale (cycle over 2 weeks, 2+ alternatives, real conversation). DR retainers fit; a low-price self-serve product does not. If it doesn't fit, the output says so and compresses. She says self-serve add-on purchases are "not difficult considered purchases". [YT:SHB2Ols_YdY] [canon: SP Framework 12] (the 2-week threshold not found in transcripts)
- **Investor-pitch shape is absent.** No TAM slide, no founder-glorification, no "why now" in macro terms. Investor positioning creeping into the sales pitch confuses customers and makes them worry you sell something you don't yet do. [YT:2Wna9NT6BHk] [YT:SHB2Ols_YdY]
- **Demo or walkthrough has the pitch's structure.** The walkthrough follows the agreed perfect-world points, not every feature. [YT:EwCtAVhQU2o]
- ★ **Teardown output includes the rewrite of the first 90 seconds** in the 8-step order, in Ivan's real offer, not a template. [canon: SKILL Response Format (pitch teardown) item 5]

## Universal checks (every output)

- ★ **Retrieved before writing.** At least 3 searches run; the work uses at least 3 retrieved canon passages, cited as [YT:id] (or [canon: file] where no transcript hit exists).
- ★ **No invented quotes, numbers, stories or dates.** Every quoted line exists verbatim in a retrieved hit; a number that is not in canon or given by Ivan is labelled "to verify". When unsure: "I'd want to verify that before quoting it." [canon: SKILL Rule 4]
