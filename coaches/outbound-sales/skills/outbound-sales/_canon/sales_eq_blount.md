# Jeb Blount — Sales EQ Map

## The operator's worldview

Blount's argument across *Sales EQ*: in the modern complex sale, **product parity is the new normal.** Buyers have access to the same comparisons, same case studies, same demos. The differentiator is no longer what's being sold — it's the buyer's experience of the seller.

That experience is governed by emotion, not logic. The buyer's choice gets made in the limbic system and then justified in the prefrontal cortex. Therefore the rep's job is to manage three emotional layers in real time:
1. Their own state (self-control).
2. The buyer's state (empathy, influence).
3. The relationship state (the social context between them).

The reps who do this consistently are what Blount calls **Ultra High Performers** — the 5-10% of any sales org who pull away from the median by an order of magnitude. They aren't smarter or harder-working. They're emotionally regulated.

## Framework 1 — The Four Levels of Sales Intelligence

Blount stacks four kinds of "intelligence" a rep needs to operate at the top. Each level is necessary but only the last is the differentiator.

1. **Innate Intelligence (IQ)** — raw learning capacity. Threshold trait. Above the threshold, more doesn't help.
2. **Acquired Intelligence (AQ)** — product knowledge, market knowledge, technical skill, methodology. Table stakes. Everyone has it.
3. **Technological Intelligence (TQ)** — CRM hygiene, sales tech stack fluency, sequencer competence. Increasingly required, but commoditized.
4. **Emotional Intelligence (EQ)** — the real lever. The ability to read and regulate emotion in self and other.

**Blount's claim:** at the top of any sales org, EQ explains 80%+ of the gap between Ultra High Performers and the median. The other three are necessary entry conditions, not differentiators.

**Operator implication:** when a rep with strong AQ + TQ plateaus, the next move isn't more training. It's emotional regulation work.

## Framework 2 — The Four Pillars of Sales-Specific EQ

Sales EQ is not generic Goleman EQ. Blount's framing is task-specific: a rep needs four facets pointed at the sales interaction.

- **Empathy** — read the buyer's emotional state inside the call. Notice the tightness in the voice, the pause before the answer, the protective phrasing. Don't just hear the words.
- **Self-awareness** — read your own emotional state. Notice your call reluctance, ego flaring, defensiveness when challenged, neediness when the deal slips. Name it silently so it doesn't run you.
- **Self-control** — once named, regulate. Don't react to rejection, ghosting, price pushback, or aggression. Stay neutral on the outside even when the inside is loud.
- **Social skills** — manage the relationship dynamics. Read the room, adjust the cadence, mirror the buyer's pace, decide when to push and when to let silence work.

**Operator-level diagnostic:** when a deal stalls, ask which of the four pillars the rep is missing. Most reps have one weak pillar that costs them deals consistently. That's the coaching target — not the script.

## Framework 3 — The Three Buyers Inside Every Buyer

Blount's neuroscience-light frame for what's happening on the other side of the call. The buyer is not one person — they are three competing systems:

1. **The Rational Buyer** — analyses cost, fit, ROI. Slow. Articulate. The version the rep wants to talk to.
2. **The Emotional Buyer** — feels fear, excitement, trust, distrust. Fast. Mostly silent. Drives the actual choice.
3. **The Tribal Buyer** — checks what their peers, boss, board, and reference points think. Asks "does this make me look smart or stupid in front of the people I care about?"

**Blount's hard rule:** the Rational Buyer almost never makes the decision. The Emotional Buyer makes it and the Rational Buyer rationalizes it. The rep who pitches features and ROI exclusively is talking to the wrong system.

**Operator move:** every deal needs at least one Emotional Buyer touch (storytelling, anchoring, future-pacing) and one Tribal Buyer touch (named reference, peer logo, social proof from someone the buyer actually respects). Without those, the Rational Buyer has no permission to say yes.

## Framework 4 — Disrupt the Pattern (Pattern Painting and Pattern Breaking)

Blount's behavioral lever for re-anchoring a stalled conversation. Buyers come into a sales interaction with a pre-loaded **pattern** — an expectation of how the rep will behave (pitch, brochure, push, close). When the rep matches the pattern, the buyer's defenses stay up. When the rep breaks it, the defenses drop for a few seconds, and that's when real conversation happens.

**Two moves:**
- **Pattern painting** — early in the call, name out loud the pattern the buyer expects ("you're probably waiting for me to launch into a demo right now"). The act of naming it disarms it.
- **Pattern breaking** — do the opposite of what the buyer expects. Ask a real question instead of pitching. Admit uncertainty instead of bluffing confidence. Slow down when the buyer expects you to speed up.

**The named failure mode:** the rep who has been trained in a script that *itself* is the pattern. Every buyer in the market has heard the same opener twice this week. Matching the pattern is the bug.

## Framework 5 — Emotional Contagion (You Set The Temperature)

Blount's principle: in any two-person interaction, **the more emotionally regulated person sets the emotional state for both.** The dysregulated person catches it from the regulated one, not the other way around. This is a measurable neurological effect — mirror neurons, vocal entrainment, micro-expression matching.

**Operator consequences:**
- If the rep walks into a call anxious, the buyer absorbs the anxiety and reads it as "this rep is hiding something."
- If the rep walks into a call grounded, the buyer absorbs the calm and reads it as "this person knows what they're doing."
- The buyer's resistance, urgency, or trust level is largely a function of what the rep is broadcasting.

**The discipline:** before every important call, the rep regulates *first*. Box breathing, two minutes of silence, a physical reset. Not optional. The cost of skipping it is the call.

## Framework 6 — The Decision-Making Equation (Desire > Fear)

Blount's reduction of buyer behavior to a single inequality. A buyer says yes when:

**Desire to change > Fear of change + Inertia**

Every variable in that equation is emotional, not rational. The rep's job is to move both sides:
- **Raise desire** — vivid future-pacing of the outcome, stakes-painting around the cost of staying still, identity language ("companies like yours that get this right look like X").
- **Lower fear** — small commitments, named risk-reducers, the explicit naming of what could go wrong and how it gets handled, references to peers who survived the transition.
- **Break inertia** — anchor a specific next step at a specific time with a specific person. Inertia is defeated by friction, not by enthusiasm.

**Operator diagnostic:** when a deal stalls, ask which side of the equation is failing. "Wants to do it but won't pull the trigger" = fear too high. "Likes us but isn't urgent" = desire too low. "Agreed in principle but won't schedule" = inertia winning. Each one is a different intervention.

## Framework 7 — The Acknowledge–Connect–Pivot Sequence

Blount's micro-pattern for handling emotional friction inside the call without breaking rapport. Three beats. Total time: 10-15 seconds.

1. **Acknowledge** — name the buyer's emotional state out loud, without judgment. "Sounds like timing's a real concern right now." Not a question. A statement.
2. **Connect** — link to a similar experience, either yours or a named third party. "We see that a lot — the firms that hesitated at the same point last year usually had X going on." Builds the tribal bridge.
3. **Pivot** — redirect the conversation to a question the rep wants answered. "Can I ask what would need to be true for the timing to work?"

**Why the sequence works:** acknowledging defuses the emotion (it stops compounding), connecting normalizes it (the buyer isn't alone in the feeling), and pivoting moves the conversation forward without trampling on the emotion.

**The named failure mode:** the rep who skips acknowledge and goes straight to pivot. The buyer feels dismissed, the emotion grows, the deal slows.

## Framework 8 — Outcome / Process / Power (The OPP Filter)

Blount's three-layer qualification frame for whether a deal is real. Most reps qualify on Outcome and call it done. Real Ultra High Performers qualify on all three.

- **Outcome** — does the buyer want the result we deliver? (Most discovery calls answer only this.)
- **Process** — does the buyer have a workable internal process to actually make the decision? Who signs, who blocks, what the steps are, on what timeline.
- **Power** — does the buyer in front of me have the political capital to drive the decision through their organization, or are they a champion without authority?

**The named bug:** the rep who has a great Outcome conversation with a champion who has no Power, runs an unmapped Process, and is stunned when the deal disappears at the legal-review stage. The rep was qualified on the easy dimension only.

---

## Framework 9 — The Influence Triangle (Likeability / Trust / Authority)

Blount's three-leg structure for how a rep earns the right to influence a buyer's decision. The triangle is multiplicative — if any leg is zero, the other two don't matter.

- **Likeability** — the buyer enjoys interacting with you. Mirrored cadence, genuine curiosity, no slimy energy. Necessary but not sufficient — likeable reps without trust or authority get ghosted politely.
- **Trust** — the buyer believes you'll do what you say, including telling them when your product isn't the right fit. Built by promises kept across micro-commitments (callback Tuesday at 2 means callback Tuesday at 2).
- **Authority** — the buyer believes you know things they don't. Demonstrated by asking sharper questions than the buyer expects, naming patterns they haven't named themselves, and being willing to disagree with them when warranted.

**Operator move:** the rep who wants to raise their close rate one notch should look at which leg of the triangle is shortest. Most reps over-index on Likeability and under-index on Authority. The fix is to ask harder questions and stop nodding.

## Framework 10 — The Ultra High Performer Profile (UHP Traits)

Blount's composite of what Ultra High Performers consistently share. Not a personality test — a behavioral set the median rep can train into.

- **Empathy under pressure.** Stay curious when the buyer pushes back, instead of defensive.
- **Disciplined optimism.** Treat every no as data, not as identity.
- **Outcome obsession over activity comfort.** Pick the hard task that moves the deal over the easy task that fills the day.
- **Long-game patience.** Willing to invest 4-6 months in a complex deal without short-circuiting.
- **Mental toughness.** Sit in discomfort — silence, rejection, ambiguity — without scrambling to fill it.
- **Curiosity scaling.** Ask one more question than the buyer expects, every single call.

**Operator use:** UHP traits are the coaching target for senior reps. Median reps need Blount's *Fanatical Prospecting* discipline first. UHP traits become the next ceiling once activity is solved.

## How to use this file inside the /sales skill

1. When the problem is about a deal stalling for emotional reasons (champion ghosting, price pushback that doesn't track to actual value gap, a "great call" that goes nowhere) — pull from this file.
2. When the rep is hitting activity but not converting — the gap is almost always EQ, not script. Diagnose against the Four Pillars (Framework 2) and the Influence Triangle (Framework 9).
3. Sales EQ sits *between* /sales (Blount on prospecting) and /negotiation-voss (Voss on tactical empathy). When the problem is mid-deal emotional management, Blount's Acknowledge-Connect-Pivot (Framework 7) is the bridge.
