## Framework 1 — The 5 P's (Key Person of Influence, core frame)

**What it is:** The five-step sequential path from invisible to key person of influence in any field.

**Rungs (in order — do not skip):**
1. **Pitch** — clarity. A single compelling sentence describing what you do, for whom, and what shifts. If your pitch is fuzzy, every downstream step amplifies confusion.
2. **Publish** — proof. A book, signature talk, white paper, or flagship piece of public work that demonstrates depth and is searchable for years.
3. **Product** — leverage. Productized offerings that decouple revenue from hours. The ascending offer ladder belongs here.
4. **Profile** — distribution. Third-party validation through podcasts, stages, press, partnerships. Your name appearing in front of audiences you didn't build.
5. **Partnerships** — compounding. Other influential people send you business because association with you raises THEIR status. This rung only opens once the prior four are real.

**Diagnostic principle:** find the lowest broken rung. Fix that one. Most founders try to fix Profile (visibility) when Pitch (clarity) is the broken rung — that's why their visibility campaigns underperform.

**Failure mode:** trying to "build a personal brand" without a Pitch and a Publish. Brand without depth equals noise.

## Framework 2 — The 5-Stage Business Ladder (Entrepreneur Revolution)

**What it is:** Priestley's classification of where a business sits on the path from idea to mature multi-asset portfolio.

**Stages:**
1. **Wantrapreneur** — idea exists, no income. Mostly emotional, not yet a business.
2. **Self-employed** — income exists, but it's tied to the founder's hours. Owner-dependent. Capped.
3. **Key Person of Influence** — the founder is the recognized authority in a niche. Attention is scalable, pricing is premium, demand exceeds supply for their specific offer.
4. **Performer (small giant)** — the business is leveraged through assets. 7-8 figure profit on lean headcount. Oversubscribed by default. The founder can step away from delivery without revenue collapsing.
5. **Vitruvian Entrepreneur** — multi-asset portfolio. Capable of launching new ventures inside the existing system. The founder is now a builder of builders.

**Diagnostic principle:** the move from Stage 2 to Stage 3 is authority + clarity. The move from Stage 3 to Stage 4 is assets + ascending offer + campaigns. The move from Stage 4 to Stage 5 is repeatability.

## Framework 3 — Oversubscribed Method: Create The Gap

**What it is:** The engineering of a positive demand-minus-supply gap so that you choose customers, not the other way around.

**The Gap formula:**
> Gap = Demand − Supply.
> Positive gap = oversubscribed (you select, you price freely, you don't discount).
> Negative gap = discounting (you chase, you cut, you compete on price).

**Four-step engineering protocol:**
1. **Choose a market with built-in demand or visible intent.** Don't manufacture demand from zero — concentrate demand that already exists.
2. **Build the campaign.** A specific window with start and end dates, a specific offer, a specific audience. Reject always-available.
3. **Tell the story repeatedly during the campaign window.** Build-up, peak, scarcity, close. The story is the campaign; the campaign isn't an ad.
4. **Cap supply BEFORE the launch.** The cap must be real and honoured. The cap creates the gap.

**Founder failure mode:** fearing the cap. Founders raise the cap as soon as it sells through, destroying the gap and turning the campaign back into always-on.

## Framework 4 — The Campaign-Driven Enterprise (Oversubscribed)

**What it is:** Replacing always-on marketing with three to four major campaigns per year.

**Three-phase cycle, repeated:**
- **Pre-campaign (4-6 weeks):** content, partnerships, list-building, signal-of-intent events, a free gift. Demand is concentrated, not yet released.
- **Campaign window (1-2 weeks):** the offer is live. Scarcity is real. Energy is focused. Sales and delivery are temporarily on the same axis.
- **Post-campaign (2-3 weeks):** deliver, capture case studies, generate social proof, feed the next campaign's pre-phase.

**Compared to always-on:**
- Same revenue, a fraction of team-energy expenditure.
- Compounding asset stack — every campaign leaves behind content, case studies, partnerships.
- Customer quality improves because the cap forces selection.

**Where DR uses it:** quarterly campaigns per niche (immigration, franchise, vocational) replacing the current always-on outbound model. Each campaign anchored by a NO BS publish moment + free audit gift.

## Framework 5 — The 24 Assets Matrix (24 Assets, core frame)

**What it is:** An 8-column × 3-row matrix of 24 leverageable business assets. The alternative to scaling through headcount.

**Columns (the eight asset categories):**
1. **Brand**
2. **Marketing**
3. **Sales**
4. **Intellectual Property (IP)**
5. **Product**
6. **Operations / Systems**
7. **Financial**
8. **Team / Culture**

**Rows (the three asset altitudes):**
- **Public** — market-facing, visible. Book, signature talk, podcast, branded content, brand identity.
- **Private** — internal IP not shown to the market. Methodology documents, frameworks, proprietary data, internal playbooks, financial models.
- **Productized** — repeatable delivery without founder presence. Productized service, SaaS, course, certification, license, fulfilment SOP.

**Diagnostic protocol:** walk the 24 boxes. Mark each as filled / partial / empty. The empty boxes ARE the strategy. Most businesses fill 4-8 boxes — usually concentrated in Public/Brand and Public/Marketing, occasionally Public/Product. Leverage lives in the Private and Productized rows.

**Where DR's gaps are likely:**
- Public/Marketing — NO BS Ads not yet at KPI scale (asset exists, not yet leveraged).
- Private/IP — Case Flow methodology under-documented.
- Productized/Product — productized service still bespoke per client.

## Framework 6 — Ascending Transaction Model (24 Assets)

**What it is:** A three-altitude offer ladder. Every business should have all three altitudes.

**The three altitudes:**
1. **Gift** — free. A genuinely valuable asset that the market would pay for, given away to build the list and surface intent. Must NOT be a content tease.
2. **Core** — paid entry. Typically $100-$1,000. Low friction. Designed to convert gift consumers into paying customers and into ascending tier candidates.
3. **Ascending** — premium tiers from $5K to $250K+. The economics live here. The ascending tier converts your best buyers into your highest-leverage customers.

**Failure modes:**
- Gift but no core → list grows, no revenue.
- Core but no ascending → revenue ceiling capped at "core × repurchase rate".
- Ascending but no gift → high friction acquisition, hard to scale demand.

**DR application:** Gift = free audit + NO BS content. Core = paid audit / Lead Qual diagnostic. Ascending = Case Flow installation + ongoing management retainer.

## Framework 7 — The Vitruvian Entrepreneur (Entrepreneur Revolution / KPI)

**What it is:** Priestley's "complete entrepreneur" archetype — eight skills, named after the Vitruvian Man's eight-pointed proportions.

**The eight skills:**
1. **Creator** — invents the product, the IP, the methodology.
2. **Performer** — public-facing communicator. Talks, content, on-camera.
3. **Producer** — turns ideas into shipped artifacts. Project completion.
4. **Promoter** — markets and sells the visible offer.
5. **Connector** — relationships, partnerships, networks.
6. **Salesperson** — closes deals one-to-one.
7. **Negotiator** — protects the gap, structures terms.
8. **Steward** — financial, legal, operational continuity.

**Diagnostic principle:** the question isn't "which am I best at" — it's "which is the bottleneck right now". Hire or partner for the bottleneck, develop strategically the ones core to your stage.

## Framework 8 — Small Giant (24 Assets)

**What it is:** Priestley's anti-unicorn frame. A small giant is a 7-8 figure business with single-digit or low-double-digit headcount, leveraged assets, premium pricing, a key person of influence at the helm, and oversubscription as the default state.

**Characteristics:**
- Profit per employee in the high six figures.
- Founder-led, but not founder-dependent for delivery.
- 24-asset matrix substantially filled (especially the Productized row).
- Campaign-driven, not always-on.
- Selects clients rather than chasing them.

**Why it matters:** the small giant is the actual goal of the 24-asset architecture. Building 24 assets is not academic — it's what makes a small giant possible. The opposite of the funded-startup playbook (raise capital, hire fast, burn for growth, exit or die).

## Framework 9 — The Lighthouse Effect (KPI / Oversubscribed)

**What it is:** The principle that a clear, repeated signal attracts the right audience more efficiently than chasing the audience.

**Mechanism:**
1. Stand for something specific — even something unpopular.
2. Make the signal visible and consistent across all five P's.
3. Repeat it until the market sorts itself around the signal.
4. Resist the urge to broaden when growth is slow. A lighthouse doesn't get more powerful by spinning faster.

**Use:** the lighthouse is the foundation of the KPI rung. Without a clear position, the rest of the 5 P's amplify noise.

---

## Framework 10 — The Entrepreneur Seasons (Entrepreneur Revolution)

**What it is:** Priestley's seasonality frame for the operator's year. Most operators run flat-out year-round. Priestley installs four seasons:

- **Spring (growth)** — launch, hire, expand visible activity.
- **Summer (harvest)** — deliver at peak, monetize, capture proof.
- **Autumn (reflect)** — review numbers, audit assets, sunset what doesn't compound.
- **Winter (build)** — strategy, IP development, new asset construction.

**Use:** maps cleanly onto Campaign-Driven Enterprise — campaigns sit in Spring/Summer, asset-building sits in Autumn/Winter. The founder's energy follows the seasons. Burnout comes from running summer all year.
