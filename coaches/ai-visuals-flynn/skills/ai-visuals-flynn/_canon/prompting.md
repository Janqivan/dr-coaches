## PART 1. Prompting: structure, vocabulary and exact recipes

### Rules (ranked by how often they say it)
- **Break every image into building blocks and prompt each one; any block you skip, the model invents anyway.** Non-negotiables: shot type, subject and action, environment, color scheme, camera/lens/film, composition, mood, lighting, textures, details. The goal is control, not a longer prompt. [YT:PJ347ccVJBs, YT:jBcL-XrHqzY, YT:QLbjv3CbfIo, YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (n.d., 2024-10, 2025-10) · mentions 17
- **Negatives and constraints weigh as much as positives, and placement matters.** In Midjourney use the `--no` parameter (his standing `--no tattoos, jewelry`); naming the unwanted thing in the prompt text tends to include it (Fast Hours). In system prompts that expand one idea into ~50 prompts, list what the output must not be (not centered, not shot straight on). A student's upside-down diver only worked in Midjourney with negative prompting; Nano Banana did it in one click. [YT:u7EI0Z-0ULE, YT:AOuXCRzjsS0, YT:AxV8TR3BdqI] (2024-10, 2026-02, n.d.) · mentions 15
- **Use an LLM as vocabulary and category partner, then test every term in the generator.** Ask for lists (about 20 motion effects, 25 lighting techniques, 50 cameras or film stocks), ask why 100 mm vs 50 mm, ask for genre-specific categories. [YT:Mg9oeq5T5Lg, YT:MpcDWmhzNb0, YT:k8rciHNdlvs] (n.d., 2025-10, 2024-10) · mentions 15
- **Camera, lens and film words are the cheapest steering wheel.** Cameras carry visual signatures (iPhone vs Polaroid), so name one. When the frame reads wrong, swap one variable instead of rerolling (8 mm too wide, try 85 mm). His default realism token is `35 mm`. [YT:26bjN6CwwJs, YT:_nT6fav4qFA, YT:jP9vWmf0TG4, YT:QLbjv3CbfIo] (2025-10, 2024-10) · mentions 14
- **A system prompt is the standing brief; for every failure mode, write a rule around it.** Skeleton: Act as, Goal, Output, Core focus, Format, Limits (Limits is his most important). Build it by running outputs repeatedly and adding a rule per failure; what not to do matters as much as what to do. [YT:PJ347ccVJBs, YT:AxV8TR3BdqI, YT:N-tmQ_Can_o] (n.d., 2026-09) · mentions 13
- **Tokens are categories that pull from tagged training images; lead with a holding category.** Each token retrieves from its tag set and the results get mashed together; editorial, documentary, fashion, product and cinematic still look radically different, and photographic tokens (35 mm, film grain) steer into the photoreal pool. Tokens are context-specific: test per prompt. [YT:NmIcjfPNcsE, YT:_nT6fav4qFA, YT:JskVAZqt34w] (2025-10, 2024-10) · mentions 12
- **Word order is weight: whatever goes first dominates, so reorder before you rewrite.** Midjourney reads left to right (no documentation; he found it by running prompts). Lead with the holding category, with lighting for wild light, or with motion blur when the image goes to a video tool. [YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:dcrhhIKNaRQ, YT:NmIcjfPNcsE, YT:swamNhl1pIs] (n.d., 2025-10) · mentions 11
- **Aspect ratio is a prompt element: it changes content, not just crop.** A 2:1 face close-up gives eyes and nose; low or high angles fight 4:5; he starts at 5:4 or 3:2 (Drew starts people at 4:5) and he and Drew prefer 7:3 to 16:9 for a less symmetrical frame. If a prompt will not deliver, check the ratio before rewriting text. [YT:u7EI0Z-0ULE, YT:SQIGjY_Rs2s, YT:NmIcjfPNcsE] (2024-10, 2025-12, 2025-10) · mentions 11
- **Test one token alone in a very simple prompt, then stack; use permutations; re-test per prompt and per version.** Do not drop a token after one try: vary hierarchy, multi-prompt weight, stylize, personalization on or off, ratio (Fast Hours). Tokens drift between versions. On 8.1 he ran stylize 100 to 1000 and saw little difference. [YT:JskVAZqt34w, YT:EaENLPTAt2E, YT:NmIcjfPNcsE, YT:FlnyD8Eqabk] (2025-10, 2025-12, 2026-05) · mentions 11
- **When an action or pose fails, describe the mechanics as to someone who cannot see.** Bottle in the right hand, held parallel to the face, lips on the bottle; cradling a baby in his right arm, looking down at the baby; a selfie is a person holding a phone over their head or at eye level, looking at the phone. Pair an emotion word with its mechanism (`visceral` plus screaming mouth open). [YT:QLbjv3CbfIo, YT:JskVAZqt34w, YT:YD5V0hKlRew] (2024-10, 2025-10, n.d.) · mentions 10
- **Prompt the face: expression, mouth, eye direction and one distinct feature, or you get the blank "model face".** Be hyperbolic or mechanical. He thinks the fashion photography Midjourney pulls from is about clothes, not faces. The face is an open canvas (eyes closed, a scar on the left cheek). [YT:JskVAZqt34w, YT:26bjN6CwwJs, YT:QLbjv3CbfIo] (2025-10, 2024-10) · mentions 10
- **A reference carries what words cannot: stack it with text, and have an LLM turn it into a prompt.** Both hosts: a wireframe teaches ChatGPT layout; co-host Drew Brucker says an image reference works about 10x better than describing the layout. Rory expects less giant-script prompting, more "use this exact thing". [YT:F6P2EAfAwjw, YT:ZY5GqIXkET4, YT:Rzr8TDN_0y8] (2025-10, 2026-06) · mentions 10
- **Isolate each variable in its own slot so you know exactly what to swap.** Dislike the lighting: change only the lighting category ("find, execute, iterate"); turn red and white into blue and green without redoing the prompt. [YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:MpcDWmhzNb0, YT:jBcL-XrHqzY, YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (n.d., 2025-10) · mentions 9
- **Name the model's default composition and reject it: centered, front-on, looking at camera.** Default test: draw an X through the frame and the subject sits at the vanishing point. Co-host Drew Brucker: name no camera angle and you get the symmetrical frontal default. [YT:AOuXCRzjsS0, YT:AxV8TR3BdqI, YT:SQIGjY_Rs2s, YT:QLbjv3CbfIo] (2026-02, n.d., 2025-12, 2024-10) · mentions 9
- **Short, punchy keyword phrases first; add only what is missing.** No full sentences at the start (each word is a token; paragraphs bring words with 3 or 4 meanings), comma-separated, boiled down like a pot of water, then add water. His preferred shape: lots of detail in short, concise phrases. [YT:ssxPDDjSVY0, YT:jBcL-XrHqzY, YT:k8rciHNdlvs] (n.d., 2024-10) · mentions 8
- **Clear and direct in, clear and direct out; ambiguity hands Midjourney creative liberty.** Teaching exercise: `a woman in the park` gives everyone a different picture; add a cold night, cozy clothes, one spotlight and the pictures converge. Detail beats brevity: `photo of a juicy hamburger` is stock, adding `juicy beef, oozing cheese, bold and satisfying` gives a magazine look. [YT:ssxPDDjSVY0, YT:dcrhhIKNaRQ, YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (n.d., 2025-10) · mentions 8
- **Swap weak words for powerful, specific ones; `intricate` is his most-used booster.** `woman with tattoos` is generic, `woman with intricate tattoos` changes the whole image; enormous over big, vibrant over colorful; a specific adverb (`lavishly dancing`) juices motion. He always adds `intricate details`. [YT:dfjMjg_Cy7M, YT:JskVAZqt34w, YT:swamNhl1pIs, YT:Dl64zUCJniU] (n.d., 2025-10, 2026-05) · mentions 8
- **Brief an LLM like a junior employee: context, examples, then `ask me questions`.** The model is literal and will not tell you context is missing. Interview-me works: ChatGPT as a creative director interviewing a client (his frame: unique person, unique place, unique position or profession). [YT:E41H2WIHMXg, YT:2H0AluIDW50, YT:k8rciHNdlvs] (n.d., 2024-10) · mentions 7
- **Models are optimized for writing, not for thinking: `write a LinkedIn post` triggers the trained pattern.** Route the same idea through another genre (editorial opinion article) and inject your own POV. [YT:xLLtYn932qY] (2026-08) · mentions 3

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Photo formula (visual building blocks)
- When they use it: any image prompt; he says this one formula "basically started an entire business".
- Steps: 1. Pick blocks: photo type, subject and action, environment, color scheme, camera/lens/film, mood, lighting, composition, textures, details. 2. Write them in that order as short comma-separated phrases, parameters last. 3. Fill by hand, or have ChatGPT fill them Mad Libs-style (ask for 10 or 50). 4. Swap one block per run; not every block every time.
- Sources: [YT:dcrhhIKNaRQ, YT:PJ347ccVJBs, YT:ssxPDDjSVY0, YT:jBcL-XrHqzY] (n.d.)

#### Granular V6 formula
- When they use it: V6 follows very long prompts and he wants every aspect controlled.
- Steps: fields in order: photo type; subject; subject position (lying down, facing left or right); subject focus (foreground, mid, background, in or out of focus); exact clothing; environment; environment focus (blurred or crisp); environment description; emotion; lighting; tone; time of day; textures; image type.
- Their example: `satisfied Brazilian woman laying in bed with a cigarette exhaling facing right`, medium shot, editorial, sharp focus, oversized blue shirt, headband, lipstick, even light interacting with smoke.
- Sources: [YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:jP9vWmf0TG4] (n.d., 2025-10)

#### Asset hacking: describe, extract, rebuild
- When they use it: many on-brand images from one asset, with no dependence on client assets.
- Steps: 1. Run `/describe` on the asset; alone it read like a video game ("call BS", do not stop there). 2. Paste the image into ChatGPT with the extraction prompt (Recipe library). 3. Blend with the closest /describe prompt. 4. Image-prompt the original plus that prompt (higher image weight when realism matters). 5. Lock color, lens, film, lighting, composition; change only 1 to 3 blocks (photo type, subject, environment).
- Their example: one Red Bull race-car image became a motorcycle, dirt bike and skier.
- Sources: [YT:dcrhhIKNaRQ, YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:2H0AluIDW50] (n.d.)

#### Three-structure test
- When they use it: new model version or subject; deciding how long to write (his v5.2 testing).
- Steps: 1. Ask ChatGPT for three versions of each idea: (1) high-level generic keywords, short and punchy; (2) lots of detail in short, concise phrases (his preference); (3) a super long narrative. 2. Run all three. 3. Keep the one with the most control.
- Sources: [YT:k8rciHNdlvs] (2024-10)

#### Learn a medium's structure (category builder)
- When they use it: unfamiliar genre, or client work needing specifics; only worth it when you want a lot of control.
- Steps: 1. Ask the LLM how you would teach the medium. 2. Ask what an art director could control in that genre (food: plating, garnish, props; real estate: architecture type, structure, textures, design style; fashion: jewelry, fabric type and texture). 3. For an unfamiliar part ask for typical options and test each term. 4. Slot the categories beside the standard ones, let the LLM fill them, swap one piece (parsley for dill).
- Their example: living room inspired by Cappadocia caves: circular layout around a central fireplace, hand-woven rugs, low wooden tables, plush cushions.
- Sources: [YT:E41H2WIHMXg, YT:MpcDWmhzNb0, YT:k8rciHNdlvs] (n.d., 2025-10, 2024-10)

#### System prompt skeleton, built by failures
- When they use it: anything run more than once; fanning one idea out to 10 prompts.
- Steps: 1. Six categories (add or drop): Act as; Goal; Output; Core focus; Format; Limits. 2. Get good repeatable output by hand first, then ask what inputs it needs, then turn your prompting into the formula. 3. Test "a million times", keep the good examples, feed them back to ask for replication, fold into the prompt. 4. Add a rule for every failure (asymmetry rule, aesthetic lock, imperfection level). 5. Put the changing part in a variable.
- Sources: [YT:PJ347ccVJBs, YT:AxV8TR3BdqI, YT:N-tmQ_Can_o] (n.d., 2026-09)

#### Start small, then expand
- When they use it: any new image prompt; video prompts.
- Steps: 1. Subject, action, environment (the story) only. 2. Add lighting, color scheme, post-production hues, contrast. 3. If it fails, add granular direction (position, foreground, midground, background, light direction). 4. Video: short, punchy, visceral first; expand only on failure and become "overly communicative".
- Their example: feet walking away came back walking backward until he wrote the character walks forward and we shoot from behind.
- Sources: [YT:QLbjv3CbfIo, YT:jBcL-XrHqzY, YT:YD5V0hKlRew] (2024-10, n.d.)

#### Reverse-engineer why it works
- When they use it: you want a reusable pattern, not a one-off.
- Steps: 1. Take something that works (a joke, a viral quote). 2. Have Claude analyze why it works and write out the pattern. 3. Reuse the pattern on new topics.
- Their example: a Knicks player's press-conference line gave the pattern: common business or personal topic, reframed through a funny physical metaphor, ending on a practical truth.
- Sources: [YT:IppsMX-LqDk] (2026-06)

#### Token test loop (Fast Hours) and prompt elevator
- When they use it: new vocabulary (expressions, color or effect tokens); beginners.
- Steps: 1. Ask an LLM for a batch. 2. Test each in a very simple prompt, or bare. 3. Stack (prismatic rainbow light streaks with cinematic fashion). 4. Fold into a longer prompt only once it works. Beginner version (Rory, "prompt elevator"): copy a prompt from Explore, change one thing (the color), then build car, car in a village, with a fire in the background.
- Sources: [YT:EaENLPTAt2E, YT:2sY91XsiRuw, YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (2025-12, 2025-10)

### Fix library (problem → fix → why)
- **Problem:** a decent prompt makes the wrong element the star (car vs city) → **Fix:** move the element you want to star to the front; reorder before rewriting → **Why:** the first term is weighted most. [YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** soft, plastic look → **Fix:** add texture words (he thinks "we probably don't prompt for enough") → **Why:** texture is a core lever of his look. [YT:PJ347ccVJBs, YT:wlsmAeMQNEM] (n.d., 2024-10)
- **Problem:** mixed personalization profile gives artsy results to a detailed product prompt → **Fix:** put `photo` first (or editorial or fashion photography), or keep one profile per style → **Why:** the profile was trained on realism and other styles. [YT:FlnyD8Eqabk] (2026-05)
- **Problem:** bare `cinematic still` comes out saturated, very warm, intensely lit → **Fix:** add a camera or film stock (`35 mm` or `16 mm`) → **Why:** it balances the look. [YT:NmIcjfPNcsE] (2025-10)
- **Problem:** subject stares into the camera, or a simple action fails (bottle in the eye) → **Fix:** remove interpretation and give direction: bottle in his right hand, parallel to his face, lips on it; cradling a baby in his right arm, looking down at the baby. Fast Hours adds `documentary`, `photojournalism` or `street photography` as a candid mode switch → **Why:** Midjourney loves the camera, and granular beats generic. [YT:QLbjv3CbfIo] (2024-10)
- **Problem:** blank, perfect "model face" → **Fix:** `everyday` plus the role, `natural look`, `natural skin texture`, `blemishes`, negative `no makeup` or `no airbrushing`, or a mood board of non-model iPhone photos; co-host Drew Brucker: stylize S40 to S80, `--weird 1`, name the photo source → **Why:** the fashion data is about clothes; per Drew `--chaos` will not fix it. [YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (2025-10)
- **Problem:** result too polished for a period look → **Fix:** `documentary photography` or amateur photography, film grain, light leaks, decade and year, stylize about 70 to 75, lower `--q` (0.5 untested); promote the winning image to sref, then a board → **Why:** editorial and cinematic push perfection; high stylize loses fidelity. [YT:26bjN6CwwJs] (2025-10)
- **Problem:** a reference overpowers the text (live-action Charmander turned cartoon) → **Fix:** image weight about 0.5 to 0.8, style weight 150 to 200, live-action wording in the text, `--no illustration` or `--no cartoon` → **Why:** image weight is the first lever. [YT:Fa79Iv3QFJk] (2024-10)
- **Problem:** video subject walks backward or the wrong way → **Fix:** state it literally (the character walks forward and we shoot from behind) → **Why:** everything is tokens; fluff pulls random memory. [YT:YD5V0hKlRew] (n.d.)
- **Problem:** auto-written close-up prompts mention shoes and become full-body shots → **Fix:** if-then rule: a close-up prompt mentions only visible parts (shirt, hat) → **Why:** surgical precision, fewer iterations (Fast Hours; Rory calls if-then his unlock). Opposite case: to force a full-body shot, mention the shoes (Fast Hours). [YT:RjGYZlVywd8, YT:O9g2mwlwQcQ] (2025-12, 2025-10)
- **Problem:** Midjourney does not know an object, or won't give a true night look (golden-hour default) → **Fix:** have the LLM list other names used globally (a pump display became a billboard until `digital sign`); for night repeat two or three synonyms, weight a multi-prompt, spell out the light logic (only the flash lights the environment) (Fast Hours) → **Why:** defaults are strong. [YT:MpcDWmhzNb0, YT:VKANULbm2ns] (2025-10)
