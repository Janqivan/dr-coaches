# The Emotional Triggers Taxonomy — ~223 Triggers, Organized

Talia Wolf's research at Conversioner and GetUplift documented roughly 223
distinct emotional triggers across consumer and B2B buying decisions. The full
list lives inside the Emotion Sells Masterclass worksheet; this document
captures the structural taxonomy — the clusters those triggers fall into — and
maps the dominant triggers to Ivan's three DR verticals.

The taxonomy matters because most marketers reach for the same 3-4 obvious
triggers (fear, scarcity, urgency, social proof) and ignore the 200+ others.
Wolf's work shows the leverage often hides in the less-obvious clusters —
self-image, social image, belonging, certainty, transformation — especially in
considered B2B and high-stakes consumer purchases.

---

## THE TWO DOMINANT CLUSTERS (Wolf's "focus here first" guidance)

Wolf's repeated public guidance: when in doubt, start with these two. They
drive the majority of B2B and considered-purchase decisions.

### Cluster 1 — SELF-IMAGE
*How the customer wants to feel about themselves after the purchase.*

The customer is not buying the product. They are buying a future version of
themselves they prefer. Triggers in this cluster include:

- **Pride** — "I made the right call. I figured this out."
- **Competence** — "I'm smart. I'm capable. I know what I'm doing."
- **Control** — "I'm in charge of my situation now."
- **Progress** — "I'm moving forward. I'm not stuck."
- **Worthiness** — "I deserve this. I'm worth this."
- **Strength** — "I'm not weak. I can handle this."
- **Identity shift** — "I'm a different/better person now."
- **Achievement** — "I accomplished something."
- **Confidence** — "I can walk into the room knowing this is handled."
- **Self-respect** — "I'm someone who takes care of themselves / their family."

This cluster is the strongest for: B2B SaaS, professional services,
education, franchise ownership, self-development, financial products.

### Cluster 2 — SOCIAL IMAGE
*How the customer wants to be perceived by others after the purchase.*

- **Status** — "People see I've made it."
- **Authority** — "I'm the go-to person on this."
- **Respect** — "Colleagues / family / peers respect me more."
- **Belonging to a tier** — "I'm in the room with the people I want to be in
  the room with."
- **Being seen as smart** — "Others think I'm thoughtful, strategic."
- **Being seen as a good provider** — "My family knows I take care of them."
- **Being seen as successful** — "People who knew me before see I've made it."
- **Being seen as a leader** — "My team / peers look to me."
- **Being seen as on-trend** — "I'm not falling behind."
- **Being seen as ethical / responsible** — "People know I do things the
  right way."

This cluster is the strongest for: luxury, B2B status purchases, franchise
ownership (escape-the-corporate narrative), professional certifications,
high-ticket consumer.

## THE OTHER MAJOR CLUSTERS

### Cluster 3 — TRUST
- Credibility, social proof, transparency, authenticity, reliability,
  honesty, accountability, third-party validation, brand familiarity,
  reputation, longevity, expertise signals.
- *Trigger tactics:* customer logos, star ratings, testimonials, press
  mentions, case studies with numbers, transparent pricing, founder story,
  team faces, accreditations, awards, guarantees, money-back, contract-free.

### Cluster 4 — LEADERSHIP / AUTHORITY
- Expertise, mastery, "the only one who can," market leader, definitive
  source, original thinker, category creator, recognized authority.
- *Trigger tactics:* original research, named frameworks, books, speaking,
  media appearances, "we wrote the playbook," "the official ___ partner."

### Cluster 5 — FOMO / FEAR OF MISSING OUT
- Missing personal growth, missing a transformation, missing a window,
  missing a price, missing a community, missing a chance to escape current
  situation, missing what peers are doing.
- *Trigger tactics:* deadlines, cohort starts, limited spots, "X people
  enrolled this month," "the last time we ran this we filled in 48 hours."

### Cluster 6 — BELONGING
- Community, tribe, "people like me," shared identity, in-group, validation
  by similar others, not-alone-in-this, found-my-people.
- *Trigger tactics:* alumni networks, peer communities, group programs,
  customer Slack/forum, "you're not the only one going through this,"
  user-generated content showing peer faces.

### Cluster 7 — CERTAINTY / SAFETY / SECURITY
- Predictable outcomes, removed risk, guaranteed result, proven system,
  step-by-step path, can't-fail framing, financial safety, relationship
  safety, family safety, legal safety.
- *Trigger tactics:* money-back guarantees, "in X cases out of Y we
  delivered Z," proven processes, step counts, transparency, escrow,
  contracts that protect the buyer.

### Cluster 8 — TRANSFORMATION
- Becoming a new person, new identity, life chapter, before/after,
  metamorphosis, "the day you stop being ___ and start being ___."
- *Trigger tactics:* before/after stories, identity-shift testimonials,
  graduation narratives, "I was a X. Now I'm a Y."

### Cluster 9 — HOPE / OPTIMISM / AMBITION
- A better future is possible, this could be different, the upgrade is
  achievable, dreams are still on the table.
- *Trigger tactics:* aspirational visuals, future-state language, "imagine
  if," success stories from "people just like you."

### Cluster 10 — FEAR / LOSS AVERSION
- Losing money, losing time, losing reputation, losing health, losing
  family, losing freedom, losing opportunity, losing identity.
- *Trigger tactics:* cost-of-inaction calculators, "what happens if you
  don't fix this," loss-framed testimonials, deadline pressure tied to
  consequence.
- Wolf's caution: fear is loud but fatigues fast. Use it to open, not to
  close. Pair with hope/outcome to convert.

### Cluster 11 — POWER / CONTROL
- Agency, autonomy, owning your time, owning your decisions, freedom from
  a boss / system / dependency, being able to say no.
- *Trigger tactics:* "fire your boss," "own your schedule," "your rules,"
  "stop asking for permission."

### Cluster 12 — RELIEF
- The exhaustion ends, the worry stops, the chaos calms, "finally."
- *Trigger tactics:* "stop spending your nights worrying about ___,"
  "finally a system that ___," exhale-language imagery.

### Cluster 13 — JOY / PLAYFULNESS / LIGHTNESS
- Fun, ease, enjoyment, delight, surprise, gratification.
- *Trigger tactics:* humor in copy, playful visuals, "this is actually
  enjoyable," gamified UX.

### Cluster 14 — CURIOSITY
- Wanting to know the answer, completing an open loop, learning a secret,
  insider knowledge.
- *Trigger tactics:* "the one thing X firms get wrong," "what nobody tells
  you about Y," curiosity-gap headlines.

### Cluster 15 — GUILT / OBLIGATION (use carefully)
- Not letting family down, doing right by employees, fulfilling a
  responsibility, "if not now, when."
- *Trigger tactics:* "your family is counting on this," "what does it cost
  THEM if you don't ___."
- Wolf's caution: guilt converts but erodes brand. Use sparingly and
  truthfully.

## DR VERTICAL MAPPING — Which Emotions Fire for Ivan's Three Markets

### IMMIGRATION LAW APPLICANTS

**Current-pain emotional state:**
- Fear (deportation, separation, denial)
- Powerlessness ("the system is bigger than me")
- Shame ("I'm being judged for wanting a better life")
- Anxiety (years of waiting, government opacity)
- Exhaustion (paperwork, English, bureaucracy)
- Isolation ("nobody in my circle has been through this")

**Desired-outcome emotional state:**
- Safety (family is safe, status is secure)
- Dignity (treated as a human, not a case number)
- Control ("someone is finally in my corner")
- Hope (the American dream is still on the table)
- Belonging (becoming part of a new home officially)
- Pride ("I did this for my family")

**Dominant clusters:** Fear → Hope, Certainty/Safety, Trust, Self-image
(dignity, pride), Belonging (family reunion narrative).

**Wolf's prescription:** Lead with pain acknowledgement (clusters 7+10:
they need to feel SEEN before they'll trust the lawyer). Then bridge to
the desired outcome (dignity, family safety, control). Most immigration
law pages lead with feature lists (visa types, attorney credentials). The
winning page leads with: "You're not just filing a form. You're protecting
your family's future. We've helped 12,847 families do it." Emotion first,
trust second, feature third.

### FRANCHISE BUYERS (resales + first-time)

**Current-pain emotional state:**
- Trapped (corporate ceiling, bad boss, no equity)
- Resentment ("I built someone else's company")
- Restlessness ("there has to be more")
- Fear of starting from scratch / no proven path
- Self-doubt ("am I really capable of running a business")
- Pressure (family expectations, mid-career anxiety)

**Desired-outcome emotional state:**
- Self-image: be the boss, prove I could do it, build something mine
- Social image: successful entrepreneur, escaped the rat race
- Control / Power (cluster 11): own my time, own my decisions
- Certainty (cluster 7): a PROVEN system, not a wild bet
- Pride (provide for family on my own terms)
- Belonging (other franchisees, owner community)

**Dominant clusters:** Self-image, Social image, Power/Control, Certainty
(the franchise = proven system = de-risked entrepreneurship), Belonging
(franchisee community).

**Wolf's prescription:** Lead with desired outcome — the self-image
transformation. "The day you stop reporting to anyone else." Then bridge
with certainty signals (unit economics, ramp time, support system).
Feature-led franchise pages talk EBITDA and territory maps. Emotion-led
franchise pages sell the identity of business owner first, the unit
economics second.

### VOCATIONAL TRAINING ENROLLEES

**Current-pain emotional state:**
- Stuck (dead-end job, retail/warehouse/gig economy)
- Embarrassment (still living with parents, can't afford basics)
- Invisible ("nobody respects my work")
- Fear of debt / wasted time on a 4-year degree
- Anxiety about future (AI taking jobs, recession, automation)
- Identity gap ("I don't know what I am yet")

**Desired-outcome emotional state:**
- Transformation (cluster 8): "I'm a welder / electrician / HVAC tech"
- Belonging (cluster 6): tradespeople community, brotherhood/sisterhood
- Status (cluster 2): "skilled trade is respected and well-paid"
- Self-image: competence, pride in hands-on work, "I build things"
- Certainty: a job at the end, not a degree with no path
- Power/Control: high wages, mobility, can't be outsourced

**Dominant clusters:** Transformation, Belonging, Social-image (status),
Self-image (competence, pride), Certainty (job placement).

**Wolf's prescription:** Sell the IDENTITY, not the curriculum. The hero
shot is a graduate in work clothes, holding tools, on a job site —
confident, employed, respected. Headline: "Become a [trade] in 6 months."
Sub: "Skilled. Hired. Paid." Most vocational school pages sell the
curriculum (modules, hours, accreditation). The winning page sells the
new self.

## HOW TO PICK THE TRIGGER

Three rules:

1. **The customer's words pick the trigger, not your taste.** Mine the
   research transcripts for emotional language. The trigger that shows up
   most often in their words is your dominant trigger.

2. **Stage of awareness changes the trigger.** Unaware/problem-aware
   audiences need pain acknowledgement first (fear, relief, isolation).
   Solution-aware and product-aware audiences need desired-outcome triggers
   (self-image, social image, transformation).

3. **Vertical maturity changes the trigger.** In crowded verticals
   (immigration, franchise) where everyone uses the same 2-3 triggers,
   the white space is in the unused clusters. If every immigration firm
   uses fear, the firm that uses dignity wins.

---

## ANTI-PATTERNS

- **Defaulting to scarcity/urgency.** These are FOMO sub-triggers. They
  work, but they're overused and they fatigue. Don't make them your only
  move.
- **Defaulting to fear in non-crisis verticals.** Fear works for
  immigration, medical, debt. It backfires in B2B SaaS, franchise,
  professional services — buyers there want to feel smart, not scared.
- **Stacking triggers without coherence.** A page that yells fear, then
  pride, then urgency, then community feels chaotic. Pick a dominant
  trigger and a supporting one. Two clusters, not seven.
- **Picking a trigger without research.** If the trigger isn't validated
  by interview transcripts and survey data, it's a guess. Wolf does not
  guess.
