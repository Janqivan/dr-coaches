## PART A. Typography, color and layout

### Rules (ranked by how often he says it)
- **Settle the hierarchy first: one clear first read, then second and third** — "what's the most important thing we want to look at on this poster?" Resize until the answer is obvious (HELP over EXIT, title over credit, headliner biggest); the biggest contrast in value, size or texture wins. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:vomjcfFKHoE] (2017–2019) · mentions 23
- **Too many tricks: one good typeface, few sizes, few weights** — His most common diagnosis. He counts point sizes (five on the "it's time" poster) and restarts at one size, one weight; flourishes make it ugly unless you know what you are doing. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017–2018) · mentions 15
- **Anchor to a shared edge, axis or baseline; align clearly or clearly not** — "what is that aligning to?" Hunt near-misses. A shared left edge ties pieces together; any move cascades, so recheck the whole artboard. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:aPguPSY-e_4] (2017–2018) · mentions 14
- **Use the canvas but do not fill it; scale down for negative space** — Beginners jam type into corners: "That's not how you get good type." Oversized type cut the usable canvas by about a third. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017–2018) · mentions 14
- **Legibility beats cleverness and decoration** — Letters must read as their own name: barcode-style RACE, a bean in a G, a looped I and an S built from a figure all fail; condensed faces fail when counters close. [YT:4ZeEfF2O8-Q, YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:yKHc2or01Iw, YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2017–2018) · mentions 13
- **Strip first, add back one at a time** — Delete competing boxes, repeated copy, extra logos, borders and lines that neither anchor nor separate; shrink sponsor logos small at the bottom. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:yKHc2or01Iw] (2017–2018) · mentions 13
- **The typeface carries the meaning and the voice** — Building collapse needs a solid, bold, condensed geometric sans; too delicate felt foreign on Lufthansa; extra-bold goes balloon-like; a competitor's face (Avant Garde/Adidas for Reebok) embarrasses you. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:qIvhzoCHzpA, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017–2019) · mentions 13
- **Default to flush left and asymmetry; lead the eye along a lazy S** — Fully centered layouts feel old-fashioned; if centered, stay centered. Set elements large, mid, small along a path so the eye moves in and out of the frame. [YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:gx5hEcIwhnY] (2017–2018) · mentions 13
- **Group by proximity: one gap visibly smaller than the next; contain with rules** — Equal gaps above and below leave a block unclaimed; he halves the distance to about one-third / two-thirds. A thick 8 pt rule wraps scattered pieces into one framework. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:gx5hEcIwhnY] (2017–2018) · mentions 12
- **Contrast is the most important lever** — "contrast is always the most important thing to play with": size, weight, texture, empty against dense. Make one element smaller and one bigger; if a design lacks contrast, put it in the background. [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:ITcFFuKO2pU, YT:EQWsDA8D-54] (2017–2023) · mentions 11
- **Never stretch or squash type; horizontals stay thinner than verticals** — "please never ever ever ever stretch your type"; if you want condensed, pick a condensed face. A stretched horizontal measures thicker than the vertical. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ] (2017–2019) · mentions 11
- **The grid is a metronome, not magic** — "knowing where to put things makes it better"; which grid hardly matters. If you do not know what you are doing, start with one. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017–2018) · mentions 10
- **Color signals category, culture and price** — Blue on food reads clinical; green, white and red reads Italy, not tomato and basil; pastels are too soft for boxing; chemical look reads scientific, soft pastels natural. [YT:vomjcfFKHoE, YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:FDhK1R3GJzs] (2018–2019) · mentions 10
- **Classic, un-trendy faces from a short list; customize, do not decorate** — About 15 to 20 typefaces over 20 years, no dabbling in display faces; "nothing wrong with Helvetica"; techno faces like Bank Gothic date fast. [YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:CQMf71m3JOw, YT:ckSpE0Oh43c, YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2017–2018) · mentions 9
- **Space letters by the volume of negative space, not math or touch points** — Kern in trios, hardest pair first; math works only with strong verticals, round letters need the eye. [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:0Nh7h0JMOpk] (2017–2018) · mentions 9
- **Package as a six-sided sculpture; let the hero show** — Wrap borders and labels onto the next side; the shelf is a billboard; "when people cover things up they have something to hide". [YT:gx5hEcIwhnY, YT:vomjcfFKHoE, YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ] (2018–2019) · mentions 9
- **Lettering is consistent and real** — Stress sits in the same place on every letter and follows one pen angle (35°, 45°, flat, 90°). "If you're going to do something handwritten, handwrite it." [YT:V6e-rFgkZzI, YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:ckSpE0Oh43c] (2017–2019) · mentions 9
- **Test at the size it will live** — Half-inch square, 2 x 3.5 in card at about 9 pt, printed, zoomed out; lotion text looked fine on a 5K screen and was tiny in print. [YT:6ZAD6jxgGwk, YT:FsyJodhEze0, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:-JYiXyzDEFI] (2017–2019) · mentions 8
- **Pair for contrast, not similarity** — Serif with sans; a genuinely light word against a bold one; two sans in one logo must be the same face unless clearly different; never pair a bespoke wordmark with Helvetica. [YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:0Nh7h0JMOpk, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017–2018) · mentions 8
- **Type over color or photo needs value contrast** — A busy photo behind type kills legibility, so use textural backgrounds; white on pink is hard; low-contrast pastels vanish on a shelf. [YT:eN-Tti_iqwM, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:EQWsDA8D-54] (2016–2019) · mentions 7
- **Break words on syllables; keep the rag consistent** — Hyphenate "feedback"; never break where it makes two words ("gr eat"); a crazy rag hides the start corner; set ragged, not force-justified. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:2HY_vl9a6JA, YT:gi0uwMeU8wk, YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ] (2018–2019) · mentions 7
- **Leading: tighten until you stop feeling air between lines** — Close it on big lines and small bottom type; reopen by eye after scaling a title up. [YT:qIvhzoCHzpA, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:gi0uwMeU8wk] (2018–2019) · mentions 6
- **All caps for short headlines; upper-and-lower for the rest** — "most headlines can stand in all caps because it's not that much to read"; upper-and-lower word shapes aid legibility. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:gi0uwMeU8wk, YT:6ZAD6jxgGwk] (2018) · mentions 5
- **Color proportion: lots of one, a little of another** — Never complementary colors in equal proportions (red and green equals Christmas); metallic gold sparingly; even a "bad" color works used sparingly. [YT:CQMf71m3JOw, YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017) · mentions 5
- **Black and white first, then two or three colors** — Prove it holds in black and white, then add color; black, white and red is very strong. [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:yKHc2or01Iw] (2017) · mentions 5

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Hero and read-order diagnostic
- When he uses it: first move on any busy poster, lockup or pack.
- Steps: 1. Ask what you see first, second, third. 2. Ask what matters most; if two elements say the same thing, "change the relationship". 3. Squint, invert, or cover the icon to see shapes alone. 4. Resize until the answer is obvious.
- His example: HELP over EXIT (the figure already exits); title big, credit small.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:vomjcfFKHoE, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017–2019)
#### Typography drill
- When he uses it: type that is overcomplicated.
- Steps: 1. One point size, one weight, laid out over and over until the type dances. 2. Add a second size. 3. Add a second weight. 4. Then get complicated.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
#### Three-box drill
- When he uses it: a layout that will not settle.
- Steps: 1. Replace the elements with plain boxes. 2. Flip sides until it balances asymmetrically in a lazy S, the eye moving in and out of the frame. 3. Restore real details, recheck spacing.
- His example: pictogram poster whose first pass read as a C.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
#### Rules as containers
- When he uses it: many pieces that should read as one; a short word beside heavy blocks.
- Steps: 1. Wrap in a thick rule (8 pt, passing the outer letter edges). 2. Group. 3. Or lay a thick bar across the short word.
- His example: bento box against a supermarket plate; CAC given a thick white block across its top.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017–2018)
#### Kerning in trios
- When he uses it: any wordmark or script.
- Steps: 1. Set the problem-child pair first (T–A in SPARTAN). 2. Work three letters at a time. 3. Judge the volume of space, not touch points. 4. Match the rest to the hardest pair. 5. Flip the design 180 degrees. 6. Print large; one-pixel nudges vanish on screen.
- His example: R–A in RACE tightened to a near ligature, or a custom or lowercase a.
- Sources: [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017)
#### Logotype measuring routine
- When he uses it: building or auditing a logotype in Illustrator.
- Steps: 1. Snap guides to baseline and cap line. 2. Use the most complicated letter (the S) as the unit. 3. Copy a Shift-drawn rectangle across letters as a stroke bar; check thin lines. 4. Check curve overshoot. 5. Round joins numerically. 6. View at several sizes; print.
- His example: join rounding, 10 pt too much, 8 with 4.2, same 8 "really weird", settled on 6 pt.
- Sources: [YT:0Nh7h0JMOpk] (2018)
#### Make a typed wordmark ownable and integrated
- When he uses it: client says "you just typed it into the computer", or symbol and type feel tacked together. [quote src: YT:aPguPSY-e_4]
- Steps: 1. Find the angles in the icon. 2. Cut one or two letterforms at that angle (slice the A crossbar, extend the T below the baseline). 3. Align type flush to the icon's edges, or knock it out in white inside the icon. 4. Or build the type on the symbol's grid: same stroke, same gap.
- His example: GUILD on the 322 mark's stroke and gap; "I want a really tight integration." [quote src: YT:w6nvr_V0E68]
- Sources: [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:V6e-rFgkZzI] (2017–2019)
#### Grid on request
- When he uses it: poster with no order; starts gridless, then checks.
- Steps: 1. Copy the group. 2. Object > Path > Split Into Grid; he chose seven columns, "I don't care what kind". 3. Snap to it. 4. For rhythm, size blocks in 1, 2, 5, 8 units.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
#### Script and tapered strokes (Jelvin's workflow, endorsed; his Illustrator method)
- When he uses it: script wordmarks, thick/thin strokes.
- Steps: 1. Scan the pencil draft, lock it as template. 2. Slant grid from two blended lines (50 steps), baseline and x-height. 3. Lowercase first, easiest letter (i) first; one letter (the n) is the unit. 4. Draw tapers in parts: ellipse, Scissor tool, Option-drag, average endpoints (Cmd-Opt-J), join (Cmd-J). 5. Check spacing with circles matching counter width. 6. Center optically.
- His takeaways: optical balance; legibility over cleverness; one stroke angle.
- Sources: [YT:4ZeEfF2O8-Q, YT:V6e-rFgkZzI] (2018–2019)
#### Super comp and cropping
- Steps: 1. Design the whole story as one giant board. 2. Crop frames with a fixed-ratio marquee; no grid, hunt for "cool little moments". 3. Crops sharing an element relate; a 90° rotation is fine; stay open to happy accidents. 4. Crop to create tension (an 8 cut by the frame edge); anchor type on a left edge. Sources: [YT:CQMf71m3JOw] (2017)
#### Steak and sizzle
- Steps: 1. Steak is content; sizzle is typography, graphics, photography. 2. Task pages (a job application) get steak: strip sizzle. 3. Behind type use textural, not busy, backgrounds. Sources: [YT:eN-Tti_iqwM] (2016)
#### Client mandates an ugly color
- Steps: 1. Make a third neutral the primary (warm gray). 2. Use the client color sparingly. 3. Once trust is earned, propose three nearby shades and a new brand style guide. Sources: [YT:CQMf71m3JOw] (2017)

### Fix library (problem → his fix → why)
**Hierarchy and scale**
- **Problem:** Lockup mixing two sizes and two weights, credit competing with title → **Fix:** one face (Gotham Bold stand-in), credit "a lot smaller", centering allowed → **Why:** "too many tricks"; contrast and hierarchy. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Boxing poster with caps, brush script, outlined caps, two colors, 2–3 sizes → **Fix:** Futura Bold only, every detail line one size (eyedropper the attributes), flush left, hyphenation off, point type for big short lines → **Why:** strip tricks first. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Collapse poster in a calligraphic-pen face with diagonal stress → **Fix:** change only the typeface to Trade Gothic Bold Condensed No. 20, then break it → **Why:** big, powerful, destruction needs a solid face; "the difference between an amateur and a pro". [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Booklet cover with logo, script line and descriptor all about one size → **Fix:** enlarge the descriptor, shrink the logo, move the stack up to close gaps → **Why:** one bigger, one smaller; resizing one thing re-tunes the rest. [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017)
- **Problem:** Word block filling nearly half the poster → **Fix:** box cap line to baseline, scale vertically to one third (33.33%), group with lockup, then lift it → **Why:** even thirds sit low and bore. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** "est. 1912" outranks everything on a vinegar label → **Fix:** "organic" on top, flank "apple cider vinegar" with "est. 1912" → **Why:** heritage matters but is not first. [YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ] (2019)
- **Problem:** Toothpaste pack with net weight (3.4 oz) missing from the front → **Fix:** pull it out and make it much more prominent → **Why:** it is essential. [YT:gx5hEcIwhnY, YT:FsyJodhEze0] (2018)
**Typeface choice and pairing**
- **Problem:** "Miami" too thin and condensed beside a serif-and-symbol lockup → **Fix:** regular-width sans (Akzidenz-like, or Gotham) with a little tracking → **Why:** the pairing feels like architecture, the word must read. [YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2018)
- **Problem:** Thick condensed face fights the paperclip form → **Fix:** endorses Jelvin's swap to Abel (round forms, stroke near the clip's), opened kerning, pulled the I apart, broke the clip top from its bottom → **Why:** repetition plus contrast; the clip is the contrast. [YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2018)
- **Problem:** Lone sans line on a poster otherwise in italic serif → **Fix:** delete it, reset that line in the same italic serif, bigger → **Why:** the only sans on the page. [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017)
- **Problem:** Personal wordmark with medium against bold, plus an underscore → **Fix:** light against bold, lighter m and underscore, tighter kerning underscore to s, name flush left → **Why:** the underscore read as a third weight in no man's land. [YT:YXf-KbLCpvI] (2017)
- **Problem:** Bold condensed display face used for small timeline text → **Fix:** body-appropriate face; shrink the engine illustrations much smaller, no repeats → **Why:** display faces fail small; repeats are decorative. [YT:gi0uwMeU8wk] (2018)
- **Problem:** Pure-type covers in one typeface; Catcher title weights jumping → **Fix:** a face per book linked by color or grid; weights thick, medium, thin, thinnest → **Why:** each book has its own character; readers predict the progression. [YT:2HY_vl9a6JA, YT:EQWsDA8D-54] (2019)
**Spacing, leading and letterforms**
- **Problem:** Menu body copy with open leading and many sizes → **Fix:** tighten leading until air stops showing; fewer sizes; bold against regular or light of one family → **Why:** he starts reading the negative space between lines. [YT:qIvhzoCHzpA] (2019)
- **Problem:** Wide tracking on wordmarks (Sensodyne, THE FRENCHMAN) → **Fix:** tighten for a compact lockup → **Why:** compact marks scale; on a card everything scales to the widest element. [YT:gx5hEcIwhnY, YT:-JYiXyzDEFI] (2018–2019)
- **Problem:** Secondary line under a logotype (room wanted it thin) → **Fix:** uppercase, smaller, bold, tracked out a little (too much opens gaps) → **Why:** nice x-height, lines up. [YT:0Nh7h0JMOpk] (2018)
- **Problem:** Squared D in custom GUILD letters reads poorly → **Fix:** round only the D, slightly → **Why:** a legibility decision; one rounded letter is fine. [YT:V6e-rFgkZzI] (2019)
- **Problem:** Kink at a stroke transition → **Fix:** copy the line, scale it into a guide, put the offending Bezier handle on it, nudge optically where the taper looks thick → **Why:** clean taper. [YT:SPmqQb7AaME] (2019)
- **Problem:** Hand lettering with thick bottoms, flat spot on the E, pen angle off (about 22–35°), heavy texture → **Fix:** redraw until right, cut apart and tape together → **Why:** letterers practice thousands of hours. [YT:ckSpE0Oh43c] (2017)
**Breaks and rag**
- **Problem:** Four-word phrase set huge → **Fix:** break into lines ("always" alone, then "do your best"); odd rag is fine if applied consistently; or hyphenate to a 2–3 character column → **Why:** the break steers the reading. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
- **Problem:** "We moved" poster too rigid → **Fix:** words run into each other, zigzag margins (space bar before the first "We"), tighter leading (Option+Up/Down), unaligned exclamation marks, color to separate lines → **Why:** the message yells. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
- **Problem:** Labels of very different length stacked → **Fix:** pink rules as long as the longest word, heavier stroke, slightly offset, spaced at the leading (Cmd+T reads 189, use 190; Move > Copy, Cmd+D) → **Why:** anchors them. [YT:gi0uwMeU8wk] (2018)
**Alignment, grouping and lockups**
- **Problem:** First syllable stranded above a stack → **Fix:** align it to the group's edge, tune spacing, raise the stack to use the space → **Why:** "you're far away but you're still part of us". [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Venue poster with scattered pieces, EST apart from 1935 → **Fix:** one-third grid; EST on the 1935 baseline, or EST at half size tucked aside → **Why:** alignment ties lone pieces; moving one element moves another. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** TM floats off the mark; mixed faces and cases (Top Story) → **Fix:** more extended face under the TS, "Leader in World News" stacked as subhead, TM pulled close → **Why:** nothing talked to anything. [YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2018)
- **Problem:** Symbol left, long horizontal line, tiny tagline (chevron mark) → **Fix:** drop RACE to the next line, justify left; run the red underline from the S of SPARTAN to the E of RACE → **Why:** reduces better; the underline groups. [YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017)
- **Problem:** Symbol too big, gaps to the wordmark, arc too far from the mark → **Fix:** symbol to about 70% (D mark to 80% in the Scale dialog), type closer, arc tightened by about half → **Why:** compact, numeric not by eye. [YT:yKHc2or01Iw, YT:-JYiXyzDEFI] (2017–2019)
**Space and flow**
- **Problem:** Type crammed into corners or bled across the page ("Do it. It's time") → **Fix:** scale down for air; place elements large, mid, smaller along a path → **Why:** oversized elements eliminate the space you can work with. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
- **Problem:** Three unrelated shapes not gelling → **Fix:** arrange along a gentle S (bottom element to the other side), type bigger; after scaling down, move some lower → **Why:** posters need big bold things; lower implies "there's more momentum". [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Heavy black band on top, light bottom → **Fix:** invert the bottom bar to black with white type → **Why:** visual weight must balance; any adjustment affects everything else, so abandon it or make it work. [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017)
- **Problem:** Movie poster without the small billing block → **Fix:** set the credits copy at the bottom → **Why:** as soon as you add it the poster feels real; only in school can you skip it. [YT:ckSpE0Oh43c] (2017)
**Packaging**
- **Problem:** Thin wrap borders on a toothpaste pack → **Fix:** thicken from rule to shape, reverse some out, put a word on the edge for tension, extend grid lines across → **Why:** groups elements and draws the eye. [YT:gx5hEcIwhnY] (2018)
- **Problem:** Black band with a pattern covers the soap's colors → **Fix:** strip graphics, no patterns, Clinique-style restraint, try honey and sandalwood versions → **Why:** patterns turn decorative "frou-frou". [YT:gx5hEcIwhnY] (2018)

**Color**
- **Problem:** Boxing poster in lavender, pastel yellow, turquoise → **Fix:** red "like blood" (black lost the figures) → **Why:** a little grittier; pastels still work with enough contrast and good silhouettes. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- **Problem:** Giant 3 and its phrase both black → **Fix:** make the 3 red, or add one splash of color to the black and white → **Why:** separates number from message. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
- **Problem:** Low-contrast pastel covers vanish on a shelf → **Fix:** add white and black (one silhouette filled black); or white books on a hot pink background → **Why:** "bite"; put contrast in the background. [YT:EQWsDA8D-54] (2019)
- **Problem:** Blue food box reads clinical → **Fix:** hand-done quality, textured paper in the top half, or a warmer added color → **Why:** blue reads sterile like cosmetics or medicine. [YT:vomjcfFKHoE] (2019)
- **Problem:** Mango can: red and orange contrast too much, green with red and white reads Italy → **Fix:** more orange so it reads mango not tomato, drop the green → **Why:** proportion. [YT:tS7fF7NKoRQ] (2019)

- **Problem:** Infographic color scheme → **Fix:** endorses John Beatty's idea: black for the dead, white for the least affected, a halftone transition between, black as secondary color beside the green → **Why:** the transition shows severity. [YT:gi0uwMeU8wk] (2018)

### Critique moves and questions
- Force a gut call (love or hate), ask why, turn the reason into the fix (usually reduce what the eye must process). [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- Read-order challenge — "what am I supposed to look at?" / "what's the most important thing we want to look at on this poster?" / "we have to make a decision, what do you want people to look at?" [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- Eye path on packs — "where do you want my eye to go?" [YT:vomjcfFKHoE] (2019)
- Alignment audit — "what is that aligning to?" [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo] (2018)
- Optical checks asked of the room — "does it feel like it's falling short?" / "does it feel optically the same?" / "how do you feel about that?" [YT:0Nh7h0JMOpk] (2018)
- Make the viewer feel the fix: not only can you see the difference, but do you feel it and know why. [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)
- Intent questions on a designer's exaggeration: why is the x-height so high, why draw the belly below the baseline "in such an exaggerated way, what was the thinking there?" [YT:V6e-rFgkZzI] (2019)
- Ask the team which words matter most and how they decided, then size word by word. [YT:6ZAD6jxgGwk] (2018)
- Change one variable at a time when comparing options; cold-read a cover as a stranger and judge only what is visible. [YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:EQWsDA8D-54] (2018–2019)

### Specs and numbers

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Logo proof tests and five-point checklist
- When he uses it: before judging any mark; closing recap of a logo review.
- Steps: 1. Black and white only. 2. Squint: if the form still reads, silhouette is good. 3. Scale to a half-inch square or print it (a phone app icon is barely bigger). 4. Check a square or circle alternate for avatars and a 16 x 16 favicon. 5. Invert (a white-tipped pencil read as a pie). Recap: six-foot rule; black and white; other mediums, shape over line; not trendy; has an idea.
- His example: climbers' mark with rotated side letters, unreadable at web-app size; "nobody's ever going to see it like that" is "totally wrong". [quote src: YT:aPguPSY-e_4]
- Sources: [YT:YXf-KbLCpvI, YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4, YT:6ZAD6jxgGwk, YT:aPguPSY-e_4] (2017-2018)

#### Read-order and what-else-does-it-read-as check
- When he uses it: first pass on any entry, before fixing anything.
- Steps: 1. Ask what you see first, second, third (biggest plus highest contrast wins). 2. Squint. 3. Invert to expose accidental letters in negative space. 4. Cover the icon to judge the type alone; cover the wordmark to see what the icon reads as. 5. Ask what else it looks like.
- His example: "did this read to you as a helmet or no?"; shields read as eggs, an S-helmet as pizza. [quote src: YT:w6nvr_V0E68]
- Sources: [YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:yKHc2or01Iw] (2017)

#### Judging order for a brief
- When he uses it: contest or client brief reviews (Spartan Race).
- Steps: 1. Brief compliance (wordmark present, supplied template, black and white plus one color, no gradients, vector). 2. On-brand (bold, gritty, thick lines). 3. Design principles (legible, scalable, balanced, symbol not overpowering wordmark). 4. Inventive and enduring.
- His example: "A foul." for ignoring the tee template; a bolt monogram out for no wordmark; most repeated question: where is the wordmark?
- Sources: [YT:yKHc2or01Iw, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017)

#### Integrate icon and wordmark
- When he uses it: symbol and type feel like strangers.
- Steps: 1. Align type flush to the icon's edges (same cap height, baseline, point size). 2. Copy the icon's angles into letter cuts. 3. Or knock the type out of the icon, wrap a shape around both, or set both on a solid slab. 4. Finger-cover test: if it looks better with a finger over the tagline, it is not integrated.
- His example: bolt-A mark, RACE at about 80% of SPARTAN, off the mark.
- Sources: [YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:yKHc2or01Iw] (2017)

#### Strip-out and all-configurations test
- When he uses it: a complicated mark or finalist.
- Steps: 1. Deconstruct into components and test each alone. 2. Strip texture in your head, judge the clean mark. 3. View symbol alone, type alone, small TV "bug". 4. Stress it: pattern, stencil, award, medal; widen the lockup for numerals or a location. 5. Imagine the kit on hotel towels, carpet, a lobby floor inlay.
- His example: lion and Greek key tee, too complicated as a single logo.
- Sources: [YT:yKHc2or01Iw, YT:aPguPSY-e_4] (2017)

#### Brand words to stylescape to sketch
- When he uses it: new brand or rebrand, before drawing.
- Steps: 1. Pull the brand's own copy and ads; write tone, voice, look and feel in your own words. 2. Empathy graph: what the brand's world physically looks like. 3. Image buckets from real artifacts, plus unrelated logos to steer style. 4. Stylescapes graded mild to spicy; client feedback. 5. Thumbnails, tissues, then vectorize.
- His example: Spartan (mud, fire pits, old shoes, no costumes), so script flourishes are for a bakery, not this brand.
- Sources: [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:w6nvr_V0E68] (2017)

#### Insight to touchpoint (self-storage)
- When he uses it: humanizing a cold category.
- Steps: 1. Session insights to requirements (90%+ women, many single moms: safe, secure, kid-friendly, simple). 2. Visual keywords (safe, strong, secure), not big words. 3. Attributes to voice, feel, impact. 4. One touchpoint line per attribute (entry mat). 5. Mock it in real space.
- His example: "lights, camera, safety"; "smile, your belongings are on TV". [quote src: YT:FYHD1fBKZDk]
- Sources: [YT:FYHD1fBKZDk] (2015)

## PART C. How he critiques, his process, presenting visual work

### Rules (ranked by how often he says it)
- **Pick one lane and say it plainly: what you do, and the benefit to the buyer** — Bento-box brain: people file you in one compartment to save mental calories, so be one clear thing ("I don't want a dabbler, I want an expert"). Confidence is specificity (a hundred identity projects beats a scattershot list). About 80% of jobs want specialists, 20% a generalist. [YT:PRWN28sd3kQ, YT:ckSpE0Oh43c] (2017, 2018) · mentions 15
- **Research and understand before you make anything** — "Think more and make less": designers rush, fall in love with their own design, then the art director says it is off brand. Research is learning everything about the project (the history of Sparta), not collecting images; without it "you're just making up stuff". Check what has been done first. [YT:aPguPSY-e_4, YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2017, 2018) · mentions 14
- **Strip first, add back one element at a time** — "Too many tricks" is his most common diagnosis. Turn layers off, flood the page with one flat color, judge the bare layout (hero scale vs page, negative space), re-add one element at a time. Reset variant: select all, one point size and one color, rebuild the hierarchy on purpose. Gate for every addition: "Is it contributing to the message or is it taking away from the design?" Piling on elements signals you are not confident in the icon or the type. [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg, YT:0fvEFIkT7pM, YT:VH8QT73Gxwo, YT:aPguPSY-e_4] (2017, 2018) · mentions 13
- **Mockups and staging are part of the work** — A strong mockup can make or break a presentation; the logo in colorways and on applications, big and small, "makes it look more expensive" and justifies a higher fee. ### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Base-emotion critique
- When he uses it: first reaction to a poster or layout, instead of "it's too busy" plus random changes.
- Steps: 1. Force a gut call in one base emotion, love or hate, "get off the fence". 2. Ask "explain to me why you don't like it". 3. Turn the reason into the fix. [quote src: YT:0fvEFIkT7pM]
- His example: Aaron says confused and distracted, too many things to look at, so reduce what the eye must process.
- Sources: [YT:0fvEFIkT7pM] (2018)

#### Slowed-down critique sequence
- When he uses it: a layout "80% there" with something off.
- Steps: 1. Process what he sees and what it means. 2. Ask about technique and thinking. 3. Name what works. 4. Pick apart what is off, one thing at a time. 5. Strip back, re-add one element at a time, judge after each.
- His example: Anton's neon "AF" poster; italic serif, lowercase sans, all-caps sans, one large cap and a different neon F is a lot of typefaces.
- Sources: [YT:xjAQ5CK4oqg] (2017)


#### Read-order, squint, invert, cover tests
- When he uses it: before fixing anything on a mark or poster.
- Steps: 1. "What do I see first?" then second, third; biggest plus highest contrast wins. 2. Squint to compress values. 3. Invert to expose accidental letters in negative space. 4. Cover the icon to judge the type; cover the wordmark to see what the icon reads as.
- His example: Spartan mark; red and gray were close in value, so the red vanished and odd negative space showed before the tagline.
- Sources: [YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:rQ_Zx_pN4V4] (2017, 2018)


#### Package critique order
- When he uses it: judging a consumer pack redesign.
- Steps: 1. Inventory the original without attachment. 2. Name specific wins. 3. State the biggest problem outright ("my biggest critique," "the biggest crime"). 4. Draw alternatives over the pack. 5. One-line close: simplify, refine, pick your best mark.
- His example: judge high-end feel, appetite appeal, shelf presence (recognizable from six feet away), features drawn out, appropriateness (no co-opted design language).
- Sources: [YT:vomjcfFKHoE, YT:gx5hEcIwhnY] (2018, 2019)

#### Logo judging screen
- When he uses it: contest or live logo critiques.
- Steps: 1. Brief compliance. 2. On brand (gritty means thick lines, simple shapes). 3. Principles: legible, scales, balance, symbol not overpowering the wordmark, wordmark integrated. 4. Inventive, enduring.
- His example: most repeated question is where the wordmark is and where RACE went; would he pay $35 for the shirt.
- Sources: [YT:yKHc2or01Iw] (2017)


#### Portfolio review
- When he uses it: judging a portfolio as a whole, not nitpicking pieces.
- Steps: 1. Read headline and skill order (first skill = primary competency). 2. Open what catches the eye, say what he sees. 3. Filter: what do you do; what is in it for me; are you credible; would I pay good money for this; is it consistent. 4. Duck, duck, goose: find the piece that does not fit, check the worst first. 5. Range test: a second strong project in a different genre.
- Sources: [YT:PRWN28sd3kQ, YT:ckSpE0Oh43c, YT:7Z1XaGDFPoE] (2017, 2018)

#### Big-picture website critique
- When he uses it: an agency or service site.
- Steps: 1. Brand aligned to the clients you want. 2. Value proposition clear and upfront (what you do for clients, not about yourself). 3. List the visitor's top three objections ("why wouldn't they hire you"). 4. Only then the design.
- Sources: [YT:LMBIfTDuIxY] (2017)

#### Stylescape to comp
- When he uses it: brand and web work before layout.
- Steps: 1. One client culture keyword at a time ("healthy"), hunt matching images. 2. Competitive audit, tiers above and below, to match price point. 3. Stylescape: fonts, logo references, photography, texture. 4. Comp near-literally translates it (logo, fonts, copy from brand attributes, menu from the audit). 5. Cast each direction by designer temperament, never a weak one.
- Sources: [YT:lGmPCutgI2o, YT:PZfk-Pi0EXk, YT:FYHD1fBKZDk] (2015)

#### Fun-to-functional axis and straight-line system
- When he uses it: taming client taste and runaway rounds.
- Steps: 1. Define the poles (wacky and fun vs Swiss, bold, boxed). 2. Let the client reject both. 3. Mood board for the middle, walk inward. 4. Ceiling and basement on iterations; if a round "goes bananas," pull back. Fit check early.
- Sources: [YT:FYHD1fBKZDk] (2015)

#### Chris's logo process
- When he uses it: any logo from scratch (he wants designers "to think more and to do less"). [quote src: YT:w6nvr_V0E68]
- Steps: 1. Research project and brand. 2. Image buckets (history, culture, society, obvious icons). 3. Brand attributes, empathy graph, adjacent cultures. 4. 2 to 3 stylescapes, mild to spicy, to the client. 5. Only then thumbnails and tissues (flip them backward and upside down). 6. Tighten, vectorize.
- Sources: [YT:w6nvr_V0E68, YT:aPguPSY-e_4] (2017)
