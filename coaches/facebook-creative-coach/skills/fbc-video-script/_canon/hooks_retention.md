## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Four-part hook structure (Denney, credits Callaway) / three-part hook (Cottrell, Levinger)
- When they use it: writing and reviewing any video hook; Cottrell's agency writes all three for every hook.
- Steps: 1. Text-overlay hook (what they read). 2. Sound/spoken hook (what they hear, incl. sound effects). 3. Visual hook (what they see in frame zero). 4. Vibe — lighting, overlay style, fonts, overall feel (Denney only). Overlay can differ from the VO. Pair two hook types where possible (Cottrell: protein-bar line + white-liquid pour, a favourite in VSLs). Levinger: sound can be silence; the visual carries the first second.
- Their example: VISUAL (Denney) — Jones Road frame: blonde middle-aged woman, pink product near lips, white box If you're new to Jones Road, you should start with these, arrows labelled what the viewer reads / hears / sees → overall vibe.
- Sources: [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-06), [YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ] (2026-05), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

### Three-layer hook breakdown (guest: Evan Lee, Motion)
- When they use it: deconstructing a competitor or winning ad.
- Steps: 1. Visual — why did I stop? 2. Audio + text — did it reinforce, add or explain? 3. Psychological — tactic (explicit pain call-out vs nuance). 4. Ask "what carries the ad?" [quote src: YT:DqFjak694PQ]. A hook has three jobs: an emotional reaction in familiar language, target/disqualify, connect to the story.
- Sources: [YT:DqFjak694PQ] (2026-06)

### Levinger's 1-second neuroscience timeline and 300-ms elements
- When they use it: diagnosing why a hook fails to stop; designing the 0–2 s "grab".
- Timeline (citing Neurons EEG work): 100 ms brain decides ad or content; 300 ms "I either feel something towards this or I don't" [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]; ~700 ms knows what's going on; 1 s decides to stay/leave and starts memory encoding — "by the time you hit that 3 second mark, the whole thing is over." [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]
- Four 300-ms elements: 1. Human face mid-emotion (animals work too, humans most). 2. Motion that doesn't match the scroll (feed moves up, so downward/odd motion stops the thumb). 3. Visual that breaks format (laptop in a fridge; partial view that becomes something else — "Is it real or is it cake?" [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]). 4. Precognitive familiar sound (e.g. a phone conversation).
- Sub-1-s checks (2025-12): sound — interesting or none (ASMR, tonal, singing); visual — surprising/bizarre.
- "Two second grabs" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]: separate the 0–2 s grab from the full hook (3–5 s, elsewhere "five to seven" [quote src: YT:4g4WgCad_k4]). Hooks needn't be loud or questions — can be "just a black screen with like a dot in the middle" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk].
- Their example: AutoBrush mouth close-up (section 4).
- Sources: [YT:DEoj28QXUYE] (2026-06), [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12), [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02)

### Emotion-wheel "predictive hook generation" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc] (Levinger)
- When they use it: before loading hooks, to make each hook aim at one emotion for one customer type.
- Steps: 1. Start at the wheel centre (happy, surprised, bad, fearful, angry, disgusted, sad). 2. Move outward to micro-emotions (~200) and pick the exact feeling — one step past the core and comparing two neighbours is enough. 3. Use outer-ring vocabulary (child-development ladder: ages 3–5 core words; 6–8 confused vs startled; teens confident vs respected). 4. Team exercise: play hook + first clips, ask "How did you respond to this?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc], "Which avatar were you going after?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc], "Which emotion?" — different answers = the hook isn't targeting one emotion.
- Their numbers: "respected" scored scroll-stop 28/100, CTR ~35, conversion 55; curious beats inquisitive (bigger information gap); pressured beats rushed on scroll-stop and click — "I feel pressure deeper in my body than I feel rushed." [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc]
- Sources: [YT:LxWwRykBtfc] (2026-03)

### Distinction / von Restorff — blend, then break one thing (Levinger)
- Steps: 1. Blend — look like an organic post. 2. Inside it, make one thing the opposite of normal (one red ball among blue; beautiful breakfast table with one burnt, smoking pancake). 3. Call out what "shouldn't be doing what it's doing" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU], then make your point. 4. Product looks "not supposed to be that way" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk] on a very simple background; use red on contrast ("we're built to look at things close, especially if it's red" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]).
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05), [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02)

### Hooks by funnel stage (Cottrell; Denney how-to ladder)
- Cottrell steps: 1. Group hooks by stage. 2. TOF (never heard of you): educate and qualify; curiosity; often "just showing the problem hooks your customer" [quote src: YT:svW3EyoYAiw]. 3. MOF (problem-aware, shopping, unsure who to buy from): don't re-tell what they know. 4. BOF (ready): product + offer right after the hook. 5. Ask What information would hook you in… if you are in that stage of the customer journey? [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg]
- Denney how-to by stage: (a) "How to know if" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] — problem-aware TOF; (b) "How do I pick / how do I choose" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] — choosing between solutions (top performer 6 months for a home-decor/apparel client); (c) "How to start" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] — very TOF or most-aware getting started; good for services and deep consideration. Superlatives ("world's best", "strongest") for product-aware fence-sitters.
- VISUAL: Google-style search bar How to know if you need rehab; red box How To Know If You Have + "INSOMNIA"; How to inspect a used car.
- Sources: [YT:93gJ8_qvOQg] (2026-03), [YT:Mqj05tYuDI0] (2026-09), [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-06), [YT:k533N5RxXPY] (2024-06)

### Hook-volume production (Heath)
- Steps: 1. Brief creators: 10 x different visual hooks (These hooks can be different opening lines, different locations, camera angles, clothing, lighting etc) — under an hour to record. 2. 10 hooks × 2 full 30–60 s bodies = 20 ads for roughly the price of 3. 3. Test hooks in a creative-testing tool. 4. Put the winner in an ad set alongside very different ads (UGC vs founder vs demo). 5. Iterate winners by re-recording the opening — new background, top, person, location, slightly changed line, selfie vs camera. 6. Every variant must pass the "I've seen this before" [quote src: YT:Ak6I-EIbBd4] filter (Every hook needs to Look Different).
- Sources: [YT:IRyR9PzSnM8] (2025-09), [YT:onFwSud9C2Y] (2026-02), [YT:M2VReAjHiN8] (2025-10)

### Hook-testing mix and iteration (Denney)
- Steps: 1. New footage: first test = six variants differing only in the first 3 s, copy/headline/description/CTA identical. 2. Mix per test: one product-oriented, one problem-oriented, one wild card. 3. Go deep with 2–4 formulations inside the winning category. 4. New creator batch = one green-screen, one TikTok-response, at least one normal hook. 5. On winners, make 3–6 big-swing reformats, not one-word changes ("we can't place all of our hope in just one hook" [quote src: YT:wfENnO7PA5Q]).
- Their example: VISUAL 2026 "REFORMAT" board — Hooks 1–5 incl. sticky note 7:00 am WAKE UP, dark bedroom LIFE WITHOUT BREX.
- Sources: [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q] (2025-02), [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-06), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05)

### "So what" test and intro-chop (Hott) / "boring test" (guest: Ovadia, Perfect Jean)
- Steps: 1. Watch the first pixels at the top of frame, first sound, first words. 2. If a viewer could say "so what", you've lost them. 3. Diagnose post-hoc on ads that failed to scale. 4. Chop the first 3–5 s of an underperformer (expanding logo, meandering, "too hooky" intro) to reach a "non-hook hook" [quote src: YT:fawsUmNeCkg] or the problem — only when you believe the ad should do better, not instead of new concepts. 5. Pull the most outrageous story moments to the front. Ovadia's team raises a hand the second they're bored (often 4–8 s).
- Sources: [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12), [YT:qoOBIPWH_qw] (2024-12), [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09)

### Hook analysis moves (Denney, Cottrell, Levinger, Hott)
- Denney: add a hook-rate column, compare each ad to account and campaign average, check spend before trusting a high rate, then open the asset and inspect first-3-s messaging, footage and thumbnail.
- Cottrell: work backward from the winning hook's elements and replicate; editors map where a first-time viewer's eyes go; "remove any kind of visual chaos" [quote src: YT:GCugREdurJk].
- Levinger: play only ~7 s of an organic video and ask "what millisecond in the video, you decide to keep watching" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk].
- Hott: what could I pour on my head that would that would make sense to my audience immediately? [quote src: YT:FTotRZL9vAg] — "maybe the most valuable tip" [quote src: YT:FTotRZL9vAg].
- Sources: [YT:CdK0jdqxXOs] (2022-07), [YT:_FMXAlfgYQo] (2026-01), [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02), [YT:FTotRZL9vAg] (2025-04)

## 3. Hook types and exact wording

### Identity and call-out
- **Demographic call-out** (Denney): age (easy entry; "incredibly necessary" for 50+), job title, lifestyle, health condition, (aspirational) community. Job-title template: Freelancers, here's how to land high-paying clients. VISUAL: nurse in navy scrubs collapsed on sofa, caps bars THIS IS FOR MY NURSES WHO SPEND ALL DAY ON THEIR FEET; THE PERFECT THONG FOR POSTPARTUM MOMS with red arrow; grey-haired woman, no text (age shown visually); POV: you're a girl dad.
