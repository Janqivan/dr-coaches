Corpus status: 199 transcripts (1398654 words) in the raw layer plus canon items. Checks re-sourced on 2026-10-20; tags without YT ids were not found in transcripts.

This file is the bar. Every message, script or plan the coach produces or judges is graded against the checks for its task type. A check is PASS only with evidence pointed at the work itself (the exact sentence, the pause marker, the count). "Sounds empathetic" is not evidence. State the evidence type next to each verdict.

Scoring: count PASS / total for the task type. Deliver only at >= 85% with zero FAIL on checks marked ★. Otherwise fix and regrade (max 2 loops), then deliver with the remaining FAILs listed honestly.

Canon shorthand (where a [canon: ...] tag remains): NSTD = never_split_the_difference_frameworks.md, FFA = full_fee_agent_frameworks.md, PCE = public_corpus_excerpts.md, FAA = free_authored_articles.md, SKILL = SKILL.md.

Two canon tensions to respect when grading:
1. Label stems. NSTD canon says use only "It seems / sounds / looks like". PCE A3 (Black Swan Group blog) also lists "it feels like", "you seem", and for accusation audits "you're probably thinking / wondering", "you may think". PASS on any stem in those lists; FAIL on "I feel", "I understand", "I hear what you're saying". Voss lists "it seems, it sounds, it looks like" and "you seem, you sound, you look", and says one of his negotiators prefers "it feels like" [YT:_3YjumgSteU] [YT:yCIZ9NrXzqs]; he warns against "what I'm hearing is" [YT:_3YjumgSteU]; "I understand" is the phrase he says to cut [YT:0LnzeQ1GAS4]. The other bans: [canon: NSTD Framework 3; PCE A3] (not found in transcripts)
2. Bargaining. The Ackerman Model is canon, but PCE A9 puts bargaining as Plan B, behind negotiating with tactical empathy. Any output that goes to numbers before tactical empathy has been done fails T3. Voss: with lots of tactical empathy people often settle on the second Ackerman move [YT:89iQAAxBBaQ]; they no longer haggle over price, since haggling means the value is not there [YT:hq_jw9MlHms]; "if you're gonna bargain" Ackerman is the best model [YT:bnleaSnBd8I]. ## T2. Pricing pushback and discount requests (live or written reply)

Evidence type: the reply text; quote the sentences.

- ★ **No flinch, no defense, no split.** The reply does not discount, does not justify the price ("here's why we're worth it"), does not offer a percentage off or the midpoint. Any of the three = FAIL. "We don't bargain over price anymore" [YT:hq_jw9MlHms]; splitting the difference is a sucker's or a fool's move [YT:U3a36GUE-o8] and leaves the problems unsolved [YT:yPsvgmZlVuQ].
- ★ **Reply starts with a label or an accusation audit on the pushback.** First sentence names the emotion or the thought behind the pushback ("It sounds like the number is bigger than you expected."). Not a restatement of the price, not "I understand". Voss: "The first thing was a label. Sounds like the value is not there for you", then mirror their answer [YT:0VOKbIgIaMA]; same move [YT:hq_jw9MlHms].
- ★ **Ends on a calibrated question that puts the next move on them,** without accepting the discount premise. Allowed forms: "How am I supposed to do that without changing what you'd get?", "What about the price is the issue specifically?", "What would need to be true for this to make sense for your firm?" "How am I supposed to do that" is the first and best way to say no, and it pivots them away from price [YT:5gnLafz3jpM]; it asks them for implementation within your constraints [YT:hOo1nIDlXVI]. The other two forms: [canon: FFA Framework C; SKILL pricing pushback example] (not found in transcripts)
- **Scope, not rate.** If any concession is offered, it removes named scope, not dollars from the same scope. The rate stays. Voss: if the price is too high, it is what they are getting for that price that needs adjustment; a discounted suit that doesn't fit is still a bad buy [YT:0VOKbIgIaMA]. Named-scope wording: [canon: FFA Framework D]
- **Time concessions, not price concessions.** "If you sign today" language = FAIL; "if we both commit by Friday, here's how the timeline shifts" = PASS. [canon: FFA Framework D] (not found in transcripts; related: deadlines make people act impulsively against their interests [YT:bnleaSnBd8I])
- **One full-fee tier posture.** The reply does not introduce a cheaper tier that undercuts the main fee to save the deal. If entry-tier pressure is chronic, the output says it is a positioning/ICP problem and hands off to the positioning coach. [canon: FFA Frameworks D, E] (not found in transcripts)
- **"Fair" is labelled, not argued.** If the prospect says "fair", the reply labels it ("it seems the original number didn't feel fair") and asks what would have to be different; it does not defend the number on merits. Voss: "fair" is the F-bomb, often used defensively or manipulatively, and it triggers defensiveness [YT:5gnLafz3jpM] [YT:bnleaSnBd8I]. The label-and-ask reply: [canon: PCE A7] (not found in transcripts)
- **Walk-away is real.** The reply or the plan states that Ivan is willing to lose the deal and has no resentment about it. Voss: "the best deal possible is to walk away" when you do not fit [YT:fTJklJp75Is]; "let's walk away now and then we maintain our relationship and we don't waste time" [YT:S9N_DJoEAmU]; he does not fake a walk-away as a tactic [YT:U3a36GUE-o8].
- **Competitor-price objection handled by question, not comparison.** "Your competitor charges half" gets "how are you comparing the two?" style question, no disparagement of the competitor. Voss: a comparison bid is often a test, find out if you are being tested [YT:0VOKbIgIaMA]. The "how are you comparing" wording: [canon: FFA "Why this book matters"; Framework C] (not found in transcripts)
- **Short.** Written replies: no paragraph defending anything; the message could be read aloud in the FM DJ voice without a breath problem (sentences about 8-15 words). [canon: PCE section C] (not found in transcripts)
- **Real driver considered.** The output names the likely real concern (risk, fit, speed-to-value) behind a price objection rather than treating price as the issue. Voss: it is "never an issue of price ever"; they may be testing you or using you as a comparison bid [YT:0VOKbIgIaMA]; haggling over price is talking about the wrong thing [YT:hq_jw9MlHms].
- **Tests whether the discount ask is a script.** The reply finds out if they were told to ask: label the missing value, mirror the answer; if the value is fine, no discount is given. Voss: "I was just told I got to get a discount" is a possible answer, and then you are not discounting [YT:0VOKbIgIaMA]. (new)
- **In text, mirror the price words.** A "too high?" style mirror or a label replaces any defense, because tone cannot be controlled in text. [YT:erOuDr3WXI8] (new)
