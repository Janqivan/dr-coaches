## Section 1 — How to Grow Your Small Business: The Airplane Framework

### Framework 1 — The Airplane Metaphor (master frame, the spine of the book)

**What it is:** Miller's central operating metaphor. A small business has six functional parts; they map to six parts of an airplane. To fly, you need all six built correctly. Most failing businesses have built three parts beautifully and ignored the other three.

**The six parts:**

1. **The Cockpit — Leadership.** Three economic priorities, a clear mission, three named priorities, the team aligned.
2. **The Right Engine — Marketing.** Clear message, sales funnel, predictable lead flow. (This is where Building a StoryBrand + Marketing Made Simple plug in.)
3. **The Left Engine — Sales.** A trained team, a written process, a closing rhythm.
4. **The Wings — Products.** Profitable products, optimized for margin, organized as a value ladder.
5. **The Body — Overhead and Operations.** Productive team, low waste, working systems.
6. **The Fuel Tanks — Cash Flow.** Five fixed accounts, simple discipline, no surprises.

**Mechanism:** an airplane with one missing engine flies sideways and crashes. The metaphor enforces holistic thinking — operators love to over-invest in their favourite part (usually marketing or product) and starve the rest.

**Failure mode:** founders who think they have a marketing problem when they actually have an operations problem, or vice versa. Diagnose the WHOLE plane before treating one part.

---

### Framework 2 — The Three Economic Priorities (the Cockpit, leadership layer)

**What it is:** Miller's leadership-clarity rule. Out of dozens of possible business priorities, pick the three that most directly drive revenue or margin THIS quarter. The whole team aligns on these three.

**The three-question test for any candidate priority:**
1. Does this priority directly increase revenue or decrease cost?
2. Can it be measured weekly?
3. Will hitting it materially change the business in 90 days?

If yes to all three, candidate priority. Pick the top three.

**The communication rule:** the three priorities must be repeated, visible, and reviewed in every weekly meeting. Out-of-sight priorities die.

**Failure mode:** seven priorities = no priorities. Founders' instinct is to add, not subtract. The leverage is in subtraction.

---

### Framework 3 — The Sales Process / Sales Funnel as Storied Conversation (the Left Engine)

**What it is:** Miller's sales process. The sales call IS a StoryBrand BrandScript being delivered live. The sales team is trained on the story, not on closing tactics.

**The four-step structure for any sales call:**
1. **Qualify** — confirm the prospect has the problem your product solves.
2. **Tell their story back to them** — using the SB7 lens (problem layers, stakes, success vision).
3. **Present the plan** — 3 steps, simple, customer-facing.
4. **Direct ask** — book the contract, ask for the close, no waffle.

**Mechanism:** sales conversion is a function of how seen the customer feels, not how clever the salesperson is. The story-back step is the conversion lever.

**Failure mode:** founders who think they need a CRM upgrade when what they need is a written sales process the team can repeat without them.

---

### Framework 4 — The Product Ladder (the Wings)

**What it is:** Miller's product-portfolio frame. Every business should have a ladder of products at different price points so customers can ascend the value chain.

**The classic three-rung ladder:**
1. **Entry product** — low price, low risk, fast value. Builds trust.
2. **Core product** — the main offering, where most margin lives.
3. **Premium product** — high price, deeper transformation, fewer customers but higher LTV.

**The ladder rules:**
- Each rung leads naturally to the next.
- The customer journey from rung 1 to rung 3 is intentional, not accidental.
- Margin tends to be highest on rung 1 (% basis) and on rung 3 (absolute basis).

**Mechanism:** single-product businesses leak revenue because they offer one price point. Customers self-select out the moment that price is wrong for them. A ladder catches more customers and grows them over time.

**Failure mode:** ladders that aren't really ladders — three SKUs that have no logical ascension path. Customer wants the next step to feel obvious.

---

### Framework 5 — The Operations / Overhead Discipline (the Body)

**What it is:** Miller's operations principle. Overhead must serve revenue. Every role, tool, and process is justified by its contribution to revenue or to cost reduction. Roles that do neither get cut.

**The three operations questions, asked quarterly:**
1. **What activities are eating time but not producing revenue or saving cost?** (Kill them.)
2. **What roles have unclear scorecards?** (Define them or restructure them.)
3. **What systems are bottlenecks?** (Document them, automate them, or hire for them.)

**Mechanism:** as a business grows from $1M to $10M, the biggest hidden tax is overhead bloat. The discipline is constant pruning, not constant adding.

**Failure mode:** founders who add people to solve every problem. Most "we need to hire" calls are actually "we need to kill a process" calls.

---

### Framework 6 — The Five-Account Cash Flow System (the Fuel Tanks)

**What it is:** Miller's small-business cash discipline. Five separate bank accounts, money allocated by percentage as it comes in, fixed rhythm of distribution.

**The five accounts:**
1. **Operating account** — the working account. Pays bills, runs payroll.
2. **Personal account** — owner pay, on a fixed schedule.
3. **Profit account** — set aside automatically, distributed quarterly. The reward for ownership.
4. **Tax account** — set aside automatically. Eliminates April surprise.
5. **Investment account** — for growth — new hires, new equipment, new product launches.

**Mechanism:** small businesses fail from cash mismanagement more than from any other cause. The five-account system makes cash discipline visible, mechanical, and impossible to ignore.

**Note for the operator:** the five-account system is conceptually adjacent to Mike Michalowicz's Profit First. Miller's version is simpler and runs on percentages set by the operator. Use whichever model creates the discipline.

**Failure mode:** running everything out of one account and "feeling the bank balance." Feels work for a while, then catastrophically doesn't.

---

### Framework 7 — The Small Business Flight Plan (the integrating doc)

**What it is:** The one-page operating doc that integrates all six airplane parts. A printable, reviewable artifact updated quarterly.

**The Flight Plan contents:**
1. **Mission Statement** — definite, measurable, time-bound.
2. **Key Characters** — named team, customers, partners.
3. **Three Economic Priorities** — for the current quarter.
4. **Cultural Themes** — the daily drumbeats.
5. **Income Streams** — every revenue source, mapped.
6. **The plane diagram, scored** — visual scorecard of all six airplane parts, 1-10 rating, lowest score = next intervention.

**Where DR uses it:** the DR Flight Plan (the agency) and the NO BS Ads Flight Plan (the media brand). Two businesses, two Flight Plans, reviewed quarterly.

**Failure mode:** Flight Plans built once and never reread. The artifact is worthless without the quarterly review rhythm.

## Section 2 — Coach Builder: The Solo Expert Path

### Framework 8 — The 8-Step Coach Builder Plan (master frame, the spine of the book)

**What it is:** Miller's playbook for the solo professional transitioning into a profitable coaching business. Eight sequential steps, with mile markers. Designed for the burned-out corporate professional, the consultant trying to productize, or the in-house expert going solo.

**The eight steps:**

1. **Define your niche.** Specific industry, specific role, specific problem.
2. **Develop a service / product menu.** What you sell, named, priced, scoped.
3. **Set realistic income goals.** Tied to client count and lifetime value, not magical thinking.
4. **Create a marketing message.** Lean on the StoryBrand BrandScript — one per service.
5. **Generate leads.** Lead generator + nurture sequence + weekly outreach.
6. **Manage clients well.** Onboarding, retention, results delivery, referrals.
7. **Get your website right.** Header test, wireframe, conversion clarity.
8. **Build or join a coaching community.** Don't try to do this solo forever.

**Mechanism:** most solo coaches fail at steps 1-2 (no niche, no menu) and try to compensate at step 5 (more leads, more posts, more hustle). Hustle on a broken niche kills the business.

**Failure mode:** trying to be a generalist coach. The market pays a premium for specialists. "Executive coach" loses to "executive coach for newly-promoted CFOs in tech."

---

### Framework 9 — The Niche Equation

**What it is:** Miller's specific test for whether a coaching niche is viable. Three components must be present.

**The three components:**

1. **An audience that can be reached.** They congregate somewhere — a LinkedIn segment, an industry conference, a specific community. If you can't find them, you can't sell to them.

2. **A problem they pay to solve.** Pain that has measurable cost. "Lonely founders" is a real pain but rarely a paid pain. "Founders losing $100K/year to bad hiring" is a paid pain.

3. **A solution you can deliver.** Your actual expertise + experience + repeatable process. Not aspiration — capability.

**Niche test:** all three must score high. If any one is low, the niche is broken.

**Failure mode:** picking a niche based on what the coach finds interesting rather than what the market pays for. Passion is not a niche; pain-with-budget is a niche.

---

### Framework 10 — The Coaching Service Menu structure

**What it is:** Miller's product structure for a solo coach. Three offers, ascending price, designed to catch every client at the right entry point.

**The standard three-offer menu:**

1. **The lead offer** — a low-priced product or workshop. $200-$2,000. Builds the list, qualifies serious buyers, generates testimonials. Could be a paid assessment, a one-day workshop, a small group cohort.

2. **The core engagement** — the main 1:1 or small-group coaching engagement. $5,000-$25,000 per engagement, typically 3-6 months. This is where most revenue lives.

3. **The premium / retainer** — long-term advisory, board seat, or retainer. $25,000-$100,000+ annually. Few clients, deep relationships, highest absolute revenue per client.

**The transition mechanism:** the lead offer should naturally surface clients who want more. The core engagement should naturally produce clients who want ongoing advisory. The menu does the selling.

**Failure mode:** coaches who only offer the core engagement. Everyone below the price point bounces; no one above the price point grows in. Solo coaching businesses cap at one tier without a ladder.

---

### Framework 11 — The Coach's Marketing Rhythm

**What it is:** The specific weekly rhythm Miller prescribes for a solo coach generating their own leads. No agency, no team, just the coach.

**The four-touchpoint weekly minimum:**
1. **One piece of public content** — LinkedIn post, podcast guest spot, YouTube video, or blog. Same niche audience.
2. **One nurture email** to the list — same as Marketing Made Simple's nurture rhythm.
3. **Five direct outreach messages** — to specific named prospects matching the niche.
4. **One "conversation"** — a discovery call, a sales call, or a referral conversation.

**Mechanism:** lead flow is a function of weekly touchpoints, not occasional bursts. Most solo coaches do month-long sprints, then nothing for weeks. The rhythm is what creates predictable revenue.

**Where DR uses it:** the model for any solo-expert positioning client — including the NO BS Ads media-brand cadence.

**Failure mode:** coaches who post when they "feel inspired." Inspiration is not a strategy. Rhythm is.
