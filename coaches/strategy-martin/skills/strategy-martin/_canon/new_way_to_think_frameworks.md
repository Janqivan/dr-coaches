# A New Way to Think — Framework Map

**Primary source:** A New Way to Think — Your Guide to Superior
Management Effectiveness
**Author:** Roger L. Martin
**Publisher:** Harvard Business Review Press, May 2022
**ISBN:** 978-1647823511

**Source treatment:** Framework structures described in own words for
synthetic-coach use. Book text is NOT reproduced. Each model is named,
mapped to its chapter, and described mechanically so the /martin skill
can apply it. To read Martin's own prose, get the book.

---

## What this book is

Martin's premise: every executive runs on inherited mental models —
most wrong, or right in 1980 and never updated. The book is 14 short
chapters, each taking a single dominant model in management orthodoxy,
naming the flaw, and offering a replacement.

Format of each chapter: **dominant model → why it breaks → better
model**. Memos, not theory. Martin wrote each as the memo he wished
he could hand a CEO showing up with the wrong model in their head.

**The 14 areas Martin re-thinks:**

| # | Area | Dominant (broken) model | Martin's replacement model |
|---|------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Competition | Be the best at the same game | Choose a different game |
| 2 | Stakeholders | Maximise shareholder value | Optimise for the customer; investor return follows |
| 3 | Customers | Customer satisfaction | Customer loyalty + customer love |
| 4 | Strategy | Strategy = a plan | Strategy = an integrated set of choices |
| 5 | Data | Big data answers the question | Data describes the past, not the choice |
| 6 | Culture | Change culture directly | Change behaviours; culture follows |
| 7 | Knowledge work | Measure knowledge workers like factory workers | Measure their decisions, not their hours |
| 8 | Corporate functions | Functions exist to be efficient | Functions exist to enable strategy |
| 9 | Planning | A plan is the destination | A plan is the trailhead; reality re-routes |
| 10 | Execution | Execution is separate from strategy | Execution IS strategy in motion |
| 11 | Talent | Pay drives retention | Feeling special drives retention |
| 12 | Innovation | The innovation IS the product | The intervention that lands it is half the job |
| 13 | Capital investment | Sunk cost has value | Value is reset the moment capital embeds |
| 14 | M&A | Buy what we want | Buy what we can give to, not just take from |

Below: the 10 highest-leverage models for operator coaching. Frameworks
9, 10, 11, 12 from `playing_to_win_frameworks.md` cover Strategy ≠
Planning and Customers ≠ Shareholders — those are not repeated here.

## Framework 1 — Competition (Ch 1): Choose a Different Game

**Dominant model:** competition means being better at the same game as
the incumbents. Faster, cheaper, more features, more sales effort.

**Why it breaks:** in any game with multiple competent players, the
best-practice baseline converges. Everyone copies the winner. Operational
effectiveness becomes table stakes. The only winner is the customer
(lower price, no profit pool left for any player).

**Martin's replacement:** competition is the choice to play a
**different game** than the incumbents — different customers, different
value proposition, different activity system. Not better. Different.

**Operator diagnostic:** if your competitor's description of their
business is the same as yours with the names swapped, you are in the
same game and the game has no winner. Find the differentiator that the
incumbent **cannot** copy without dismantling their current business.

**Application to DR:** the Case Flow System sits inside this frame.
Same game = "another agency that runs Meta ads better." Different game
= "the only operator who guarantees signed retainers in a defined
vertical." The vertical lock-in + guarantee combo is the different game.

## Framework 2 — Stakeholders (Ch 2): Customer First, Not Shareholder First

**Dominant model:** the corporation's job is to maximise shareholder
value. All decisions optimise the share price.

**Why it breaks:** share price is a **forecast** of future cash flow.
You cannot directly drive a forecast. You can only drive the underlying
business reality — customer behaviour — which the forecast then reflects.
Optimising for the forecast is optimising for the side effect, not the
cause. Companies that explicitly prioritise customers outperform
companies that explicitly prioritise shareholders, on shareholder
returns, over multi-decade windows.

**Martin's replacement:** the chosen customer comes first. The chosen
employee comes second (they deliver the customer experience). The
shareholder comes third — and reaps the benefit of the first two being
done right.

**Operator diagnostic:** when a decision is framed as "what will the
board / investors / acquirer think?" reframe it as "what does this do
for the chosen customer in the chosen arena?" The customer answer
leads. Everything else follows.

## Framework 3 — Customers (Ch 3): Loyalty Over Satisfaction

**Dominant model:** customer satisfaction surveys, NPS, CSAT — measure
how satisfied customers are and chase the number.

**Why it breaks:** satisfaction is a low bar. A satisfied customer can
still defect. Satisfaction asks "did we meet expectations?" — a
backwards-looking question. It does not predict the next purchase.

**Martin's replacement:** measure **loyalty behaviours** — repeat
purchase, share of wallet, willingness to bring you new business under
their own reputation. Loyalty is built by giving the customer reasons
to choose you on autopilot — habit, identity, switching cost,
relationship.

**Operator diagnostic:** ask, "do our customers buy from us because
they decide to each time, or because they default to us?" Default
buying is loyalty. Decision buying is satisfaction at best, and
vulnerable.

## Framework 4 — Data (Ch 5): The Past Is Not the Choice

**Dominant model:** with enough data, the right strategic answer
emerges. Bigger data sets, better algorithms, fewer wrong calls.

**Why it breaks:** data is a record of what has already happened.
Strategy is a bet on what has not yet happened. No amount of historical
data tells you what to choose for an unprecedented future. The data
illusion: leaders use data to delay the choice, calling it "more
analysis," when the missing input is courage, not numbers.

**Martin's replacement:** use data to **describe the world as it is**
(industry economics, competitor moves, customer behaviour). Then use
**judgement** to make the choice about what to do next. Data is input
to judgement, not a substitute for it.

**Operator diagnostic:** when someone says "we need more data before
deciding," ask "what specific decision-relevant question would more
data answer?" If they can't name it, the data ask is procrastination.

## Framework 5 — Culture (Ch 6): Behaviour First, Then Culture

**Dominant model:** to change a culture, run a culture programme —
posters, off-sites, values workshops, training modules.

**Why it breaks:** culture is the **emergent result** of how people
actually behave inside the organisation, day to day. Posters do not
change behaviour. Reward and consequence change behaviour. Culture then
catches up to behaviour, not the other way round.

**Martin's replacement:** if you want a different culture, change
**what gets measured, what gets rewarded, what gets promoted, and what
gets tolerated**. The culture will reorganise around the new behaviour
pattern within 12-24 months. Trying to change culture without changing
those four levers is theatre.

**Operator diagnostic:** list the five behaviours you say the culture
needs. For each: is it measured? Rewarded? Promoted for? Tolerated when
absent? If "no" to any, the culture statement is just a poster.

## Framework 6 — Knowledge Work (Ch 7): Measure the Decision, Not the Hours

**Dominant model:** measure knowledge workers like industrial workers —
hours logged, units shipped, tickets closed, lines of code.

**Why it breaks:** the output of a knowledge worker is a **decision**.
The quality of the decision matters far more than the quantity of
decisions. A junior engineer can write 1000 lines of bad code. A senior
engineer writes 50 lines that solve the problem. Counting lines
penalises the senior, rewards the junior, and ships worse software.

**Martin's replacement:** identify the **critical decisions** the
knowledge worker is being paid to make. Measure quality of those
decisions over time. Quality = was the choice the right one given
what was knowable at the time, and did it produce the intended outcome?

**Operator diagnostic:** for each knowledge-work role, list the 3-5
decisions where the role's value lives. If you can't name them, you
don't have a knowledge-work role — you have an activity in search of a
purpose.

## Framework 7 — Corporate Functions (Ch 8): Functions Serve Strategy

**Dominant model:** corporate functions (HR, finance, IT, legal,
procurement) exist to be **efficient** — minimise their own cost,
standardise their own processes, deliver service inside their function's
scope.

**Why it breaks:** functions optimised for their own efficiency become
**obstacles to strategy**. Procurement that minimises unit cost kills
the supplier relationship the strategy depends on. Finance that
minimises tax exposure misses the investment the strategy demands. HR
that standardises the talent pool kills the differentiated capability.

**Martin's replacement:** functions exist to **enable the strategic
choices** of the business. They are judged on whether they make the
business's Where-to-Play and How-to-Win more achievable, not on whether
they hit their own internal KPI.

**Operator diagnostic:** for each function, ask "what would this
function do differently if its only goal was to make our How-to-Win
work?" If the answer is "a lot of things," the function is misaligned.

## Framework 8 — Planning (Ch 9): Plan as Trailhead, Not Destination

**Dominant model:** the annual plan is the year's destination. Hit the
plan = success. Miss the plan = failure. The plan is the goal.

**Why it breaks:** the plan was built on assumptions that the year will
falsify within 90 days. Treating the plan as the destination forces
the team to defend the plan against reality instead of adapting to it.

**Martin's replacement:** the plan is a **trailhead** — the best
current guess at where to start walking. Reality re-routes the walk.
The plan is updated, not defended. Strategic discipline is to keep the
**choices** stable (cascade Q1-Q4) while the **tactics** change with
reality.

**Operator diagnostic:** when reality breaks the plan, what gets
re-examined first — the choices, or the tactics? If the choices are
the first to flex, the strategy was never real. If the tactics flex
while the choices hold, that's a working strategy with a healthy plan.

## Framework 9 — Execution (Ch 10): Execution Is Strategy in Motion

**Dominant model:** strategy is set at the top. Execution is the job
of everyone below. When strategy fails, blame execution.

**Why it breaks:** the execution layer makes thousands of micro-choices
every day. Each is a strategic choice in miniature. If the top hasn't
given execution the cascade to choose against, execution defaults to
local optimisation and strategy dies in the middle layer.

**Martin's replacement:** execution **is** strategy, distributed. Every
operator at every level needs the cascade translated to their level so
they can make locally-strategic choices when reality presents an option
the plan didn't anticipate.

**Operator diagnostic:** pick a frontline employee. Ask "what should
you say no to this week, given our strategy?" If they can't answer,
strategy hasn't reached them and execution is on its own.

## Framework 10 — Talent (Ch 11): Feeling Special Beats Pay

**Dominant model:** to attract and retain top talent, pay above market,
stack perks, run polished onboarding, scale benefits.

**Why it breaks:** above a threshold, pay is hygiene. It must be
fair-market or people leave; above that, more pay does not produce
more loyalty. Top performers are not for sale to the highest bidder
indefinitely. They are looking for **something pay can't buy**.

**Martin's replacement:** treat every talented employee as an
**individual** with unique needs, ambitions, and contributions. Make
each one feel that **this** company sees them, uses them well, and
matters to them in a way no other employer can replicate. Mass-market
HR systems destroy this; one-to-one engagement creates it.

**Operator diagnostic:** for each top performer, name the specific
thing about working here that they could not get at the next employer.
If you can't name it for them, they're already partly out the door.

## Framework 11 — Innovation (Ch 12): Design the Intervention, Not Just the Product

**Dominant model:** the hard part of innovation is inventing the
artifact — product, feature, algorithm. Once invented, the business
"just" has to launch it.

**Why it breaks:** half of innovations that work technically die on
the way to market because the **intervention** — how the organisation
inserts the innovation into existing processes, channels, customer
habits, and incentives — is treated as an afterthought.

**Martin's replacement:** design the intervention with the same rigour
as the artifact. Map every process, channel, role, and incentive the
innovation crosses. Redesign each so it lands. The intervention design
is **half the innovation**.

**Operator diagnostic:** list everything that has to change for the
innovation to take hold — sales scripts, comp plans, pricing, support,
marketing, ops. If undesigned before launch, the innovation dies in
the gaps.

---

## Framework 12 — Capital Investment (Ch 13): Value Resets at Embedment

**Dominant model:** capital investments retain their book value over
their depreciation schedule.

**Why it breaks:** the moment capital is embedded, its value is
**reset by the cash flow the embedded asset can now produce, not by
what was paid for it**. Sunk cost is not value.

**Martin's replacement:** at each review, value each embedded asset
by **forward cash flow potential**, not historical cost. Divest the
ones reset to near-zero. Reinvest in ones reset upwards. Stop
defending past spend.

**Operator diagnostic:** for each capital asset, ask "if I were
buying this asset today at market, what would I pay?" If materially
less than book value, the books are lying.
