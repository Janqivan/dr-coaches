## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 Persona process (2026 version) [GEN]
- **When:** new account, or when the account is stuck. He says "I'm not saying this is the way" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU], just how his team does it; "The principle is more important than the execution." [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]
- **Steps:**
  1. *Persona identification* — list every possible persona, ranked by how often each comes up in VOC; pick core ones.
  2. *Deep analysis per persona* — new doc per brand; run one long 10-step persona prompt per persona (only the persona name changes); output is a skeleton to layer manual research on, not as gospel.
  3. *Creative consumption analysis* — ask Parker/Claude to "reverse engineer the search terms" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] the persona would use from their fears/desires; type them into TikTok/YouTube; note creator type, high vs low production, ring light, location, background.
  4. *Persona ranking* — table scoring each persona /10 on revenue, LTV, CAC, strategic priority; average. Don't pick only by what's working in the account or revenue to date.
  5. *Allocate creative* against the ranking.
  - *Part 2:* audit the ad account for underserved personas vs VOC.
- **Deep analysis sections:** Gary Halbert paragraph (overview built from VOC quotes); five Fs (fears, frustrations, failures, fantasies, failed solutions); emotion and trigger moment (what they feel as they pull out their card); awareness + market sophistication; buying journey; cognitive bias map; objection stack; competing solutions; VOC copy phrases; confidence check.
- **The four-question test** — fail one and "I do not believe it to be a persona" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]: What do they already believe about the problem? What have they tried and what failed? What are they afraid of and what do they secretly want? What exact words do they use when talking to themselves about this?
- **Rules:** 3-5 persona tabs (not fewer than three, not more than five), ranked primary / secondary / expansion; docs run 20-30 pages but must stay readable. "a living breathing document" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] — most brands make it once because it is "boring unsexy work" [quote src: YT:c_6XsMe9eXE]. Don't run one prompt and call it personas; challenge Parker and Claude — even an iterated prompt gives some off personas. Watch for "identity overlays": "the gifter" spans many personas; "skeptical scroller" says how they came to the ad, not what they believe. Buying-journey section: built mainly from post-purchase surveys plus Triple Whale attribution — the section he works on most outside Parker.
- **Refresh, don't redo:** periodically give Parker/Claude "Here are my current personas. Here's my voice of customer." [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] and ask for new personas worth promoting to core; re-rank, re-allocate, circulate to the team.
- **Example:** Laura Geller (makeup for mature skin) — see §4.
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU] (2026-06)

### 2.2 Persona-gap / underserved-persona audit [GEN]
- **When:** one of the first prompts on any new account.
- **Steps:** in Parker: "look at my customer reviews and look at my ad account" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]; "cross reference the personas that show up in our reviews" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] vs "what's in our ad account" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]; find personas with high VOC share and low ad share; brief ads for them.
- **Result:** 50% of reviews, 10% of ads → new ads did really well. Caveat: reviews aren't "the gospel" (bias in who leaves them) but a decent indicator.
- **First move on a new account:** pull personas from reviews. The Perfect Jean: ~40,000 reviews scanned; biggest persona = comfort; another = big/plus-size guy (15-18% of reviews). Every persona, headline or script must be backed by evidence (e.g. 10 supporting reviews), not "out of like thin air." [quote src: YT:vD8eLrjAxEM]
- Sources: [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03) · [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06) · [YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2026-02)

### 2.3 Awareness × framework selection (Schwartz) [GEN]
- **Steps:** place the majority audience on the awareness ladder → unaware/problem aware: AIDA or PAS, agitate the problem; solution/product aware: FAB, sell why this one. Reactively analyze top performers' frameworks, then branch the messaging into other frameworks for other awareness stages.
- **Add market sophistication:** stage 1-2 market → a first-of-kind claim is enough; stage 4-5 → "world's best" fails, you need a new mechanism and how you differ.
- **Examples:** bidet brand (PAS, then why a bidet before "why this company" [quote src: YT:MAwkpQWEQS8]); Huel/supplements (FAB); Eight Sleep smart mattress (sophistication) — see §4.
- Sources: [YT:3wLk3D99JyA] (2024-03) · [YT:MAwkpQWEQS8] (2024-10) · [YT:79doYBhLLFU] (2026-06)

### 2.4 Trigger events [GEN/DTC]
- **Definition:** "a trigger event is the moment a problem stops being tolerable" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] — the exact moment someone decides to solve it.
- **Steps:** (1) mine VOC (ad comments, post-purchase surveys, Reddit, reviews — read the longest and most emotive) for trigger events; (2) use them as the foundation of scripts; (3) find more inspiration: ask Claude Code via Parker MCP for "more ads like Quasi's ads" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (AI animation, emotion-led storytelling), save a swipe file, or filter AI animation ads over 10 minutes sorted by impressions.
- **Rules:** don't invent them; "The more specific and detailed, the better." [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]; don't worry about alienating other personas — separate ads per trigger. Ask about the trigger event in every post-purchase survey; run a regular team brainstorm on it.
- **Examples:** acne, teeth whitening, snoring openers (§3); Open Farm dog food (§4).
- Sources: [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08) · [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03) · [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01)

### 2.7 Review / comment mining moves [GEN/DTC]

### 2.10 Competitor and ad-library intelligence [GEN/DTC]

### 2.13 Creative ops system: Notion + Google Sheets + Motion [DTC/GEN]
- **Definition:** creative operations = the systems behind output, "the engine" that lets you ship 100, 200, 300 ads a week; ties performance back to strategy (one of the biggest e-com pain points) and monitors output across sources. Built over 2 years and ~20 versions, for an agency shipping thousands a month (fine under ~5,000 ads/month).
- **Three parts:** Notion = internal production (research, ideation, scripting, shooting, editing) in a master creative database tracked at concept level; Google Sheets = log of all creative from every source (internal, agencies, creators/freelancers, customers) by source and week, for custom charts, no analytics; Motion = performance read fed back mainly to the internal team.
- **Master database fields:** new vs iteration, dates, priority, status, assignees, product/SKU, creator (linked to creator database). Everyone sees each person's workload.
- **Spin-off dashboards:** filtered views (strategist, editor, creator, reviewer) that write back to master; status + time log; "radical transparency". Example: editor Alli has 7 in progress, Sarah 0 in progress and 3 in review → new edit goes to Sarah; lead editor assigns once footage is in.
- **Concept page:** brief (script, additional shots, editor notes; internal info hidden from the creator version) + time log ("strategy developed", "ready for review" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4], manager tagged) so a concept touched 14 March and idle by 15 April gets questioned.
- **Sheet "meta" tab:** one row per concept, not per ad (3 hook variations = 1 row): date, concept code, UID, concept name, # variations, SKU, creative source, type, style, format → formula naming convention pasted into Meta at upload; plus page it runs from (creator or "publication" page for whitelisting), primary text, landing page + URL, Frame.io link, status, launch date.
- **Reports:** weekly output (why did a February week drop from 6 to 4?), by strategist (3 concepts/week vs 1 — slower, or better?), by product/editor/designer; total and by-source output by month — to go from 50 to 100 ads a month "it's important to know what A is first" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4].
- **Six principles regardless of tool:** (1) see workload and output quickly; (2) everyone knows priorities and deadlines each morning; (3) feed ad-account learnings back to strategy; (4) monitor output by source over time; (5) cost per concept, concept share and cost share by source; (6) compare against ad data to kill or scale agencies, freelancers, creators.
- **Scale ladder:** Trello kanban works at 3–4 concepts/week with one strategist; $20K → $50K → $200K → $500K/month spend means ~10 → 20 → 50 ads/week, which breaks simple boards and over-complex sheets.
- **Tools are replaceable:** "All of them do 80 to 90% of the same thing" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4].
- **Ad set naming (2024):** creative source + number + concept (ADCR01 three reasons why; INTERNAL01); iterations get new numbers (ADCR02) and also launch in testing.
- Sources: [YT:EOZp34uDIc4] (2025-04) · [YT:qhVdp32ebWM] (2024-05)
