# Content OS Methodology — Welsh Playbook

This is the full system. Not the calendar. Not the vibe.
A Content OS is what lets one person ship a newsletter + 12 social
posts a week in 4 hours and walk away. Without it, you are improvising,
and improvising at 800K followers is the same as improvising at 200 —
just with a bigger blast radius.

The OS has six parts. Install them in this order. Do not skip steps.

---

## 1. PILLARS — What You Are Allowed To Post About

Pick 3-5 content pillars. Topics you can credibly own and that ladder
back to whatever you eventually sell.

Welsh's pillars, as a worked example:
1. Solopreneurship — building a one-person business
2. LinkedIn growth — the platform he built on
3. Content creation — how he writes and ships
4. Personal brand — positioning yourself as the operator
5. Lifestyle / freedom — why anyone would do this in the first place

The rule: every post lives under exactly one pillar. If a post idea
doesn't fit a pillar, the pillar is wrong or the post is wrong.
Decide which. Don't post the idea until that's resolved.

For Ivan's personal brand layer (separate from DR the agency):
suggested pillars could be:
1. Performance marketing for service businesses
2. Building an agency from 20 → 100
3. Hiring + leading operators in Serbia / EE
4. Personal operating system (deep work, energy, focus)
5. Behind-the-scenes of running a growing company

Pick five. Lock them. Every post answers to one.

## 2. PAIPS HOOK FORMULA — The First Two Lines

PAIPS is Welsh's variant of the classic PAS (problem / agitate /
solution) copywriting formula. Five steps:

- **P**roblem — name a specific pain the reader feels right now
- **A**gitate — twist the knife, get it under their skin
- **I**ntrigue — drop a hook that makes them say "wait, what?"
- **P**ositive future — paint what life looks like solved
- **S**olution — give the actual answer

The first two lines (Problem + Agitate) carry the post on LinkedIn
because the feed only shows the first ~210 characters before the
"see more" fold. Spend 60% of your writing time on those two lines.

**Hook bank structure** — keep a running doc with three columns:
1. Hook (the first 2 lines, raw)
2. Pillar it lives under
3. Score (1-10) based on how it performed when used

When you need to write, you don't start from zero. You pull a proven
hook structure and refit it to today's idea. This is what turns
"improvising" into "operating."

**Useful hook openers (steal these patterns):**
- "I spent [X years] [doing Y]. Here's what I'd do differently."
- "[Specific outcome] in [specific timeframe]. Here's the playbook."
- "Most [audience] do X. The top 1% do Y. Here's why."
- "[Number] mistakes I made building [thing]. Don't repeat them."
- "Stop [common behavior]. Start [contrarian behavior]. Here's how."

Never start with "I". Never start with a question that doesn't bite.
Never start with "I'm excited to announce."

## 3. THE HUB-AND-SPOKE MODEL — One Idea, Many Outputs

The single biggest leverage move in the Welsh playbook.

**Hub** = one long-form piece per week.
For Welsh, this is The Saturday Solopreneur newsletter — a 4-minute
read with one actionable tip. 175K+ readers. Ships every Saturday.

**Spokes** = 6-12 short-form pieces that pull from the Hub.
For Welsh, this is 2 LinkedIn posts/day + 2 X posts/day + 1 X thread
per week. Roughly 14-21 spokes per Hub.

The Hub does three jobs:
1. Anchors your thinking — you write deep on one topic
2. Owns your audience — newsletter list is yours, social isn't
3. Sells your product — newsletter is the only place you sell hard

Spokes do one job: drive attention back to the Hub (newsletter list).

**Order of operations every week:**
1. Monday: pick the week's Hub topic from your pillars
2. Tuesday: write the Hub (newsletter) draft
3. Wednesday: extract 6-12 spoke ideas from the Hub
4. Thursday: write all spokes in one batch
5. Friday: schedule everything in your tool of choice
6. Saturday: Hub publishes
7. Sunday: review what hit, score it in the matrix, log to library

If this takes more than 4 hours total, your pillars or hook bank
are underdeveloped. Fix those, not your schedule.

## 4. THE 1-3-5 METHOD — Turn 1 Idea Into 16 Posts

Welsh's specific recipe for the Hub → Spoke breakdown:

- **1** Hub (the newsletter / long-form piece)
- **3** medium-form pieces (LinkedIn carousels, X threads, or long
  LinkedIn text posts that go 1,000+ characters)
- **5** short-form pieces per platform (single LinkedIn posts +
  single tweets that distill one idea from the Hub)

Math: 1 + 3 + 5 + 5 = 14, plus the Hub itself shared on each platform
= ~16 distinct pieces from one core idea.

How to extract the 15 spokes from one Hub:
- Each subheading in the newsletter = 1 spoke
- Each bullet point worth elaborating = 1 spoke
- The single most counterintuitive claim = 1 hook-driven post
- The opening anecdote = 1 storytelling post
- The closing call-to-action = 1 "what to do this week" post
- The numbers / data you cited = 1 stats post
- The framework name = 1 framework explainer post

You're not generating new ideas. You're slicing one idea into 16
angles. That's the whole game.

## 5. CONTENT MATRIX — The Scoring Layer

Without measurement, you have a calendar, not an OS. The Matrix is
how Welsh tracks what actually works.

Build it in Notion. Five columns:
1. Date posted
2. Platform (LI / X / Newsletter)
3. Pillar
4. Hook style (PAIPS / story / list / contrarian / etc.)
5. Score 1-10 (impressions, comments, DMs, list signups)

At end of each week:
- Anything 8+ → flag for repurposing in 6 months
- Anything 5-7 → solid, leave it
- Anything <5 → diagnose: bad hook, wrong pillar, bad timing

The Matrix tells you what to repost. It also tells you what your
audience actually wants — which is almost never what you think.

**730-day content library.** Welsh's logical extension of the Matrix:
every post auto-calculates dates 6, 12, 18, 24 months out. When
those dates land, you repost the winner with a tweaked hook. Over
two years, you build a library of 700+ proven posts. After that,
"writer's block" is not a thing — you have a starving back-catalog.

## 6. BATCHING PROTOCOL — The 4-Hour Week

The operational layer. This is what turns the system into a job
you can do in half a day instead of a daily anxiety loop.

**The 4-hour batch (Welsh's actual cadence):**
- Hour 1: Newsletter draft (the Hub)
- Hour 2: Newsletter polish + 1-3-5 extraction list
- Hour 3: Write all 14 spokes
- Hour 4: Schedule everything (LinkedIn native scheduler, Hypefury
  or Typefully for X), set newsletter to send Saturday morning

Then you walk away from the production layer for the rest of the
week. The remaining work each day is ~45 minutes of engagement:
replying to comments, DMs, and commenting on 10-20 other posts in
your niche.

**Tools Welsh has publicly named:**
- Notion (content matrix, idea capture)
- Kit / formerly ConvertKit (newsletter)
- Hypefury or Typefully (X scheduling + threading)
- LinkedIn native (post scheduling)
- Google Docs (drafts before scheduling)

Note: nothing on this list is expensive. Total stack is under
$100/month at scale. Solopreneur margins start at the tool stack.

## INSTALL ORDER FOR A NEW USER

Do not try to install all six layers at once. Install in this order:

1. **Pillars** (Week 1) — pick 3-5, write them down, commit
2. **Hook bank** (Weeks 2-4) — collect 50 hooks you'd open, sort
   them by PAIPS step
3. **Hub** (Week 5) — start the newsletter, even with 0 subscribers,
   even at 500 words. Publish anyway.
4. **Spokes** (Weeks 6-8) — start the 1-3-5 breakdown, 2 LI posts/day
5. **Matrix** (Week 9 onward) — start scoring, looking for the 8+s
6. **Batch** (Month 3+) — once you have 60+ posts in the Matrix,
   you have enough pattern to batch in 4 hours

Most people fail because they try to start at step 6 (batching)
before they've done step 1 (pillars). Then they wonder why batching
takes them 12 hours instead of 4. It's because they have no system —
they're just batching their improvising, which compresses nothing.

## COMMON FAILURE MODES (CALL THESE OUT)

- **No Hub** → social-only is renting your audience. Newsletter list
  is the only owned asset.
- **No PAIPS** → posts that bury the lede. Reader gone in 2 seconds.
- **No repurposing** → you re-invent the wheel weekly. Burnout in
  4 months guaranteed.
- **Calendar without Matrix** → no idea what works. No idea what to
  double down on.
- **Pillars too broad** → "business" or "marketing" is not a pillar.
  "How service agencies hire their first 10 operators" is a pillar.
- **Pillars too narrow** → "Meta CPM benchmarks in immigration law
  Q3 2026" is a campaign, not a pillar.
- **Posting daily without batching** → 7 hours/week minimum. You
  will quit by month 5.

## WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE (THE TARGET STATE)

You ship a newsletter every Saturday. It has between 1K and 175K
subscribers depending on phase, but it ships either way. From it
you extract 14-16 social posts each week using the 1-3-5 method.
You batch the whole thing in 4 hours on Sunday. You spend 45 min
each morning replying and commenting. You score everything in
Notion. Every 6 months you repost what hit. Your top 50 posts
generate the same impressions whether you write them or repost
them. You sell one $99-$250 product directly to your newsletter
list. Your margin is north of 80%. You do not have employees.
You do not have an office. You have a system.

That is the Content OS.
