## Framework 1 — The 4 Behaviors of High-Performing CEOs (HBR, May-June 2017)

The headline finding from the CEO Genome Project. Of ~2,000 CEO assessments analyzed against actual business performance, **four behaviors** showed a statistical separation between CEOs who beat expectations and those who didn't. These behaviors were predictive — pedigree, charisma, and confidence were not.

The four:

1. **Decide with speed and conviction**
2. **Engage for impact**
3. **Adapt proactively**
4. **Deliver reliably**

**Operator implication:** the 4 behaviors are not personality traits. They're observable behaviors that show up in the candidate's career record. The Topgrading chronological interview is the right instrument to surface them — because each behavior leaves a trail across past jobs.

> ghSMART public framing on the research design: "data-driven insights from ghSMART into what makes successful CEOs" (CEO Genome project site)

## Framework 2 — Decide with Speed and Conviction (CEO Genome)

The first of the four. The data: CEOs in the top performance tier were **12x more likely** than bottom-tier CEOs to be described as decisive.

**The counterintuitive finding:** decisive CEOs were NOT more right. They were faster. The research showed top-tier CEOs would rather make a slightly-wrong call quickly than a slightly-right call after extended deliberation. Speed-of-decision compounds; perfection-of-decision doesn't.

**What this looks like in candidate evaluation:**
- A-pattern: "I had 70% of the data. I decided. Here's what I'd do differently with hindsight."
- B-pattern: "We needed more data. We pulled in McKinsey. We deliberated for six months."
- C-pattern: "I'm still not sure that decision was right. My team was divided. I went with consensus."

**Operator implication:** the candidate who tells you about extensive analysis as a strength is showing you a weakness. The candidate who tells you about a decision they made too fast — and what they learned — is showing you the A-player signal.

URL: https://hbr.org/2017/05/what-sets-successful-ceos-apart

## Framework 3 — Engage for Impact (CEO Genome)

The second of the four. NOT about likeability. About the ability to align stakeholders around a direction even when those stakeholders disagree.

**The mechanic:** top-tier CEOs spent time mapping stakeholder positions, understood who would resist what, and worked the dissent directly rather than around it. They sought ALIGNMENT, not CONSENSUS.

**The distinction matters:**
- Consensus = everyone agrees (rare, slow, often fake)
- Alignment = everyone commits to the direction even if they disagreed in the room

**What this looks like in candidate evaluation:**
- A-pattern: "I had three skeptics on the exec team. I met with each one. I committed to a 60-day check-in. They committed to execute."
- B-pattern: "I built consensus through a six-week roadshow."
- C-pattern: "We had a tough exec team. Some never bought in. I went around them."

**Operator implication:** "engage for impact" is the answer to the Relationships axis of Geoff's Power Score from a different angle. Power Score asks: do you work through or around people? CEO Genome asks: when you worked through them, did the org actually align?

## Framework 4 — Adapt Proactively (CEO Genome)

The third behavior. The data: top-tier CEOs spent ~50% more time on long-horizon strategy than bottom-tier CEOs — but more importantly, they UPDATED that strategy faster when new information arrived.

**The two halves:**
1. They scanned harder — read more, talked to more outsiders, watched adjacent industries
2. They updated faster — held their priors loosely, rewrote plans when the data changed

**Failure pattern Brad's Topgrading interview surfaces here:** the candidate who can describe a plan but can't describe a moment when they CHANGED that plan. A-player careers have multiple inflection points where the candidate updated. B-player careers have a single thesis stretched across a decade.

**What this looks like in candidate evaluation:**
- A-pattern: "We built the company for the SMB segment for 3 years. The data shifted. We pivoted to mid-market in 90 days. Here's what broke. Here's what we learned."
- B-pattern: "We stayed the course. Discipline matters."
- C-pattern: "The market changed. Our investors made us pivot."

URL: https://hbr.org/2017/05/what-sets-successful-ceos-apart

## Framework 5 — Deliver Reliably (CEO Genome)

The fourth behavior — and the most boring-sounding, which is exactly why most boards underweight it.

**The data:** top-tier CEOs were **15x more likely** than bottom-tier CEOs to deliver consistently on commitments. NOT 15% more likely. 15 TIMES.

**The mechanic:** these CEOs ran predictable forecasting cycles, hit their numbers, met their dates, and when they missed they communicated early and re-baselined honestly. The board could MODEL them.

**Why this matters more than the other three:** decisiveness, engagement, and adaptability can be theatrical. Delivery can't. Either the numbers hit or they don't. Either the dates moved or they didn't.

**What this looks like in candidate evaluation:**
- A-pattern: "We committed to $X by Q4. We hit $X minus 4% and we communicated the gap to the board in week 9 of 13."
- B-pattern: "We missed Q4 but Q1 was strong."
- C-pattern: "Our numbers are hard to compare year over year because we had several reorganizations."

**Cross-link to Geoff:** this is why the Topgrading interview's chronological structure works. Delivery leaves an audit trail across jobs. The candidate either hit their numbers or didn't, year by year.

## Framework 6 — The Charisma Myth Finding (CEO Genome Project)

A specific empirical finding from the ghSMART dataset that contradicts standard board hiring practice.

**The finding:** boards consistently selected candidates who appeared charismatic, polished, confident, and pedigreed. The ghSMART data showed those traits **did not predict CEO performance**. Some of the highest-performing CEOs in the database were unremarkable in interview — and many of the most impressive interviewers underperformed.

**The operator implication:** the candidate who shows up polished is the candidate every board over-selects on. The selection bias is automatic. The countermeasure is the structured Topgrading interview — because it forces the conversation off the impression-axis and onto the record-axis.

**The deeper point:** if charisma doesn't predict, what does? The 4 behaviors. None of which are visible in a 60-minute Voodoo-Hiring interview (per Geoff's frame in *Who*). They're only visible in a 3-4 hour chronological walk with TORC pressure.

URL: https://ceogenome.com/

## Framework 7 — The Introvert Surplus Finding (CEO Genome Project)

A specific data point worth isolating. The CEO Genome data showed introverted CEOs were **slightly more likely** to exceed expectations than extroverts. The board's gut preference for "executive presence" — usually code for extroversion — predicted the wrong direction.

**Why introverts over-perform (per the ghSMART research):**
- They listen more in stakeholder conversations (Engage for Impact)
- They deliberate written-form before committing (Decide with Speed once data is in)
- They're less performative under pressure (Deliver Reliably without theater)

**Operator implication:** Ivan's read on candidates should not down-weight introversion. In the Integrator seat — where the role is internal coordination, not external evangelism — introversion is a feature, not a bug.

## Framework 8 — The SmartAssessment Methodology (ghSMART firm IP)

The firm's proprietary executive evaluation method. Mechanically: a roughly 4-hour chronological interview covering the candidate's entire career, conducted by a trained ghSMART advisor, scored against 30+ leadership traits, producing a detailed report with hiring recommendation.

**What it shares with Brad's Topgrading interview:**
- Chronological backbone (career walk, job by job)
- Length (3-4+ hours)
- Pattern detection across jobs (consistency tests)

**What's distinctively ghSMART:**
- Trait library — 30+ ghSMART-defined competencies scored individually
- Comparison against the ghSMART database (the candidate is rated against 17,000+ prior assessments)
- Output is a written report with a hire/no-hire recommendation and developmental flags
- Used pre-investment by private equity firms acquiring portfolio companies (the "CEO assessment before close" use case)

**Why this matters for DR:** Ivan won't run a SmartAssessment internally. But the existence of the methodology is the proof point — there is an entire firm built on the thesis that the 4-hour chronological interview, calibrated against a large database, is the most predictive hiring instrument available. When Ivan defends the time spent on Topgrading interviews, this is the data behind the defense.

URL: https://ghsmart.com/solution/executive-selection/

## Framework 9 — The 90%+ Hiring Success Rate Claim (ghSMART firm IP)

The ghSMART firm publicly states a **90%+ hiring success rate** on placements made through its methodology, versus the industry baseline of roughly 50% (the "Voodoo Hiring" baseline Geoff names in *Who*).

**What "success" means in the ghSMART framing:**
- Hire is rated A-player by their boss at the 12-month mark
- Hire stays in the role through expected tenure
- Hire delivers against the Scorecard outcomes

**The operator implication for DR:**
- Baseline (no Topgrading): ~50% hit rate. Half your senior hires are mis-hires. At 5-15x salary mis-hire cost, that's ruinous.
- With Topgrading: 90%+ hit rate. The 4-hour interview pays for itself the first time it stops one mis-hire.

The 90% number is what the entire Topgrading and ghSMART business is built on. It's the proof that the methodology is worth the time. Without that claim, the methodology is just a long interview.
