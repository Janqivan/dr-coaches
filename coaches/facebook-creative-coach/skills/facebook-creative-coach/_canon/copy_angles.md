## 1. Core positions (ranked by mentions × specificity)

- **Offer first and instantly clear (Heath's lead-gen lens).** A good offer carries average creative, not vice versa; no leads/sales is "nearly always an offer problem". - **Specific beats vague; vague reads as puffery and raises skepticism.** Levinger (8): "Vague claims feel like marketing. ## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Social Judgment Theory / belief ladder (Levinger)
- When she uses it: judging any headline, especially purchase ads; replaces "is this headline good?" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] with where does it land vs the viewer's current belief?. Single-source framework (2026-04), from a 1961 psychology model.
- Steps: 1. Write the scroller's current category belief (anchor belief) in their words. 2. Sort each headline into a zone — indifference ("that's true," move on; "where most ads sit" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]), acceptance ("I didn't think about that before" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]), rejection ("I don't believe anything you're saying" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]). 3. Aim to move them one step: challenge the belief or open their mind, never leap into rejection (ridiculous claims, claims outside their experience). 4. For purchase ads ask: what do they believe most that blocks buying, and does this ad move them one step closer? 5. Test mid-range intensity first ("most marketers just go all the way to five" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]), push harder only if needed. 6. Run several ladder rewrites at once, "just differently enough" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]. 7. A true purchase ad carries an offer (two-for-one, bundles, deals). Put ingredient jargon on the lander, not the ad.
- Her example: rewrites judged live (§4) — "More cushion is making your feet weaker" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] picked for barefoot shoes; #1 bestseller scar serum only confirms she needs a serum.
- Sources: [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04) · mentions 15

### Valence × intensity emotion map (Levinger)
- When she uses it: choosing which headlines, hooks and stories to build, and matching music and delivery to words. Based on James A. Russell (1980).
- Steps: 1. Place the customer's emotion on valence (thumbs up/down) and intensity. 2. Zone 1 positive/low (calm, reassuring); Zone 2 positive/high (joy, pride, confidence, hype); Zone 3 negative/low (annoyed, frustrated, fatigued); Zone 4 negative/high (panic, loss, anxiety) — "This is typically where marketers love to live" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A] but "very expensive to live there" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]. 3. Test across zones, including mixed: intense language with calm delivery, or the reverse. 4. Keep word, music and delivery congruent (zone-4 music needs insecure words, otherwise an "I don't believe you" [quote src: YT:bd9MGQ0-Vqc] reaction). 5. Don't open in zone 1/2 and then dip into fear urgency. 6. Prefer specific, complex emotions over core ones; pair deliberately (bittersweet = grief + joy).
- Her example: "your beard is fantastic" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] vs "god level beard" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] — same message, two intensities; a calm "therapist style" brand vs golf buyers who want funny and direct.
- Sources: [YT:HuI3o3TBI4A, YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-03, 2025-05, 2026-06) · mentions 12

### Three selves: actual / ideal / ought (Levinger)
- When she uses it: iteration — "where I personally believe iteration lives" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]; from self-discrepancy theory.
- Steps: 1. Identify which self the category switches on. Actual = me now ("I wish I could lose 30 pounds, but I also love Snickers bars" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); Ideal = who I want to be ("Be the runner you were always meant to be" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); Ought = who I should be — "should" is the ought word. 2. Write the same claim per self (sun oil: "your level of tan" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] / "the tan that you wish you had" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] / "everyone expects you to use" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). 3. Show a version "maybe two steps ahead" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] — "this is not aspirational, it's attainable" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]; don't be too aspirational too quickly.
- Her example: Nike = ideal; dentistry = ought (Hott pushed back that dentistry has future-self material; she held it's ought). She labeled Hott's closer "I don't know what you're waiting for" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] ought-self activation.
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:HuI3o3TBI4A, YT:FTotRZL9vAg] (2025-05, 2026-03, 2025-04) · mentions 7

### Buying stages: trigger / exploration / evaluation / purchase (Levinger)
- When she uses it: matching copy to where the buyer is; same stop visual can serve any stage if copy changes.
- Steps: 1. Trigger — the moment a need becomes conscious (life event, social comparison, internal frustration tipping point, external disruption); "Trigger creative is not about your product" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]. 2. Exploration — not looking at products yet; speak to the category (Exploration is where we tell them about the industry). 3. Evaluation — short list; they want "a logical reason to make an emotional decision" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]; social proof works; reframe features as decision confidence ("Here's why people like you choose our product" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y] + why they didn't regret it). 4. Purchase — decision is made; remove friction and reasons not to move; "Please just let them buy" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU].
- Sources: [YT:po85BKDTG-Y, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-04, 2026-06, 2026-02) · mentions 7

### Micro-moments: "market to the when, don't market to the who" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (Levinger)
- When she uses it: generating angles under broad targeting.
- Steps: 1. List every moment someone needs the product (hair loss: pregnancy, gender transition, menopause, PCOS, alopecia). 2. Go to sub-moments ("we got to get tedious" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]: hairline only, patch at back, postpartum spot). 3. Write the scene, not the demographic (57-year-old woman getting dressed for lunch with a friend who just lost weight, jeans don't fit). 4. Make it relevant "on a Wednesday or a Tuesday" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU].
- Sources: [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2025-12, 2026-06) · mentions 5 · related guest material: Mirella Crespi ("I feel broken" [quote src: YT:_9f0rTKKIOY]), Lucas Hamby (go one layer deeper than the surface problem).

### Trigger events (Cooper)
- When he uses it: any problem-solution brand; lead with the moment instead of benefits/features — "a blue ocean" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] vs the red ocean of shared claims.
- Steps: 1. Find "the moment a problem stops being tolerable" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]. 2. Open on it in first person. 3. Needn't be as blunt as "my wife left me" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ].
- His example: acne "I started angling my face in photos before I turned 16" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]; whitening "Nobody told me to stop smiling in photos, I just did." [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]; snoring "My wife started sleeping in the guest room." [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] A toothbrush trigger-event ad was #2 in its library by impressions.
- Sources: [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08) · mentions 3

### Awareness × sophistication script selection (Cooper) and awareness expansion (Denney)
- When they use it: Cooper when choosing a script framework; Denney only when analyzing an account ("I never ever start" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] a brief from it).
- Steps (Cooper): 1. Infer each persona's awareness from VOC. 2. Unaware/problem aware → AIDA or PAS; solution/product aware → FAB. 3. Check market sophistication — at launch Eight could say "the world's first smart mattress" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]; by 2026 (stage 4–5) you need a new mechanism and how you differ. 4. Branch top performers' messaging into other stages.
- Steps (Denney): 1. Audit which stage most of your messaging speaks to. 2. Unaware = hardest but most scalable (education, curiosity, demographic call-outs 40+, 50+, busy executives). 3. Product aware ("three reasons why you should try X" [quote src: YT:hSy6X065x0g], testimonials). 4. Most aware (offer-front) converts but least scalable cold.
- Examples: bidet brand sold why a bidet before "why this company" [quote src: YT:MAwkpQWEQS8] (Cooper); Loop earplugs "here's why you need to try our new and improved earplugs" [quote src: YT:hSy6X065x0g] (Denney).
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU, YT:3wLk3D99JyA, YT:wRA2VgYIDVs, YT:ZydGeio1DGQ] (2026-06, 2024-03, 2025-01, 2025-12)

### Messaging ladder / scalability lens (Denney, learned from Hott)
- When she uses it: briefing and reading test results.
- Steps: 1. Place messages on a ladder from offer-based (narrow) up to routine/benefit (broad). 2. Test messages that target different journey points side by side. 3. Expect the low-funnel message to win out of the gate and the broader one to run longest. 4. Push winners further up-funnel to extend life.

### Primary text mechanics (Heath)
- When he uses it: every lead-gen and product ad.

## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree

**What changed**
- **From which format/angle/offer to how do we communicate this message** — Levinger (2026-06): creative strategy used to be formats, angles, offers; now it is delivery and emotion. Denney (2026-04): "formats alone are not enough to grow on Meta" in 2026. [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:ePcEcT2PH8E]
- **Persuasion → resonance; pitch → micro story.** Levinger: "this year it's all about resonance more than persuasion of any kind" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]; "participation era" replaces "direct response era" [quote src: YT:zDHkSSsjFDw]; AIDA built like 2018 flatlines. Cottrell reversed (2025-11): "I was wrong" that story didn't matter — AI slop makes story matter more. [YT:UAvSFd3qF8c, YT:-q2lqfNysUs]
- **Call-outs moved from demographics to situations** post-Andromeda (Cottrell 2026-08; Levinger micro-moments). [YT:nUTzR9ilTts, YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]
- **Market sophistication rose** — "world's best"/"world's first" claims that worked ~2019-20 fail in stage 4–5 markets; need a new mechanism (Cooper 2026-06). [YT:79doYBhLLFU]
- **Consumers want directness, not "bestie" empathy, and price honesty** (Levinger study 2026-05): "Just be straight with me… stop trying to be my friend"; "be warm, be human, but please be direct"; saving-money angles "especially in this economy" (Savannah). [YT:qUn6OV_70fQ, YT:3TzdwzAG1X8]
- **Relevance through words matters more** as algorithms target broadly and audiences fragment (Hott 2026: "Relevance... is everything" [quote src: YT:6jvIXIjieyA]; Cooper 2026-07). [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q]
- **AI in copy:** Cooper (2026) — Claude copy with no context is weak; use a copywriting skill/project. Levinger: "you don't need AI when you understand what the people are doing". Guest Harry Delmege: AI hands juniors years of bad copy advice. Current cold winner (Denney 2025-01): educational "knowledge share" listicles teaching the broader industry. [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q, YT:RCrvd8pTUzo, YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]
- **Ad-LP congruency:** Denney moved from "little needed" (Thesis era) to "massive effect" for big strategic pushes. [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM]

**Disagreements**
- **Awareness levels as a planning tool.** For: Cooper (scripts by stage), Savannah. Against/qualified: Denney — "I really caution people against thinking too deeply about those awareness levels when ideulating creative" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg]; Hott — full-funnel ads built "like you would an infomercial" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] take someone from no awareness to purchase in one ad; guest Anze Mikac — ask "unaware of what?", truly problem-unaware audiences mostly don't exist; Levinger — "Awareness does not equal intent." [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] [YT:79doYBhLLFU, YT:ZydGeio1DGQ, YT:KSm0pvevV9k, YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]
- **Us-vs-them.** For: Cottrell ("twist the knife" [quote src: YT:k533N5RxXPY]), Hott (name the competitor), Denney (often the first win). Against: Levinger — 65% turned off; punching down "makes the brand look insecure"; retire the "unlike other brands" hook. Legal caution: comparison claims must be objectively true (speaker unclear; guest Ryan McKenzie on lawsuits). [YT:505rIrOKhWI, YT:qUn6OV_70fQ]
- **Discounts/LTOs.** Levinger "hates discounts": LTOs aren't believable, bundle/upsell instead; "Discounts are not going to close the gap" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]. Hott: customers "overwhelmingly want to feel like they're getting a deal"; don't drop the LTO when competitors run one ("A lot of people do fall for that") — Levinger concedes it depends; she reframes Hott's "findable discount" as resource guarding.
