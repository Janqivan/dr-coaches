# 24 Assets — Framework Map

**Author:** Daniel Priestley
**Book:** *24 Assets: Create a Digital, Scalable, Valuable and Fun Business That Will Thrive in a Fast Changing World* (Rethink Press, 2017)
**Source treatment:** Framework structures described in own words for synthetic-coach use. Book text is NOT reproduced. Each of the 24 assets is named, mapped to the relevant quadrant of the matrix, and described mechanically (what it is, how to build it, how it lifts enterprise value) so the AIPriestley skill can apply it. To read Priestley's full treatment — including the case stories, the asset audits, and the funding-vs-bootstrap analysis — get the book.

## Why this book exists (the operator's view)

By 2017, Priestley's own portfolio (Dent Global, ScoreApp, Triumphant Events, Rethink Press) had crossed the 7-8 figure threshold on lean headcount. *24 Assets* is his answer to the question that follows *Key Person of Influence* and *Oversubscribed*: how does a personally-led business scale without becoming founder-dependent or capital-hungry? The book reframes "scale" away from headcount and venture-funded burn, toward owning 24 specific leverageable assets that compound and detach revenue from the founder's hours.

Position in the Priestley sequence: *Entrepreneur Revolution* (worldview) → *Key Person of Influence* (authority engine) → *Oversubscribed* (demand engine) → **24 Assets (scale engine)**.

## The Four Quadrants (the core architecture)

*24 Assets* is built around **four quadrants × six assets = 24 assets**. The four quadrants are:

| Quadrant | What it owns | Why it matters |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Intellectual** | Ideas, frameworks, methodology, expertise | Drives premium pricing; the "why us" |
| **Brand** | Identity, trust, attention, public surface | Drives customer acquisition and pricing power |
| **System** | Processes, products, technology, operating machinery | Drives founder-independence and gross margin |
| **Customer / Community / Cash** | Relationships, audiences, financial assets | Drives recurring revenue and enterprise resilience |

Each quadrant contains six named assets. A business that owns 18+ of the 24 assets at meaningful depth is, by Priestley's standard, a **Small Giant** — premium, lean, durable, sellable, and oversubscribed by default.

**Enterprise-value principle:** an unleveraged service business sells for 1-3x earnings (essentially the founder's salary). A 24-asset business sells for 5-10x+ EBITDA because the assets continue producing without the founder. Filling the matrix IS the valuation play.

## Quadrant 1 — Intellectual Assets (six)

The IP layer. These are the proprietary ideas, methods, and bodies of work that make the business non-substitutable.

### Asset 1.1 — Vision and Culture
**What:** Articulated vision, mission, and cultural values the team can recite and customers can feel.
**Build:** founder writes vision; values sessions; embed language in hiring and meetings.
**EV lift:** A-player retention, faster decisions, acquirer confidence in self-running culture.

### Asset 1.2 — Brand Story
**What:** Founder origin, the why, the customer-transformation arc, the future-state vision.
**Build:** write origin, transformation, and future stories; use in every podcast, talk, deck, onboarding.
**EV lift:** premium pricing, non-cloneable brand, lower acquisition cost.

### Asset 1.3 — Methodology / IP
**What:** Proprietary frameworks, named methods (e.g. "5 P's", "Oversubscribed Method"). Owned, named, copyrighted.
**Build:** turn what the business does into a named, sequenced, teachable method; trademark; document in book / course / certification.
**EV lift:** premium pricing for the method (not the labour), licensing potential, defensibility, founder-independence.

### Asset 1.4 — Strategic Plan
**What:** Documented 1-year / 3-year / 10-year plan with measurable milestones. A living operating document, not slides.
**Build:** quarterly rhythm, 90-day rocks, annual strategy session, leadership-team owned.
**EV lift:** acquirers pay for clarity-of-future-cash-flows; banks lend against documented plans.

### Asset 1.5 — Education / Training
**What:** Training program (internal for team, external for customers) that captures and transmits the IP.
**Build:** modular training; onboard new hires through it; sell external version as profit centre or lead source.
**EV lift:** faster ramp, external training becomes lead asset, reduces key-person dependency.

### Asset 1.6 — Data and Research
**What:** Proprietary data the business collects through operating — benchmarks, conversion rates, outcomes.
**Build:** instrument every interaction; publish aggregate insights; use data to defend the methodology with proof.
**EV lift:** unique data is the moat in an AI-commoditized world; acquirers pay aggressively for proprietary datasets.

## Quadrant 2 — Brand Assets (six)

The public-facing trust and attention layer. These are the assets that make customers choose this business over alternatives.

### Asset 2.1 — Visual Identity
**What:** Logo, type, colour, voice, design system. Coherent and recognizable.
**Build:** invest in a proper brand identity once; apply consistently across web, deck, email, product, packaging.
**EV lift:** raises perceived price floor 20-40%; reduces friction in every commercial conversation.

### Asset 2.2 — Marketing Materials
**What:** Sales decks, one-pagers, case studies, brochures, video assets — the on-shelf artefacts that close when the founder is not in the room.
**Build:** systematic library, indexed and updated quarterly; each major segment has its own materials.
**EV lift:** sales productivity rises; founder removed from individual calls; acquirer sees a sellable machine.

### Asset 2.3 — Publication (book / signature work)
**What:** A book, white paper, or flagship public work. The KPI "Publish" rung.
**Build:** write the book; self-publish if needed; the book is credential, lead magnet, speaking platform, and IP container in one.
**EV lift:** premium pricing, inbound leads, speaking opportunities, recruiting magnet. Highest-leverage marketing asset a B2B operator can build.

### Asset 2.4 — Digital Presence
**What:** Website that ranks and converts, plus a content library (podcast, blog, YouTube, social) that compounds attention.
**Build:** SEO foundation, conversion-optimized landing pages, consistent content on one core channel.
**EV lift:** inbound flow independent of paid; compounding traffic asset.

### Asset 2.5 — PR / Media
**What:** Earned media — features, podcasts, stages. The KPI "Profile" rung made tangible.
**Build:** systematic outreach, quarterly podcast pitches, media kit, industry award applications.
**EV lift:** third-party validation the brand cannot self-claim; drives trust and acquisition.

### Asset 2.6 — Reputation / Social Proof
**What:** Testimonials, case studies, reviews, referenceable customers.
**Build:** capture proof at every delivery moment; document before/after; secure permission.
**EV lift:** cheapest conversion lever; acquirers discount businesses with weak proof; shortens sales cycle.

## Quadrant 3 — System Assets (six)

The operational machinery. These assets make the business run without the founder doing the work.

### Asset 3.1 — Productized Service
**What:** Repeatable, scope-fixed offers with clear deliverables, pricing, timelines. Not bespoke per client.
**Build:** take the best-selling bespoke service and productize; lock scope; document delivery; price for repeatability.
**EV lift:** gross margin expansion (no scope creep); junior team can deliver; acquirer values predictable revenue.

### Asset 3.2 — Technology / Platform
**What:** Software, internal tools, or platform the business runs on. SaaS, automation, proprietary tooling.
**Build:** invest in tech that removes manual labour or creates network effects; own it; do not over-rely on third parties.
**EV lift:** tech assets command higher multiples than service revenue; reduces opex over time.

### Asset 3.3 — Operations / SOPs
**What:** Documented standard operating procedures for every recurring task.
**Build:** record processes as you do them (Loom + checklist); build a wiki; owners accountable for currency.
**EV lift:** removes key-person risk; reduces training time; acquirer pays premium for documented operations.

### Asset 3.4 — Customer Delivery System
**What:** Systematic delivery model that ensures every customer gets the promised outcome.
**Build:** standardize onboarding, milestones, success criteria, offboarding; measure NPS at each step.
**EV lift:** retention and referrals rise; acquirer sees recurring revenue that holds through transition.

### Asset 3.5 — Sales Process
**What:** Documented, repeatable sales process — pipeline stages, scripts, qualification criteria, conversion targets.
**Build:** map the journey; tighten qualification; document scripts; train against the playbook.
**EV lift:** founder removed from sales; predictable conversion economics; acquirer values sales independence.

### Asset 3.6 — Quality / Compliance
**What:** Systems that ensure outputs meet standard — QA checklists, audits, certifications, risk frameworks.
**Build:** define the bar; build the check process; audit regularly; document non-conformances and fixes.
**EV lift:** reduces rework; lowers liability; in regulated industries determines sellability.

## Quadrant 4 — Customer / Community / Cash Assets (six)

The relationship and financial asset layer. These are the assets that determine ongoing revenue resilience.

### Asset 4.1 — Customer List / CRM
**What:** Clean, segmented database of current, past, prospective customers. Owned, not rented.
**Build:** capture every interaction into one CRM; segment by stage, value, behaviour; maintain hygiene weekly.
**EV lift:** the list is the most direct enterprise asset; acquirers value clean lists 2-3x higher.

### Asset 4.2 — Community
**What:** Owned community of customers, prospects, adjacent professionals — events, mastermind, online group, alumni.
**Build:** create the gathering (summit, mastermind, forum); maintain as strategic asset, not marketing tactic.
**EV lift:** generates referrals, retention, case studies for free; defensibility against AI commoditization.

### Asset 4.3 — Partnerships / Alliances
**What:** Documented partnerships with referral sources, complementary providers, channel partners. The KPI "Partnerships" rung.
**Build:** identify 10-20 entities with overlapping customers; build formal alliances with rev-share or co-marketing.
**EV lift:** acquisition cost falls; acquirer values channel relationships as transferable revenue.

### Asset 4.4 — Recurring Revenue
**What:** Revenue that recurs without re-selling — retainers, subscriptions, memberships, licenses.
**Build:** convert one-time engagements into recurring structures wherever defensible.
**EV lift:** largest single multiplier on EV; 70%+ recurring trades at 2-4x the multiple of a project business.

### Asset 4.5 — Financial Systems
**What:** Bookkeeping, management accounts, cash reserves, debt structure, tax optimization.
**Build:** monthly accounts; 90-day cash forecast; 6-month reserve; tax-efficient structure; owner separated from business finances.
**EV lift:** acquirers walk on messy books; clean financials are table stakes; cash reserve = survivability and negotiation leverage.

### Asset 4.6 — Equity / Ownership
**What:** Equity in adjacent ventures, ownership structure, team share scheme, exit-readiness documentation.
**Build:** legal structure that supports investment; clean cap table; employee share scheme; documented exit options.
**EV lift:** affects the multiple directly at sale; clean equity = faster, higher-multiple exit.

## How to use this file inside the /priestley skill

1. **Run the 24-asset diagnostic.** Walk the 24 assets. Mark each as: empty / partial / strong. The empty boxes ARE the strategy — they are next quarter's rocks.
2. **Prioritize by quadrant gap.** Most operators are over-built in one quadrant (often Brand) and skeletal in another (often System). The gap quadrant gets investment.
3. **Quadrant rotation.** Do not try to build all 24 at once. Pick the quadrant with the biggest EV-lift gap; build 2-3 assets per quarter; rotate.
4. **Link to enterprise value, always.** *24 Assets* is a valuation book disguised as an operations book. Frame every recommendation as "this lifts the multiple at sale".
5. **DR's likely gaps:** Quadrant 1 — Case Flow methodology under-documented (1.3), data asset weak (1.6). Quadrant 2 — book not yet written (2.3); PR asset modest (2.5). Quadrant 3 — productized service partial (3.1); SOPs in-progress (3.3); tech platform absent (3.2). Quadrant 4 — CRM hygiene gap (4.1); community asset under-developed (4.2).
6. **Never substitute these summaries for the book.** For case stories, asset audits, and the funding-vs-bootstrap analysis, point at *24 Assets* (Rethink Press, 2017) and the Dent Global YouTube channel.
