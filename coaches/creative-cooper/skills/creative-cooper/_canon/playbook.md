## 1. Working now (2026) — ranked plays

Ranked by how often he repeats it in 2026 × how specific the evidence is.

**1. Persona-first: find the underserved persona, then make "stupid simple" ads for it.**
- What to make: cross-reference voice of customer (reviews, surveys, tickets, comments) against the ad account, tagged by persona. Find the persona with a big share of customer voice and a small share of ads/spend, and give it plain, specific ads.
- Why per him: "You don't have a creative problem." [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] / "You have a personas problem." [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]. A persona is psychographic (how they decide, what they fear, what makes them pull out the card), not a demographic. Examples: a beta brand with "50% of reviews, 10% of uh ads" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]; a long-run client whose older-men persona now holds 3 of the top 4 ads with undesigned statics (persona, not design).
- Who: any account with enough VOC. GEN.
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU] (2026-06), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06). → msg §1.2, §1.3, §2.1-2.2; test §1.7, F22

**2. Pick an end of the spectrum and lean all the way in.**
- What to make: either (a) super ugly/organic (yappers, faceless hands-on demos, group ugly ads, founder ads, real podcasts) or (b) obviously an ad with strong DR copy (B-roll mashup + DR script + AI voiceover, AI animations, wall-of-text). Nothing in between.
- Why per him: half-ugly ads leak "I've paid someone to read a script" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] through the soundtrack, tripod, setup or location, and people stop watching. "The middle ground is where ads go to die." [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] AdCrate is both "super AI forward" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k] and "super ugly", and both ends work.
- Who: GEN. Same rule for statics (clarity vs curiosity).
- Sources: [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07). → craft §1.1, §2.1

**3. Yapper ads (unscripted creator storytelling) instead of scripted UGC.**
- What to make: a creator who can talk for 1-2 minutes and ideally has felt the pain. Brief = structured hook + talking points (pains/benefits to hit) + reference yapper ads. Then let them riff. Handheld or phone propped, in the place people would really talk about the product (car, bed, bathroom, kitchen counter, walking).
- Why per him: scripted UGC "does not work on paid social anymore"; personality is "way more likely to work". Yappers are UGC's evolution and one form of ugly ad. "If you script it for them, you're going to ruin it" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w].
- Who: GEN; strongest evidence in food, beauty, supplements.
- Sources: [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06). → craft §1.3, §2.3, §3.5; msg §3

**4. AI animation ads (Pixar, claymation, song-based).**
- What to make: animated story ads built in Claude → Nano Banana stills → Kling 3.0 / Seedance (via Higgsfield). Talking characters, voiceover-only, or a Suno song as the audio. Lock script and visuals before animating; first image sets "the world" and is reused as reference.
- Why per him: "AI animation ads are crushing it in ad accounts right now" [quote src: YT:A29szOW7YDE]; fast, cheap, open loops are easy. One of five working styles in a 500-account / $1B+ spend analysis. Works regardless of industry, age group, e-com or local business. Script plus visual direction decides great vs okay.
- Who: GEN (he explicitly includes local business).
- Sources: [YT:A29szOW7YDE] (2026-05), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08). → craft §1.4; test §1.4, F3

**5. Trigger-event stories.**
- What to make: open on the exact moment the problem stopped being tolerable, agitate with repeat incidents, discovery, mechanism, payoff. Pulled from real VOC, never invented. Long versions (up to 10 minutes) work with older audiences, often as AI animation.
- Why per him: "a trigger event is the moment a problem stops being tolerable" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]; leading with it makes the viewer feel heard and is "a blue ocean". Quazi's #1-#3 ads by impressions were trigger-event AI animations, ~125 variations live.
- Who: any problem-solution offer. GEN/DTC.
- Sources: [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08), [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03). → craft §2.4; msg §1.9, §2.4; test §4.8, F21

**6. Customer words as the headline/hook ("customers are your copywriters" [quote src: YT:tVXjXJSEjeA]).**
- What to make: mine reviews, Reddit, ad comments, surveys, tickets for first-person lines that make sense to a stranger; use them verbatim (even slightly ungrammatical) as static headlines, video hooks and script lines.
- Why per him: customer-to-customer voice beats a feed of generic claims; "the copy is already written in our prospect's head" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]. Mined angles (beard dandruff from "page seven of a Reddit thread" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]; senior-dog comment with 46 likes) became evergreen winners.
- Who: GEN; needs a review/comment corpus.
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2026-02), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:aD7vKl1nkcE] (2026-07). → craft §1.7, §2.15; msg §1.5

**7. Make the invisible visible (intentionally unrealistic AI visuals).**
- What to make: render the feeling: fire/red glow on the painful body part, thorns around feet, ring marks from socks, a sweat patch. Ask Claude how to make the invisible visible from review/comment problems.
- Why per him: a visceral pain shot you could almost never get from a creator; anyone with that feeling stops. Hollow leg-marks hook ad was #1 for days, top 5 for ~1.5 weeks.
- Who: GEN/DTC, mostly pain/body problems.
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06). → craft §2.11; test §1.5

**8. Statics from the 12-format list, headline-first.**
- What to make: in priority order: headline + benefits, testimonial, offer, persona, collection, comparison, publication, press hit, native, apology, post-it, meme. One oversized headline (25-40% of frame), product secondary, social proof smallest.
- Why per him: statics "can and do perform at the top of the funnel when they're done correctly" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q]; ~90% of 12 months of AdCrate static winners were comparison, headline + callouts, or offer. The 12 cover ~95-98% of what his team makes.
- Who: GEN; strongest in DTC.
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11). → craft §1.6, §2.5, §4.3

**9. Hyper-relevance: one persona per ad (and per funnel).**
- What to make: each ad made for one persona, called out in copy or shown purely visually. Separate funnels per persona (Grüns runs weight loss, pre-menopause, menopause, male fertility, gut health, "girly").
- Why per him: broad relevance was the old advice; now zero in on one person. Don't worry about alienating other personas; they get their own ads. Hollow Socks persona myth-bust ("every tradesperson believes these three things about work socks" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]) ran #3-4 by impressions.
- Who: GEN/LEADGEN (Hormozi statics are his non-e-com proof).
- Sources: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07). → craft §1.12; msg §1.14

**10. Negative-framing and fake-exposé hooks.**
- What to make: open negative ("proving this is a scam" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ], "exposing this", "I'm going to try this because it's not even that good" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ], "I might get in trouble for this" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]) and flip into the pitch.
- Why per him: people don't expect ads to start negatively; it opens a loop. Works for small brands. Laura Geller fake exposé = "a masterclass".
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06). → craft §3.1, §3.6; msg §3

**11. Challenge / gamified ads.**
- What to make: a challenge with a mechanic tied to the offer (Carpe: 1% off per wheelie spin) or a low-lift test ("see if I can get this Amazon knife sharper than a razor blade" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]), flavor rankings.
- Why per him: harder to pull off, but "when you get them right, they will absolutely crush" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]. Carpe sat at the top of its library for 1-2 weeks.
- Who: GEN, DTC evidence.
- Sources: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06). → craft §2.2, §2.19

**12. Founder, group and real-human ads as the counterweight to AI.**
- What to make: founder phoning top customers, group taste tests, mom-and-daughter car reactions, warehouse POV, real gifting reactions, multi-person in-person shoots.
- Why per him: "The more AI stuff we see on our feed, the more I become bullish" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] on real human creative; a raw aesthetic becomes a credibility signal. Breeze founder call ($1M+ spend per a tweet) and the licorice group taste test ("there's no way that can be an ad" [quote src: YT:_9f0rTKKIOY], multiple six figures) are his reference wins. AI can't recreate multi-person reactions yet.
- Who: GEN; founder version needs a founder willing to talk 5-10 minutes.
- Sources: [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01). → craft §1.4, §2.3; msg §4

**13. Port winning copy across formats.**
- What to make: when a script works, hand it to a creator as a yapper, turn it into native-ad primary text, or rebuild it as an AI animation; add a Suno song version; wrap winners in Twitter/IG story/Reddit/Quora frames; pull frames into statics; add a static's headline as a bar on a top video.
- Why per him: AI-animation copy ≈ native-ad primary text ≈ yapper script; this has worked "many times for us" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]. A Perfect Jean static headline added as a bar on a top video spent six figures (2024).
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08), [YT:A29szOW7YDE] (2026-05), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11). → craft §1.14; test F19, F21

**14. Reverse-engineer statics from the Ad Library sorted by impressions, then one-shot them.**
- What to make: pull top statics by impressions (competitors, influencer brands, non-competitors sharing your audience), pick ones worth recreating, write the copy from your own VOC, generate in Claude + Higgsfield (Nano Banana 2) from a 40-type template-prompt playbook.
- Why per him: impressions rank correlates with performance (a client's top six by impressions were all in its top 10 over 90 days). "there really is no ad that you cannot one shot in Claude and Higgs Field" [quote src: YT:ibzfKRRSHhU]; 90-95% of generations usable. Check invented claims (a "7600" figure was made up by the model).
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:ibzfKRRSHhU] (2026-03), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2026-02). → craft §2.13-2.14; test F1, F25

**15. Atypical-text and post-it ads.**
- What to make: the message written somewhere unusual and instantly relevant: marker on a foot sole, a stomach, a cardboard sign, mirror condensation, a car window; or a post-it on the product/body with review words.
- Why per him: doesn't look like an ad; body text grabs attention most. "12 YEARS OF NERVE PAIN GONE IN MINUTES" [quote src: YT:ITM4faRVzF8] seen working "again and again" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw]. Post-its are evergreen, static or video, TOF and especially BOF during sales.
- Who: GEN/DTC.
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01). → craft §2.2, §2.12

**16. Price-objection and skeptic-to-believer scripts.**
- What to make: turn the top objection on the cheap alternative ("You think 80 bucks is expensive? You know what's actually expensive?" [quote src: YT:vD8eLrjAxEM]), comment-reply ads answering "this is way too expensive" [quote src: YT:vD8eLrjAxEM], and skeptic tests ("How good can this popcorn really be?" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]).
- Why per him: a skeptic gradually converting is more believable than "best popcorn ever" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (run both). Don't spend the script trashing the competitor.
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2026-02), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06). → craft §3.6; msg §3

**17. Wall-of-text video (outside DTC especially).**
- What to make: text-heavy video carrying the full message.
- Why per him: it "would crush" for newsletters, info products and SaaS and still does because it conveys the message well; it has also worked on e-com for about a year, below digital-product levels.
- Who: LEADGEN-adjacent (info/SaaS/newsletter).
- Sources: [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06). → craft §1.15, §6

**18. Fund real top-of-funnel; don't let offer ads eat the account.**
- What to make: keep a steady share of TOF concept ads alongside offer statics, and judge ads by spend share, not CPA alone.
- Why per him: Meta data favors offer ads ("30% off") but they buy no incremental reach — "that's the game we're playing" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k]. Clients who fired AdCrate for not beating bottom-funnel creative came back ~6 months later. "I care more about the ads that get the most spend inside the ad account" [quote src: YT:aD7vKl1nkcE].
- Who: GEN.
- Sources: [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06), [YT:aD7vKl1nkcE] (2026-07), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04). → test §1.8-1.9; msg §1.15

## 2. Converts in general

Principles present in 2025 or older evidence AND repeated in 2026.

1. **Research is where the money is made.** 2024: "the research up front and that is where the money is made" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA]; 2025: "if it's a messaging problem then it's a research problem" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]; 2026: the persona problem and VOC audits. → msg §1.4, §6
2. **Customers write the copy.** "our customers can be our copywriters" [quote src: YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09) → "Your customers are your copywriters" [quote src: YT:tVXjXJSEjeA] (2026-07). Exact words, not paraphrase. → msg §1.5; craft §1.7
3. **Assume no one knows or cares.** Rule 1 of the 2024 static rules; still the biggest headline mistake in 2026. Cold-built ads convert cold and warm; warm-only ads ("Love these.", "I feel like a new person" [quote src: YT:7c0aEpo8-bg]) convert only warm. → craft §1.8, §2.6; msg §1.7
4. **Clear over clever (with a curiosity exception).** "fancy words don't sell, clarity does" [quote src: YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11). 2026: still the default, but a deliberate curiosity native ad that sells after the click is allowed. The middle is "no man's land" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q]. → craft §2.9
5. **Messaging beats format.** Proven formats, effort on the message. 2024: iMessage/AirDrop/Apple Notes formats "yet to see" [quote src: YT:M2ObOuFQSv0] perform at spend. 2026: "Messaging is more important than format." [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] Copying a Foreplay layout and shoehorning a message is the common static mistake. → craft §1.5
6. **Congruency is king.** Image and message must speak to the same persona; tone must hold ad → LP → PDP → post-purchase (2024-11, 2025-01). 2026: Grüns palette match ad → advertorial. → craft §1.13; msg §1.13
7. **Statics have hooks; design for the scan.** Sizing, contrast, white space; hook → supporting statement → product → social proof; red reserved for the pain word (2024-03 → 2026-07). Headline must land in ~0.2-0.5 s. → craft §1.9, §2.8
8. **The first frame telegraphs who it's for and whether it's staged.** Setup, creator, angle and location decide before anyone speaks (2024-09, 2025-01, 2026-03). Go broad and let creative target: a jeans gifting angle shifted delivery ~90% male → ~60% female (2024-09). → craft §1.10; test §1.15
9. **Ugly = mimicry of the organic feed, not bad production.** 2024: ugly testimonials (Perfect Jean shaky-camera guy); 2026: "can you mimic the experiences that they are already consuming on their organic feed." [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] → craft §1.2
10. **Insider language: "there's no way these guys are not one of us" [quote src: YT:fls5dwYVY1M].** Ovi's FUPA killer (2024-11, 2025-08); Reddit slang research prompt (2026-02). → msg §1.6
11. **Inspiration from the organic feed and other industries, not direct competitors.** 2024: 10-15 brands from other industries plus competitor reviews; 2025: competitors' "85–90%" likely don't perform; 2026: another industry's message aimed at your audience "goes and crushes" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]. → msg §1.10
12. **Awareness and sophistication pick the framework.** Unaware/problem aware → AIDA or PAS; solution/product aware → FAB (2024-03). 2026: craft per persona per awareness level; sophistication changes the copy (Eight Sleep "world's first smart mattress" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] worked at launch, not now). → msg §1.8
13. **Model/creator selection is underrated; reuse proven creators.** Reshooting a winner with a new creator has a "very high success rate" [quote src: YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01 → 2026-04). Bottom 80-90% of creators need scripting, top ~10% riff. → craft §1.11
14. **Spend is the algorithm's verdict.** "if the algorithm thinks it is a better ad it will give it more spend" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4] (2025-04); don't kill the top spender on CPA (2024-05, still seen in 2026-04). → test §1.8
15. **Most iteration is wasted; squeeze true winners.** If an ad is going to work, it works the first time unless you make a huge change (2024-09, 2025-08). But list every reason a winner might have worked and act on each. → test §1.10, F19
16. **Test against your own taste.** He approves ads he thinks are bad when they are a new style/format/message; "90% of the stuff that we do sucks" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk]. Testing mix: confident concepts + data-backed + a few big swings (2025-01 → 2026-03). → test §1.11, F20
17. **Judgment beats tooling.** 2025: the only people who I know that make good ads with AI make good ads without AI [quote src: YT:fls5dwYVY1M]; 2026: "It was the judgment and taste on knowing what ads to make." [quote src: YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]. → msg §1.1; test §1.1, §1.3

## 4. Hooks and watch time

**How he thinks about the hook**
- The hook comes before the concept: it is how you "pierce someone's attention" [quote src: YT:9ikhnQi2ghE] (2023-11). The yapper brief leaves everything loose except the hook: "the hook is where you'd want to be very very particular about" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03). → craft §2.16; msg §3
- The first frame is part of the hook: staged vs real, and which persona, is decided "before you open your mouth as a creator". Winning first frames: face plus product up close, low/eye-level angle, messy home light, no logos. A hook swap that changed the person (from a slim guy at a 25% hook rate) made it "a completely different ad". [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01) → craft §1.10, §4.3
- Statics have hooks too: one dominant headline landing in ~0.2-0.5 s, highlighted key phrases, warped/distorted headlines to buy an extra half-second (O Positiv). → craft §1.9; msg §4
- Metrics are diagnostics: "Stop glorifying hook rates and hold rates" [quote src: YT:Be9YLcOory4]. Hook-swap SOPs are a symptom of over-systemizing. → test §1.12

**Retention moves**
- **Open loops.** Negative framing, challenges and character-led AI animations keep people watching to resolve something (the visceral-fat character makes viewers hunt for their false belief). AI animations make open loops easy but are not inherently good. → craft §2.2; test §1.4, §4.3
- **Don't look like an ad early.** Brain Weave's AI animation set to a song: nobody would know it's an ad until 45 s-1 min in, "which is what you want from your ads" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]. Product enters ~2 minutes into the Quazi animations. → test §4.7; craft §4.1 #19
- **Long can work.** Yappers run ~1-2 min (Stars and Honey: no captions, riffing almost 2 minutes, running 110 days); stitched winners run 4-5 min; 10-minute trigger-event animations with older audiences. → craft §4.1 #10, §5
- **Strip the ad tells.** No soundtrack/SFX/emojis on ugly ads; one hook text overlay then no captions, because "A real organic creator is not going to caption every word" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026; he admits his own praised ads still carry captions). → craft §2.3, §6
- **Continuous story in animation.** Claymation with each shot's end frame as the next shot's start, so it plays as one blended story with no hard cuts (varicose veins ad). → test §4.4
- **Don't cram.** Cover some value props, not all (4 of 5 value props, 2 of 4 pains in his jeans script); keep giving a reason to click through. → craft §2.17
