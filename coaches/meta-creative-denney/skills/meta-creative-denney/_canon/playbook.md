## 1. Working now (2026) — the plays with the strongest 2026 evidence, ranked

1. **Founder ads: the founder tells the why, on a phone.** Make: founder name + "founder" in the overlay → the personal problem → the turning point → product → proof → soft CTA; Denney's go-to start is the founder answering a comment-bubble objection; Cottrell cuts in customer testimonials ("absolute dynamite"). Why: instant trust, a story nobody can copy, and "a real person talking to another real person through a screen" [quote src: YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA]; often a brand's first top performer. Who: Denney and Cottrell (both S tier), on Cottrell's Oct 2026 always-win list; Hott: test it at least once. 2. **Partnership ads and whitelisting: run proven creator content from the creator's handle.** Make: when a phone-shot, person-led ad works from the brand page, negotiate to run the same video as a partnership ad; test ~5–7 days for incremental lift, then a one- or three-month deal; skip high-production ads from a person. Why: Andromeda is persona-based, so creators your personas engage with are the route to them; partnership ads add net-new reach when rolling reach falls; a second name adds social proof and looks less like an ad. Who: Denney (S tier; "the eight and nine-figure brands have figured out partnership ads" [quote src: YT:ePcEcT2PH8E]; partnership ads at 30–50% of spend, under 25% means trouble), Cottrell (many clients run most ads twice: brand + partnership). ## 2. Converts in general — principles in 2025 top performers and repeated in 2026, ranked

1. **Research first; lift the customer's exact words.** Make: read reviews, Reddit and ad comments before ideas, formats or casting (manually at least once, then with AI); put exact pain phrases into headlines, hooks and scripts; treat comments as pre-purchase objections. Why: people skip research because they think they know more than the customer (Cottrell); inside phrases make viewers think "there's no way that these guys are not one of us" [quote src: YT:c_6XsMe9eXE]; "if it's a messaging problem then it's a research problem" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (Cooper). 2. **Don't look like an ad.** Make: strip ad signals ("suspiciously good lighting", clean cuts, tripods, backing tracks, word-by-word captions, a read-sounding voice); post from a creator handle; frame it the way a creator would; native means whatever the audience consumes (news clips, podcast clips, security-camera footage), not only UGC. Why: the scrolling brain keeps asking "is this an ad?" (Levinger); the ad blocker is now a "subconscious content blocker" (Hott); Meta first judges an ad as content and only then sends it to auction ("Two doors, not one" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY], Levinger; Cottrell's AI pre-screen, 2026-10). 12. **Clear over clever, one focal point.** Make: one message and one focal point (usually the headline on a static); pass the 1-second stranger test; headlines of 8 words max (4–7 the sweet spot); one font, two at most; steer the eye with size, contrast and white space. Why: easy reading feels true and crowded screens get scrolled past (Levinger, Cottrell); "Assume no one knows or cares." (Cooper); "Clear over clever" (Hott). Exception: curiosity works when the selling happens after the click (Cooper).
