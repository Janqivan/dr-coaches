# Creative strategy, research, briefs, creators and production ops
> Scope: how the roster finds what to say (customer research, personas, triggers, competitor/organic inspiration), turns it into a diagnosis and roadmap, briefs and casts creators, and runs production at volume. ## 1. Core positions (ranked by mentions × specificity)

- **Research is where the money is made; most ad problems are research problems** — Too few ideas, too many to prioritize, or no scalable ad all trace back to thin research, and research must be ongoing (new comments, reviews, organic posts keep arriving), not a one-off at onboarding. Without knowing why people buy, why they don't, and how they speak, you cannot pick an angle or cast a creator. Cottrell often spends more time researching than writing; Denney says most of her strategist time is research. Who: Cooper, Cottrell, Denney, Levinger, Hott + guests (consensus). ## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Research source checklist and order (Cottrell, Cooper; guests Mikac, Crespi)
- When they use it: at every onboarding, and again whenever no scalable ad appears (go back to the well).
- Steps (Cottrell 2025-11): 1. Reddit. 2. Reviews (site, Amazon, Trustpilot). 3. Own ad comments. 4. Competitor ads (Meta Ad Library, Foreplay). 5. AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini). Save everything in one Google Drive folder, which feeds AI context later and lets you compare today's customer with the one from three years ago. Ask clients for review exports and post-purchase surveys ("absolute gold"). Product-page FAQs list the problems already solved.
- Cooper's list: ad comments; competitor reviews over competitor ads; the ad account and Motion reports; support tickets and surveys; Reddit/Quora; the articles customers read; burner accounts.
- Variant (guest: Anze Mikac): questionnaire → reviews → post-purchase surveys → Reddit → organic search of the pain → competitors lightly → ad account LAST, to "trick my brain" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k] out of bias. Cooper agrees not to over-index on the account, because it only holds what you've already tested.
- Sources: [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2025-11), [YT:3wLk3D99JyA] (2024-03), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01)

### Review mining (Cottrell, Cooper, Hott, Denney)
- When they use it: to build statics, hooks and angles. It doesn't need to take you a full day to write a set of statics.
- Steps (Cottrell): 1. Read reviews manually at least once "with our marketing cap on" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]. For each line ask: is this an angle to build out, or a direct quote for the top of a static? 2. Skip generic praise ("very tasty") and hunt specific outcomes and use cases (pregnant reviewers → "That's an ad angle" [quote src: YT:gP-68LmgAQ4]). 3. Note the comparisons customers made (what they replaced) to use as swap hooks. 4. Export the reviews into a Claude project: "Identify me the common themes and reasons why people purchase this product" [quote src: YT:RSa4wUuw_2k]. 5. Mine competitors' and similar products' reviews too.
- Cooper's "emotive mining": paste reviews into a sheet, =LEN(cell), sort Z–A, feed the ~500 longest into GPT with an emotive-language prompt. The statics this produces sit atop the account for months or years.
- Hott: only 1 in 5 to 1 in 10 reviews says "I was having this blank problem" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE]. Look for big, hyper-relatable problems in third-to-fifth-grade language.
- Denney: auto-export all reviews into a review-mining sheet plus manual standout lines (Reddit, Amazon), and rank products by review count and rating. Also mine every comment on the top 20 ads from the last year.
- Sources: [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:Mqj05tYuDI0] (2026-09), [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12)

### Reddit Answers workflow (Cottrell; also Cooper, Levinger)
- Steps: 1. Skip subreddit scrolling and ask Reddit Answers, e.g. why do people not like <brand>? 2. Open the AI summary themes, then the underlying posts. 3. Follow the suggested questions and your own follow-ups. 4. Repeat for competitors if your brand isn't mentioned. 5. Collect the nuggets in one doc. 6. Feed the doc to the client's Claude project to write scripts, statics and hooks.
- Cooper: Google "how to get rid of problem plus Reddit" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA], trust upvotes as validation, and sprinkle exact phrases so the ad sounds like a customer speaking to a customer. Levinger: open the largest visible community, sort by top, pull threads and ask Claude "What do you see?" [quote src: YT:SmNpVtfIRHw]. Pick the thread that matches your target phase ("Are they a good idea?" [quote src: YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] signals evaluation).
- Sources: [YT:RpNcnKS2WYc] (2025-08), [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] (2025-08), [YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] (2026-02)

### Skeptic to superfan SOP (Denney)
- When she uses it: in a new brand's first two weeks, together with the "million-dollar iteration playbook" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg]. Both are part of her "authenticity framework".
- Steps: 1. Take the top 15–20 ads. 2. Mine all their comments. 3. Have AI list the biggest pre-purchase objections and questions. 4. Find real buyers who had the same concern, note the language they use and why they got past it, and turn that into counter-testimonials.
- Sources: [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05)

### Audience one sheet (Cooper; Cottrell)
- Cooper's sections: benefits, features, pain points, objections, failed solutions, other. Fill from your own knowledge first, then paste 5–20 supporting reviews under each point. Add "one-liners" (20–40 customer phrases at a time mined with GPT) to use in statics, headlines, hooks and landing pages.
- Cottrell's sections: problems solved, benefits, reasons to buy (with quotes), core USPs. Make one per brand and per product, with no fixed template. His "endless ads" brainstorm: research the product → mine reviews and comments → find competitors → search the problem on YouTube/TikTok → find long-running ads in Foreplay → study other niches → compile the sheet.
- Sources: [YT:3wLk3D99JyA] (2024-03), [YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09), [YT:NNg9-TDiFDA] (2024-03)

### Cooper's persona process (5 steps + account audit)
- When he uses it: as the 2026 foundation. A persona must answer four questions or "I do not believe it to be a persona" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]: what they already believe about the problem; what they've tried and what failed; what they fear and secretly want; what exact words they use when talking to themselves about it.
- Steps: 1. Identify every persona, ranked by how often each appears in voice of customer. 2. Deep analysis per persona with one long prompt (Gary Halbert paragraph; five Fs: fears, frustrations, failures, fantasies, failed solutions; emotion and trigger moment; awareness and sophistication; buying journey; cognitive bias map; objection stack; competing solutions; VOC phrases; confidence check). Treat the output as a skeleton, not gospel. 3. Creative consumption analysis: have the AI "reverse engineer the search terms" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] the persona uses, search them on TikTok/YouTube, and note creator type, production level, setting and lighting. 4. Score each persona /10 on revenue, LTV, CAC and strategic priority, then average. 5. Allocate creative against the ranking. Then audit the account for underserved personas.
- Rules: keep 3–5 persona tabs (primary, secondary, expansion) and treat it as a living document. Ignore "identity overlays": "the gifter" and "skeptical scroller" are not core personas.
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU] (2026-06)

### Personas two ways / persona gap (Denney)
- Steps: 1. Who actually buys: scrape reviews to CSV and prompt extract five core personas and give each a name that fits the trigger or their main problem [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo]. Rank them by volume AND by emotional intensity, and make the AI cite review signals. 2. Who the ads target: review all creative from the last 3 months, or run Claude Cowork on the brand's Ads Library link ("from the POV of their ads only" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo]). 3. Find the gap: personas never targeted, or a low-volume persona with high emotional intensity. 4. Put the persona doc in Google Docs (it doubles as Claude project context), then build a deck. Also check Amazon ("Ask Amazon" on who buys) and Ads Manager demographic breakdowns.
- Her rule: current buyers aren't always the best champions. Personas pulled from viral organic content gave her "lightbulb moments".
- Sources: [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06), [YT:yRgPbqywUJ8] (2026-07), [YT:p4yXXdnCgWg] (2026-08)

### The diagnosis (Denney)
- When she uses it: before any roadmap, re-done about quarterly. "Without it, you do not have a real strategy. You actually only have testing." [quote src: YT:4wM-i1cxtAM]
- Steps: 1. Research ("onion peeling"). 2. Personas both ways. 3. Awareness and sophistication of current creatives. 4. Creative diversity level (Meta's creative diversity cheat sheet). 5. Write a 1–2 sentence diagnosis: the connective tissue behind what gets tested.
- Her example diagnoses: learnings not iterated or not carried into other formats or creators; all content serving one persona; the older segment wins, so next quarter uses only 40+ creators; too many promotional, time-limited ads; two dominant review personas missing from the creative.
- Sources: [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM] (2026-09), [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06)

### Levinger's three realities and 12-question system
- When she uses it: weekly for clients. Nailing the questions gets you ~80% of the way; nothing goes to production until every cell of the one "creative strategy source of truth" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] doc is filled.
- Three realities: (1) Customer reality, built as a "cake": generational insights at the bottom, micro-moments in the middle, emotional mapping on top (which emotion, private or social, acute or chronic). (2) Brand reality: health (what the team can produce, at what cost) and alignment (this month's single priority). (3) Presentation reality: which format the brain responds to, chosen by psychology, not trends.
- 12 questions across four phases: Research = WHEN (micro-moment), WHY (the emotion beneath), WHO (identity layer only; what all avatars share). Ideation = WHICH problem, WHAT ad type (by how it delivers the message, never because it's trending), WHERE (trigger/exploration/evaluation/purchase). Then concept development and production (WHO falls out of format and platform; HOW long, e.g. "we need this UGC and b-roll back in exactly 4 days" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]).
- Before production, answer two questions: which emotion and how intense (valence + intensity)? which self is protected or proved (ideal, actual, ought)?
- Cadence: a customer "big dump" on the first Monday of each month; brand research monthly and quarterly; presentation research every Monday for 30–60 minutes.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06), [YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06), [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05)

### TEAP mental stages (Levinger)
- Stages: Trigger → Exploration → Evaluation → Purchase. These are mental states, not funnel stages; people stall, re-enter and cycle.
- Steps: 1. Audit every running ad: "What mental state am I speaking to?" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y] 2. Find the gaps; most brands are absent from trigger and exploration, which is "not a targeting problem, it's a messaging problem" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]. 3. Re-brief by state "ridiculously specifically". 4. Run all four stages all year, each with its own CAC. To cut CAC fast, target evaluation (people choosing between options and looking for social validation).
- 2026 update: "All of your te ads now need to be wrapped around story" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]. Her objection to awareness levels: "I'm highly aware of Coca-Cola, but I will never buy." [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]
- Sources: [YT:po85BKDTG-Y] (2026-04), [YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] (2026-02), [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12)

### Behavior-based avatar / working card (Levinger)
- Steps: 1. Stop building the person; find the when. 2. Feed the AI real evidence, not opinions. 3. QA the AI's output. 4. Write a behavioral working card: trigger and category entry point; current vs desired behavior; emotional need and gaps; supporting evidence; what you're testing now. Never set and forget.
- Readiness check: no fictional person? behavior, not demographics? every claim traceable to evidence? does the team know which ad to write next? Diagnostic question: "Do you know what her Tuesday at like 2:00 p.m. looks like?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]
- Sources: [YT:in1l2TPFc1c] (2026-09), [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06)

### Brand alignment ritual (Levinger; guest Evan Lee)
- Monthly: founder, C-suite, buyer, designer and videographer each answer "If you could only optimize for one thing right now, what would it be?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] Map the answers against each other and hunt for disagreement. Then write a one-sentence alignment statement for 30/60/90 days (cut costs, volume, quality, or more top of funnel).
- Before opening any research tool, ask "What decision will this research help me make?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; if you can't answer, close the browser. Also ask "what happens if it works?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU], because a spike fulfillment can't handle means you've paid to create angry customers.
- Guest Evan Lee: work at three altitudes (organizational → team → individual). Hott: always ask why the client cares about a metric.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-06), [YT:DqFjak694PQ] (2026-06), [YT:vXA7E1lcbaU] (2026-05)

### Cottrell's ad layers, four levers and idea matrix
- Layers (2026-10): angle/concept (what you're saying) + message, persona, format (how you deliver it to that persona). At 4–5 ads/week, varying these gives "a really nice round ad account" [quote src: YT:RExPkxyRB6o].
- Four levers (2026-02): persona, messaging, hook (first 3 seconds), format.
- Idea matrix (2025-11): angle → idea → hook → format, in any order you like; you need all three.
- Standard angles every account needs: problem-solution, testimonial, us-vs-them, objection handling (his favorite).
- Basic 3-step strategy: audit what's missing (media mix + angles) → read comments → idea = gap + insight, filled with a true review story.
- Sources: [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10), [YT:CPWoW09WCCo] (2026-02), [YT:svW3EyoYAiw] (2024-08)

### Big idea, pattern recognition, prioritization (Denney)
- Framework 1: take the top 20 creatives of the last year, extract each one's big idea, see which recur, and move them into a more emotionally resonant format (creator content, ideally partnership ads).
- Framework 2, viral content SOP (by partner Miguel): take the big idea (e.g. "parents of picky eaters" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg]), search TikTok/Reels for what's viral now (especially small accounts going viral), and pattern-match phrases, visuals, settings and creator types.
- Prioritize on two axes: easiest/quickest (validate with a static, internal creator or founder before a shoot) and most evidence. Evidence = "repetition" of winning elements, customer feedback, scaled assets, and what competitors keep testing for months.
- Evidence ladder: high confidence = pressure-tested formats, messages and creators. Low = "our competitors did it" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo]. Still leave room for gut: "Sometimes, it literally is gut and you need to honor that" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg].
- Sources: [YT:p4yXXdnCgWg] (2026-08), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05), [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06)

### Roadmap and icebox (Denney; Cooper, Savannah)
- Denney's template: 1. Creators to work with. 2. Icebox of every ungraded research idea. 3. Quarterly plan (holidays, launches, persona by season; don't test every persona at once). 4. Monthly roadmap per persona: a 1–2 sentence idea + three messaging options.

### Concept definitions (guests)
- (guest: Mirella Crespi) Concept = who (persona) + why (emotional motivator) + what (the offer they convert on). "A concept is not a format." [quote src: YT:J3TiyuWqYng] Concept card: inside the consumer's mind, platform, motivator, USPs, style (polished or native), POV (brand, customer or expert).
- (guest: Jess Bachmann) Resonant messaging Venn: true / what the market wants to hear / what others cannot say. (guest: Emily Hickey) Motivator Map: top-3 purchase motivators, identity emotion, support points, visual codes; "Winners win".

### Control decision: raws vs finished ads (Cottrell)
- Decide before casting. Option A: the creator does everything. It costs more, caps scale (no edit files for new hooks), and brings usage-fee surprises. Option B (Fraggle's choice): you do research, script and direction; the creator fronts the ad and shoots raw, so you can re-hook, mash up and rebuild. This needs in-house editors and strategists, plus a full shot list, because you can't blame a creator "if you haven't told them how to do it" [quote src: YT:xB4jykCt_yU].

### Creator brief fields (Savannah, Cottrell, Denney; guest Janae Lavander)

- (guest: Janae Lavander, Caraway) "a great brief does half of the creative work" [quote src: YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms]: filming guidelines with visuals, annotated references, mandatory vs optional shots (mandatory: product in hand in the hook), hook and CTA variations.

### "Idea Plus freestyle" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] yapper brief (Cooper)
- Fields: overall instructions; optional inspiration; recommended structure ("Talk about this, then this, then this." [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w]); must-get extra shots (e.g. eating reactions); visual notes, especially the first frame; key value props; scripted hooks (the only scripted part); a link to the ugly-ads board.
- Rule: "Make sure you cover all the talking points, but do not script it for them." [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] Keep several templates (full script, editing, iteration, freestyle), because "if every brief looks the same, then your ads are going to look pretty similar" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4].

### Creative-ops system (Cooper)
- Three parts: Notion master creative database (concept-level production from research to edit, with strategist/editor/creator/reviewer views and time logs); a Google Sheets log of all creative from every source by week; Motion for the performance read fed back into strategy.
- Sheet row = one concept (3 hook variations = 1 row): date, concept code, UID, name, # variations, SKU, source, type, style, format → a formula naming convention pasted into Meta. Ad sets are named source + number + concept (ADCR01, INTERNAL01).
- Measure cost per concept and output share vs cost share by source, then kill or scale partners. Don't KPI people on output: "I could make 10 awful ads in one week" [quote src: YT:EOZp34uDIc4].
- Scale breakpoints: a Trello board is fine at 3–4 concepts/week. $20K→$50K→$200K→$500K/month in spend means roughly 10→20→50 ads/week, which breaks simple boards.

- Denney: monthly roadmap, weekly task stand-up, daily production stand-ups, monthly retro. Tier 1 = iteration edit, tier 2 = post-production, tier 3 = production. Six parts: research → roadmap → production → QA → launch → analyze. QA is where things get held up.

## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree

- **Funnel → mental states:** Levinger (2026-04) says the TOFU/MOFU/BOFU framework "no longer works" and replaces it with TEAP, with every ad "wrapped around story" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]. Denney still uses awareness levels, but only in retrospect (bad to START from them, 2024-10). Hott ignores the prospecting/retargeting split. [YT:po85BKDTG-Y, YT:hSy6X065x0g]
- **Long-running ads:** older Cottrell (2023) and Denney (2022) treated run length as a winner signal. Denney reversed by 2025 and Cooper found "barely any correlation" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]; shares and impressions replaced it. [YT:Ak6I-EIbBd4, YT:2jVcLu3Earg, YT:M2VReAjHiN8]
- **Creator relationships:** Denney (2026): "the churn and burn of creator relationships is totally gone" [quote src: YT:_VtpfVeB2Kc]; creator selection is the #1 2026 skill; strategy goes "creator first". Cooper hired two full-time creator sourcers, betting AI-heavy feeds make people crave real, ugly content. [YT:9UBSGSS2Yuo, YT:mpj0A4Prxu4]
- **Script freedom:** Savannah moved from creator freedom (2021) to tight direction (2024+); Denney shifted by 2022. Heath (speaking as a sponsored creator) still prefers flexibility with guardrails. [YT:hS47--F7290, YT:Ss7bdFf0bsk, YT:fBrC1_VPTRU]
- **AI and shoots:** over the last 12 months Cooper's team does more in-person shoots AI can't copy (group and gifting reactions), alongside hundreds of AI-animation ads. Cottrell: static-to-animated went from hours to minutes. Savannah: AI scripts "all sound the same" [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]. [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ, YT:MT2MzrTGC5w]
- **Volume:** Cooper takes "the anti approach" to volume packages and Hott refuses "30 turds of creative", while (guest: Alicia) says "In 2026, Meta rewards volume and speed". [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk, YT:R0k0rVzv_yQ, YT:mprXXGL42oU]
- **Strategist role:** Denney (2026-09) says management dissolves into ICs who strategize and execute with AI, and top strategists now own LPs, offers and product selection. Strict role separation (Savannah, Cottrell) vs hybrids (Denney, Savannah's ex-creator strategists). [YT:pMbN-RefgUk, YT:MT2MzrTGC5w]
- **Avatars:** Cooper builds deep 20–30-page persona docs. Levinger says "Traditional customer avatars just kind of suck", favors whens and brand POV, and skips the generational layer for small businesses. [YT:79doYBhLLFU, YT:in1l2TPFc1c]
- **Taste:** Denney ("boring" conversation, "It might perform even if you hate it.") and Levinger dismiss taste as liking. Cooper uses "taste" to mean judgment, which he calls the moat. Hott fights pretty-brain vetoes. [YT:-E9sjb2msjo, YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:MbqiexQiyGs]
- **Ideas:** Levinger runs ideation games (bisociation, animal mashups). Hott is almost allergic to ideas and wants relevant inspiration and stories, bucketing angles afterward. [YT:_iMPT2SsjeU, YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]
- **Organic testing:** Denney calls "testing for free" on organic "a myth" but ports organic pop-offs into paid. Levinger: test convictions on organic, because organic is cheap attention, not sales. [YT:-E9sjb2msjo, YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0]
- **Selling across identity:** Cottrell and Cooper say research lets outsiders write for any persona. Levinger is skeptical (period-panty ads by a male strategist failed) and suggests hiring someone who fits. [YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]
- **Awareness campaigns:** Denney used to say skip them, but reconsidered for her own brand in 2026 (Cody Plofker spends 5–10% on reach). [YT:GbXeAYTXhP4]
