## Framework 1 — The A-Player Definition (Who, Ch 1)

**The 90/10 formula:** an A-player is someone who has at least a **90% chance** of achieving an outcome that only the **top 10% of possible candidates** could achieve.

**Operator implications:**
1. **Role-specific.** An A in one seat is a C in another. "A-player" without a seat attached is a meaningless compliment.
2. **Probabilistic, not personality.** You're estimating odds against a defined outcome, not judging character.
3. **The Scorecard defines what A means.** No Scorecard, no A-player verdict possible.
4. **A's hire A's. B's hire C's.** If the hiring manager is a B, their interview process will unconsciously screen A-players out.

**Failure mode:** founder calls someone an A-player based on charisma, energy, or first-impression. Geoff's frame: until the seat has a Scorecard and the candidate's probability against each outcome is mapped, the claim is empty.

## Framework 2 — Voodoo Hiring (Who, Ch 1-2)

**The 10 patterns most managers use INSTEAD of structured hiring.** Each is a substitute for the A-Method, and each produces sub-50% A-player hit rates.

1. **The Art Critic** — judges on first impression and aesthetic, "I just know talent when I see it"
2. **The Sponge** — passive, absorbs whatever the candidate offers, no calibrated questions
3. **The Prosecutor** — aggressive interrogation, makes candidate clam up, blocks real signal
4. **The Suitor** — sells the role without ever evaluating fit
5. **The Trickster** — riddles, brain teasers, manhole-cover questions — no signal on actual performance
6. **The Animal Lover** — hires for personality similarity, ignores outcome capability
7. **The Chitchatter** — small talk for 45 minutes, never gets to chronological depth
8. **The Psychological Interviewer** — applies pop psychology and unvalidated personality tests
9. **The Pre-cognitive** — decides in the first 5 minutes, spends 55 minutes confirming the snap judgment
10. **The Webster** — reads the resume aloud, asks for clarification, never probes behind the dates

**Diagnostic use:** when an interview pattern matches one of these, name it. Naming the pattern is how the founder sees what they're doing.

## Framework 3 — The A-Method (Who, Ch 3-8) — Scorecard / Source / Select / Sell

The operating system. Four steps. Skip a step, hire a B. Skip two, hire a C.

### Step 1 — Scorecard (Ch 3)

A Scorecard is NOT a job description. Three components:

**Mission** — a single-sentence statement of why this seat exists.
- Bad: "Manage the marketing team."
- Good: "Build a marketing engine that produces 20 qualified opportunities per month for the immigration vertical by Q4."

**Outcomes** — 5-8 ranked, measurable, time-bound results.
- "Hit $X in net new MRR in 12 months."
- "Reduce client churn from Y% to Z% by month 9."
- "Hire and onboard 3 A-player team members by Q3."

**Competencies** — the behaviors required to hit those outcomes.
- Role competencies (e.g. "leads through influence")
- Cultural competencies (e.g. "no-bullshit operator", "data-first")

**Test:** could two different managers read this Scorecard and agree on whether a hire succeeded? If no, Scorecard isn't done.

### Step 2 — Source (Ch 4)

A-players are sourced, not applied. Continuous, not panic-driven.

**The four sourcing channels Geoff prioritizes:**
1. **Referrals from your A-players** — A's know A's. Ask them by name.
2. **Targeted outreach** — pick the 50 people you wish were available, reach out to them.
3. **The "best you've ever worked with" question** — asked to 10+ trusted operators in adjacent companies.
4. **Professional sourcers** — for senior seats, an executive search firm (the ghSMART thesis).

**Sourcing volume rule:** generate 3-5x the candidate volume vs the number of seats you'd consider filling. A-Method runs on selection, not on hope.

### Step 3 — Select (Ch 5-7)

Structured, multi-stage interview sequence. The bottleneck where most companies fail.

**The four interview types:**
1. **Screening Interview** — 30 minutes, 5 standard questions, kills the bottom 80%
2. **Topgrading Interview** — 3-4 hours, chronological (see Brad Smart's framework in the companion canon file)
3. **Focused Interviews** — 1 hour each, one per Scorecard outcome
4. **Reference Interviews** — 45+ minutes each, on bosses the candidate names

**The five screening questions Geoff asks every candidate:**
1. What are your career goals?
2. What are you really good at professionally?
3. What are you not good at, or not interested in doing professionally?
4. Who were your last five bosses, and how will they rate your performance on a 1-10 scale when we talk to them?
5. (TORC — Brad's contribution, deployed here in Geoff's process)

That fourth question alone surfaces 60% of the signal you need.

### Step 4 — Sell (Ch 8) — the 4 F's

A-players have options. The hire is recruited, not evaluated, at this stage.

**Fit** — does the work itself, the team, the mission match the candidate's career goals and operating preference?

**Family** — does the candidate's spouse, kids, life situation, and location support the move? Family-axis blockers kill more accepted offers than comp.

**Freedom** — will the candidate have real autonomy to execute the role, or will they be micro-managed?

**Fortune** — does the comp structure deliver the upside an A-player deserves? Base + bonus + equity / profit share + long-term growth?

**Sell throughout the process,** not just at the offer. A-players read sales energy at every touchpoint.

## Framework 4 — Power Score (Power Score, 2015) — PWR

Geoff's post-hire performance audit framework. Three keys, each scored 1-10. Multiplied (not added) into a composite Power Score.

**Priorities** — is the leader working on the 1-3 things that matter most, with absolute clarity?
- 10 = crystal clear, written down, reviewed weekly
- 5 = vague, shifting
- 1 = drowning in inbox, no Priorities

**Who** — does the leader have the right people in the right seats around them?
- 10 = every direct report is an A-player in their current seat
- 5 = mix of A's and B's, no obvious C's
- 1 = C-players in critical seats, hires not made, gaps unfilled

**Relationships** — is the leader getting work done THROUGH people, or around them?
- 10 = leverages team, builds trust, conflicts handled directly
- 5 = transactional, gets things done but at relationship cost
- 1 = working around the team, avoids conflict, builds resentment

**Composite score:** multiply. P × W × R. A leader scoring 9 × 9 × 9 = 729 is materially different from 8 × 8 × 8 = 512. Geoff treats anything below 8 on any axis as a flag, not a passing grade.

**Operator use:** quarterly audit on yourself, your direct reports, and any candidate already in seat for 90+ days. Not a hiring tool — a performance/coaching tool.
