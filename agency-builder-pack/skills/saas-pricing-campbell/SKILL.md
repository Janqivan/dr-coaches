## CORE FRAMEWORKS

### 1. WILLINGNESS-TO-PAY (WTP) RESEARCH (master frame)
Pricing is not a guess. It's a research problem. Stack:
1. Quantified buyer personas — 3-5 named personas
2. Feature-preference survey — rank features per persona (relative-
   preference methodology, not Likert)
3. Price-sensitivity survey — Van Westendorp's four questions per persona
4. Conjoint (when budget allows) — for high-stakes decisions
5. Re-run every 6 months

Most operators skip 1-4 and jump to "what should I charge?". Failure mode.

### 2. VAN WESTENDORP (the four-question stack)
Ask each persona:
- At what price would this be **so expensive you wouldn't consider it**?
- At what price would it be **getting expensive but you'd still consider it**?
- At what price would it be **a good deal**?
- At what price would it be **so cheap you'd question the quality**?

Plot the four curves. Intersections give floor (cheap-quality cliff),
ceiling (too-expensive cliff), and optimal price.

**Sample-size honesty:** 30 respondents is directional. For a $10M
decision you need a bigger N. For a $100-$1000 product, 30-100
respondents per persona is good enough to act.

### 3. THE PRICING PAGE TEARDOWN METHODOLOGY
Campbell has torn down 744+ pricing pages, studied 30,000+ companies.
Teardown lens:
- **Value-metric clarity** — can a stranger tell what scales the price?
- **Persona signaling** — does each tier obviously map to a buyer?
- **Anchor structure** — is the high tier doing decoy work?
- **Feature density per tier** — too many bullets = noise
- **Annual default vs monthly default** — which is visual default?
- **Decoy pricing** — middle tier ~2x cheap tier (Economist trick)
- **Friction to convert** — self-serve / sales-call wall / hybrid
- **Localization** — currency toggle? country flag?

### 4. VALUE METRICS (the axis your price scales on)
Some sort of value metric beats tier-based pricing on every measure.
Properties: easy to understand (5-second test), aligned with value
(scales with what customer gets), grows with usage (automatic expansion).

**Campbell's data:** multiple value metrics → ~30% higher growth vs
single-metric peers. Value-metric pricing → ~75% better retention vs
purely tier-based competitors.

### 5. ANNUAL-VS-MONTHLY ECONOMICS (the discount math)
- **Humans can't do percentages.** Don't say "18% off". Say "two
  months free". Whole numbers convert; percentages don't.
- **30% is the dropoff cliff** — discounts beyond 30% at original
  purchase create a WTP collapse at annual renewal.
- **Discount is a scalpel, not a chainsaw** — used to lower activation
  energy, not as a default.
- **Don't discount month 1.** Offer annual switch at month 2-3.

### 6. FREEMIUM MATH (the 90% failure cliff)
Freemium is an acquisition strategy, not a pricing strategy.
Mishandled, it fails ~90% of the time. The math:
- Free-to-paid conversion median: 1-2% pure / 3-5% PLG with strong funnel
- CAC for freemium-converters: ~50% of standard
- Retention uplift: ~20% better than cold-acquired paid
- Break-even: does (conversion × ARPU × retention uplift) exceed
  (cost of serving free + free-tier surface cost)?

If no, freemium isn't a strategy, it's a leak.

### 7. PRICE LOCALIZATION (the 25-40% revenue most SaaS leaves on the table)
If >15% of your base is outside your home region, you should be
localizing. Cosmetic localization (currency symbol only) ≠ true
localization (region-specific price points).
- True localization companies: ~11% MoM growth
- Cosmetic localization: ~9% MoM
- Multi-region true-localized: ~2x growth vs no-localization
- Emerging-market conversion lift: up to ~30%

For DR: US, AU, NZ, UK have different agency-fee WTP. One global
quote leaves money on the table.

### 8. EXPANSION REVENUE & THE 12-MONTH BAR
The most successful subscription companies see expansion revenue
**outpace churned revenue within 12 months** of customer acquisition.
NRR >100% = existing customers generate more revenue this year than
last, before new sales. Top-quartile B2B SaaS historically 120%+ NRR.

For DR / agency: expansion = scope expansion (add channel, vertical,
market) or seat expansion (add partner, paralegal). The retainer
needs to allow expansion to happen automatically through the value
metric, not require renegotiation.
