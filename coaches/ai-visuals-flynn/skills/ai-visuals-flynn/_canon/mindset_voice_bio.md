## PART 8. Mindset on AI and creativity, how he talks, his background

### Rules (ranked by how often they say it)
- **Learn baseline skills, not a tool: master one tool per category and the skills transfer.** A Midjourney skill set plus a Flux skill set covers image generation; text-to-video skill carries to image-to-video; ChatGPT carries to Claude and Perplexity, Kling to Veo 3 and Runway. Get good at one LLM, one image generator, one video generator. His line to newcomers: "don't attach yourself to a singular tool"; think outcome and leverage, meaning what scales or can be operationalized. [YT:26bjN6CwwJs, YT:EP3WupKVL50, YT:ssxPDDjSVY0, YT:ktSEt_8T9MA] (n.d.; 2025-10; 2026-09) · mentions 12
- **Think in micro-decisions: every small choice changes the result, and there is always a way.** He thinks in a decision tree (if not this, then this). People quit because they decide the tool cannot do it, but most of the time Midjourney can through its many small features. For video, camera motion and subject motion plus direction move the output most ("four-dimensional"), so be granular there. "maybe one to two words changes the entire output significantly"; "whenever you put something first it gets weighted heavier". Early inconsistency forced rerolls for "the one", and that grind is how you get good; "you have to run to the problems head on to find the solutions". [YT:t0dgrFHEcwM, YT:YD5V0hKlRew, YT:NmIcjfPNcsE, YT:P3w112Qr-2s] (2024-10; 2025-10) · mentions 9
- **Let Midjourney be Midjourney: take the surprise, do not force control.** The only tool that "allows you to be that creative and not have to force control". He wants about 30% of the output to be something he did not prompt (speaker inferred); the weird outputs (very strong remix, very subtle remix, added style references) are what make him push the button. What hooked him on every new tool was the outlier generation, not the consistent ones. Midjourney is the creative outlet, ChatGPT the clean professional route. [YT:t0dgrFHEcwM, YT:5tjzTF6MR5A, YT:Dl64zUCJniU, YT:NKr-YwYPqmY] (2024-10; 2025-10; 2026-05) · mentions 8 [quote src: YT:2sY91XsiRuw]
- **You cannot chase every release: pick tools that ship steadily and accept limits.** "I don't keep up", it is too much; Kling is the model case (2.1 landed four weeks after 2.0). Follow people on X who do similar but different things (with Drew). He keeps a burner X account following only AI people (no politics) plus an AI-only Instagram, saves examples on X, and uses threads to attach video, images and description. Releases went from one every couple of weeks to about one every five days; he and Drew hit an "event horizon". [YT:EP3WupKVL50, YT:qhxUbwnJ-9g, YT:AOuXCRzjsS0, YT:Mw5iwBFp9EE] (n.d.; 2025-10; 2026-02) · mentions 8
- **Do not judge a tool on launch day; it takes 3-4 weeks to figure out.** Day-one critics are "quick on the trigger" and do not explore, so he and Drew usually do not hype updates. His own stance: hunt for what is wrong, usually be underwhelmed first, then find in real content work that small details matter; "can't be too big of homers". With Drew: ask "what is the capability itself and then what does that mean?" because big announcements get mimicked. Against "we're cooked" and "X product is done" posts (brutal engagement bait) and hype posts that "Doesn't make anyone feel good". [YT:F6P2EAfAwjw, YT:wlsmAeMQNEM, YT:qv9_8mJT7h4, YT:VKANULbm2ns] (2024-10; 2025-10; 2025-11; 2026-05) · mentions 8
- **Sameness is the failure mode: add branding, references and creative direction, and build a signature.** Lazy creation looks the same everywhere (modern cars designed on mass preference data vs a 1959 Cadillac or 1962 Ferrari that took risks); the go have fun, create whatever Opus outputs on X all look alike. Fix: go beyond "just create this for me". He will not replicate celebrity personas or IP and builds his own process and "visual signature". A signature carries across mediums ("that's a Drew image"). - **Raise the floor, but experts still win: craft and domain knowledge carry over.** AI gave everyone baseline design and the talented still rise. Designers, 3D artists, photographers and videographers start about "30% ahead" because they know composition and vocabulary; copywriters are great with ChatGPT because they know how to instruct it ("we've almost got two jobs to do"). Best placed: 5-10 years in an industry who knows the roadblocks; amplify skills you have. Post-production people are best placed because there will be far more content; "I cannot edit stuff together to save my life". [YT:2vHM6owNx6c, YT:P3w112Qr-2s, YT:BwFzyeHozFk, YT:qhxUbwnJ-9g] (n.d.; 2024-10; 2025-10) · mentions 8
- **Teach and post what you learn; start before it is polished; consistency compounds.** Explaining a tool out loud made him step back and package complex ideas simply; posting his view retains far more than reading. Focus on one or two channels: posting five days a week compounds versus two or three; zero to one is hardest. LinkedIn is "still social media at the end of the day", so show personality. Give each post a second hook beyond the tool ("second level of connection": Midjourney mixed with a Lord of the Rings-style restaurant, golf, travel, fishing; a bare `--sref` dump has none). Do not hope for virality (speaker inferred): strangers arrive without your two years of context. [YT:GtxJdBTXKwE, YT:OJFF_UIgAgc] (2025-10; 2026-09) · mentions 8
- **Start for fun and for yourself; adoption has to be fun before it is work.** I need an asset for X product or campaign is not fun; do it for yourself, nerd out with a friend, then learn the tool, prompt structure, then parameters (aspect ratio, stylize, chaos) instead of copying prompts. With teams, make people do fun things first ("put yourself in a 1980s like kung fu movie poster", not "make this ad for a soap company"). Newcomers: start simple and personal (a children's book for his daughter, memes roasting friends, birthday cards); "being curious" is the best advice. [YT:P3w112Qr-2s, YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:EP3WupKVL50, YT:0217lav5Dlo] (n.d.; 2024-10) · mentions 7
- **Do not expect a finished product: AI takes you 70-80% of the way and you finish.** "Give me an ad and bang there's an ad" does not work yet. Use it for small jobs (textured background, icon, flare), layer text in the layout yourself, expect hours to learn. Endorses the host's 5-95-5 framing (human 5% to set up, AI heavy lifting, human last 5%) as "perfect". Top myth from graphic designers: push-button finished product; chef frame: put the ingredients on the table, generate pieces, assemble them yourself. [YT:E41H2WIHMXg, YT:2vHM6owNx6c, YT:EBSPo2XCml8, YT:o-D4-EYP1Qc] (n.d.; 2024-10) · mentions 7

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Five-stage AI adoption model
- When they use it: placing a team and choosing the next move; most people sit at stage 2 or 3 pushing toward 4.
- Steps: 1. Discover and test. 2. Adopt in your own life. 3. Operationalized (team uses tools, everyone differently, no SOP). 4. Systematize (SOPs, guidelines, tools strung together). 5. Customize (custom-trained models, agents doing multiple tasks); he has no idea what stage 6 is.
- Their example: the Dec 2023 version had four stages (personal, siloed organizational, SOPs, custom-trained models).
- Sources: [YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:E41H2WIHMXg] (n.d.)

#### Hole-finding (the Systematiq method)
- When they use it: a marketing team or agency stuck on volume, margin or sameness.
- Steps: 1. Look into the business and find the holes. 2. Plug one small problem with a conventional AI tool. 3. Reverse-engineer from the target (yearly revenue to monthly to weekly to revenue-generating activities) and define the end product exactly. 4. Aim to cut 70-80% of the work. 5. Write the SOP, then translate it to the next problem.
- Their example: old agency, 90+ clients, about 10 emails per client per month, five designers; a Facebook advertiser runs 50 creatives a week and three work; he partnered with Superside to operationalize AI for mass production.
- Sources: [YT:E41H2WIHMXg, YT:aBgRhbYlF6Q, YT:ssxPDDjSVY0, YT:dcrhhIKNaRQ] (n.d.)

#### Micro-decision tree
- When they use it: output is wrong or weird, or someone says the tool cannot do it.
- Steps: 1. List the small choices in play (parameter, reference type, feature, one or two words). 2. If not this, then this; change one at a time. 3. On a surprising output ask "where does this take me, where does this leave me?" and use it as a style reference or jump-off. 4. After three failed tries ask: do I need first and last frame, is the input image bad, am I missing context in the prompt. [quote src: YT:Mw5iwBFp9EE]
- Sources: [YT:t0dgrFHEcwM, YT:Mw5iwBFp9EE, YT:AxV8TR3BdqI] (n.d.; 2025-10)

#### Creative intelligence layer
- When they use it: creative work. Role prompts ("you're an expert accountant") give a flat "valedictorian" with no other life: fine for data and process work, not for creative.
- Steps: 1. Have AI interview him with very broad questions for about a month. 2. Each answer becomes a micro-skill (being an only child, getting a driver's license). 3. It surfaced analogies as his persuasion tactic, now an analogy engine. 4. Goal: differentiation, bake himself into responses ("make Claude like me").
- Sources: [YT:Dl64zUCJniU] (2026-05)

#### Curation without paralysis
- When they use it: "let's just see this" culture and piles of options.
- Steps: 1. Firm, decisive direction and a strict operating process. 2. Run options past a small custom GPT, Gemini gem or a few agents that hold your criteria. 3. His Weavy visual-forensics workflow picks a winner on things like visual heat mapping. 4. Guard rail: run the loop five times and stop. Co-host Drew Brucker adds a visual profile (dos, do-nots, personality).
- Sources: [YT:AOuXCRzjsS0] (2026-02)

#### Tool-intake test
- When they use it: every new tool or model drop.
- Steps: 1. 20 minutes on a real marketing job (take a picture of this can, place it anywhere, vary it a million ways). 2. It must impress within five or six tries (endorses Drew). 3. Ask whether you would change your habit, not whether it can. 4. Fast Hours protocol (speaker unclear): keep a mood-board prompt, a physics test (girl dancing in a room of mirrors) and one complex job from another tool.
- Sources: [YT:6w6pKyCCQFg, YT:IppsMX-LqDk, YT:5tjzTF6MR5A, YT:AOuXCRzjsS0] (n.d.; 2025-10; 2026-02; 2026-06)

### Recipe library (exact, reusable)
- **Multi-tool loop** — Describe or build an image in ChatGPT, create it in Midjourney, animate it in Kling, pull a frame from the video, describe that frame with ChatGPT, loop; the win is cutting time from idea to first draft. [YT:ssxPDDjSVY0] (n.d.)
- **Decompression run** — Throw a bunch of images into style reference, add a simple prompt like `car`, press enter, see what comes out. [YT:qhxUbwnJ-9g] (2025-10)
- **Originality mix** — Black-and-white photo plus a 2D vector image as style references, vivid color on top; bash mood boards, SREF codes and absurd ideas together to overload the model. [YT:haCwSYl99ks] (2026-08)
- **AI to physical product** — Design in Weavy (about 10 minutes), download the PNG, put it into Printify, "sent myself t-shirts" in a night; the old route was Illustrator, Photoshop to texture, vectorize and flatten, then an exact file size for the printer. [YT:qv9_8mJT7h4] (2025-11)
