# The Pumpkin Plan — Framework Map

**Source:** Michalowicz, Mike. *The Pumpkin Plan: A Simple Strategy to Grow a Remarkable Business in Any Field.* Portfolio, 2012.
**Treatment:** Framework structures described in own words for synthetic-coach use. Book text is NOT reproduced. Each framework is named, mapped to its source chapter or concept, and described mechanically. For Mike's parables — the prize-winning pumpkin farmers, Frank the client-firing story, the colossal-pumpkin growers — buy the book.

---

## What The Pumpkin Plan is for

The Pumpkin Plan is the CLIENT-BASE LAYER of Mike's Services Operating System. Where Profit First fixes cash, Clockwork fixes the owner, and Fix This Next fixes the diagnostic — Pumpkin Plan fixes WHO the business serves. It is a surgical pruning protocol, not a marketing strategy.

The central claim: most service businesses fail not because they have too few clients, but because they have too many wrong-fit clients. Bad-fit clients consume disproportionate support capacity, block the team from serving good-fit clients well, and erode margin invisibly. The fix isn't more lead generation — it's surgical removal of the bad-fit base, paired with concentrated investment in the top.

The Pumpkin Plan is the operator's manual for niching down without going broke.

---

## The Parable (the source metaphor)

Prize-winning pumpkin growers — the ones who produce the colossal, world-record pumpkins — don't try to grow ten medium pumpkins. They plant many seeds, identify the single most promising vine in the patch, **kill every other vine on that plant**, and then pour every resource (water, nutrients, sunlight) into the one remaining vine. The result is a single pumpkin that's larger than ten medium pumpkins combined.

The translation: most services businesses are growing ten medium pumpkins. The colossal pumpkin requires a different discipline — concentration, not expansion.

## Framework 1 — Plant the Right Seed (the foundational match)

**What it is:** the pre-condition for the Pumpkin Plan. Before pruning the patch, the operator must verify they're growing the right seed. The "seed" is the intersection of three things:

1. **What you're best at** — the thing you can do at a higher level than competitors.
2. **What you love doing** — the work that doesn't drain you.
3. **What the market values enough to pay for** — proven willingness to buy.

**The diagnostic:**
- Map your current revenue across services.
- Score each service on the three dimensions.
- The "right seed" is the service that scores high on all three.
- If no service hits all three, the seed is wrong. The Pumpkin Plan won't work until the seed is corrected.

**Failure mode:** operators run the Pumpkin Plan on a wrong-seed business and discover they've pruned themselves into a niche they don't actually want to be in. The seed correction is upstream of the pruning.

## Framework 2 — The Client Assessment (the patch audit)

**What it is:** the inventory of every current client, scored on four dimensions, to identify which clients are colossal-pumpkin candidates and which are weed-vines.

**The four scoring dimensions:**

1. **Revenue** — what they pay you. Top quartile, middle, bottom?
2. **Profit / margin** — what they pay you NET of the cost to serve them. Some high-revenue clients are bottom-margin clients.
3. **Communication / energy** — how draining is the relationship? Are they hands-off and trusting, or constantly escalating?
4. **Fit / strategic value** — does their work map to your seed? Do they generate referrals? Does serving them make you smarter?

**The scoring rule:** rank each client across all four dimensions. Top-quartile across all four = colossal-pumpkin candidate. Bottom-quartile on three or more = weed-vine.

**The 80/20 reality Mike points out:** in almost every services business, the top 20% of clients generate 80% of profit AND require 20% of support capacity. The bottom 20% generate marginal revenue AND consume disproportionate capacity. The middle 60% is the ambiguous zone.

## Framework 3 — The Pumpkin Plan 7 Steps (the core protocol)

The full sequence Mike publishes in the book. Run linearly. Do not skip steps.

### Step 1 — Identify and leverage your biggest natural strengths
What can you do that competitors can't, or won't? Audit honestly. The Pumpkin Plan demands a real competitive advantage. Without one, you're just niching into a niche that someone else owns.

### Step 2 — Sell, sell, sell
While you're auditing, keep cash flowing. The Pumpkin Plan is a 12-18 month transition, not a weekend pivot. Revenue continuity is non-negotiable. Don't fire bad clients before you've replaced their cash with good-fit clients.

### Step 3 — Implement a strict "client filter"
A written list of criteria that any new prospect must meet before you'll serve them. Type of business, type of problem, decision-maker rank, budget threshold, geography, willingness to follow your process. The filter prevents new bad-fit clients from entering while you're pruning existing ones.

### Step 4 — Identify the colossal-pumpkin clients (the top-tier audit)
From the Client Assessment, name the top 5-10 clients by composite score. These are your seed clients — the ones whose pattern you'll replicate.

### Step 5 — Spend disproportionate time WITH the top clients
Not selling. Listening. Mike's prescription: structured conversations with top clients to learn:
- What other problems they have that you could solve.
- What other vendors they use that you could replace or partner with.
- What they wish someone would build for them.
- Who they trust in their network with the same problem profile.

This is the source of organic expansion. Top-client wishlists become new services. Top-client networks become new clients.

### Step 6 — Kill the weed-vines (the firing protocol)
The hardest step. Identify the bottom 20% of clients from the audit. Fire them. Cleanly, politely, with a 30-90 day off-ramp. Refer them elsewhere if possible. Replace their revenue from the top of the funnel — pre-filtered new clients only.

### Step 7 — Replicate the top pattern
Re-tool the offer, marketing, hiring, and delivery around the top-client profile. Refuse business that doesn't match. Hire team members who fit the top-client work. Build case studies around top-client outcomes. The business reorganizes around the colossal-pumpkin vine.

**The discipline check at the end of step 7:** does the business now serve a clearly defined client profile, with a clearly defined offer, at clearly defined margins? If yes, the Pumpkin Plan worked. If you've just changed the names of the clients while keeping the same scattered service mix, you've failed.

## Framework 4 — The Client-Firing Protocol (the operator's hardest move)

**Why operators resist firing clients:**
The bad-fit revenue feels like safety. Losing it feels like risk. The math says otherwise — bad-fit clients eat capacity that good-fit clients would pay 2-3x for. But the math doesn't beat the fear in the moment.

**Mike's firing logic:**
- Firing a bad-fit client is a revenue INCREASE in disguise (capacity freed → can be sold to good-fit at higher margin).
- Firing is also a TEAM-MORALE increase (the team knows which clients are draining them).
- Firing is also a POSITIONING increase (you can now talk about who you serve without hedging).

**The firing script template (Mike's tone):**
> "We've taken a hard look at where we can deliver the most value. The work you need is better served by [specific alternative]. We'll wind down by [date], hand off cleanly, and refer you to [name]. We've appreciated working together."

No apology. No "we hope to work together again." No vague "we're going in a different direction." Specific, clean, fast. The off-ramp is finite. The referral is real. The relationship ends with respect on both sides.

**Operator failure modes:**
- Firing one client at a time over six months instead of running a batch wind-down → drags out the pain.
- Offering a discount-to-stay during the firing conversation → confuses the message and re-traps the relationship.
- Firing without lining up replacement revenue → cash flow shock.
- Firing the wrong clients (firing high-friction high-margin clients instead of low-margin low-fit ones) → loses the wrong vines.

## Framework 5 — The Niche-Down Logic (why concentration beats expansion)

**The default operator instinct:** if revenue is unstable, broaden the service mix and accept more client types. "We'll take whoever pays."

**Mike's counter-logic:** broadening the mix dilutes capability, increases delivery complexity, fragments marketing, and worsens unit economics. The fix is the opposite — narrow the mix until you own the niche.

**The three concentrations that compound:**
1. **Concentration of client type** — same industry, same problem, same stage.
2. **Concentration of service offer** — one to three repeatable engagements, not bespoke each time.
3. **Concentration of marketing message** — one specific value proposition for one specific audience.

**Why concentration compounds:**
- Operational: same problem, repeated → SOPs, faster delivery, lower cost-to-serve.
- Commercial: targeted reputation → referrals, inbound, premium pricing.
- Strategic: you become the obvious choice in a defined market → you stop competing on price.

**The objection Mike anticipates:** "but if I niche down, my addressable market shrinks." Yes — and your CLOSE RATE in that market rises by 5-10x. A 1% market share of a $100M niche beats a 0.01% market share of a $10B market.

## Framework 6 — The Pumpkin Patch Growth Thesis (the compound effect)

**What it is:** Mike's argument for what happens to a services business 12-24 months after the Pumpkin Plan is complete.

**The compounding effects:**
1. **Margins climb** — same work, repeated, at higher prices because of specialist positioning.
2. **CAC drops** — reputation in the niche drives inbound; cold outreach gets warmer responses.
3. **Hiring improves** — specialists want to work for specialists; talent quality rises.
4. **Delivery quality improves** — repeated reps in the same problem create unconscious competence.
5. **Adjacent expansion becomes possible** — having mastered niche A, the business can move laterally to niche B (same problem, different vertical) or vertically (deeper problems in same niche) from a position of authority, not desperation.

**Mike's prediction:** the Pumpkin Plan business looks slower for the first 12 months (firing clients, narrowing focus) and then accelerates dramatically. The "pumpkin patch" begins to produce colossal pumpkins repeatedly because the operating system has been re-engineered around the right vine.

## Framework 7 — The Wishlist Method (organic offer expansion)

**What it is:** the technique from Step 5 (spend disproportionate time WITH top clients) that surfaces the next product to build.

**The method:**
1. Schedule a 60-90 minute conversation with each top-quartile client.
2. The frame: "I'm investing in serving you better — I'd like to understand your business at a deeper level."
3. Ask:
   - What's the biggest unsolved problem in your business right now?
   - Who do you currently pay to solve adjacent problems?
   - What would you build internally if you had the bandwidth?
   - Who in your network has the same problem profile you do?
4. Listen. Don't pitch.

**What surfaces:**
- New service offers the client would buy from you tomorrow.
- Adjacent vendors you could partner with or replace.
- A referral pool that's better-qualified than any cold outbound.
- A clearer picture of the seed client's full ecosystem.

**The operator move:** synthesize the answers across 5-10 top-client conversations. Patterns emerge. The pattern is the next product.

## Framework 8 — Pumpkin Plan in a B2B Services Context (operator translation)

**The framework's translation for a media-buying agency, law firm, or other B2B service:**

| Pumpkin Plan element | B2B services translation |
|----------------------|--------------------------|
| The seed | Your core capability + favorite client problem + paying market |
| The patch | Your current client base |
| Weed vines | Bottom-quartile clients (low margin, high friction, off-ICP) |
| Colossal-pumpkin clients | Top-quartile clients (high margin, low friction, on-ICP) |
| Killing weed vines | Firing bad-fit clients with a 30-90 day off-ramp |
| Pouring resources into the top vine | Reorganizing offer, hiring, marketing around the top-client profile |
| The new pumpkin patch | A re-niched business serving a clearly defined ICP at premium margins |

**The Ivan-test (for any DR or DR-prospect application):** can the operator name their top 5 clients, the pattern they share, and the corresponding 5 bottom clients they should fire by name? If not, the Client Assessment hasn't been done. Start there.

## How The Pumpkin Plan interacts with the rest of the Mike system

- **Fix This Next gate:** open Pumpkin Plan when the diagnosed floor is Sales (demand quality), Impact (transformation quality), or when the client base is creating Profit and Order problems downstream.
- **Profit First interplay:** Pumpkin Plan should run after or alongside Profit First. The cash discipline of Profit First exposes which clients are actually profitable — that data fuels the Client Assessment.
- **Clockwork interplay:** Pumpkin Plan is a pre-req for Clockwork. You cannot redesign delivery for a fragmented client base. Niche the base first, then systemize.
- **Get Different downstream:** once the niche is set, Get Different builds the demand engine. Before the niche is set, Get Different produces vague messaging.
