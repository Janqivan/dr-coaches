## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Static anatomy and layout consensus (Denney, Cottrell, Cooper, Hott, Levinger)
- When they use it: building or critiquing any designed static.
- Steps: 1. ONE heavy condensed headline (often all caps) in the top third / top-left — Hott: never over the busiest part of the photo. 2. ONE product or hand shot below, ONE flat saturated or neutral background. 3. At most ~3 small callouts (pills/tags/check rows) or ONE oversized number/offer badge as a second focal point. 4. Social proof device (stars, Trustpilot, review count). 5. Small CTA. 6. Run the 1-second test and the "Is this eye-catching enough? Can I skim read this?" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI] check.
- Their example (VISUAL): Black Stuff BUY 6 SOAPS; sock SOFTER / WARMER / SMARTER; DSC $8 starter set — all one oversized caps headline, everything else small, single hero shot (Cottrell). Bad: grey static with small bullet copy Are your habits... holding back your hair growth? — labelled "Unclear" (Denney).
- Variants: Cottrell 3 pieces (headline, image, CTA); 3 needs (skimmable in ~1s, handle objections e.g. price → daily cost, social proof); 2024 checklist (scroll-stopping image + customer-tailored benefits + headline describing the image + social proof/guarantee).
- Sources: [YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] (2026-06), [YT:QKRd7FTZ7vM] (2026-05), [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:e54fR6s6WSw] (2023-08)

### Denney's two-layer static framework (strategy, then design)
- When: before writing any static copy.
- Steps: Layer 1 — 1. One goal ("Don't try to do two jobs in one ad." [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]). 2. One persona (type, stage, objection — drives vocabulary, proof). 3. Format (decides what copy survives, what the image carries). 4. Awareness level — "the bridge" to the framework. Pick production level first: graphic-style, hi-fi, or lo-fi creator. Layer 2 — hierarchy: headline → product/key visual → support; 1-second comprehension test.
- Example: AI sunscreen set — same bottles, headline + background swapped per persona ("STILL SKIPPING SUNSCREEN BECAUSE IT'S TOO GREASY?" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] / "Yeah, we fixed that." [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]), critiqued "a little too much text" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc].
- Sources: [YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] (2026-06)

### Cooper's 9 rules for evergreen performance statics (AdCrate)
- Steps: 1. Assume no one knows or cares. 2. Keep it simple stupid. 3. Congruency is king. 4. Your customers are your copywriters. 5. Make people feel something. 6. Statics have hooks too. 7. Reverse-engineer winning copy. 8. Clear and concise. 9. Repurpose winning messages. 2026 additions: test with intention (white space in account + VOC), specific personas, pick clarity or curiosity, visual hierarchy, make the invisible visible, atypical text ads; "messaging is more important than format" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q].
- Sources: [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### Us-vs-them in 4 steps (Denney)
- Steps: 1. Clear visual comparison, "us" on the LEFT for left-to-right readers (testable). 2. 3–4 specific differentiating benefits that solve true pains, most evidence first. 3. A matching competitor weakness per benefit, mined from competitor reviews — factual, never exaggerated. 4. Visual cues — emojis, green check vs red/grey X, parallel rows.
- Example (VISUAL): Your Tee vs Our Tee diagonal white/orange split, red X (160gsm, Fabric Shrinkage) vs green checks (210gsm, Shape Retention), 30 Days Money Back Guarantee.
- Sources: [YT:NsHk8o3jVvM] (2025-06), [YT:8r1im4divXQ] (2024-06), [YT:MiurhGwirfY] (2023-03)

### Post-it / text-in-context method (Hott)
- Steps: 1. Write a direct problem line (write a direct message on a relevant thing) in Sharpie. 2. Stick it on your product, a competitor's, or something emblematic of the problem. 3. Shoot on a phone in a believable real setting, off-center (perfect centering/computer handwriting = "perfect ads are easy to make" [quote src: YT:XIn0vQdErsM]). 4. Test opposite claims as separate ads ("parents hate this" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] vs "parents love this" [quote src: YT:pMAxIOL8dtE]). 5. No line yet? Shoot a blank Post-it and add text later. 6. Do 20 = near-free copy test. Denney rules: one or two notes max; 3+ checklist notes never worked.
- Sources: [YT:frk28Y6nOHg] (2024-12), [YT:pMAxIOL8dtE] (2023-12), [YT:MbqiexQiyGs] (2023-06)

### Native storytelling static (Cottrell)
- Steps: 1. Write the story first (spend the time on copy); first line above the fold = headline, e.g. "A customer shared this with us the other day" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]. 2. Short paragraphs, mundane specific numbers, arrow bullets, honest disclaimer. 3. Find a supporting image: bizarre/shocking problem close-up (Google Images reference → Nano Banana, change enough, keep the shock). 4. Leave the product out unless it looks strange. 5. Run whitelisted where possible.
- Sources: [YT:5R2grcV0WzY] (2026-09), [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09)

### Cottrell's static system
- Steps: 1. Research the customer/brand → insights. 2. Start from a popular objection — "can normally spiral a couple of ideas" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI]. 3. Sharp angle (AI-assisted) → great copy. 4. Social proof wherever possible. 5. Design for the scroll (Canva or a Creative OS template; "Think before you design" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI]). 6. Launch with single-variable variations; "Test fast, test messy" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI].
- Sources: [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] (2025-08), [YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (2025-06), [YT:QKRd7FTZ7vM] (2026-05)

### Levinger's attention levers for statics
- "About to fall" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]: product halfway off a table triggers the catch reflex ("Put things slightly off of what they're supposed to be on." [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]). Odd environment: product where it shouldn't be. Hands holding products raise conversion. Sequence product colors in natural order. Extreme italics (~35°+) — 3x email clicks, +31% purchase intent (cited study). Multi-series: reuse the same ad type so by sighting 3–4 people stop quicker. Rule of thirds, one element per section, max two fonts.
- Sources: [YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-02), [YT:kHUSl110eUM] (2025-01), [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04)

### AI static production (Cottrell, Cooper)

## 3. Static types — templates and wording

- **Headline + benefits / "the headliner"** (Cooper, Denney, Cottrell): big bold claim top, product lower, benefit callouts either side, social proof; reorder callouts to lead with the pocket the headline targets. - **Benefit callout** (Denney, Cottrell): product large, small pill/tag labels with leader lines; benefits in customers' words, not features (Ogilvy-era recall format). ## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree

### Shifts
- **Native statics took UGC's old role** — lo-fi IG-story / creator statics now perform "incredibly well across the board" (Cottrell 2025-11 → 2026-09); Denney 2026-06: lo-fi creator statics "moving the needle right now"; Meta Foresight cited on "perfection-fatigued" Millennials/Gen Z. [YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]
- **Native storytelling statics (raw image + long story) rose in 2026** — Cottrell calls it fast becoming popular; may be a trend that drops off. [YT:5R2grcV0WzY]
- **New angles, not variations** — post-Andromeda, brands want new static angles rather than tweaks (Cottrell 2026-02); headline-only iteration is framed as a pre-Andromeda tactic (guest Sophia Beauvoir 2026-06); identical-looking libraries are treated as one ad (Cottrell 2026-05). [YT:_BuCO3Jf-K0, YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms, YT:QoGFOGEqijw]

F" unless golden nuggets; headline ads from top format (2024) → B tier if messaging amazing. [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:8r1im4divXQ, YT:2jDQ3aPRX8U]
