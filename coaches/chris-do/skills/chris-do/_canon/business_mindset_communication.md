> Scope: Part A covers running a creative business or agency (clients, team, hiring, leadership, focus, partnerships) and communicating (storytelling, presenting, listening, questions). Part B covers his mindset and philosophy (money, fear, confidence, failure, identity, growth) and how he challenges beliefs. - **Listening is the competitive advantage** — treat their words as "the most important thing you've ever heard", proven when "the very next question should be a followup"; play back, because "a machine understands nothing"; take notes "like a reporter" instead of staring. ### 2. Frameworks

#### I do / we do / you do
- When: handing off a role or client, after codifying it into "five major steps... no more than five".
- Steps: 1. I do: trainee shadows calls silently, debriefs "what was said versus what was meant", writes the playbook. 2. We do: ~70% them / 30% you, you check everything. 3. You do: 90–95% them, spot checks to zero, "only come to me when you're not clear"; stay out or clients "go around that person". 4. "let me coach you up to the last 30%". 5. They train the next person. [quote src: YT:Qn5PPMhHkXM, YT:-LB3OX7jLQQ, YT:KflWLnaMzkY]
- Sources: [YT:Qn5PPMhHkXM, YT:-LB3OX7jLQQ, YT:Y3S-y-mluZA] (2023–2025)

#### Talent-gap hiring ladder
- Steps: art-direct a junior → shadow → paid one-day test, repeated until easy (takes direction, prompt, "a great freaking attitude") → longer bookings → "tie the knot" → clone → promote the leader. Keep a maker database: "you cannot rely on one or two". [quote src: YT:HJ9IW1w2bDc]
- Specialist trial: "I'm willing to try you for X number of days"; benchmarks missed → "I would rather shake hands with you and part ways". [quote src: YT:wd0ejVP_g78]
- Sources: [YT:uOWi13jSAPA, YT:Y3S-y-mluZA, YT:Suz_mGDTTwA] (2016–2026)

#### The 5x rule
- When: judging whether a hire pays (an advisor's rule he cites).
- Steps: 1. Sort roles: marketing/sales; delivery; "other" (legal, admin, IT) = expense, kept tiny. 2. For every dollar you pay one of these people they should help you make $5. 3. If not, "it's your fault it's your responsibility". [quote src: YT:aSzZiCp9GWg]
- Sources: [YT:aSzZiCp9GWg, YT:GtrVGkIVbhc, YT:uOWi13jSAPA] (2019–2026)

#### Five stages to $100K
- Steps: 1. Survival: too many yeses, work "for the exposure". 2. Stability: say no to "supposed opportunities", pick an ICP. 3. Systems: document "five things that every project goes through", productize → "consistent $5 to $8,000 months". 4. Scale: People, Product, Promotion. 5. Sustain: protect your peace; past $100K become quality control. [quote src: YT:Zys3dt503Q4]
- Rule: "the best work that they paid for, not the best work that you can do". [quote src: YT:Zys3dt503Q4]
- Sources: [YT:Zys3dt503Q4, YT:e2zox3-QL5Q] (2025–2026)
- ➕ Worked example of the $10K-a-month target: a coach selling one course a month at $200/hour works 20 hours ($4K), leaving a $6K deficit, so the video production, editing and Teachable setup must be priced at $6,000. Also: at $100/hour you need 100 hours a month, double to $200 and you work half; for $200K a year double the monthly number, $300K triple it; miss $10K in a month and "you don't have a really good shot". — [YT:_fIIa6FeFAY] (2023)

#### Five-step directional feedback
- Steps: 1. Say what you feel ("a little bit confused"). 2. Name the cause (hierarchy). 3. Ask them first: "Ben what would you do to fix the hierarchy here". 4. Only if stuck, recommend, then "what do you think about that Ben". 5. Leave room to negotiate; agree next actions against the brief. [quote src: YT:O6Zordo0OXE]
- Sources: [YT:vURlu5VhFMM, YT:O6Zordo0OXE, YT:DLeSPCTA9Wg] (2016–2025)

#### Score and filter clients
- Steps: 1. Grade A–F, "fire the fs cherish the A's"; per Blair Enns, hunt two better clients while firing two, hold ~10. 2. Filter: can you deliver value, will it give joy, do you like them, are budget and timeline right? 3. Red flags: "so what are you going to do for us"; "wow you guys are really expensive"; "I'll know it when I see it". 4. Keep a deal-killer list. 5. After each regret, write a protocol ("haven't secured financing, see you later"). [quote src: YT:9HYGTjIG64M, YT:7FWGJ3CZdCM, YT:7IwTua4AaRY, YT:UHrjn11F6hc]
- Sources: [YT:LJVP2wRk7Cc, YT:Bm0nYSJikwo, YT:7IwTua4AaRY, YT:b0drEg4TqC0] (2016–2025)

#### The train (firing and other uncomfortable messages)
- When: conflict-averse creatives who blurt only the outcome.
- Steps: 1. Engine, what drives it and how you feel: "I've been up all night. I've been thinking about this for three weeks". 2. Middle, the reason: "you can't consistently show up late and still be here, and I've warned you before". 3. Caboose: "as much as I don't want to do this... I have to let you go". Decide the outcome, "build it backwards", keep it short or you "lose the courage". [quote src: YT:MbGVbB5mkzA]
- Warning beforehand: "It's been brought to my attention..." → "Do you think you can show up on time...?" → "I will have no other choice but then to let you go. Are we clear?" [quote src: YT:tXY5CIDRNhc]
- Sources: [YT:MbGVbB5mkzA, YT:tXY5CIDRNhc] (2024–2025)
- ➕ rapid-fire on firing a client — don't get into the emotion or the reasons; say you will be "better served by somebody else other than me" — [YT:3EZNSdhaovE] (n.d.)
- ➕ lowest career points are layoffs when business slows, about three rounds in 25 years; sits down with three or four people and says the company is going a different direction, "i'm super sorry but i gotta let you go"; some are still upset with him and his answer is I'm sorry, to survive we make hard decisions — [YT:gOCJ3t7sr-E] (n.d.)

#### Character, want, obstacle
- Steps: 1. A specific, flawed character. 2. A want (no want, no tension). 3. An obstacle, usually a mindset. 4. Transformation: "instead of getting what we want, we get what we need". 5. With clients you're the guide: "in this story I'm just the guide". [quote src: YT:FFPaVFBEIhI, YT:Xmt2eg-oAlU]
- Add-on: If you know how the story ends, the writing of the story becomes really easy.
- Sources: [YT:0zmIAhEI09A, YT:FFPaVFBEIhI, YT:j8yGn1v8OgU, YT:s9h8bXPLJMg] (2016–2026)

#### Start at the friction
- Steps: 1. Drop us in ("too much of a setup is really boring"); begin "at the point of greatest conflict". 2. Keep only the conflict points. 3. Include the fear moments. 4. Land the surprise. 5. Say why: "the reason why I tell you the story is because...". [quote src: YT:j8yGn1v8OgU, YT:RA2qEWrY9w0]
- Craft: swap "and then, and then" for "but"/"instead"; sensory detail and dialogue; "Storytelling is story delaying."; Kindra Hall's template: "This is how my life was." / "Something crazy happened." / "This is how my life is now." [quote src: YT:sYyj--Yci6M]
- Sources: [YT:j8yGn1v8OgU, YT:tMAnP6EmF8g, YT:OlZa_n_bGE4, YT:UE3OufWmnMY] (2016–2026)

#### Talk = big idea + story + recap
- Steps: 1. A big idea. 2. A story. 3. A recap with a framework or exercise ("it's called multimodal learning").
- Prep: "I spend 90% of my time thinking and writing": index cards → "paper edit" → slides; record, replay, "unlearn" it; "say less and go deeper". Live: ask real questions ("why are you here I'd love to hear from you"), use your whole body, "build in car crashes". [quote src: YT:KflWLnaMzkY, YT:Ed9OqOQAfBs]
- Sources: [YT:HtZDQ9xoyeE, YT:HJ9IW1w2bDc, YT:g31_p8T8vck] (2018–2025)

#### Rule of five
- Steps: break anything into five, each part into five, "until the step is so easy a monkey can do it"; first = most important; usually ≤3 layers ("Two's too few, seven is too many"). [quote src: YT:A3i6IqRg-pQ]
- Sources: [YT:11z9_iwPq_w, YT:ihh6kJrUoSk, YT:xx5hEGLLvQA] (2017–2025)

#### C = O + J (criticism)
- Steps: 1. Split it: observation (neutral) + judgment (value). 2. Strip the J, keep the kernel ("What a waste of time" → "Please make this video shorter"). 3. Confirms you? "Thank you. I already knew that." 4. Challenges you? Useful data, other meaning, anything actionable? [quote src: YT:piwDPirw0E0]
- Giving it: "What I'm seeing is...", "What I'm feeling is...", "What's working for me", "What's not working for me", relative to the goal; "replace the word you with I". [quote src: YT:piwDPirw0E0]
- Sources: [YT:piwDPirw0E0, YT:U1anI0b1nvI, YT:MqWUPpjMj3o] (2016–2025)
- ➕ Applies the judgment filter to self-talk and to chasing feedback. Catches a participant (Yana) saying "but not a good one" and flags that "good" is itself a judgment. Replacement self-script: "I'm not good. I'm not bad. I'm just working on it." Some feedback helps you judge whether you are connecting, but don't chase it, because it will lead to a path of depression (he adds he is almost sure of that); stripping evaluative words from internal language leaves you a little happier, less on edge, off the emotional roller coaster. — [YT:3Xv8G8KZMhY] (2020)

#### Levels of listening
- Steps: L1 the words ("a vast improvement"); L2 tone ("the strained pregnant yeah"); L3 what isn't said; then what else it could mean → the follow-up. Play back in their words ("Use his language. Don't introduce a new word."); nod so clients aren't "transmitting blind"; name it neutrally ("I notice is a great phrase to use"). [quote src: YT:vhBqPKzArok, YT:vvqV-sN1xRc]
- Sources: [YT:vvqV-sN1xRc, YT:yyZ-d8wWJc4, YT:HcaFgzZx-Qk] (2015–2026)

#### Question ladder
- Value: low = quantitative (due date, budget), "pedantic", makes you an order-taker; higher = qualitative ("how will you judge if this is successful"); highest = purpose questions. Form: open, neutral, concise; not leading or binary. "how" rushes to tactics; "why" can "sound accusatory if the tone isn't correct"; middle ground: "What's motivating our conversation today?" [quote src: YT:ewhdonLymDk, YT:my_p1fDOz00]
- Sources: [YT:o5iqEfJxJ7c, YT:ivKnj9ffcmE, YT:pc66141WYEI, YT:hJ35jP1tKDI] (2017–2026)
#### More from the second pass
- "Invert the pyramid" for products: instead of years of accountants, engineers and researchers first, design the top of the pyramid (what it looks like and how it performs) and make it appear to work to see if people get excited; if they do, an engineer finds a technology to do it in a much smaller form factor; skips research up front because "if you can't get excited about that that's not a product". — [YT:8he7nM4W1vw] (2018)
- "Entrepreneurial checklist" as a reality check, not a pep talk: the business is on your mind 24/7 (vacations, weekends, holidays); you will manage people, which some creatives hate because they want to do the work themselves; you will face clients and very subjective reactions (the client shows it to his wife or daughter and "they didn't like the design"); and you must like being in front of people — [YT:-cGDr8MkADU] (2017)
- The "legal kit" built with his attorney and included in the bootcamp: a massive service agreement, payment terms, IP and indemnification, employee/freelancer agreement, NDA, non-compete, non-solicit (they don't go after your clients), portfolio-usage terms, copyright assignment, arbitration, a release form for people who appear in your videos and projects, and a collection letter ("pay up pay up"). — [YT:eqhSn3HIgMc] (2018)
- Capabilities deck, his "layperson definition" for a student: a deck of few slides ("the fewer the better") showing the breadth of work and the problems you solve, i.e. your services. Usual contents: principals' bios, services, a rough working methodology, then examples proving each service you just claimed (branding agency example: naming, messaging, identity, packaging, competitive research). — [YT:ezQPayph-To] (2018)
- Runway (his name for Mike Janda's "worry date," from the book "Burn Your Portfolio"; he says he is telling it "like it's mine but it's not"): accounts receivable (cash in bank plus unpaid invoices, everything coming in as of today) divided by monthly overhead = months of runway (100 / 25 = 4); worry date = runway minus one month (3 months); a plane needs runway to land or you die. — [YT:gm66Etsi2-c] (2017)
- Labor-vs-volume matrix for choosing what to sell: avoid high labor plus high volume (Fiverr-style, "two logos a day" for almost nothing) and low labor plus high volume (the assembly line, profitable only with lots of clients); stay in the low-volume quadrants: high labor/low volume (labor intensive) or low labor/low volume (mostly thinking, little production, few clients). — [YT:_fIIa6FeFAY] (2023)
- "Personal relationships are the fastest way to grow your business"; his acronym MICSS: Mentorship (a mentor has the answers to your questions), Introductions (more people met, more opportunities), Companionship (when struggling you need someone to walk arm in arm with you), Subcontractors ("when I give up the day-to-day minutia of my business is when it exploded"), Sales — [YT:Q7CL69AphR8] (n.d.)
