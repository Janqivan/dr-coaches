## 2. Frameworks and methods (named models, step sequences)

### Social Judgment Theory headline check: move the belief one step (Levinger)
- When they use it: every purchase-ad headline and hook, replacing "is this headline good?" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] with where it lands.
- Steps: 1. Write the belief held right before purchase: "what do they believe the most that might block this behavior?" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] 2. Sort each line by reaction: indifference ("that's true"), acceptance ("I didn't think about that before" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]), rejection ("I don't believe anything you're saying" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]). 3. Keep lines that move them one step; cut lines that sit inside the belief. 4. Make them question the belief and add proof "in the right spots" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]. 5. Start mid-intensity; climb one notch at a time only if the category needs it. 6. Run several ladder rewrites, "just differently enough" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]. 7. Write for a beginner.
- Their example: #1 bestseller scar serum only confirms the need, yet runs as a purchase ad; ridiculous claims ("drive bananas" to work) are rejected instantly. Rewrites in §4.
- Sources: [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04); speaker unclear: the scale sits beside unaware→most-aware, "it would fit perfectly".

### Valence × intensity (Levinger, after James A. Russell, 1980)
- When they use it: deciding how a message is said — headline, hook, story, music, delivery.
- Steps: 1. Zones: 1 positive/low (cozy, calm, reassuring); 2 positive/high (joy, pride, thrill, "hurry, hype"); 3 negative/low (annoyed, frustrated, fatigued); 4 negative/high (panic, sadness, loss, anxiety). 2. Find the customer's zone: run reviews in a separate context and ask where they sit. 3. Match the category (her brand: "very therapist style" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]; golf: funny, direct — "that's the industry" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]). 4. Pick the exact emotion and its pair: outer-ring emotions (Plutchik; Gloria Wilcox) usually beat core ones; "annoyed" sits nearer zone 4 than "frustrated". 5. Hold one zone (pet peeve: a zone 1/2 opener, then fear urgency). 6. Test across zones: intense words + calm delivery, or "kind of a more boring language" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] + intense delivery.
- Their example: "Barry, your beard is fantastic" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] vs "god level beard" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]; for a private, years-long problem "Join others just like you" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] is "a little too hypie" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU], while "you don't have to suffer alone anymore" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] draws a different customer.
- Sources: [YT:HuI3o3TBI4A, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:LxWwRykBtfc, YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2026-03, 2026-06, 2026-03, 2025-05)

### Buying phases: trigger → exploration → evaluation → purchase (Levinger)
- When they use it: matching copy to where the buyer is.
- Steps: 1. Trigger: the need becomes conscious — life event, social comparison, internal frustration tipping point, external disruption. 2. Exploration: "not looking at products yet" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y], doing "identity work" — sell the category. 3. Evaluation: short list and risk scan ("Is this smart? Should I make this decision today?" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]) — give certainty and peer proof. 4. Purchase: remove friction, reinforce, strip reasons not to move ("If I don't have to move, I won't" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y]), add an offer, keep the message consistent.
- Their example: one stop visual, your creative strategy is on ice — trigger copy "Where is your creative strategy right now?" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]; exploration copy "time is running out" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk], "Learn how the industry is evolving" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk].
- Sources: [YT:po85BKDTG-Y, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:RCrvd8pTUzo, YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-04, 2026-06, 2026-04, 2026-02)

### Three selves (Levinger, self-discrepancy theory)
- When they use it: choosing which self a headline validates; her main iteration lever.
- Steps: 1. Find which self the moment switches on. 2. Actual: me today ("I wish I could lose 30 pounds, but I also love Snickers bars" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]). 3. Ideal: the future ("Be the runner you were always meant to be" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]); everyone targets it, it's rarely on. 4. Ought: past guilt ("I should have done something about this" [quote src: YT:HuI3o3TBI4A]); very sensitive. 5. Write one variant per self (§3).
- Their example: Nike "just do it" [quote src: YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] = ideal; dentistry has no ideal self, so ought ("you should care about your teeth" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU]).
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:HuI3o3TBI4A] (2025-05, 2026-03)

### Four psychology formats (Levinger)
- When they use it: saturated categories; format 1 worked "so incredibly well" [quote src: YT:TouPE2n7mzo] in a couple of accounts.
- Steps: 1. "before the problem had a name" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]: name a years-old, wordless feeling ("I just feel awkward" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]) to get "Oh my god, that's me." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]; sell "the relief to be seen" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]. 2. "social mirror": "not aspirational, it's attainable" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4], "maybe two steps ahead" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]; too aspirational too fast widens the "current me and ad me" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] gap. 3. "reframe": "belief updating" where "everything sounds the same" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]; win the "Oh, wait, really?" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] moment ("he put up the tent but you brought the flask" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]). 4. "specificity": "weirdly almost uncomfortably specific" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4].
- Their example: "The gift that says, 'I cherish who you were and who you're becoming.'" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] — "The specificity itself is actually doing a lot of the work." [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]
- Sources: [YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] (2026-05) · GEN/DTC

### Three-question sequence (Levinger persona study)
- When they use it: checking any creative (pattern across six personas).
- Steps (roughly in order): 1. "What did this do for me today?" [quote src: YT:vXA7E1lcbaU] 2. "Will it taste or feel good?" [quote src: YT:vXA7E1lcbaU] (taste was a "left behind opportunity" [quote src: YT:vXA7E1lcbaU]). 3. "Why should I trust it and is it worth it?" [quote src: YT:vXA7E1lcbaU]
- Their example: "If the creative didn't answer all three of those questions in roughly that order" [quote src: YT:vXA7E1lcbaU], the customer bounced, however good the product or founder's voice.
- Sources: [YT:vXA7E1lcbaU] (2026-05) · DTC

### Market to the moment (Levinger; Cottrell)
- When they use it: broad targeting, where the story does the qualifying ("we got to get tedious" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY]).
- Steps: 1. List every life moment that creates the need. 2. Split each into sub-moments. 3. Write the scene closest to reality, relevant "on a Wednesday or a Tuesday" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]. 4. Add an oddly specific call-out (§3). 5. Cottrell: a situation, not a demographic — "Hey, you a woman that's 30 to 60" [quote src: YT:nUTzR9ilTts] is too broad.
- Their example: hair loss — "big three" pregnancy, gender transition, menopause, then hairline-only thinning, a back patch, a postpartum spot. Weight-loss supplement for a 57-year-old: not "she wants to lose weight" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] but dressing for lunch with a friend who just lost a lot of weight, jeans not fitting.
- Sources: [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY, YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2025-12, 2026-06, 2026-02, 2026-08)

### Layer 3 copy checklist for statics (Denney)
- When they use it: writing or auditing static copy, her most important layer; a winning static does at least one.
- Steps: 1. Specific: demographic, exact numbers, real outcomes, ideally time, effort or cost ("5 minutes", "under $5", "2 inches in just one use" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]). 2. Call out the audience: "Self-selection is absolutely a copy mechanic" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]. 3. Taboo: "Say what your competitors are too polite to say." [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] 4. Primal desire (status, sex, belonging, safety, approval): "The product is just the mechanism" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]. 5. Curiosity loop: "show the setup, but hide the payoff through the click" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc], paid off on the landing page. 6. Name what they fear. 7. Show the transformation; don't describe the after state.
- Sources: [YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] (2026-06) · NOW · GEN

### Awareness, sophistication and the cause reframe (Cooper; guest: Anze Mikac)
- When they use it: briefing "unaware" creative, or when "world's best" claims stop working.
- Steps: 1. Cooper: give each persona an awareness level inferred from VOC; build per level. 2. Check market sophistication (Schwartz) separately: a more sophisticated market needs a new mechanism and how you differ. 3. Mikac: ask unaware of what — problem, cause, or why alternatives fail; problem-unaware audiences "just doesn't exist most of the time" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k]. 4. Research the believed cause ("I'm just a light sleeper" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k]; "I'm just getting old" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k]) and teach the real one (e.g. inflammation) for an "aha moment".
- Their example: Eight's "the world's first smart mattress" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU] worked at stage 1–2 (~2019–2020); by 2026 the market is stage 4 or "arguably even five" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU].
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06, 2026-06)

### Hero vs villain (Cottrell; Denney's variant)
- When they use it: framing a competitor or the "traditional" version as insufficient; contested (§6).
- Steps: 1. First portion all "them". 2. Mirror each "us" point to a "them" point (chemicals, infertility, losing manhood ↔ natural ingredients, women can't keep their hands off you). 3. Make "them" "10 times worse than it actually is" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI], in absolutes ("linked to infertility" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI], never "linked in small doses" [quote src: YT:505rIrOKhWI]) backed by research. 4. "twist the knife" [quote src: YT:KKp3rA0QK9A]: research the villain's downsides (nail biting → infections, hangnails). 5. Final half sells the solution with social and product proof. 6. Villains: an industry, a competitor, the "traditional" version, "the internet"; stack a bonus villain.
- Their example: Denney tells the original brand story instead, "a little bit more objective" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM]: "You don't have to name competitors. You don't have to name names." [quote src: YT:4wM-i1cxtAM]
- Sources: [YT:505rIrOKhWI, YT:KKp3rA0QK9A, YT:4o46eGI6XwQ, YT:NsHk8o3jVvM] (2026-06, 2026-01, 2025-04, 2025-06) · DTC/GEN

### Customer-language mining (Cooper; Denney; Cottrell; Hott)
- When they use it: before any headline or script.
