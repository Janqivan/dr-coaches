# Hooks and the first three seconds

> Scope: winning the opening seconds of a Meta ad — hook anatomy, named hook types and exact lines, visual, text and audio hooks, hook-rate benchmarks, and the 2025 → 2026 changes. Tags: [YT:<id>] = YouTube video id · (YYYY-MM) = upload month · NOW = 2026 evidence, EVERGREEN = 2025 top performer, BOTH = 2025 and 2026 · DTC / LEADGEN / GEN = who it applies to · "mentions" = separate items supporting it · Denney is the voice; others are named (Hott, Cottrell, Levinger, Cooper); guests and unclear speakers are labeled.

## 1. Core positions (ranked by mentions × specificity)

- **A hook is the whole opening package, not the first line** — What the viewer sees, hears and reads in frame zero, plus the vibe, decides who the ad reaches and whether it profits. Cottrell's agency writes every hook in three parts (visual; audible, incl. sound effects; text) because "all of those combined" [quote src: YT:Mqj05tYuDI0] make a winner. Denney's "true hook structure" (credited to Callaway, as captioned): text overlay + sound + visual + vibe (lighting, fonts, overall feel) inside the first 3 s; changing the visual hook tends to move performance more than changing the text-overlay or verbal hook. Levinger: visual + sound (or silence) + text. [YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:IX-2-uSJDk8, YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ, YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] (2026-10, 2026-06, 2026-05, 2025-08) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 12
- **You get one second, not three — so the grab must be visual** — Levinger (2026-06, citing Neurons EEG data): stay-or-leave is decided by about 1 s; "by the time you hit that 3 second mark, the whole thing is over." One second holds five or six spoken words, so "hook doesn't mean words."; the spoken hook "can come at that 3 seconds". Seconds 0–2 need something interesting or open-loop text, or viewers never reach 5 s; check hooks sound on and off. [YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2026-06, 2026-02, 2025-12) · NOW · GEN · mentions 11
- **Don't look like an ad in the opening — blend in to stand out** — Hott: the "ad blocker" is now a "subconscious content blocker"; personalized feeds leave people "on high alert to anything that doesn't matter to them immediately", so look like what they expect to see. Cottrell: "People don't like ads"; a creator handle instead of the brand handle "can massively help your hook rate". Levinger: look like an organic post ("This is why I love ugly ads"). Denney: conversational and founder openers win because they don't signal an ad. (guest: Anze Mikac) a brand name in the first 3 s buys no incremental reach (§6). Condition: recognition hooks when viewers know the brand (Primal Queen BOF) or it is someone else's famous brand or person (McDonald's, a celebrity). [YT:6jvIXIjieyA, YT:q_PsIjZl6e4, YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2025-05, 2025-09, 2025-05, 2026-06) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 11
- **The hook is your targeting** — Cottrell: "creative is your targeting, but more particular, the hook is your targeting." Call out a customer, a problem or the category (naming "leather bag" keeps the uninterested out and, he says, lowers CPMs); treat the hook as any-attention and CPAs are "going to be incredibly high". Denney: Meta reads creative metadata (imagery, messaging, text overlay), so with results coming from Advantage+ or broad, demographic and "If you" callouts are the targeting signal. [YT:93gJ8_qvOQg, YT:IX-2-uSJDk8, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0, YT:wfENnO7PA5Q] (2026-03, 2026-06, 2026-09, 2025-02) · NOW · GEN · mentions 10
- **Grab the right attention — a thumb-stop without relevance loses** — Hott: hook rate can't tell "thumb-stopping" from thumb-stopping and relevant; a lower thumb-stop that stops the right people can win; irrelevant engagement bait, even accidental, is "the worst possible scenario" to spend on, and the setting must fit audience and product (a luxury watch in front of a back-alley dumpster is "not a great first second" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]). Cottrell: a crude grab earns "the most incredible hook rate" from unqualified viewers. (speaker unclear) names it "Hook rate baiting". (guest: Anze Mikac) curiosity without relevance "will just trick the wrong people"; hooks must be "important and um not just interesting". [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs, YT:opWKMc6iavo, YT:Lkx7nOGIslw, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2025-01, 2025-10, 2026-07, 2026-06) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 8
- **Aim each hook at one exact emotion** — Levinger: decide which emotion each hook targets for which customer, using specific outer-ring words from the feelings wheel; "blending it makes it harder to understand", and "very general" hooks get blended reactions; if the team can't name it, the customer can't either (§2). [YT:LxWwRykBtfc] (2026-03) · NOW · GEN · mentions 8
- **Trigger words stop people** — Denney: words that trigger desire, embarrassment, loathing or anxiety sit in many top performers; Levinger adds "high potency" words on mundane subjects, never political. Wording in §3a. [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:t-Xf12o4jt4, YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (2026-06, 2025-08, 2025-12) · BOTH · GEN, DTC · mentions 8
- **Break the pattern with one weird thing that stays relevant** — Levinger's distinction effect: blend in, then make one thing the opposite of normal (one red ball among blue balls; one burnt, smoking pancake on a beautiful breakfast table); "Contrast equals memorability." Hott's condition: a weird surface stops thumbs, but writing in soap on a wall isn't necessarily relevant to the product or audience. [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:opWKMc6iavo] (2025-05, 2026-02, 2026-06, 2025-10) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 8
- **Reuse the principle, never the template** — Levinger: the industry recycles "basically four different hooks" from "4,000 different hook swipe files"; "the exact same hook" shows up at 10 brands. Borrow a strong organic hook only "as long as you don't copy word for word" (as-is only if nobody in your space uses it; otherwise reword, or Meta will ding you). Cottrell: take principles, not lists; most ad hooks read like generic AI copy. [YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:cO1wlDtG5TY, YT:93gJ8_qvOQg, YT:opWKMc6iavo] (2026-06, 2026-06, 2026-03, 2025-10) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 8
- **Iterate with net-new hooks on proven videos — but a new opening is a new ad** — Denney tests four unique hook variations per video creative; in iteration the best results usually come from net-new hooks on existing video, and adding an extra hook to a high performer is one of the easiest lifts. Cooper's condition: hooks aren't interchangeable — swapping the slim guy (25% hook rate) in a Perfect Jean opening for a big guy telegraphs a different segment before he speaks, making "a completely different ad"; the new opening must fit the body's audience. [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:2jVcLu3Earg, YT:t-Xf12o4jt4, YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-02, 2025-05, 2025-08, 2025-01) · EVERGREEN · GEN, DTC · mentions 8
- **Obsess over the first frame; put the relevance signal at the top** — Hott's mistake #1: "you got to obsess over the first frame of the video". Judging ads that fail to get spend as a consumer, he almost always sees why: not relevant to me, boring, doesn't make sense, or unclear in the first frame or second. Relevance takes empathy (what the audience consumes, styles, camera, lighting; "The setting is like the most important thing"); put the signal at the top because that is "what scrolls in first and it's what swipes in first", and build outward from it as MrBeast does ("go study what he does"). Cooper: the first frame decides whether it looks staged; even a mic in a stand vs out of it shifts perception. [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:s4mLWmECoKI, YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-04, 2026-05, 2026-03) · NOW · GEN · mentions 5
- **Curiosity and open loops do the work** — Cottrell: curiosity is key in most successful TOF hooks; open a loop and close it later, as MrBeast does ("I locked 200 people in an ice cave, and here's what happened"); copy only trendy hooks and you "can't really call yourself a marketer." Levinger: the hook only has to make the brain look for the loop — even "just a black screen with like a dot in the middle" can; curious beats inquisitive (a stronger information gap). [YT:KKp3rA0QK9A, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0, YT:gP-68LmgAQ4, YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-01, 2026-09, 2025-11, 2026-02) · NOW · GEN · mentions 6
- **Match the hook to the funnel stage** — A hook can win at TOF and fail at BOF: the TOF audience "has no idea who you are", so the hook must "educate and qualify"; MOF hooks skip what viewers know and answer the objection; BOF puts product + offer right after the hook (method in §2). [YT:93gJ8_qvOQg, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0, YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-03, 2026-09, 2026-06) · NOW · GEN, DTC · mentions 6
- **Stack two or three hooks** — Denney: a multiple-hook "hook reel" up front prevents early drop-off and hits several triggers (identity, controversy, demographic). Cottrell: stacking is #10 of his top 10, and his best hooks work on multiple levels. [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:t-Xf12o4jt4, YT:opWKMc6iavo, YT:93gJ8_qvOQg] (2025-02, 2025-08, 2025-10, 2026-03) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 6
- **Control the eye** — Cottrell: the strongest ads control where you look first and how long you stay. Map what a first-time viewer's eye goes to, which background elements distract and which audio holds attention in the first ~5 s, then "remove any kind of visual chaos"; start mid-movement, because someone posing and about to act "instantly feels like an ad". [YT:zpUsN6xQSGQ, YT:GCugREdurJk, YT:opWKMc6iavo] (2026-05, 2026-01, 2025-10) · NOW · GEN · mentions 4

## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Three-layer hook (Cottrell) / "true hook structure" [quote src: YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] (Denney)
- When they use it: writing, briefing or iterating any video hook.
- Steps: 1. Visual — what happens on screen (start mid-movement; show the product doing something). 2. Audible — the line plus sound effects. 3. Text — plain captions, or something different such as meme text above people's heads that sets the scene. 4. Vibe (Denney) — lighting, font choices, overall feel, all inside the first 3 s. 5. Pair the line with a visual that carries it; when writing the script, focus on the words. Cottrell's 2026-10 extras: lead with a product-related problem; open a loop you close later; "mirror hooks" that mirror who the viewer is and the problems the product solves.
- Their example: "This is what goes into most protein bars" [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg] over a random white liquid poured into a bowl (Aloha), a VSL favorite.
- Sources: [YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:IX-2-uSJDk8, YT:93gJ8_qvOQg, YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ] (2026-10, 2026-06, 2026-03, 2026-05)

### Four 300-millisecond hook elements (Levinger)
- When they use it: building the 2-second grab from what the brain processes instantly ("No more templates." [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]).
- Steps: 1. A human face mid-emotion (animal faces work too, human most; "Anytime you put an animal in an ad, it's probably gonna work" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]). 2. Motion against the scroll — the feed moves up, so something moving down, or odd motion, stops the thumb. 3. A format-breaking visual — something in the wrong spot (a laptop in a refrigerator, with text, product or CTA on screen) or a partial view that becomes something else ("Is it real or is it cake?" [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]; a remote cut in half that is cake). 4. Precognitive sound, e.g. a familiar phone conversation. Add arrows or pointing hands to direct the eye (they raise engagement, CTR and conversion).
- Their example: AutoBrush's inner-mouth opening beat every alternative; the Lust Brands phone call (§4); a street musician's scrunched eyes with no context: "We have no idea. So, we have to stay." [quote src: YT:DEoj28QXUYE]
- Sources: [YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:vBotjaDGNQI] (2026-06, 2026-02, 2026-07)

### The one-second timeline and "Two second grabs" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (Levinger)
- When they use it: auditing a hook frame by frame.
- Steps: 1. 100 ms — ad or content? 2. 300 ms — feel something or not. 3. ~700 ms — knows what's going on. 4. 1 s — stay-or-leave decided, memory encoding starts. 5. Seconds 0–2 — something interesting happens or open-loop text appears. 6. The full hook runs on: "typical for hooks is like 3 to 5 seconds" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk] or "Typically hooks I see five to seven" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]; a point landing at 4.3 s is "pretty short". Sub-second stoppers (2025-12): an interesting sound (or none) plus a surprising or bizarre visual.
- Exercise: play ~7 s of an organic video, ask "what millisecond in the video, you decide to keep watching" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk], then split the 2-second grab from the payoff people credit (they credited the car crash; the text had held them).
- Sources: [YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2026-06, 2026-02, 2025-12)

### "Predictive hook generation" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc] — the feelings wheel (Levinger)
- When they use it: before loading hooks into a test, and in team hook reviews.
- Steps: 1. Pick the customer, then one emotion per hook. 2. Start at the wheel's core (happy, surprised, bad, fearful, angry, disgusted, sad) and move outward toward ~200 micro-emotions; one step past the core, comparing two neighbors (playful vs amazed/excited), is enough. 3. Score candidates with the tool: "respected" read 28/100 scroll-stop, ~35/100 CTR, 55/100 conversion (maybe not visceral enough); "pressured" beat "rushed" on scroll-stop and click with conversion only okay (so TOF); curious beat inquisitive. 4. Team test (~12 people): play the hook + first clips; ask "How did you respond to this?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc], "Which avatar were you going after?" [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc], "Which emotion?"; narrow ring by ring and let the tool pick between the last two. Scattered answers mean the hook isn't targeting one emotion.
- Their example: Emmy's "Why is instant ramen bad for you? Well, let's break it down." [quote src: YT:LxWwRykBtfc] read as curiosity + skepticism (tool: curious 53 vs skeptical 51); its other hook split ten viewers across four emotions.
- Sources: [YT:LxWwRykBtfc] (2026-03)

### Distinction effect (Levinger)
- When they use it: a pattern break that isn't the lazy "I need a good hook" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] reading of it.
- Steps: 1. Blend — look like an organic post. 2. Make one thing the opposite of normal; call out something that "shouldn't be doing what it's doing and make it weird" [quote src: YT:VtEoAOfQsCU], then make your point. 3. Put the product on a very simple background so it looks "like it's not supposed to be that way" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]. 4. Use red on a contrasting background ("we're built to look at things close, especially if it's red" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]). 5. Show size, color or sound differences, not taste: "show as many differences between your product and theirs as possible" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk].
- Their example: square Babybel (§4); a smooth oval vs a square cube capsule ("nobody's going to take a sharp pill" [quote src: YT:CwSAZdtqemk]).
- Sources: [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU, YT:CwSAZdtqemk, YT:DEoj28QXUYE] (2025-05, 2026-02, 2026-06)

### Funnel-stage hook matching (Cottrell, Denney)
- When they use it: picking each ad's hook.
- Steps: 1. Place the viewer: heard of the problem but never heard of you vs "ready to be sold" [quote src: YT:Mqj05tYuDI0]. 2. TOF — educate and qualify: listicles, "I swapped this" [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg], "Most X isn't X anymore" [quote src: YT:Mqj05tYuDI0], Denney's "How to know if" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4]. 3. MOF — skip what they know, answer the objection: "so you don't have to" [quote src: YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] diaries, "This is not your regular X" [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg], "why is it important" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4], price-objection openers. 4. BOF — product + offer right after the hook (the shirt-rip at 40% off; Primal Queen "double discount"). 5. Ask "What information would hook you in" [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg] "if you are in that stage of the customer journey?" [quote src: YT:93gJ8_qvOQg]
- Sources: [YT:93gJ8_qvOQg, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0, YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:qZhOnITrN0Q] (2026-03, 2026-09, 2026-06, 2026-05)

## 3. Hook types and exact wording

### 3a. Spoken and written hooks
- **Demographic hook** (Denney, Cottrell): call out how people categorize themselves — age, job title, lifestyle affiliation, even health conditions — as a top caption or spoken line. Denney: "honestly the top performing hooks so far in 2025"; skip them and "you are probably leaving a lot of money on the table"; combine with controversy or an anti-traditional product angle. Cottrell: viewers self-qualify; hook rate may not be the highest, but viewers are very qualified: "For any Christian parent who's looking for entertainment that actually builds faith, this is it." [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:opWKMc6iavo] (2025-02, 2025-10) · EVERGREEN · GEN · mentions 4
- **"If you"** (Denney): "if you're over 40…", "if you're someone who hates…" — triggers tribal identity so people self-select; call out a persona, lead with a pain point, or lead with the transformation; "it's probably impossible to not increase your hook rates using something like this"; reformatting your top 5–10 hooks this way "tends to always result in winners" (still behind many top performers in 2026-06). [YT:t-Xf12o4jt4, YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2025-08, 2026-06) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 2
- **Trigger words** (Denney; Levinger): "hate" tends to "reach out and hook people really well"; "POV, you hate X" [quote src: YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] (~10–15% of her top-performing creative contained "POV", so nearly every first round has one); "I hate XYZ" [quote src: YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] worked for several brands; test the literal word "controversy" ("quite a bit of performance"); 2026: add "ruining my life". Levinger's "high potency" words on mundane subjects: "today's victim", "they thought it was safe", "she accidentally did...", "buckle up" — "Meta will start to look for it and feed it a little bit better." [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:CCsty8R0UaA, YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (2026-06, 2025-02, 2025-01, 2025-12) · BOTH · GEN, DTC · mentions 7
- **Scam hook** (Denney): "I thought this was a scam" — recently the top-performing, top-spending ad in several accounts ("I almost don't want to share it"); loss aversion plus curiosity about why it wasn't one; an easy iteration on a top video. [YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] (2025-08) · EVERGREEN · GEN · mentions 2
- **Controversy, rage bait, going against tradition** (Denney, Cottrell): a common enemy, hot take or controversy gets shared and debated, so delivery grows; suits products that challenge a norm; viewers want to "see what the fuss is all about". Cottrell: works if the brand accepts some negativity — "I just needed a bar of soap that smells like a man." drew comments like "You want soap that smells like a man?" that kept pushing the ad. Denney's go-against-the-tradition hook names a common belief and challenges it at once, for product education with before-and-afters. [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:zpUsN6xQSGQ] (2025-02, 2026-05) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 6
- **Conversational opener** (Denney): "no because…", "okay these…", "wait why is this [X]" — the viewer feels they joined a conversation already under way and pauses to catch up; works from B2B to beauty to apparel; add it to creator briefs. Her first try (at Julia Bloom's suggestion): "no because the creatives that people love to share online do not actually work". [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q] (2025-02) · EVERGREEN · GEN · mentions 2
- **How-to by stage** (Denney): "how do I know if [common consumer objection]" mirrors "how do I know if it's right for me". 2026: "How to know if" for problem-aware TOF; "How do I pick / how do I choose" between solutions (one client's top performer for 6 months; "still crushing"); "How to start" for very TOF or most-aware buyers ("How do I start to eat a carnivore diet?"), good for services and deep consideration; "most brands need more education in their funnel. This is just a fact." [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:wfENnO7PA5Q] (2026-06, 2025-02) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 3
- **"Why is it important"** (Denney): from Pact's "Why is it important that your undies are organic?"; pair with your biggest value prop; it voices the question fence-sitters think but don't ask — "a really good middle of funnel ad". [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:R3Z1VvvgtzU] (2026-06, 2026-04) · NOW · GEN
- **"a day without pain"** (Denney): lead with the transformation instead of problem→solution; if ads leading with failed attempts work for you, this likely will; she studies Brex for it. **"this one thing"**: "This one is a psychological killer" — one thing promising clarity and relief gets attention, even if it only gets you 30–50% of the way. [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-06) · NOW · GEN
- **"Give me time", "Why did no one tell me", emotional cliffhangers** (Denney): ask up front for time to entertain or educate (a recent top spender; committed viewers lift hook and hold rates); "Why did no one tell me" implies the viewer was misled and makes even an ordinary creator the authority; "people are mad at me because…", "why did I start crying…" came from organic and converted. [YT:t-Xf12o4jt4] (2025-08) · EVERGREEN · GEN
- **Apology and "you're welcome"** (Denney): she calls the apology hook "bottom of the barrel", yet it has worked and "you're welcome" has made top performers — "a certain type of intimacy"; "It's a cheap trick. But try it, particularly for a sale." [YT:I8tXqqfjIX4] (2026-06) · NOW · DTC
- **Exposing the truth** (Cottrell, Cooper): announce a secret because "people love gossip"; Cooper is "already bought in" after "I might get in trouble for this", and "Let me show you a little secret" implies forbidden knowledge. ## 5. Hook-rate benchmarks and numbers
- Hook rate = % of viewers who watched the first 3 s of a video ad; a custom metric (not default in Ads Manager) showing whether the opening reaches the right people; image ads only have CTR — Denney [YT:CCsty8R0UaA] (2025-01)
- No universal ideal: beat your account's average; some clients are profitable at ~12%, others at ~70%, depending on niche competitiveness and how qualified viewers must be — Cottrell, Lesson 13 [YT:Lkx7nOGIslw] (2026-07)
- One client, last 14 days: top spender nearly 40% vs 26% for the close second → work backwards from the winning hook and replicate it — Cottrell [YT:_FMXAlfgYQo] (2026-01)
- Organic creator-marketplace reads: 50% hook rate "pretty good", 7% interaction rate "also pretty strong" — Denney [YT:ePcEcT2PH8E] (2026-04)
- Paid hook rates on record: ~70% exploding melon, a worst performer (2025-10); >50% towel ad with weak CTR (2026-05); ~45–50% wellness expose (2025-10); two "ruining my life" hooks above 45% on agency DTC clients — Denney (2026-06); 36% on 8,000 spend (2026-07); 35% with "really fantastic ROAS" [quote src: YT:nUTzR9ilTts] (2026-08); 25% slim-guy Perfect Jean opening — Cooper (2025-01) [YT:opWKMc6iavo, YT:qZhOnITrN0Q, YT:I8tXqqfjIX4, YT:_9f0rTKKIOY]
- Levinger: decisions at 100 ms / 300 ms / ~700 ms / 1 s; one second ≈ five or six spoken words; full hooks 3–5 s or 5–7 s; rating 2-second grabs (an attendee's framing she endorses), a celebrity face is "a 1", an angle cut "a 1.5" [YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:CwSAZdtqemk] (2026-06, 2026-02)

## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree
- **3 seconds → 1 second.** Denney (2025-02): the first 3 s "determine whether you're going to make money or burn it". Levinger (2026-06): "First 3 seconds matter the most" and "you need a pattern interrupt" are only partly true — "We are off by about 2.7 seconds." Cottrell (2026-07): viewers attend to about the first 2 s. [YT:wfENnO7PA5Q, YT:DEoj28QXUYE, YT:Lkx7nOGIslw]
- **Hook-first vs hold-first.** Denney (2026-05): "Hooks are literally the 80/20 of ad creative." Cottrell (2026-09/10): less "glue this hook onto the front of this video and make it work", more "how can we use the hook to set up the rest of the video?"; the hook "isn't as important as it used to be", high hook rates aren't what they were, and hold rate matters far more, especially for podcast, yapper and VSL ads. [YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0, YT:RExPkxyRB6o]
- **Product in the first seconds.** Denney (2025-08): most buyers and strategists believe the problem or product must appear in the first 5 s, but with Andromeda scalable content must target and convert much higher up the funnel. Levinger (2025-12) rejects show the product within 3 seconds or all is lost: in a micro-movie test the product worked best at the emotional peak. Against: Cottrell (2025-10), product hooks "always win". [YT:t-Xf12o4jt4, YT:sdEV9rZ70BY, YT:opWKMc6iavo]
- **Faces.** Hott (2026-04): his old rule was to obscure a stranger's face in the first second (gesture, motion, overlay, captions); softened as feeds went friends → celebrities → influencers → "now it's just anything", but still cover a face that isn't relevant to your audience. Levinger (2026-06): a face mid-emotion is a top 300-ms grab. [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:DEoj28QXUYE]
- **Brand up front.** (guest: Anze Mikac, 2026-06) a brand name in the first 3 s buys no incremental reach; Cottrell (2026-09): "Primal Queen" in the first frame works BOF for people who know the brand. [YT:KSm0pvevV9k, YT:Mqj05tYuDI0]
- **Lists.** Cottrell avoided "top three reasons why" list hooks (2025-10), then taught "four truths" listicles (2026-03/09). [YT:opWKMc6iavo, YT:93gJ8_qvOQg]
- **Stopped working or losing force:** word-for-word swipe-file templates and generic AI-written hooks (Levinger, Cottrell, 2026); brand-name-first openers outside BOF (guest, 2026); irrelevant thumb-stops (Hott, Cottrell); posed openings (Cottrell); judging hooks by hook rate alone (Cottrell, 2026-07 to 2026-10); plain negative marketing, outpulled by more controversial hooks (Denney, 2025-02).
- **Working in 2026:** Denney (2026-06) — "If you", "ruining my life" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4], "why is it important" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4], "a day without pain" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4], how-to by stage, "this one thing" [quote src: YT:I8tXqqfjIX4], whisper VO, Snapchat filter; Cottrell (2026) — listicles, swaps, "Most X isn't X anymore" [quote src: YT:Mqj05tYuDI0], MOF diaries, unopened boxes, relevant AI visuals, "mirror hooks"; Levinger (2026) — face, counter-scroll motion, format-breaking visual, precognitive sound. Still winning from 2025: demographic, viral-product and reaction hooks.
