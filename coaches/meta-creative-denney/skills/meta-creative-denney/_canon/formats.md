## 2. Frameworks and methods (tier lists, selection models, step sequences)

### Denney's tier scale, S to F: unicorns vs supporting cast (Denney)
- When she uses it: ranking formats by whether they create scale. "Formats go in and out of style" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] — she admits she once told viewers to test formats she now ranks low.
- Steps: 1. Check whether the format lands in the "top eight" (or top 15) performers on Motion reports. 2. Name its job: S = "god-like" formats that almost always become "unicorn scaling ads" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] that puncture net new audiences; lower tiers = supporting cast members that build the ecosystem (top-funnel support or mid-funnel converters). 3. F = don't spend budget on it.
- Her placements (Aug 2026): **S** founder content, partnership ads · **A** authority ads, catalog/DPA (with a good catalog system), amateur investigation · **B** before/after, progression, EGC, TikTok love letter, "The real short" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8], comment reply · **C** celebrity · **E** carousel · **F** podcast ads.
- Sources: [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-08)

### Four-criteria tier scoring (Cottrell)
- When he uses it: ranking 10 formats "based on real data of what is working right now" [quote src: YT:y7TFcfgwhP8].
- Steps: score each format on 1. ease of production; 2. conversion power (does it bring sales); 3. scalability (how much budget it takes); 4. longevity (still around in 6 months, or a trend).
- His placements (Jun 2026): **S** mashup, founder, static · **A** mini VSL, UGC testimonials · **B** before/after, high-production video · **C** street interviews · **E** podcast ads · bonus "secret" format: TikTok Shop yapper.
- Oct 2026 update, formats that "always win" right now: UGC yapper, founder ads, mini VSLs, podcast ads — many paired with partnership ads.
- Sources: [YT:y7TFcfgwhP8] (2026-06), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10)

### September 2026 creator-format mix (Denney)
- When she uses it: from an analysis across all her agency's current brands; she gave a consulting client this exact mix for their creator program.
- Steps (her order): 1. authority angle — who the persona admires, aspires to be, trusts as an authority (an influencer? a doctor?); 2. personal history yappers; 3. educational breakdowns; 4. investigative journalism ads; 5. transformations and comparisons.

### Ad styles his agency is scaling (Cooper)
- When he uses it: his agency makes ads for 25–30 DTC brands; his consulting line lately: "more Yappa's, more AI animations" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ].
- Steps: Style 1 yapper (aka ramblers/organic) — unscripted storytelling; Style 2 avatar-specific — one persona, not broad relevance, because feeds are hyper-relevant; Style 3 AI animation; Style 5 challenge ads — "when you get them right, they will absolutely crush" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ], because they open a loop. Both ends work if done right: "super AI forward" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k] and "super ugly".
- Sources: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06)

### TEAP: Trigger, Exploration, Evaluation, Purchase (Levinger)
- When she uses it: deciding what to make this week; adapted from a Google study of digital purchasing (people loop in and out of exploration and evaluation, plus a post-purchase loop); tested across ~150 creative strategies.
- Steps: 1. Count ads per phase to spot over-indexing. 2. TRIGGER (pre-awareness): relatable micro moments, "this is so me" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] — name the moment so people "feel seen, not sold to" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; a trigger ad opens with "something just happened" [quote src: YT:vBotjaDGNQI]. 3. EXPLORATION (low commitment, loves novelty): short-form, ASMR, "gloopy" satisfying content, visual hooks, memes, "car chases", "dogs barking" — light, earns the right to say more. 4. EVALUATION: DR skills — testimonials, before/afters, education, case studies, comparisons, table-style ads. 5. PURCHASE (easiest): long form, founder ads, plain offers (50% off, free gift). 6. Keep a Motion library sorted by phase.
- Rules: "You're not making ads for an audience" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] but for a brain in a specific state, usually not feeling the pain right now; exploring brains reject anything too clever or deep too fast; choose content type after story structure (a "slow flex" static vs a POV video).
- Their example: AutoBrush and Ridge (§4).
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o, YT:vBotjaDGNQI] (2026-06, 2026-06, 2026-07)

### Five "money maker" formats and the three-condition test (Levinger)
- When she uses it: tested up front with each new brand — "not trends… not going to disappear in 6 months" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4].
- Steps: 1. Test the five: before the problem had a name; social mirror; reframe; specificity; earned confidence. 2. Earned confidence (one of the easiest) suits audiences who already like your ads and organic: show "receipts" — third-party validation, real customer footage, "proof that doesn't feel like it's produced" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]. 3. Keep a format only when three things line up: it matches the customer's psychological state at that moment, reduces decision friction, and carries the right emotional certainty. 4. Name the mechanism under each winner; her Jul 2026 slate (literal POV, multi-series, subtle flex, micro-learning, lo-fi yap) rests on mirror neurons, contrast effect, processing fluency, reciprocity. 5. Familiarity effect: borrow formats the customer already knows (magazine articles, CAT scans, price stickers, nutrition labels) — boomers may prefer an article-format ad to a TikTok-style hook.
- Sources: [YT:6ZBccBr5AV4, YT:a5ruZB-Urlw, YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] (2026-05, 2026-07, 2026-06)

### Funnel and awareness format map (Cottrell) — and Denney's objection
- When he uses it: sequencing formats; a format/stage mismatch is where users drop off.
- Steps: 1. TOF (never heard of you, often problem-unaware): easy, educational — UGC, street interviews, founder ads. 2. MOF (problem-aware, not product-aware): heavy education and desire — mini VSLs, mashups, podcast ads. 3. BOF (ready to buy): statics, offer ads, testimonial compilations. 4. By awareness: problem unaware → make the situation familiar ("Hey, that's me" [quote src: YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q]); problem aware → friction and emotion via UGC demos, testimonials; solution aware → us-vs-them statics with yours on top; product aware → build trust, remove the blocker; most aware → offer statics.
- Denney's objection: format doesn't make an ad TOF or BOF; ugly ads for unaware/problem-aware, polished ads for product/most-aware is oversimplified — awareness levels are for analyzing, not ideating, and great creative resonates emotionally rather than aiming at an "arbitrary" level.
- Sources: [YT:gP-68LmgAQ4, YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:2jVcLu3Earg, YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-11, 2025-11, 2025-05, 2025-01)

### Creative-diversity levels: format is only Level 1 (Denney)
- When she uses it: diagnosing how diverse an account really is.
- Steps: Level 0 — only statics or only video; "I have both statics and videos in the mix" [quote src: YT:ZydGeio1DGQ] is still level 0. Level 1 — format expansion: keep a master list of every format; cheap way to change visuals, but rarely creates unicorns; many brands stop here, "leaving so so much money on the table" [quote src: YT:ZydGeio1DGQ]. Level 5 — excel at every level across a suite of products and personas, or own a unique organic content strategy (Apothekary's documentaries, Clue's episodic content). Beginners: two buckets, static vs video; carousels are "not a really good place to start" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM].
- Sources: [YT:ZydGeio1DGQ, YT:NsHk8o3jVvM] (2025-12, 2025-06)

### Same message, different format (Cottrell, Cooper)
- When they use it: adding diversity without new messaging risk; extending a winner's life.
- Steps: 1. Fix persona × message (athletes × peak performance on long runs/marathons). 2. Build it in two formats with format-fit hooks — podcast lifts a dramatic moment to the start; UGC opens on a visual (pouring a sachet). 3. Test both: "Same persona, same messaging, different format" [quote src: YT:CPWoW09WCCo]. 4. Variant trick (Cottrell): run the plain talking version (creator + captions + comment), then a duplicate with AI B-roll cut in at matching lines (bottle shot on "That's why I switched to..." [quote src: YT:ahwbstiCVpQ]) — two launchable ads from one generation set. 5. Cooper's loop: AI-animation copy ≈ native-ad primary text ≈ yapper script, so a winner moves between them; it worked "many times for us" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ].
- Sources: [YT:CPWoW09WCCo, YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ, YT:ahwbstiCVpQ, YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2026-02, 2026-08, 2026-03, 2025-11)

### Founder ads: why they work and how to shoot them (Cottrell; Denney, Hott)
- When they use it: every brand, early — often the first top performer.
- Steps (why, Cottrell): 1. instant trust; 2. the story pulls the customer in; 3. "probably the most important" [quote src: YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA]: nobody can copy your story; 4. it feels native — "a real person talking to another real person through a screen" [quote src: YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA]. A brand-written script should come from a brand representative, not a UGC creator reading reviews.
- Steps (how): founder reads the script to camera with captions; or a voice note over B-roll; or a studio chat where the founder goes off-script; interview or prompt style (guest: Harry Delmege). Tell the why, never the pitch. Denney: founders should "do personal history angles so that they can clearly share the why" [quote src: YT:GbXeAYTXhP4].
- Their example: Humantra, The Black Stuff, Kolkata Chai (§4).
- Sources: [YT:zpUsN6xQSGQ, YT:Ry7KQFZaPGA, YT:9UBSGSS2Yuo, YT:EgnN83jIsj8] (2026-05, 2025-12, 2026-06, 2026-03)

### Partnership-ad playbook (Cottrell, Denney; guest: Danil Saliukov)
- When they use it: whenever a creator's face or handle can add trust and reach. Whitelisting = brand runs ads from a creator's page, creator's handle on top, brand in control — "borrowing their brand, their face, their voice" [quote src: YT:q_PsIjZl6e4]; partnership ad = boosted from the creator's own post, both visible — "the best of both worlds" [quote src: YT:q_PsIjZl6e4] (Cottrell).
- Steps: 1. Start from a good ad — "It has to be a good ad to start with." [quote src: YT:q_PsIjZl6e4]; winners are usually phone-shot, person-led, lo-fi, unedited UGC. 2. When a UGC ad performs from the brand page, negotiate to run the same video as a partnership ad. 3. Test ~5–7 days for incremental lift; if lift, maybe a one- or three-month deal. 4. Avoid misfits: high-production ads from a person look weird; a partner identity on a video featuring a different creator "just gets messy" [quote src: YT:q_PsIjZl6e4]. 5. (guest: Danil Saliukov) Don't bet on one piece from a top influencer; pick influencers with an ads-ready audience and UGC skills, request multiple pieces (three hooks, a couple of directions, B-roll), post-edit, run several. 6. Denney: test a creator's content before running it as partnership ads; read competitors' partnership ads for who they want to be associated with and their partnership vs brand spend split.
- Sources: [YT:m8dbcF407H8, YT:q_PsIjZl6e4, YT:ePcEcT2PH8E, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-03, 2025-09, 2026-04, 2026-08)

### Making ugly ads on purpose (Hott, Cooper, Denney)
- When they use it: any ad meant to blend into the feed.
- Steps: 1. Pick a lane (Cooper): fully ugly — must look exactly like organic feed content — or an obvious ad with great DR copy (DR script + AI voiceover + B-roll); never the middle. 2. Ask "how would a creator frame this?" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8] instead of how a professional would (Hott). 3. Emulate what the audience consumes and "just shoot ads" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]: talk to camera, or film from behind holding your phone (Hott). 4. Luxury: let setting, talent and wardrobe pass "the sniff test of luxury" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs], then make the ad that sells (Denney). 5. Team drills (Denney): what would the ad be if you had 5 minutes? Call your mom, partner or best friend and sell them a product you loved — note what you emphasize. 6. Win client approval (Hott): show relevant organic examples that are "actually a little bit polished" [quote src: YT:m8dbcF407H8], other experts, and brands already doing it.
- Ugly types (Denney, Jan 2025): Post-it note ad; one-take phone videos ("just Yap"); POV shots of the product in action; nostalgia graphics — Meta calls this lo-fi content.

## 3. Format guide and templates (job · when it works · for whom)
Founder, partnership and static are covered in §1–§2; tiers are in the §2 table.

- **Yapper / TikTok Shop yapper** — holds attention like organic; aim for "Oh my goodness, I just I just watched 2 minutes of this ad" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] Follow good yappers; comedians excel (Daniela Mora's 1–2-minute relationship stories; Daddy Wellness) (Cooper). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:4wM-i1cxtAM, YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] · DTC
- **Amateur investigation** — hire a creator to investigate your product or niche (e.g., visiting different car dealerships); her team popularized it and it scales well (Denney). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] · GEN
- **EGC** — behind-the-curtain trust, but rarely scales like founder content and fades unless it is in the job description; best version = warehouse content; no warehouse → stock warehouse footage or a green-screen effect (Denney). (guest: Mirella Crespi): "people want to see behind the curtain" (ClickUp's office-worker TikToks). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q, YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] · DTC/GEN
- **Celebrity · street interview** — celebrity can be "a massive unlock" or "a big money pit." (Denney). Street interview: TOF intro that feels like TikTok but hard to produce — unless founder-led like Carpe's (Cottrell). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:y7TFcfgwhP8] · DTC
- **Podcast ad** — if you make one, it must look and sound like a real podcast (film properly, structure, good script, great cast); a short one does the job of a glossy video and a long VSL; a creator with a cheap Amazon mic in a living room "looks like trash. It doesn't look believable." (Cottrell). Denney's exception: a founder on an actual well-known podcast. [YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] · DTC/APP
- **Mashup** — MOF message and hook testing at speed: archive raw UGC without captions, recut with an AI voiceover (Cottrell). [YT:y7TFcfgwhP8, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] · DTC
- **Mini VSL** — educates on something people never heard of or didn't know they needed (Cottrell); ~1 minute to 90 seconds, as a classic VSL, a person educating, or a mini-VSL script delivered as a yapper (guest: Anze Mikac). [YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:KSm0pvevV9k] · GEN/DTC
- **High-production video** — converts only when visuals demonstrate everything being said, not "a load of random pretty shots"; can stand out in a UGC-saturated category (Portland Leather) (Cottrell). [YT:y7TFcfgwhP8, YT:oZFiSUkJEPE] · DTC
- **Before/after · progression** — mid-funnel converters, not net new reach (Denney). Video "my life before / my life after" scales but is hard to write and often cheesy; split before/after statics are often disallowed by Meta; makeup: "people live and breathe on before and afters" (Cottrell). Progression = results over a specific time customers can anticipate (Denney). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:y7TFcfgwhP8] · DTC
- **AI animation** — skeleton/X-ray ("because it's a little bit wild, the user kind of forgives that it's AI."), Pixar-style talking characters (now in-house work for a strategist), claymation; animated statics are resurging — any clean static has "all the ingredients" (Cottrell). [YT:n_EyIK5eSWY, YT:xIpMOTNFvkY, YT:cv9GbhtGLCc] · GEN
- **Wall-of-text video** — on-screen text, static ~10 seconds or scrolling up (guest: Anze Mikac); "would crush" for non-e-com/digital products (newsletters, info products, SaaS) and also worked across e-com, less strongly; "stupidly simple" (Cooper). [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] · LEADGEN/GEN/DTC
- **TikTok love letter · viral short** — love letter = long text on a short video: call out the audience at once ("Listen, girls", "guys", "hot girls"), mine viral TikTok comments for vernacular, use creator or iPhone B-roll; viral short = one or two lines, often a POV opener. Both "dominating on Reels" (Apr 2026) but TOF "supporting characters." (Denney). [YT:ePcEcT2PH8E, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] · GEN
- **Comment reply** — when one objection or FAQ keeps popping up; moves mid/lower funnel but won't scale "to the moon" unless the question is more top-of-funnel (Denney). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] · GEN
- **Catalog / DPA** — build a catalog system with different templates and creatives inside the DPA; it can go broad (DABA, "dynamic ads broad audience") for cold prospecting — "catalog ads run on a different algorithm"; most useful multi-SKU (Denney). (guest: Kaylin Parker): fills SKU gaps with injected creative templates (Marpipe). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:_ZHS6tW3f_Q, YT:R3Z1VvvgtzU] · DTC

- **Listicle and classics** — listicle ("three reasons why") is "bottom of the barrel creative-wise" yet the fallback that saves the team; us vs them and feature/benefit callouts are often the first ad that crushes for new, budget or just-launched brands (Denney). **Rising in 2026**
