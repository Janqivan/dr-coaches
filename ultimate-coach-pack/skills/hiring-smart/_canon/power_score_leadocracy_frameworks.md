## Framework 1 — The PWR Equation (Power Score, 2015)

**The math:** Power Score = Priorities × Who × Relationships. Each axis scored 1-10. The composite is MULTIPLIED, not added.

**Why multiply, not add?** A leader scoring 10/10/2 (perfect priorities, perfect team, broken relationships) ships nothing. The 2 collapses the whole equation. Adding would mask that. Multiplying surfaces the bottleneck — and the bottleneck is always the lowest axis.

**Calibration anchors:**
- 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000 (ceiling — rare in practice)
- 9 × 9 × 9 = 729 (Geoff's working "A-leader" floor)
- 8 × 8 × 8 = 512 (solid but room to climb)
- 7 × 7 × 7 = 343 (warning zone — coachable but exposed)
- Anything below 6 on ANY axis = the leader is at risk, regardless of the other scores

**Operator use:** the composite isn't the point. The lowest score IS the point. That's the next conversation, the next coaching call, the next decision.

## Framework 2 — The Priorities Diagnostic (Power Score, 2015)

The first P. Tests whether the leader is actually working on what matters.

**The diagnostic questions:**
1. What are your TOP 3 priorities for the next 12 months?
2. Are they written down?
3. Could every direct report name them, unprompted?
4. When was the last time you reviewed them?
5. What percentage of your calendar last week mapped to them?

**Scoring band:**
- **10** — 1-3 priorities, written, reviewed weekly, calendar tracks them at 60%+
- **5** — priorities exist but are vague, shift quarterly, calendar tracks them at 20%
- **1** — drowning in inbox, no written priorities, calendar reactive

**Failure pattern:** the founder lists 11 priorities. That's not priorities — that's a to-do list. Geoff's frame: if everything's a priority, nothing is.

**Operator implication:** the Priorities score is the easiest to fix — it requires a decision, not a hire. That's why it's listed first.

## Framework 3 — The Who Scoring (Power Score, 2015)

The middle W. Tests whether the leader's team is composed of A-players in their CURRENT seats. (Not in some hypothetical future seat — the seat they hold today.)

**The diagnostic move:** list every direct report. For each, ask: "Is this person an A-player in THIS seat, by the 90/10 definition from *Who*?"

**Scoring band:**
- **10** — every direct report is an A-player in current seat
- **7** — most are A's, one B that's coachable, no C's
- **5** — mix of A's and B's, possibly one C
- **3** — multiple B's or one C in a critical seat
- **1** — C-players in critical seats, gaps unfilled, hires not made

**Failure pattern:** the founder calls their team "great" because they like them. Geoff's frame: liking is not the test. The test is the 90/10 — does each person have a 90% probability of hitting the outcome only the top 10% could hit?

**Cross-link:** the Who score is downstream of the Scorecard discipline from *Who*. Without Scorecards, the Who score is opinion. With Scorecards, it's evidence.

## Framework 4 — The Relationships Score (Power Score, 2015)

The final R. Tests whether the leader gets work done THROUGH people, or AROUND them.

**The diagnostic questions:**
1. When there's conflict on the team, do you address it directly or work around it?
2. Do your direct reports come to you with problems, or hide them?
3. When was the last time you gave a direct report hard, specific feedback?
4. Do you build trust by following through on commitments, or by being likable?

**Scoring band:**
- **10** — leverages team, conflicts addressed directly, trust is high, leader is the multiplier
- **5** — transactional, gets things done but at a relationship cost
- **1** — leader works around the team, avoids conflict, builds resentment, becomes the bottleneck

**Failure pattern:** the founder is high on Priorities and Who but low on Relationships. They've got the right team, the right targets — and they're burning the team out by going around them. PWR collapses.

**Operator implication:** Relationships is the hardest to coach because it touches the leader's identity. But it's also the highest-leverage score — moving from 5 to 8 unlocks the other two axes.

## Framework 5 — Power Score as an Audit Tool (Power Score, 2015)

Not a hiring tool. A performance and coaching tool. Run on yourself, on your direct reports, and on candidates already in seat for 90+ days.

**Cadence Geoff recommends:**
- **Quarterly self-score** — 30 minutes, written
- **Quarterly direct-report scoring** — one row per direct report, three columns (P/W/R), composite
- **Annual board-level review** — used by ghSMART when advising CEOs and boards

**The trap:** scoring yourself a 10 across the board. If you score 10/10/10, you're not auditing — you're rationalizing. Geoff's frame: real leaders almost always self-score lower than their boards score them. If you're scoring higher than the board would, you've miscalibrated.

## Framework 6 — The 3 As of Leadership (Leadocracy, 2012)

Geoff's tighter taxonomy for what makes a leader hireable into ANY high-stakes seat (his focus in Leadocracy was government, but the 3 As travel).

**Aware** — clear-eyed about the situation, the team, themselves. No magical thinking, no denial. Knows what they don't know.

**Agile** — can adapt as conditions shift. Doesn't lock onto a plan that's no longer real. Updates priors when new information arrives.

**Aligned** — operates from a stable set of values that match the seat's mission. Doesn't shift principles for convenience or political pressure.

**Operator use:** the 3 As are a 30-second sanity check on a candidate before deciding whether to invest in a full Scorecard + Topgrading sequence. If the candidate fails any one A, the deeper process won't save them.

## Framework 7 — The Non-Leader Archetypes (Leadocracy, 2012)

Geoff's catalog of the people who LOOK like leadership candidates but aren't. Each archetype fails one or more of the 3 As.

**The Turtle** — pulls into shell when stakes rise. Looks competent in low-pressure work, vanishes when accountability lands. Fails Agile.

**The Bureaucrat** — process-obsessed, decision-averse. Will study an issue for 18 months rather than commit. Fails Awareness (mistakes the map for the territory).

**The Screamer** — leads by volume and intimidation. Confuses noise with authority. Fails Alignment (their values are about being right, not about the mission).

**The Idealist** — beautifully articulate about what SHOULD happen, has no idea how to make it happen. Fails Agile (won't ship anything imperfect).

**Operator use:** when a candidate looks polished but something feels off, run them against the four archetypes. Naming the pattern is half the diagnosis.

## Framework 8 — The Public-Sector Flow Thesis (Leadocracy, 2012)

Geoff's argument for WHY private-sector A-players should rotate into government seats: the work is more interesting, not less.

**The thesis:** high-skill operators in private companies eventually hit a ceiling where the problems are well-bounded — optimization at the margin. Government seats, by contrast, run on under-resourced teams with maximum-stakes outcomes (education systems, public safety, infrastructure). That gap is where flow happens.

**Why most A-players skip it:** the perception is that government is slow, political, and capped. Geoff's response: the seats that aren't slow / political / capped are the ones where great leaders haven't been recruited yet. The recruitment gap IS the opportunity.

**Operator application (DR-relevant):** Ivan doesn't run government. But the flow logic transfers — A-players are drawn to UNDER-RESOURCED, HIGH-STAKES seats more than to comfortable ones. The Sell step (Geoff's 4 F's from *Who*) should lead with the size of the problem, not the size of the comp.

## Framework 9 — A-Method Applied to Civic Seats (Leadocracy, 2012)

The Scorecard / Source / Select / Sell sequence from *Who*, restated for government roles. The mechanics don't change. What changes:

- **Scorecard** — outcomes must be operationally specific in a context where political outcomes often dominate (e.g. "reduce average DMV wait time from 47 to 20 minutes" not "improve constituent experience")
- **Source** — pull from the private sector deliberately, not just from inside government
- **Select** — the Topgrading interview matters MORE, not less, because civic resumes hide more
- **Sell** — Fortune (comp) is structurally weaker in government, so Fit / Family / Freedom must do more work

**Operator implication:** if the A-Method works for the messiest hiring environment in the world (government), it works for everything in between. Leadocracy is, in effect, the stress test of *Who*.
