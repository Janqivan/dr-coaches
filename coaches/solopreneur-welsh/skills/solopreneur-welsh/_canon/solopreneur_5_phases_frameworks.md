# 5 Phases of Solopreneurship + The $10M Solo Roadmap

Two frameworks live in this file. They're related but distinct.

The **5 Phases** is the developmental model: where you are right now,
what your constraint is, what to fix next, what NOT to do yet.

The **$10M Roadmap** is the operational map: the 23-step path Welsh
took from corporate burnout to $10M+ in cumulative solo revenue at
~89% margin, no employees, no office, no investors. Use it as a
sequence reference — not as a literal blueprint to copy.

---

# PART ONE — THE 5 PHASES OF SOLOPRENEURSHIP

Each phase has:
- A defining KPI
- A defining constraint
- A required behavior
- A forbidden behavior (the trap)
- A graduation signal (when to move to the next phase)

Wrong-phase advice is worse than no advice. Diagnose first.

---

## PHASE 1 — SURVIVAL

You just left the job, or you're moonlighting toward leaving.
You have an opinion, no audience, no product, no revenue.

**Defining KPI:** Did you ship 5 pieces of content this week, in
public, attached to your real name?

**Defining constraint:** Identity. You don't know who you are
publicly yet. You're still writing what you think people want to
read, not what only you can say.

**Required behavior:**
- Pick 3 pillars even if you're not 100% sure
- Post daily on ONE platform (LinkedIn is the highest-ROI place
  to start in 2026 for a B2B / operator audience)
- Engage 30 minutes/day on 10-20 other posts in your niche
- Live cheap. Free tools only. Notion, Kit free tier, LinkedIn.
- Write down every comment, DM, and "what do you do" question
  you get — that's market research

**Forbidden:**
- Building a website with a Stripe checkout you don't need
- Hiring a VA
- Buying ads
- Pre-selling a course you haven't outlined
- Starting an agency

**Graduation signal:** You have 1,000+ engaged followers, you've
been posting daily for 90+ days, and people are DM'ing you asking
how you did X.

**This is where 95% of would-be solopreneurs fail.** Not because
the bar is high — because the bar is "show up daily for 6+ months
before any reward." Most quit at week 8.

---

## PHASE 2 — GROWTH

You've proven you can post consistently. Now you scale audience
and ship the first revenue.

**Defining KPI:** Newsletter list growth rate (subscribers/week)
and first product revenue.

**Defining constraint:** You don't own your audience yet. Social
followers are rented. The job in Phase 2 is to convert rented
attention into an owned email list.

**Required behavior:**
- Launch the newsletter. Even at 0 subs. Even at 500 words.
- Every social post links to the newsletter signup at least
  implicitly (bio link, comment CTA, profile banner)
- Ship a first product in the $99-$200 range. Not a $2,000
  flagship. A small, narrow, "here's exactly how I do X" course.
- Sell to the newsletter list ONLY. No social hard-sell yet.
- Hit 10K followers + 1K newsletter subscribers as the floor

**Forbidden:**
- Building an "ecosystem" before you have one product
- Cohort-based courses (you can't fill them yet)
- Trying to scale 5 platforms at once
- Spending more on tools than the business is making

**Graduation signal:** 10K+ followers on your primary platform,
1K+ newsletter subscribers, first product launched and earning
at least $5K/month.

---

## PHASE 3 — EXPANSION

You have a working machine on one platform. Now you expand.

**Defining KPI:** Multi-platform reach + product line growth.

**Defining constraint:** Single point of failure. If your one
platform suspends you tomorrow, you're done.

**Required behavior:**
- Open a second platform (LI → X, or X → LI). Don't start from
  zero — repurpose what already worked
- Launch a second product. Bigger price point ($250-$500).
  This is where LinkedIn OS + Content OS lived for Welsh.
- Newsletter sponsorship spots become available around 25K-50K
  subscribers — start a waitlist
- Build a free lead magnet that ladders to a paid product

**Forbidden:**
- Going on more than 2 platforms simultaneously
- Hiring full-time employees (contractors only, and only if
  there's a single repeatable task that you genuinely hate)
- Launching a flagship $1,000+ product before this phase

**Graduation signal:** Two platforms above 50K followers each,
newsletter above 25K, two products in market, MRR or
predictable monthly product revenue above $30K.

---

## PHASE 4 — DEFENSE

You're now visible enough to attract copycats, scammers, and
platform risk. Build the moat.

**Defining KPI:** % of revenue from owned channels (newsletter,
website, direct email) vs. rented platforms.

**Defining constraint:** Concentration risk. If too much depends
on one platform algorithm, you're exposed.

**Required behavior:**
- Professionalize the brand: real site, real design system,
  real product pages with real copy
- Start SEO content (long-form articles on your domain)
- Diversify income: course sales + newsletter sponsorships +
  affiliate revenue + (optionally) a paid community
- Document your IP — frameworks get named, templates get
  packaged, processes get written down
- File trademarks on the framework names that work

**Forbidden:**
- Selling out the audience for short-term sponsor cash
- Saying yes to every podcast (your time is now the constraint)
- Building an SaaS because "it's the next step" (it's not)

**Graduation signal:** Newsletter is the single biggest traffic
source to your site, sponsorship revenue is consistent monthly,
SEO is starting to compound, your frameworks are being cited
by other creators in your niche.

---

## PHASE 5 — AUTOMATION

The business runs as a system. You optimize, you don't grind.

**Defining KPI:** Profit margin and hours/week to maintain.

**Defining constraint:** Yourself. Bored solopreneurs make
terrible decisions — they hire teams they don't need, they
take VC they don't need, they "scale" themselves out of the
exact thing that made them rich.

**Required behavior:**
- Evergreen sales funnels — your courses sell while you sleep
- Automated email sequences — every new subscriber goes through
  a 30-60 day nurture that pitches the product ladder
- Newsletter sponsorships sold 3-6 months ahead via a simple
  Airtable + email flow
- Refresh content from the 730-day library on auto-cadence
- Optionally: invest in OTHER one-person businesses (Welsh's
  current frame — "a portfolio of one-person businesses")

**Forbidden:**
- Hiring a 10-person team because you got bored
- Raising venture capital
- Launching an agency under your personal brand
- Going on a 50-podcast tour to "get back to growth mode"

**Graduation signal:** None. Phase 5 is the destination.

# PART TWO — THE $10M SOLO ROADMAP

Welsh's "$10M journey" essay walks through 23 steps from corporate
exit (2019) to $10M+ in cumulative revenue (around mid-2025). The
business operates at ~89% margin, zero employees, zero VC, zero
paid ads. This is the canonical anti-team / anti-VC playbook.

Here is the compressed sequence, mapped onto the 5 Phases:

### Phase 1 — Survival (months 0-12)
1. Leave the job (or commit to a clear exit date)
2. Pick ONE platform — Welsh picked LinkedIn because his audience
   (operators, founders, sales leaders) was already there
3. Pick 3-5 content pillars
4. Post daily. No exceptions. For 12 months minimum.
5. Build the hook bank from your top performers
6. Spend 30-45 min/day engaging on others' content

### Phase 2 — Growth (year 1-2)
7. Launch the newsletter — even at 0 subs
8. Hit 1K newsletter subscribers by being honest about wanting
   them (CTAs in posts, bio link, the friction is the point)
9. Build the first product: $99-$200, narrow, "here's how I do X"
10. Sell to the newsletter list, not to social — this is the
    rule. Newsletter sells. Social attracts.
11. Ship version 2 of the first product based on customer feedback
12. Hit $10K/mo product revenue

### Phase 3 — Expansion (year 2-3)
13. Open the second platform (Welsh: X). Repurpose, don't restart.
14. Launch the second product. Premium tier. $250-$300.
15. Add a recurring offer — Welsh's was The Monthly Templates at
    $9/mo (a $9 upsell that did $23K/mo within 8 months)
16. Newsletter sponsorships go live around 25K-50K subs.
    Welsh charges $2,500 x 2 slots = $5K/issue at 175K readers
17. Build evergreen sales — abandoned-cart sequences, post-purchase
    upsells, welcome flows that pitch the ladder

### Phase 4 — Defense (year 3-5)
18. Rebrand professionally — site, copy, design system
19. Start the SEO content layer on the domain
20. Document and name your frameworks (LinkedIn OS, Content OS, PAIPS,
    1-3-5, Hub-and-Spoke, 730-Day Library — names matter)
21. Launch the flagship — Welsh's Creator MBA, 19 hours, 111 lessons,
    $997, which did $1.5M in a 6-day launch on the existing list

### Phase 5 — Automation (year 5+)
22. Evergreen everything. Sales funnels run without you.
23. Diversify INTO solo businesses, not OUT of them — invest in or
    acquire other one-person businesses rather than blowing up your
    own with a team

## THE LADDER OF PRODUCTS (HOW MONETIZATION COMPOUNDS)

This is the price/value ladder Welsh has publicly run. Reuse it:

| Tier | Price | Format | Job |
|------|-------|--------|-----|
| Lead magnet | Free | PDF / template / mini-guide | Trade for email |
| Newsletter | Free | Weekly email | Trust + frequency |
| Entry product | $99-$150 | Self-paced mini-course | First $ + segment |
| Flagship product | $250-$300 | Full OS course | Core revenue |
| Premium / cohort | $500-$1,000 | Deeper course | Higher-intent buyers |
| Recurring | $9-$25/mo | Templates / community | MRR + retention |
| Sponsorship | $2,500-$5K/issue | Newsletter ads | Pure margin |
| Capstone | $997-$3K | Comprehensive course | Once-a-year launch |

The ladder is one direction: free → paid → recurring → premium.
Never start at the top. Never skip a rung. Each rung qualifies
buyers for the next.

## WHEN TO HIRE (THE WELSH ANSWER: ALMOST NEVER)

The default answer is no. The exception list:
- A bookkeeper / accountant — pay for this immediately, always
- A part-time editor or VA — only after you're past $250K/yr
  AND you have a Content OS that runs without you AND you have
  a single repeatable task that's burning 5+ hours/week
- A part-time designer — only if you launch products quarterly
  and design is the bottleneck

Notice what's NOT on this list: a full-time anyone. A "marketing
manager." A "head of operations." A team of writers. Welsh's
position is that hiring usually masks a systems gap. Fix the
system, the role disappears.

This is the inverse of the agency game (which Ivan runs at DR).
Don't apply Welsh's hiring philosophy to a services agency. Apply
it to the personal-brand layer that feeds the agency.

## WHEN TO QUIT A PHASE (THE TRAPS)

- **Quit Phase 1** when you have 1K engaged, not when you "feel
  ready" — feel ready is a phase 1 emotional trap
- **Quit Phase 2** when product MRR is steady, not when you get
  bored of LinkedIn — bored is fine, broke is not
- **Quit Phase 3** when you have two real platforms AND two real
  products, not when you "want to try YouTube" — adding a 3rd
  platform mid-Phase 3 kills more solo businesses than anything
  else
- **Quit Phase 4** when your moat is built, not when you "feel
  established" — feeling and being are different

The exit from each phase is observable, not emotional. If the
numbers say you're done, you're done. If the feelings say you're
done but the numbers don't, you're not.
