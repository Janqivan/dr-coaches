## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 Two layers before you design: strategy, then hierarchy (Denney)
- When she uses it: before writing any static copy (2026-06).
- Steps: Layer 1 — 1. One specific goal (offer, education, reaching the problem-aware); doing too much for too many people is the most common static mistake. 2. One persona — not "small business owners" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] but which type, at what stage, with what objection; it sets vocabulary, proof and objections (TOF statics need a conscious choice; retargeting can span personas). 3. The format — it decides what copy survives, what the image must carry and which copy frameworks fit; first pick a production level (graphic-style, hi-fi production, or lo-fi creator content). 4. One awareness level — "the bridge" from persona and goal to the framework. Layer 2 — 5. Headline first, then product/key visual, then supporting elements. 6. 1-second test: if a stranger can't tell what you sell or say, kill it.
- Her example: an image ad with "way too much going on" [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] and no focal point — "It needs to go. We need to scrap it." [quote src: YT:5C5VhqW9HCc]
- Sources: [YT:5C5VhqW9HCc] (2026-06)

### 2.2 Psychology-based design / visual hierarchy (Cooper, Levinger)
- When they use it: every static — "Statics have hooks" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (Cooper).
- Steps: 1. Put the most important message where the eye lands first; steer the order with sizing, contrast and white space, plus placement, wording, fonts/colors, warps, highlights, arrows (Cooper). 2. Rule of thirds: three sections (or 2/3 + 1/3), one element each, so the eye bounces in the F/Z pattern (left→right, top→bottom for English; Mandarin differs) (Levinger). 3. One font, max two; weights add processing; "packaging has to be processed too" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (Levinger). 4. Don't let designers bold whatever the brand says converts (e.g., the CTA) and blur headline vs subhead — every choice affects conversion, "including click." (Levinger). 5. Fix placement when the ad is "zooming in on the wrong words" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (Denney). 6. Check: "Is this eye-catching enough? Can I skim read this?" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (Cottrell).
- Their examples: O Positive, "Nerve pain killer" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q], Rise (§4).
- Sources: [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04), [YT:2jVcLu3Earg] (2025-05), [YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (2025-06)

### 2.3 Cottrell's static checklists and production system (Cottrell)
- When he uses it: every static his agency (Fraggle) ships.
- Three pieces (2026-09): "catch people's eye with the image" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ], "give them more information with the headline" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ], "tell them what to do with the CTA" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ].
- Three needs (2025-06): 1. Skimmable in about a second (vs ~5 seconds of video commitment), so simple wins. 2. Handle objections — price: break it into a daily figure; trust: social proof. 3. Social proof in every static (review, star rating, Trustpilot icon). Good brands "don't miss any of those three things." [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI]
- Steps: research the customer deeply → start from a popular objection (it "can normally spiral a couple of ideas" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI]) → build in social proof → "Think before you design" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI] → "Test fast, test messy" [quote src: YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (2025-06); later he adds "a really sharp angle" [quote src: YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] found with AI and single-variable variations (2025-08).
- Rule: "the ads that actually held spend are the ones that had performance in mind" [quote src: YT:TouPE2n7mzo] — planned from Foreplay examples, competitor checks, Pinterest (2026-02).
- Sources: [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:2BrGawWj1aI] (2025-06), [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ] (2025-08), [YT:TouPE2n7mzo] (2026-02)

### 2.5 Native storytelling static (Cottrell)
- When he uses it: problem-solution products; brands that "don't really care too much about their brand guidelines and just want performance" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]; fast becoming an incredibly popular format across our clients (2026-09).
- Steps: 1. Write the copy first — a long caption telling a story that includes the product, in the customer's voice (shortcut: paste a long review — "it sounds like the customer because it's written by the customer" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]). 2. The above-the-fold first line is the headline. 3. Find a raw image as "a supporting piece for the copy" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY] (think Reddit post, or a boomer's Facebook tirade). 4. Choose it deliberately — "With advertisers, nothing is by mistake" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]; "The more bizarre, the more scroll-stopping the image is, the better" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]; sufferers must recognize it instantly. 5. Leave the product out ("It feels too much like an ad" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]) unless it is itself scroll-stopping (Perifit's Kegel device "looks kind of strange at first glance" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]). 6. Image cheat code: Google Images → Nano Banana reference → change it but keep "the same shocking factor" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ].
- His examples: The Black Stuff's eczema close-up under "A customer shared this with us the other day" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]; the psoriasis story (§4.2). (guest: Harry Delmege) native phone snap + long story copy "crushes", mainly when whitelisted.
- Trade-off: "fairly ugly looking" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY], draws "Oh god, I don't want to read all this" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY] comments, but "I think the performance often enough is worth it" [quote src: YT:5R2grcV0WzY]; they "spend like crazy" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]. It "might be a trend" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] that fades within months.
- Sources: [YT:5R2grcV0WzY] (2026-09), [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03)

### 2.6 IG-story static (Cottrell; Denney's creator lo-fi variant)
- When they use it: Cottrell — it "wins time and time and time again" [quote src: YT:TouPE2n7mzo] (2026-02); Denney — her "number one tip for partnership ads right now" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-08).
- Steps (Cottrell): 1. The target customer holds the product as if posting to their story (POV, a hand like the viewer's). 2. IG-story fonts, copy split into text boxes the Instagram way — match the placement's native styles. 3. Brand as an underlined @-tag (people try to tap it; lifts CTR). 4. Optional urgency in the same voice ("It's always selling out, so if you can get one now" [quote src: YT:KlhBwk1kmzI]). 5. Pair with whitelisting or partnership ads — "and boy, you are on to a winner" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]. 6. Variations: duplicate the frame, swap image, text and persona.
- Why it works: you stop on something unrecognized, consume it, then realize ("Oh, it's an ad" [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]) — "That's the type of effect you want." [quote src: YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ]
- Denney's variant: contracted creators also make a few lo-fi statics, which "develops a little bit of a funnel system" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]; "hot girl Instagram stories" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (mirror pictures, selfies, flat lays) — B tier.
- Sources: [YT:TouPE2n7mzo] (2026-02), [YT:KlhBwk1kmzI] (2026-04), [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ] (2026-09), [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-08)

### 2.7 Us-vs-them in four steps (Denney)
- When she uses it: often a brand-new business's first top performer; must-test for a US launch. 2026-08: B tier — "the OG classic" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8], often top eight but mostly mid-funnel, unlikely the unicorn.
- Steps: 1. Clear visual comparison and us-vs-them language; "us" on the LEFT so left-to-right readers see your brand first (testable). 2. 3–4 specific differentiating benefits that solve true pain points, prioritizing those with the most evidence or customer feedback. 3. One matching competitor weakness per benefit, mined from recurring complaints in competitor reviews — factual, never exaggerated. 4. Visual cues: emojis and color psychology (green = good, red = bad).
- Competitor logos: "jury's out" — not illegal, but "is it the right thing to do?" [quote src: YT:NsHk8o3jVvM]; safer against a generalized "them".
- Sources: [YT:NsHk8o3jVvM] (2025-06), [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] (2026-08)

### 2.8 Framework 1: build statics from proven big ideas (Denney; Cottrell; Levinger)
- When they use it: whenever an account already has winners.
- Steps: 1. List the big ideas inside top performers, including video audio, hooks and text overlays (Denney). 2. Make every headline in the static test a messaging point extracted from another top performer — her Framework 1. 3. Put each working message into a completely new static, not a tweak (Cottrell). 4. To rebuild a winner as a set (Levinger, AutoBrush: 1 video, 2 statics, 1 GIF + carousel): pull reusable visuals from the winning video ("the hippo"), then transcribe it to see what it actually communicated.
- Their examples: "5-minute makeup routine" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg], Everyday Dose (§4.1).

## 3. Static types, tiers and wording templates

### 3.1 Denney's static tier list (2026-08) — yardstick: reaching her accounts' top eight (Motion reports); scaling unicorn vs supporting cast [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8] · mentions 19
- **A**: grid-style statics.
- **B**: us vs. them; callout ads; educational infographics; headline ads (if the messaging is amazing); founder's letter; mood board ads (apparel — "fashion girlies" make them organically); creator lo-fi statics; "hot girl Instagram stories" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8].
- **C**: statistics ads — fine for big luxury brands with retail and awareness goals, unlikely top eight for DTC-only brands chasing Meta ROI.
- **D**: AI billboard ads ("so overdone. It's so cheesy" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]); breaking-news ads (a few sales, never business-transforming).
- **E**: text-only ads (AI copy "and it just comes out bland" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]; skip unless it's a sale); listicles ("has not done jack lately" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]). **E/F**: testimonial statics "for the way that most of you are making them" [quote src: YT:KmbEjy0UBT8].

- **Unranked**: ugly/handwriting ads — still perform, crush in sale periods.

### 3.2 Format catalog
- **Headliner / headline + benefits / benefit callout** — Denney: callouts are "tried, true and proven" (Ogilvy's recall tests); the headliner puts the "sexy messaging" front and centre, often with taboo angles; test the top headline as a core desire or a golden-nugget testimonial (2025-06, 2026-06, 2026-08). Cottrell: product-benefit statics are "an essential in every single ad account"; headliner rule "your image supports the headline", usually MOF–BOF (2025-11, 2026-02). Cooper: his #1 format — "The headline is the ad" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07). [YT:NsHk8o3jVvM, YT:5C5VhqW9HCc, YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:TouPE2n7mzo] · GEN · mentions 10
- **Grid / collage / collection** — Denney: A tier; low-hanging fruit with multiple SKUs or bundles ("you really really need to test"), always on deck for apparel and multi-SKU brands, especially in sales; storytelling often missing (2026-06, 2026-08). Cooper: multi-SKU collection ads "absolutely crush scooping up demand at the bottom of funnel". [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:5C5VhqW9HCc, YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] · DTC · mentions 6
- **Before/after and transformation** — Cottrell: a "legendary format" in health, wellness and skincare but "dodgy territory": Meta dislikes visual before/afters (easy to fake); take-downs and bans happen (2025-11). Denney: not illegal — "I can't believe people are still saying this in 2026." — though cosmetic procedures and weight loss face far more restriction; transformation is the biggest gap in most accounts for problem- and solution-aware people "just looking for proof" (2026-06). ## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree
**What changed**
- Polish → native: Cottrell pushed designed statics as a moat (2025-06), then named the trend "Less branded, more organic looking" (2025-11); IG-story statics became repeat winners (2026-02) and long-caption storytelling statics rose (2026-09), possibly a passing trend. [YT:2BrGawWj1aI, YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q, YT:5R2grcV0WzY]
- Variations → new angles: his 2025-08 system ended in single-variable variations; by 2026-02 brands said "we don't want static variations anymore. We just want loads more new static angles."; by 2026-05 look-alike statics risk being treated as one ad. [YT:HXox1I6QQvQ, YT:_BuCO3Jf-K0, YT:QoGFOGEqijw]
- Production: templates + ChatGPT image (2025-06) → one-shot remakes (Nano Banana Pro; Claude Code + Higgsfield, 2026-03/07) → Magic Layers variations (2026-06). [YT:2BrGawWj1aI, YT:wXe0B2XZTGI, YT:SJi5todbhJg]
- Formats that died (Denney 2026-08): Notes app/UX ads and press ads (F; she championed press ~6 years ago), AI billboards (D), listicles (E); text-only cooled from "a bit of a hot take" (2026-06) to E, but founder letters (which did "really, really well") stay B for sales. Cottrell saw iMessage and Apple Notes trends fade (2025-07). [YT:KmbEjy0UBT8, YT:5C5VhqW9HCc, YT:tVXjXJSEjeA]

**Where they disagree**
- Funnel role — Denney (2026-06) and Cooper (2025-01, 2026-07): statics reach cold audiences and scale when awareness level and headline are right; Cooper still runs TOF and BOF statics together. Cottrell: statics give "clarity and control" vs video's depth, so they "mop up" BOF sales (one quick message: why us, or an offer) after TOF/MOF video does the hard work — though they must still read cold (2025-07, 2025-11, 2026-10). Denney also limits some formats: us-vs-them mostly reaches mid-funnel. [YT:5C5VhqW9HCc, YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q, YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:FWWp6NcVo3Q]
- Testimonials and press — Cottrell ("Social proof is at the heart of any good static") and Cooper (testimonials = his #2 format, press hits on his list) vs Denney (testimonials E/F, press F) (2026-02, 2026-07, 2026-08). [YT:TouPE2n7mzo, YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q, YT:KmbEjy0UBT8]
- What stops the scroll — Cottrell: the image is the only scroll-stopper on a static (2026-09); Denney: the focal point is usually the headline (2026-06); Cooper: "the headline is the ad" (2026-07). [YT:1FpqxqbHxVQ, YT:5C5VhqW9HCc, YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q]
