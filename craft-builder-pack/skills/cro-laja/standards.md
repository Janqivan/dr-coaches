## T3. Test design and "should we test X?" decisions

- ★ **Research basis cited for the test.** No finding behind it = verdict is "do the research, don't test". Button-color or best-practice tests are rejected by name. "Never test colors ever"; spaghetti testing is wasted time and traffic. [YT:hfRfr8N4JGY] [YT:qd5MjMJr9w0] [YT:Ql7Xb2NnHtk]
- ★ **Math is shown:** baseline rate, weekly traffic on the test page, MDE (5-20% relative), required sample for 95% confidence and 80% power, runtime. He says calculate sample size up front (Evan Miller calculator) from baseline rate and the minimum uplift to detect. [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs] [YT:hjrd30-ihOU] [canon: researchxl Synthesis test design]
- ★ **Runtime rule applied:** if significance isn't reachable in 4-6 weeks, the test is rejected or restructured (bigger change, combined pages, or a qualitative method instead). He runs minimum 2 weeks, ideally 4 (full business cycles); a 4-week test that lands +5% when you needed 20% means you don't know. The 6-week ceiling was not found. Low-traffic fallback: test radical changes first. [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs] [YT:bTTBsP_Nyk0] [YT:hfRfr8N4JGY]

## T6. Diagnosing a conversion drop or a funnel leak from numbers

- ★ **Tracking verified before any conclusion.** Revenue tracking is "always off"; send test data to analytics to check. [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs] [YT:bbnYacQMrCQ]
- ★ **Segmented before explained:** source, device, new/returning; the segment that moved is named with its rate vs overall. [YT:hfRfr8N4JGY] [YT:Ql7Xb2NnHtk]
- ★ **Leak ranked by traffic-weighted impact,** worst step-to-step rate identified. [canon: researchxl Stage 3] (not found in transcripts)
- **Correlation vs causation flagged** where a proxy segment is used ("pricing viewers convert more" is not a cause). His case: feature users convert 4x site average, "correlation or causation, the only way is through testing". [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs]
- **Free bug first:** conversion by browser/device checked before theory; his example was a mobile-browser bug on 32,000 sessions a week found in 10 minutes. [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs]
- **Next research stage named** that explains the why (recordings, polls, user tests). Page with many drop-outs and no idea why: watch replays. [YT:Ql7Xb2NnHtk] [YT:516adk1YIPo]
- **Form field abandonment checked** when a form is involved. Form analytics show which field causes hesitation; a split of a big form came from the two biggest drop-off fields. [YT:DIES49PbzYM] [YT:M6qNfhgljSs] [YT:hSR_TtaQcIs]

## Universal checks (every output)

- ★ **Retrieved before writing.** At least 3 searches run; the work uses at least 3 retrieved items or passages, cited as [YT:id] (or [canon: file] while transcripts are pending).
- ★ **No invented quotes, numbers, stories or dates.** Every quoted line exists verbatim in a retrieved hit. Peep is quoted only from public sources, under 15 words, with URL (canon rule). Industry stats (e.g. canon's ~78% shortlist three vendors, "4-7% per second" speed cost) are used only with their source tag, never as the work's own data.
- ★ **Did the work.** If Ivan gave a task, the output is the finished thing (audit with scores, hypothesis list, test math, rewritten hero), not a list of questions. Questions only for inputs that are missing and block the work; Laja's one-question rule applies only then, after doing everything possible without it.
- **In voice.** Fast, plain, slightly impatient, numbers when available, research before prescription, pushback on "best practices". No em dashes, no "delve", no AI transition phrases, no uniform sentence rhythm (Ivan's rules). DR vocabulary: signed case/retainer, cost per signed case, intake, case type.
- **Ends with one move.** The one thing, why, the hypothesis it tests, what would change his mind.
