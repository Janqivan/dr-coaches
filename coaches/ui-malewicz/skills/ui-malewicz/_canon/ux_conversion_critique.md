## PART 3. UX, conversion and how he critiques a design

### Rules (ranked by how often they say it)
- **Precision is the job; content must look real** — Tiny flaws (off-centre arrowheads, unequal radii, totals that do not add up) are felt subconsciously and make viewers doubt you. No Lorem Ipsum; vary names, counts, prices; descriptions 3–4 lines. [YT:ThmHV38Ecqk, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2025-10, 2025-11, 2026-08) · mentions 12
- **Copy first; strip the page to a text-only "type frame"** — If the text alone does not sell, fix it before any visual; "too boring or too minimal" text-only usually means no work on fonts, hierarchy and message. Write it yourself; AI-written copy is unlikely to convert better. [YT:nq07DZq3Og8, YT:lpqTva43Bxo, YT:HnTv9J9RYrw] (2026-04, 2026-07, 2025-10) · mentions 11
- **Effects cost conversion; speed is design** — No parallax, pre-loaders, scroll hijacking or header animation; slow loading loses at least half the visitors; Linear dropped animation after testing. [YT:Toonu-cTE60, YT:tP5KHuevhSI, YT:aGTqFp7GAKE] (2024-10, 2025-10, 2024-10) · mentions 9
- **The hero does about 90% of the convincing, doubt-clearing the last 10%** — Two steps only: a problem and solution the visitor identifies with, then clear doubts; no fixed blueprint. [YT:nWbBZPjev_0, YT:9JnNtm4imqA, YT:nq07DZq3Og8] (2025-10, 2025-10, 2026-04) · mentions 9
- **The hero visual may only reinforce the copy** — Unconnected text and image make the brain work harder and leave; no random blobs, space imagery, stock look or complex dashboard (a complex image "smacks you in the face with complexity"). [YT:lpqTva43Bxo, YT:wrj4GDcLqJY, YT:HnTv9J9RYrw] (2026-07, 2025-10, 2025-10) · mentions 9
- **Kill the main objection early; build doubt-clearing from real client questions** — Subhead answers the category doubt; ask new clients what convinced them and what was missing; recurring questions become the FAQ, popular ones a visual section. [YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:nq07DZq3Og8] (2026-09, 2026-03, 2026-04) · mentions 9
- **Social proof must be checkable** — Back every logo with a project or blurb; real verifiable people, not stock photos; rough vertical smartphone video testimonials mixed with text. [YT:DEs40FcLWUI, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:Toonu-cTE60] (2025-10, 2026-03, 2024-10) · mentions 8
- **Gaze principle** — Eye contact for the first hook, then photos looking or pointing at the key text and CTA; a subject looking away from the CTA pulls viewers away. [YT:nWbBZPjev_0, YT:_PJLefMZwko, YT:1sC_KA3kTTo] (2025-10, 2026-03, 2025-10) · mentions 8
- **Annotate why a design works** — Precise annotations (why stroke matches font weight) earn respect even from people who dislike the palette; show V1 mistakes, fixes, V2; explained decisions let you charge more. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4] (2026-08, 2025-11, 2026-09) · mentions 8
- **Write about "you, your problems, your money"; cut lines that "can fit any product"** — Headline starts with the visitor; filler (empower, streamline, seamlessly) makes copy more nonsensical. [YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4, YT:HnTv9J9RYrw, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY] (2026-09, 2025-10, 2026-03) · mentions 8
- **Dashboards: design for intent, not exploration** — Users never wanted data; most dashboards say "you figure it out"; simple ones need five or six numbers; an orders tab people really use is a searchable, sortable, filterable table. [YT:6_t66Ef0Llk, YT:w2mNSOWiZbU] (2025-12, 2026-09) · mentions 8
- **Visible slop kills trust** — Slop is visibly low effort at every level; visitors judge in about 3 seconds and a page like every other reads "another one of those"; visible AI use makes clients walk. [YT:HQpQhmJi22s, YT:GzDBkU0irpA, YT:MpuMy5yipHM] (2026-06, 2026-09, 2026-04) · mentions 7
- **Test continuously, even the obvious** — A site is a loop of solving a problem and clearing doubts; 97% never change; he dropped his hidden-top-CTA rule after a test; with zero users you cannot optimise. [YT:eOuOWSmTH2o, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:HQpQhmJi22s] (2025-10, 2026-03, 2026-06) · mentions 7
- **Pricing is not three identical cards** — Low-friction entry plan, the plan you want chosen, a high-friction plan that makes it obvious; action in the title; separate pricing page when the price is high. [YT:9JnNtm4imqA, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY] (2025-10, 2026-03) · mentions 7
- **CTA label states the benefit or exact action** — Never "Get started", "Next", "Okay", "get in touch", "get a quote", bare "Search"; use "Find my voting location", "Book a design sprint", "Send me a new password". [YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4, YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:ThmHV38Ecqk] (2026-09, 2026-03, 2025-10) · mentions 6
- **One main button; a second only at higher stakes** — Then button one leads to case studies, button two to a demo; "pay us" or "buy it now" as a second button is friction; merge two nav buttons. [YT:nWbBZPjev_0, YT:nq07DZq3Og8, YT:1sC_KA3kTTo] (2025-10, 2026-04, 2025-10) · mentions 6
- **Tap targets** — 44x44 minimum via an invisible 0%-opacity rectangle grouped with the label; close X gets 48x48 and air; forgot-password link gets its own 44 pt area below the field; 16 between tappable items (8 is too small). [YT:ThmHV38Ecqk, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:FBDVzr0peO4] (2025-10, 2025-11, 2025-10) · mentions 6
- **Mobile is its own design** — Desktop first, then a dedicated mobile layout; bigger text, rewritten copy, softer CTA (`Show how it works` instead of `Try for free`), "tap" not "click"; email-only signup, max two fields; desktop copy on mobile can cost up to 50% of conversions. [YT:q8yUIbRiNRc, YT:cS1J77CLqmA] (2025-10, 2025-11) · mentions 5
- **Interrogate every element; delete pretty things with no job** — Shoe carousel posing as function, avatar plus profile tab, credit-card widgets (a card is not an account), a logo on every screen. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:ThmHV38Ecqk] (2026-08, 2025-10) · mentions 5
- **Controls and navigation follow hands and hierarchy** — Main button in thumb reach; Log out at the very bottom; selected state by shape, not colour alone; top-level screens carry the tab bar and no back arrow, deeper ones a back arrow; each destination once. [YT:ThmHV38Ecqk, YT:FBDVzr0peO4, YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2025-10, 2025-10, 2026-08) · mentions 9
- **UI audit: measure before judging taste** — Margins and spacing, typefaces and sizes, icon set, colours; spacing, colour and contrast outweigh the typeface (he built a good-looking app in Comic Sans). [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:ThmHV38Ecqk] (2026-08, 2025-10) · mentions 5
- **Contrast: essential text and controls pass AA, decoration need not** — List the measured ratio per pair; subtle borders and chip backgrounds may fail when structure is clear. [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:ThmHV38Ecqk, YT:FBDVzr0peO4] (2025-11, 2025-10, 2025-10) · mentions 5
- **Portfolio: show lots of work and the reasoning** — Recruiters scan and run an autopilot filter; grid of many screens, annotated charts, real photos (people react to photos, not text), V1-to-V2 fixes. [YT:_PJLefMZwko, YT:N-34Q9mhwvs, YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-03, 2025-11, 2026-08) · mentions 5
- **Resume words: concrete, not corporate** — Cut "aspiring/junior/beginner", "highly skilled", "team player"; write years and facts, e.g. `UX/UI designer, 8 years of agency experience, designing both web and mobile projects`; mirror the job ad; full LinkedIn URL, not an @handle; plain email, no contact form. [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08) · mentions 7
- **UX exists to make the business money** — Users must be somewhat satisfied and never lost; most design starts from existing patterns so research steps get skipped; value first, then ease of use and looks about 50/50. [YT:c3vssjpwSX4, YT:FBDVzr0peO4] (2024-10, 2025-10) · mentions 5
- **Sell strategy and iteration, not pixels** — The first redesign is never the last; deliver strategy plus a report on design decisions and next steps. [YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:nq07DZq3Og8, YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI] (2026-03, 2026-04, 2025-10) · mentions 4
- **Proof numbers must look and be real** — Non-round ("17,308", since 17,000 looks fake); never invent metrics, recruiters smell it. [YT:lpqTva43Bxo, YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI] (2026-07, 2026-08, 2025-10) · mentions 4

### Methods (named or ordered)
#### Nine-step conversion redesign (his "complete blueprint" for a landing page)
- When they use it: redesigning his own product page or a client landing page; captions garble the numbering, this is the spoken order.
- Steps: 1. Know who it is for, main and sub benefits, the moat (if it can be vibe-coded in an hour it is not viable). 2. Pressure-test: audience big enough and able to pay, benefit worthwhile, sub-benefits compelling, hard to replicate. 3. Strip all visuals into a text-only type frame. 4. Highlight power words and redundant words in headline, CTAs, everywhere. 5. Name the primary and secondary emotion and try switching it (fear to desire or power) before visuals. 6. Split into two sections only: problem vs your solution, and clearing doubts. 7. Top section simple and copy-focused, visuals only reinforce the text. 8. In the doubts section add small illustrative animations showing what each section does. 9. Write a post-launch strategy document.
- Their example: his analysis tool's hero (changes in Fix library): headline = action plus desired result, proof number (4,500 sites tracked monthly), `$13` price visible. Early doubts to clear: is the analysis worth anything, how much work is it, is it quality or vibe-coded.
- Sources: [YT:nq07DZq3Og8] (2026-04)

#### Reading a hero: leading-lines trace plus blur test
- When they use it: judging any hero, his or other people's.
- Steps: 1. Where the eye starts (big text pulls it). 2. Does each following element (line, button row) get shorter so the gaze steps diagonally to the button. 3. Do image shapes, edges and perspective lines point at or away from the button. 4. Is the button the only colourful or bright thing. 5. Blur the image and see what the brain still processes.
- Their example: in the blurred laptop hero the laptop vanishes and the colourful glare remains; complex dashboard and AI-image heroes lead away from the button.
- Sources: [YT:wrj4GDcLqJY] (2025-10)

#### UI red-flag audit, then final review
- When they use it: a weak or deliberately terrible UI (UI is "either good or it's bad", unlike client-dependent portfolios).
- Steps: 1. Measure first (four different spacings on one edge, 2–3 on the other). 2. Count typefaces (7 in his example), sizes, background colours, greys, reds, icon packs. 3. Fix: one typeface (Inter), three sizes (12/16/20), reset stray tracking (2.5), then spacing. 4. Final pass: text at least 12 pt (10 only for tab labels), no thin fonts, contrast checker (WebAIM or plugin), red squares again on every screen.
- Five most common problems: too big or small, fonts all over, bad alignment or padding, pastel or clashing colours, mixed icon sets.
- Sources: [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E, YT:ThmHV38Ecqk] (2026-08, 2025-10)

#### Flow audit (pretty app, broken flow)
- When they use it: a good-looking ordering app (banana cocoa crumble example).
- Steps, faults in order: cart badge shows an item before anything is added; ambiguous "X"; "Edit" with no object; generic "Next"/"Okay"; favourites with no add-to-cart; totals that do not add up (12.90 becomes 25.80, 1600 vs 800 points); points balance never shown; confirmation lacks price; unusable order number; three-dot map menu with no way back; no way to add a second bowl.
- Their rewrite: add-more, remove and ingredient picking in the main flow; red text "Remove" not a bin icon; same prices across steps; points balance shown; return before confirming; confirmation with driver map and message option.
- Sources: [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E] (2026-08)

#### Landing-page blueprint teardown, block by block
- When they use it: picking apart a generic wireframe "blueprint".
- Steps: header (logo, scroll menu, repeated CTA); pre-headline ("why does it matter?"); headline (doesn't say what message); description ("redundant"); CTA labelled just "CTA"; key visual (never says how it connects to the text); social proof under the hero (should it be here?); how it works (does every website need it, right here?); main benefits ("what if there is no competition?"); case studies ("why not at the top?"); pricing; FAQ; final CTA; footer. [quote src: YT:9JnNtm4imqA]
- Their example: step 2 content comes from research, ask users their main "what ifs".
- Sources: [YT:9JnNtm4imqA] (2025-10)

#### Critique plus 20-minute redesign
- When they use it: public site teardowns (the two US campaign sites).
- Steps: 1. Ask the site's main goal (Harris: get people to vote; Trump: present his ideas for the future) and judge content against it. 2. Evaluate with expertise plus heuristic tools, numbering issues on screenshots. 3. Redesign each site in a 20-minute limit, keeping original vibe, style and message. The verdict is given up front in the video.
- Their example: both bad on design, Harris slightly better, Trump stronger direct messaging; he cannot tell deliberate flaws from a cheap quick job.
- Sources: [YT:1sC_KA3kTTo] (2025-10)

#### Hierarchy strips
- When they use it: proving one important card was designed with intent.
- Steps: 1. Split the card into groups with coloured overlays: image (emotional hook), main subgroup (name, meta), categories (follow or not), description (most never read), actions (main, alternative). 2. Colours progress yellow-orange, orange, muted red, joined by same-colour same-length lines. 3. Show white space as deliberate: pink gradient with a dashed fading stroke labelled "2x". 4. Add text on look order (face, name, match, act).
- Sources: [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs] (2025-11)

#### Mini usability study (portfolio "prototype validation")
- When they use it: proving you can test, not getting real insight.
- Steps: 1. Test with 3 users (friends or family fine), one main-flow task each (product details from category; checkout with three products). 2. Script: introduce product and goal, give task, ask a couple of questions, watch and listen. 3. Report successes and where users did not know what to tap; guess the cause without being too direct. 4. Show the fix with a callout on what changed and why; admit no second test.
- Their example: two of three users never found product details, assuming "add to bag" was the only button; the card's button became "more info", one clear action per card.
- Sources: [YT:N-34Q9mhwvs] (2025-11)

### Fix library (problem → fix → why)
- **Problem:** hero CTA says "Get started" beside a dashboard picture → **Fix:** benefit CTA (`Start for free`), simple visual away from the CTA → **Why:** vague CTA implies effort; dashboards pull the eye to lines and columns. [YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4, YT:lpqTva43Bxo] (2026-09, 2026-07)

### Coaching and critique moves
- First question on every client call: how they found the agency, what convinced them most on the website, what was missing. [YT:QaJyYl0SvyY, YT:DEs40FcLWUI]
- Ask what each element is for: "Why are these shoes here?", "Do we need that little icon?", "do you need the date at all?" [YT:Kqg3vN1ms2E]
- Question every headline word: how exactly does it "empower" the business, what are the "scalable solutions", "seamlessly to millions" (millions of what?). [YT:HnTv9J9RYrw]
- Hero pass: what the eye reads first; who the words are about (you vs us); which objection the subhead removes; proof number or concrete benefit; risk lowered; CTA label carries a benefit; graphic competing with copy. [YT:Ni34Y2uSIF4]
- Simulate eye tracking: face and eyes first, next bright icon, then the next, finally the CTA (too many steps). [YT:Hzr3Q1CJPbI]
- Zoom out after placing each element; re-read the finished design as a user (a delivered row should show a dash, not an ETA). [YT:w2mNSOWiZbU]
- Use exaggerated bad examples on purpose, anonymised, and repair one issue at a time; verdict up front. [YT:wrj4GDcLqJY, YT:HnTv9J9RYrw, YT:1sC_KA3kTTo]
- Tone: name a couple of easy fixes, leave homework, close encouragingly; he spares beginners' imperfect UIs. [YT:ySbVqIMV5Ng, YT:HnTv9J9RYrw] (2021-10, 2025-10)
