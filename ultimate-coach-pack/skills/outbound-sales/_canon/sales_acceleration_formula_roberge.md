## The operator's worldview

Roberge's thesis: **sales is a function, not an art.** Every input that drives revenue can be measured, tracked, and improved. Therefore the operator's job is to engineer the machine, not to "find great salespeople" by feel.

His four sub-formulas in the book map to four operator decisions:
1. **The Sales Hiring Formula** — A-Method, scorecard-driven.
2. **The Sales Training Formula** — predictable ramp curves.
3. **The Sales Management Formula** — metric-driven coaching, leading vs lagging.
4. **The Demand Generation Formula** — channel allocation tied to the buyer journey.

Underneath all four: **the comp plan is the only honest signal of what the company actually wants.**

## Framework 1 — The A-Method for Sales Hiring (*Sales Acceleration Formula*, Ch on hiring)

Four steps. Applied specifically to sales seats.

1. **Scorecard.** Define the seat before sourcing. Three components: outcomes (lagging metrics, e.g. retainers signed, retention at 6 months), competencies (leading behaviors, e.g. dials/day, coachability, curiosity), cultural fit. Written down. Used in every interview.
2. **Source.** Multiple channels. Inbound applicants, employee referrals, recruiters, direct outreach. Volume matters because A-player conversion is low — you can't hire from a thin top of funnel.
3. **Select.** Interview against the scorecard, not against gut feel. Roberge's HubSpot data: **coachability and curiosity outpredict prior sales experience.** Score each candidate on each scorecard line. Compare.
4. **Sell.** Once you've identified an A-player, recognize that they have options. The hire is a two-way sale.

**Roberge's hard rule:** no scorecard = no hire. Hiring by "vibes" is the single largest cause of failed sales seats. He calls this **voodoo hiring** — it's malpractice in a function that's supposed to be measurable.

## Framework 2 — The Predictable Sales Hiring Formula (*SAF*, Ch on ideal rep profile)

Roberge ran a regression on his HubSpot hires asking: which interview traits predicted top-quartile performance at 12 months? His top correlated traits:
- **Coachability.** Does the candidate take feedback in the interview and apply it in the next round?
- **Curiosity.** Do they ask questions, or just answer them?
- **Prior success.** Past pattern of overcoming hard things. Doesn't have to be sales.
- **Intelligence.** Not raw IQ — the ability to learn a complex sale fast.
- **Work ethic.** Verifiable through references, not the interview.

**Notably weaker correlators:** years of sales experience, brand-name employers, charisma in the interview.

## Framework 3 — Predictable Sales Training (*SAF*, Ch on ramp)

Roberge built a defined, repeatable training curriculum at HubSpot so that new reps ramped on a predictable curve. The core ideas:
- **A defined "ideal sales process"** the rep is trained to execute, not improvise.
- **Specific competency checkpoints** before the rep takes live calls.
- **Certification gates** — rep passes a roleplay against a manager before going live.
- **Measurable ramp curve** — average new-hire bookings by month, with variance bands.

The operator-level value: when a rep underperforms, you can ask whether the *system* failed or the *rep* failed. Without a curriculum and a ramp curve, you can't tell.

## Framework 4 — Metric-Driven Coaching (*SAF*, Ch on management + HBR articles)

Roberge's lens on sales management: **coach to the leading indicators, report on the lagging indicators.**

- **Lagging indicators:** retainers signed, MRR closed, revenue per rep. Outcomes. You can't coach these directly — by the time you see them, the behavior that caused them is months old.
- **Leading indicators:** dials/day, connects/day, discoveries booked/week, proposals sent/week, follow-ups within 24 hours. Behaviors. Coachable today.

**Roberge's diagnostic:**
- Rep is hitting leading metrics, missing lagging → **skill gap.** Coach them on the conversation itself (script, objection handling, discovery quality).
- Rep is missing leading metrics → **behavior/effort gap.** Manage them. Coaching the script won't fix a rep who isn't dialing.

**The implication:** every rep has one or two leading indicators they're working on per quarter. The manager's 1:1 is built around those specific numbers, not generic "how are you doing."

## Framework 5 — Sales Comp Design (*SAF* + HBR "The Right Way to Use Compensation")

Roberge's central comp principle: **pay for the behavior you want, not the behavior you wish for.**

Comp plans are honest signals. If your reps are doing X, your comp plan rewards X — even if the leadership team says they want Y. The reverse-engineer test:
1. Write the comp plan.
2. List the behaviors a rational rep would optimize for under that plan.
3. Compare to the behaviors leadership says it wants.
4. If they don't match, redesign.

**Design heuristics Roberge surfaces:**
- **Acquisition-only comp = churn problem.** Reps over-promise to close, then the customer leaves at month 6 and there's no penalty to the rep.
- **Retention comp tier** — pay a smaller second commission when the customer renews at 6 or 12 months. Aligns rep behavior with LTV, not just acquisition.
- **Customer-success-tied bonus** — for a tighter loop, tie a portion of comp to NPS or retention at the cohort level.
- **Avoid plans the rep can't compute in their head.** If a rep can't model their own paycheck on a napkin, the comp plan won't drive behavior — it will just create confusion.

## Framework 6 — Inbound vs Outbound Channel Allocation (*SAF*, Ch on demand-gen + Stage 2 Capital writing)

Roberge's frame: channel allocation is a function of the **buyer journey**, not of operator preference.

Two diagnostic questions:
- **Are buyers aware they have the problem?** → Inbound works. Content/SEO/community/citations let aware buyers self-select.
- **Are buyers unaware, in denial, or not actively searching?** → Outbound is required. You have to interrupt them.

**Roberge's critique of "outbound everything":** many companies build outbound machines because outbound feels predictable, but their inbound infrastructure (content, SEO, brand, referrals) is so thin that they're fighting the buyer journey instead of riding it. The right move is usually both — outbound to interrupt unaware buyers, inbound to capture aware buyers cheaply.

**Stage 2 Capital lens:** for B2B services where the problem is well-understood (e.g. "we need better marketing"), inbound should carry the brand-aware portion of the funnel. Outbound covers the rest.

## Framework 7 — Buyer Journey Process Map (*SAF* + HBS lectures)

Map sales activities to where the buyer is, not where the rep wishes the buyer was.
- **Awareness stage** — buyer recognizes a problem. Sales activity: educate. Not sell.
- **Consideration stage** — buyer evaluates options. Sales activity: differentiate. Not pitch features.
- **Decision stage** — buyer compares finalists. Sales activity: close. Not discover.

The named bug Roberge catches: reps pitching features at an awareness-stage buyer. The buyer doesn't yet know they have the problem the feature solves. The result: the rep sounds like noise, the prospect ghosts.
