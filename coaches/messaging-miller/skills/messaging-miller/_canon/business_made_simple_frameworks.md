## Section 1 — The 10 Character Traits of a Value-Driven Professional

### Framework 1 — The 10 Character Traits (master frame, Days 1-10)

**What it is:** Miller's operating definition of a high-value team member. Not a personality quiz — a behavioral checklist. The traits compound: a person who scores high on these makes more money for themselves, their employer, and their customers.

**The ten traits, named:**

1. **See yourself as an economic product on the open market.** Your job is to deliver ROI to the person paying you. Act like the value you create can be measured and compared to alternatives — because it can.

2. **See yourself as a hero, not a victim.** Stop blaming circumstances. Take agency. The hero seat is the only seat from which improvement is possible.

3. **Know how to de-escalate drama.** Drama is unproductive emotion. The value-driven pro defuses it; the low-value pro feeds it.

4. **Know the right way to engage conflict.** Expect conflict. Control emotion. Affirm the person. Be willing to be wrong. Avoiding conflict is a value drain — handling conflict well is a multiplier.

5. **Be willing to be respected and trusted more than liked.** Set clear expectations, hold people accountable, reward strong performance. Trusted leaders create stronger teams than popular ones.

6. **Have a bias toward action.** Information without execution is overhead. Move the work forward, even when the path isn't perfectly clear.

7. **Don't choose to be confused.** Confusion is often a defense against doing hard work. Recognize the dodge. Push through.

8. **Be unafraid to fail.** Adopt a growth mindset. Treat failure as data, not identity.

9. **Know how to be invited to the dance.** Earn opportunity through demonstrated value, not by asking. Be the person others want on the project.

10. **Be willing to give and receive feedback.** Make feedback frequent, specific, and unemotional. Both directions.

**Mechanism:** these aren't ten skills — they're one operating posture, repeated ten ways. The value-driven pro acts as if they are responsible for the outcome, and the team is repaid in compounding trust.

**Failure mode:** treating the list as inspirational. The 10 traits are a hiring filter, a firing checklist, and a self-review document — not a poster.

### Framework 2 — The Self-as-Product mindset (Day 1, the foundation)

**What it is:** The mental shift that anchors all 10 traits. The professional sees themselves as a product on a market. Salary is the price. Output is the value delivered. The employer is the customer.

**The three implications:**
1. **You must produce more value than you cost.** If your salary is $X, you must demonstrably create at least 2-3x $X in returns for the employer. Otherwise you are an overhead drag, not an asset.
2. **You must keep upgrading the product.** Skills age. The pro who stops learning becomes commoditized.
3. **You must be marketable.** Reputation, network, demonstrable wins. These are the product packaging.

**Mechanism:** the moment a professional sees themselves as a product, they stop waiting for the employer to invest in them and start investing in themselves.

**Where DR uses it:** every hiring-scorecard conversation. Every "should we keep this hire?" call. Every quarterly review. Is this person a value-driven product, or a coasting hire?

## Section 2 — Leadership Made Simple

### Framework 3 — Mission, Three Priorities, Key Players

**What it is:** Miller's compressed operating doc for any team or company. Three artifacts — that's it. Print on one page. Reread weekly.

**The three artifacts:**

1. **The Mission Statement** — a definite, measurable outcome by a specific date. Not "be the best." A precise win condition. Example: "Sign 12 enterprise immigration firms to Case Flow by end of Q4."

2. **The Three Priorities** — the only three projects that matter this quarter. More than three means none. Three is the brain's working-memory limit.

3. **The Key Players** — named individuals (internal + external) whose work or relationships determine whether the mission is hit. Names, not roles.

**Mechanism:** most teams fail not from lack of effort but from lack of clarity. Mission + three priorities + key players is the smallest possible artifact that produces strategic alignment.

**Failure mode:** vague mission statements ("change the world"), long priority lists, and abstract role descriptions instead of named people.

### Framework 4 — The Five Characteristics of a Leader (the leadership stack)

**What it is:** Miller's leadership archetype. A composite figure. Not a personality type — a set of behaviors a leader must perform regardless of personality.

**The five characteristics:**
1. **Optimist** — leaders see and articulate a believable better future.
2. **Communicator** — leaders make the mission clear and repeat it until people are sick of hearing it (and then keep going).
3. **Decision-maker** — leaders decide. Indecision is more expensive than a wrong decision in most cases.
4. **Multiplier of value** — leaders make their team more valuable, not just busier.
5. **Refusal to be a victim** — leaders own outcomes. They take responsibility before assigning it.

**Mechanism:** leadership is a job done, not a title held. A person without authority can still lead if they perform the five characteristics. A person with authority who skips them does not lead.

**Failure mode:** founders who hide behind "I'm not a people person." The five characteristics are required behaviors, not optional preferences.

## Section 3 — Productivity Made Simple

### Framework 5 — The Five Productivity Practices

**What it is:** Miller's productivity stack. Not a tool stack — a daily-practice stack. Five behaviors that compound when stacked.

**The five practices:**
1. **Have a morning routine that boots up the brain.** Mind, body, mission. 15-30 minutes. Same time daily.
2. **Plan the day in advance.** Before the inbox opens. Three top priorities. Calendar-blocked.
3. **Block time for deep work.** Default the calendar to focused blocks, not to meetings.
4. **End the day with shutdown.** Review what was done. Close the loop. Don't drag work into evening as background guilt.
5. **Stop multitasking.** Switching cost is the silent productivity killer.

**Mechanism:** professional output is a function of focused hours, not total hours. The five practices protect focused hours from the dozen daily threats that erode them.

**Failure mode:** treating productivity as a tool problem (new app, new system). Tools don't fix the missing practice.

### Framework 6 — The Eisenhower Box, applied (Day 25-ish)

**What it is:** Miller's restatement of the classic Eisenhower urgent/important matrix, tuned for small-business operators.

**The four quadrants and the operator rule for each:**

| | Important | Not Important |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgent** | Do now (firefighting, must-not-fail) | Delegate / batch (interruptions) |
| **Not Urgent** | SCHEDULE (the real work — strategy, content, training) | Delete |

**Miller's specific add:** most small-business operators live in the Urgent-Important quadrant and treat it as a virtue. It's not. The leverage sits in Not-Urgent-Important. Time blocked, weekly, non-negotiable.

**Failure mode:** founders who reward themselves for firefighting because it feels productive. Firefighting is symptom-management; strategic work is cause-management.

## Section 4 — Strategy / Mission

### Framework 7 — The Five-Part Strategic Framework

**What it is:** Miller's strategic-clarity tool. A simplified, operator-grade strategy doc. Five questions that, answered specifically, produce a usable strategic plan.

**The five questions:**
1. **What is the mission?** (the specific, measurable, time-bound outcome)
2. **Who are the key players?** (named — team, partners, customers)
3. **What are the three economic priorities?** (the three levers that move revenue / profit)
4. **What are the daily themes?** (the cultural drumbeats — what we repeat until it's reflex)
5. **What are the income streams?** (every revenue source mapped, ranked by margin)

**Mechanism:** strategy is a set of choices, not a wish list. The five questions force the operator to make choices and write them down.

**Failure mode:** answering each question with paragraphs of inspiration instead of specifics. Strategy fits on one page. If it doesn't fit, it's not strategy yet.

**Note:** This is the conceptual seed for the 6-Part Flight Plan that gets fully developed in How to Grow Your Small Business (2023). The Flight Plan is the mature version of this framework.

## Section 5 — Sales Made Simple

### Framework 8 — The Sales-as-Story Frame

**What it is:** Miller's translation of the StoryBrand framework into a sales-call structure. The sales call IS a story being lived in real time, with the customer as hero and the salesperson as guide.

**The seven beats of a sales conversation:**
1. **Identify the customer's desire.** Open with what they want.
2. **Name the problem at three levels** (external, internal, philosophical).
3. **Position as a Guide** (empathy first, authority second).
4. **Present the plan** (3 steps).
5. **Call to action** (direct + transitional).
6. **Describe success** (vivid future-state).
7. **Name the stakes** (cost of not acting).

**Mechanism:** a sales conversation that follows the SB7 arc converts dramatically better than a pitch-then-objection-handling structure. The customer feels seen before they feel sold.

**Failure mode:** salespeople who pitch the product before identifying the problem. Customer disengages because the pitch isn't about them.

---

### Framework 9 — The "Drama in Their Story" sales question

**What it is:** A specific diagnostic question Miller teaches salespeople to ask on every discovery call. The single most important question in the conversation.

**The question, paraphrased structure:** "Right now, what's the biggest source of frustration in [the area your product addresses]?"

**Mechanism:** the prospect's own description of their pain — in their own words — becomes the ammunition for the rest of the call. The salesperson then reflects it back through the SB7 lens. The customer feels deeply understood; the salesperson has the exact internal-problem language to use later in the close.

**Failure mode:** asking generic discovery questions ("what are your goals this year?") that produce generic answers. The drama-in-their-story question forces specificity.

## Section 6 — Execution Made Simple

### Framework 10 — The Execution Operating System

**What it is:** Miller's execution layer — the meeting and rhythm system that ensures the mission, priorities, and players actually produce outcomes.

**The four-meeting rhythm:**
1. **Daily 5-minute stand-up** — each team member names: yesterday's win, today's priority, current blocker.
2. **Weekly 1-hour team meeting** — review priorities, surface blockers, decide next steps.
3. **Quarterly half-day strategy review** — confirm three priorities for the next quarter.
4. **Annual full-day vision review** — recommit the mission, key players, income streams.

**The scorecard:** a single page tracking the three economic priorities, updated weekly.

**Mechanism:** strategy without rhythm decays. The rhythm forces re-contact with priorities often enough that drift is caught before it becomes failure.

**Failure mode:** meeting schedules that fill the week with meetings but don't drive priorities. Most operators have too many meetings and too little rhythm. Meeting count ≠ accountability.

## How to use this file inside the /storybrand skill

1. When the operator's problem is messaging or marketing — use the StoryBrand canon and the Marketing Made Simple canon. This file is not the lever.
2. When the operator's problem is "the messaging is clear, but execution is a mess" — this file is the lever. Run the 10 Character Traits scan on the team. Run the Five-Part Strategic Framework on the business. Diagnose where the gap is.
3. When hiring or firing — apply the 10 Character Traits as a behavioral filter. They're a hiring scorecard in disguise.
4. When a founder is overwhelmed — Framework 5 (productivity practices) + Framework 6 (Eisenhower box) is usually the intervention.
5. NEVER substitute this file for the book or the platform. For Miller's full voice and the 60 daily lessons, point at Business Made Simple University and the Business Made Simple Podcast.
