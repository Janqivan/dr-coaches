## 1. Core positions

Ranked by mentions × specificity. 2026 positions come first where they describe what works today.

1. **Go all the way to one end of the spectrum: super ugly/organic OR super obviously an ad with great DR copy. "The middle ground is where ads go to die."** (GEN, ~7 mentions) His X post: ads that win in 2026 will be either "super ugly and organic" or super obvious it's an ad, but with such good DR/copywriting that people don't care. Most brands sit in between. Their ads are meant to be ugly but aren't fully ugly, so the soundtrack, setup, look or location tells the viewer "I've paid someone to read a script" and they stop watching. "If you are going to go ugly, lean all the way in." This doesn't mean every ad should be ugly with no captions. A DR script with AI voiceover and B-roll doesn't need to hide that it's an ad. The copy has to be great and the visuals exaggerated enough to sell. A yapper or ugly ad, though, must look exactly like the organic feed. He applies the same logic to statics with clarity vs curiosity ("DON'T get stuck in the middle"). His agency is both "super AI forward" and "super ugly", and both ends work. — [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

2. **Ugly ads are mimicry of the organic feed, not bad production.** (GEN, ~8 mentions) The definition: "can you mimic the experiences that they are already consuming on their organic feed." The common mistake is putting out traditional UGC and calling it an ugly ad. It's also not a race to the worst lighting. Creators who deliberately ruin the lighting are overcompensating, and some audiences expect studio or at-home setups. His realism test: "would you set the camera up on a tripod in your kitchen", or lean your phone on the counter? Luxury can be ugly too. The background carries it: business class shot on an iPhone 6 still appeals because frame and setting are "hard to fake". Ugly ads have worked in every niche his team has tried (fitness, beauty, supplements), and food and beverage is easiest. — [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04), [YT:Z82ehPFBY54] (2024-03)

3. **Scripted UGC is dying. Unscripted "yappers" with personality are the 2026 version of UGC.** (GEN, ~9 mentions) Scripted UGC ("Here's a script. Go and read this script... and then we'll edit it") "does not work on paid social anymore". You can still get the odd winner, but unscripted personality is "way more likely to work." In his framing, UGC has evolved from "this is the product that I like and this is why I like it" into unscripted, authentic storytelling. Yappers are "pretty much ugly ads or one form of it." His team's 2026 process: give a structured hook, then talking points (pain points/benefits to hit), plus other yapper ads as inspiration, and let creators go. Some videos are still scripted internally. — [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01)

4. **More AI flooding the feed means more value in real humans, and AI animations are also here to stay.** (GEN, ~9 mentions) "The more AI stuff we see on our feed, the more I become bullish" on real human creative: real podcast ads, ugly ads, group ugly ads, founder ads, BTS/warehouse, authentic reactions, authority figures. He cites the head of Instagram: when polish is cheap, a raw aesthetic becomes a credibility signal. In parallel, yappers and AI animations are the two formats AdCrate is most bullish on, and his consulting advice lately is "more Yappa's, more AI animations". AI animations work regardless of industry, age group, e-com or local business. — [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:ITM4faRVzF8] (2025-05), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:A29szOW7YDE] (2026-05)

5. **Messaging beats format. Use proven formats and spend the effort on the message.** (GEN, ~6 mentions) "Messaging is more important than format." Don't reinvent the wheel or be "too cute or too clever". Copying a format seen on Twitter sometimes works, which is why it's tempting: the format is easy to see and the message is harder to find. Formats marketers love online (iMessage, AirDrop, Google search, Apple Notes) he has "yet to see" perform across accounts with lots of spend. You want "where can I buy this", not wow great ad. The common static mistake is design-first: copying a Foreplay layout and shoehorning a message in. The best DTC brands' libraries are mostly B-roll mashup + DR script + AI VO, repeated with different messages. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09), [YT:MAwkpQWEQS8] (2024-10), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06)

6. **Statics can run ad accounts, including top of funnel.** (DTC/GEN, ~6 mentions) Contrary to popular belief, statics "can and do perform at the top of the funnel when they're done correctly" and can scale accounts on their own. They're under-discussed because most content is about creators, briefs and edits. Run TOF and BOF statics together. In his analysis of 12 months of AdCrate static winners, ~90% were comparison, headline + callouts, or offer-based. — [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

7. **"The headline is the ad", and the customers write it.** (DTC, ~10 mentions) Many winners came from taking customers' exact words (reviews, Reddit, ad comments, post-purchase surveys, support tickets) and saying them back as headlines. Testimonial statics use the exact words without paraphrasing, even if slightly ungrammatical, because it's "a customer talking to a customer rather than a business talking to a customer." The TOF headline must be relevant to someone who has never heard of you, which he calls one of the hardest things to do. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09)

8. **"Assume no one knows or cares." Clear over clever.** (DTC/GEN, ~6 mentions) Warm-only headlines ("Love these.", "I feel like a new person", "Always in my car", "My feet have never been happier") say nothing to a stranger. "Bye bloating" is instantly relevant whether or not you know the brand. Pick where the selling happens. Either it happens on the ad (a clear TOF static) or after the click (a curiosity native ad plus advertorial). Don't stall in between. — [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03)

9. **Statics have hooks: design for the scan, not the read.** (GEN, ~6 mentions) Nobody reads top to bottom. People scan in milliseconds for whatever appeals, so put the most interesting element where the eye lands first, using sizing, contrast and white space. Highlight key phrases so text-heavy statics skim in half a second. The image telegraphs before the headline is read (citing Sarah Levinger). If the imagery doesn't capture attention, "you can test as a thousand different messages" and still fail. — [YT:Z82ehPFBY54] (2024-03), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

10. **The first frame decides staged or real before anyone speaks.** (GEN, ~6 mentions) "Those decisions are made before you open your mouth as a creator." Tiny setup changes shift perception: mic in a stand vs out, couch, DJ mic, headphones. A hook swap that changes the person (a slim guy at a 25% hook rate vs a big guy) telegraphs to a different segment and makes it "a completely different ad". Pick creators with a distinctive feature (hair, beard, clothing) for an extra split second. — [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01), [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09)

11. **Model/creator selection is underrated.** (GEN, ~7 mentions) The best brief is worthless if the person can't execute or can't "yap" (tell a story you believe for 2 minutes). Study TikTok Shop creators and comedians. People who actually relate to the pain speak more authentically than paid actors. If a creator works, use them "again and again" until they stop. Brands drop proven creators too early. Reshooting a winner with a new creator has a "very high success rate" and can reach a new audience pocket. Match direction to the creator: the bottom 80-90% need more scripting, and the top ~10% should be let loose. (Guest citation: Archit from Reach, "model selection is the most underrated part of the creation of an ad".) — [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:fls5dwYVY1M] (2025-08), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06)

12. **Hyper-relevance: one persona per ad.** (GEN/LEADGEN, ~5 mentions) The old advice was broad relevance. Now he makes each ad specifically relevant to one persona, explicitly in copy or purely visually. Don't worry about alienating other personas, because they get their own ads. Edit style signals the audience too: fast TikTok style skews younger, while slower edits with fewer clips and bigger fonts skew older ("blend in to stand out" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA]). People buy from people like them, but aspiration matters too. A male-focused brand's delivery flipped to women in Q4. — [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:9ikhnQi2ghE] (2023-11), [YT:fawsUmNeCkg] (2024-09), [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01)

13. **Congruency is king.** (DTC, ~4 mentions) The biggest static issue is image and message speaking to different personas, e.g. copy for bigger guys over an in-shape guy, or "made for all body types" on a regular guy. The everyday version: one image tested with four messages whose value props the image doesn't portray. Reorder the callout bar so the value prop the headline targets leads. — [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11)

14. **Copy is portable across formats, and that extends lifespan.** (GEN, ~4 mentions) AI-animation copy ≈ native-ad primary text ≈ yapper script. When one works, hand it to a creator as a yapper, make a native ad, or turn a yapper into an AI animation. This has worked "many times for us". He also wraps top performers in Twitter/IG story/Reddit/Quora frames, stitches 3 winners into 4-5 minute ads, and pulls frames from videos into statics. — [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08), [YT:Z82ehPFBY54] (2024-03), [YT:fls5dwYVY1M] (2025-08), [YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09)

15. **Outside DTC: wall-of-text videos and persona text statics.** (LEADGEN) Wall-of-text videos "would crush" for newsletters, info products and SaaS and still do, because they convey the message well. Hormozi's text-only persona statics are his example of avatar targeting outside e-com. — [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:Z82ehPFBY54] (2024-03)

## 2. Frameworks and methods

### 2.1 The format spectrum (2026)
- **When:** choosing what kind of ad to make.
- **Ends:** (1) super ugly/organic: yappers, faceless hands-on demos, group ugly ads, founder ads, real podcasts. (2) Obviously an ad with killer DR copy: B-roll + DR script + AI VO, AI animations, wall-of-text.
- **Rule:** know which end you're on and lean all the way in. A yapper must look exactly like the organic feed, while a B-roll/VO ad needs great copy and visuals exaggerated enough to sell. Delivery matters for yappers. For B-roll + AI VO, "it doesn't matter how you say that" [quote src: YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk].
- Sources: [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:Ugd7a2sPgZk] (2026-03), [YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (2026-06)

### 2.2 His 2026 video styles (numbered as he presents them)
- **Style 1, yapper ads** (aka ramblers/organic): unscripted, authentic storytelling.
- **Style 2, avatar-specific creative:** one persona per ad (Hollow Socks tradespeople, Hormozi statics).
- **Style 3, AI animations:** mimic organic content, tell stories well, and are super cheap and quick vs real creators. Works across industries and ages.
- **Style 5, challenge ads:** harder to pull off, but "when you get them right, they will absolutely crush" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]. They open a loop like negative framing does.
- **Honorable mention, post-it note ads:** evergreen, static or video, work at TOF and especially BOF offers during sales.
- Source: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06)

### 2.3 Making an ugly/yapper ad (his recipe, assembled from the masterclass)
1. **Study the feed for 5 minutes:** how organic content is filmed and edited, whether it has soundtracks or SFX, the lighting. Compare with ad libraries and you'll see the gap.
2. **Place the creator where people would really talk about the product.** Ask "where would it make sense for people to talk about my product?" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] Makeup → GRWM in the bathroom. Pillowcase → in bed under covers. Protein bar → car after the gym. Basketball → gym. Skincare → phone propped on the mirror before bed. Proven yapper settings: car, walking down the road, bedroom, kitchen counter. Anything that "would never happen in real life" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] reads as staged.
3. **Handheld or propped phone, never a tripod mid-shot.** Low/eye-level angle, product held near the lens, natural light, no brand graphics in the first frame.
4. **Pick a creator who can yap**, ideally one who has felt the pain. Give a structured hook plus talking points plus reference yapper ads, then let them riff.
5. **Minimal edit:** no captions, or one hook text overlay that stays up. No soundtrack, SFX or emojis. Transitions are "cool for a mashup" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] only.
6. **Variants:** faceless hands-on (put the product down, have someone touch, pick up and use it while narrating, "just riff, go crazy, and have fun with it" [quote src: YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w]), group ugly ads (taste test with friends, mom-and-daughter in car), founder ads (founder phoning top customers).
- Sources: [YT:OzkRU3Wx_1w] (2026-03), [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06), [YT:Z82ehPFBY54] (2024-03)

### 2.4 Trigger-event AI animation structure (2026-08)
1. Open on the specific trigger event (mother-in-law comment at Thanksgiving).
2. Agitate with repeat incidents (Easter, Mother's Day).
3. Discovery of the product ("A friend recommended me this" [quote src: YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ]).
4. The mechanism: why it differs from everything she tried.
5. Payoff (the mother-in-law now says she looks great).
Once you have trigger events these are easy to produce. He suggests handing the structure to Claude Code. Long versions (10 minutes) are working with older audiences. — [YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-08)

### 2.5 Static: 12 formats in priority order (2026-07)
1. **Headline + benefits** (product with benefits around it; "The headline is the ad" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q])
2. **Testimonials** (exact customer words)
3. **Offer-based** (one for every offer; can dominate the account)
4. **Persona-based**
5. **Collection ads** (multiple SKUs; "absolutely crush" BOF)
6. **Comparison** (before/after, us vs them, timeline; show the states visually)
7. **Publication** (editorial/news look; "a very good ugly ad" [quote src: YT:7htwuw0zO0Y])
8. **Press hits** (article screenshot)
9. **Native ads** (lo-fi photo, selling in primary text or advertorial)
10. **Apology ads** ("a bit gimmicky" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] but often top of audited accounts)
11. **Post-it notes** ("massive fan")
12. **Meme ads** ("massive fan")
These cover ~95-98% of what his team makes and what he generates in Claude Code. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.6 AdCrate's 9 rules for evergreen performance statics (2024-11)
(1) Assume no one knows or cares. (2) Keep it simple stupid. (3) Congruency is king. (4) Your customers are your copywriters. (5) Make people feel something. (6) Statics have hooks too. (7) Reverse-engineer winning copy. (8) Clear and concise. (9) Repurpose winning messages. — [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11)

### 2.7 Static guide, 2026 recap checklist
Three parts: strategy and systems, messaging principles, design principles. Checklist: scale volume with Claude Code + Higgsfield. Test with intention (white space in the account and in VOC). Use proven formats. Speak to specific personas. "Assume no one knows or cares." Reverse-engineer winning copy. Customers are your copywriters. Pick clarity or curiosity. Consider visual hierarchy. Make the invisible visible. Atypical text ads. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.8 Psychology-based design / visual hierarchy
- **Levers:** sizing, contrast, white space.
- **Controllable elements:** placement and size of images and headlines, headline wording, fonts and colors, bold/italic/underline, warps/distortions, highlights, arrows.
- **Order he annotates (O Positiv MENO):** Hook → Supporting statement (qualifies) → Product → Social proof. Social proof first would read as "I don't care about this. I don't know what this brand is." [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q]
- **Color:** red reserved for the danger/pain word ("KILLER" on a red block).
- Sources: [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.9 Clarity vs curiosity spectrum
Decide where the selling happens. Clarity: a classic TOF static that says what it is, who it's for and why it matters (This is a marriage saver.). Curiosity: a native ad (crying woman in a car, no text) with selling in the primary text and mostly on the advertorial. The middle is "no man's land". He still stands by "clear over clever" as the default. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07), [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11)

### 2.10 Comparison static variants
Us vs them (competitors), before vs after, me now vs future me (six months on daily dose), traditional X or big brand Y. Only valid when customers actually tried and failed at that solution. — [YT:SmNpVtfIRHw] (2024-11)

### 2.11 "Make the invisible visible" [quote src: YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] and intentionally unrealistic visuals
Render the pain or value prop visibly: fire or red glow on the painful body part, thorns around feet for neuropathy, a sweat patch. ImageGen's best use is intentionally unrealistic visuals of the value prop. Ask ChatGPT for similar unrealistic visual ideas. — [YT:ITM4faRVzF8] (2025-05), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.12 Atypical text ads
Write the message somewhere unusual that is instantly recognizable as relevant to the prospect and doesn't look like an ad: a foot sole, a stomach, a cardboard sign, condensation on a bathroom mirror (anti-dandruff), a car window (car covers), shaving cream on skin (razor). Body text grabs attention most. — [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.13 Midjourney/AI static template (2024) → Claude + Higgsfield playbook (2026)
- **2024:** large MJ image showing a benefit or pain point, plus a simple testimonial/headline, plus a small product image/bar with CTA at the bottom. Swap visuals and copy endlessly, about 10 minutes per ad. Specify "iPhone quality shot" [quote src: YT:7htwuw0zO0Y]/cinematic/photorealistic explicitly. Food renders well. Digital products get more MJ freedom.
- **2025:** give ChatGPT a similar reference ad and swap in the product (jeans + color swatch). Less design uncertainty means more reliable outputs. He prefers this to 3-page prompts.
- **2026:** a "playbook of 40 different types of static ads" [quote src: YT:ibzfKRRSHhU] from his swipe file. Each has a brand-agnostic template prompt, and all 40 were one-shotted in Claude + Higgsfield. Check claims: a "7600" figure was likely invented by the model.
- Sources: [YT:7htwuw0zO0Y] (2024-05), [YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09), [YT:ITM4faRVzF8] (2025-05), [YT:ibzfKRRSHhU] (2026-03)

### 2.14 Reverse-engineering winning statics (2026)
Use the Facebook Ad Library sorted by impressions as a spend proxy. Follow competitors, "influencer brands" and non-competitors sharing your audience. Ask Claude Code to find their top statics, pick ones worth recreating, and draft copy from your own VOC. Works with the Ad Library API/Meta CLI if you don't use Parker. The senior-dog static was one-shotted this way with the Higgsfield MCP. — [YT:XQwJRuN8p-Q] (2026-07)

### 2.15 Review-to-headline mining
- Prompt Claude/GPT to find first-person testimonials in reviews suitable for a Facebook ad headline. Parameters: short, customer language, relevant to someone who "does not know or care about the brand" [quote src: YT:c_6XsMe9eXE].
- Give a good example with the reason and a bad example with the reason (see §3).
- Then ask for angle-specific sets (five headlines on the big-guys angle).
- Reddit works too: the first result for "how to get rid of dandruff" [quote src: YT:Be9YLcOory4] yields "itchy and irritated" [quote src: YT:3wLk3D99JyA].
- Sources: [YT:Be9YLcOory4] (2024-09), [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03)

### 2.16 Hook angle set (nine angles, 2023)
Benefit, pain point, question, humor, aspirational, direct call-out, testimonial/personal experience, social proof, how to do X without Y. Apply each to value props and pain points. Start with the hook before the concept, because it is how you "pierce someone's attention". — [YT:9ikhnQi2ghE] (2023-11)

### 2.17 Script-building rules (video)
- **Hook first:** a funny/crazy hook, then straight into value props, still "giving people a reason to click through" [quote src: YT:9ikhnQi2ghE].
- **Don't cram:** he covered 4 of 5 value props and 2 of 4 pain points.
- **Visual brainstorm:** "if money or production were no factor, how crazy can we get" [quote src: YT:9ikhnQi2ghE] to portray value props (pulling things out of jeans, stretching in situations, cheap vs perfect before/after, exercising).
- **Organic-to-ad:** find organic hooks and concepts that performed (e.g. a 4M-view TikTok), keep the first ~15s, and swap the solution for the product. Multiple top performers came from this.
- **AI direction:** tell the model to focus on the problem. Don't let a comparison spend the whole script trashing the competitor.
- Sources: [YT:9ikhnQi2ghE] (2023-11), [YT:3wLk3D99JyA] (2024-03), [YT:c_6XsMe9eXE] (2025-03), [YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2026-02)

### 2.18 Customer-content flow
A post-purchase flow 7 days after purchase: "do you want to make $50 or like $100" (or a discount) for recording a video, paid if it runs. "So many winners": imperfect light, no optimized hook, loose and uncut, but authentic. Founder version at $10-20K/month spend: phone out, talk for 5-10 minutes about why you made the product. — [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01)

### 2.19 Gamification without a big budget
- Challenge with a celebrity and a discount mechanic (Carpe).
- Low-lift challenge ("see if I can get this Amazon knife sharper than a razor blade" [quote src: YT:GdYGUirdZYQ]).
- Ranking all the flavors.
- Ask how to show the value prop visually as a competition or game.
- Source: [YT:GdYGUirdZYQ] (2026-06)

### 2.20 Authority/publication pages (2025)
Run ads through a topical Instagram page (e.g. "Popcorn Lovers"). The MVP is just setting up the page and running ads through it. Several are working. — [YT:fls5dwYVY1M] (2025-08)
