## Framework 1 — Business Hierarchy of Needs (Fix This Next, 2020)

**What it is:** A five-level diagnostic stack, modeled loosely on Maslow. Each level has health questions. You fix the LOWEST unmet level. Not the loudest problem — the lowest unmet need.

**The five levels (bottom up):**
1. **Sales** — Is cash actually coming in the door? Lead flow, conversion, demand.
2. **Profit** — Is cash being retained? Margin, allocation, debt service, owner pay.
3. **Order** — Does the business run without the owner being the daily hero?
4. **Impact** — Are clients getting transformation, not transaction? Outcomes, alignment.
5. **Legacy** — Does the business outlast and outgrow the owner?

**The hard rule Mike enforces:** owners almost always try to fix the level ABOVE their actual floor. They want to talk Impact when they have a Sales problem. They want to scale Order when their Profit is broken. The skill is diagnosing the floor honestly and resisting the urge to skip it.

**Operator use:** at each level, score 5 binary questions on the level's health. The first level with 2+ "no" answers is the floor. Fix one thing at that floor. Re-diagnose in 90 days.

## Framework 2 — Profit First 5-Account System (Profit First, 2017 revised)

**The inversion that makes the system work:**
- Standard formula: *Sales – Expenses = Profit* (whatever's left)
- Mike's formula: *Sales – Profit = Expenses* (profit comes first, expenses fit what's left)

**Why it works:** Parkinson's Law of money. Expenses expand to consume whatever cash is available in the operating account. The fix isn't more discipline — it's a smaller "plate." Allocate cash AWAY from operating before the owner can spend it.

**The five accounts (small plates, fixed cadence):**
1. **Income** — every dollar of revenue lands here. No spending from this account ever. It's a holding tank.
2. **Profit** — quarterly distribution. 50% to the owner as a bonus, 50% reserve. This account FORCES profitability into existence.
3. **Owner's Comp** — the owner's actual salary. Paid like clockwork, not "whatever's left."
4. **Tax** — set aside on every allocation, untouchable, ready when the bill comes. Kills the April panic.
5. **Operating Expenses** — what actually runs the business. Always last in the queue. Whatever's left after the other four are funded.

**The allocation rhythm:**
- Twice a month — the 10th and the 25th.
- Sweep Income into the four downstream accounts at fixed percentages.
- Percentages are set by CURRENT capacity, not aspiration. If the only honest profit allocation is 1%, start at 1%. Build the muscle.
- Increase percentages quarterly as the business adapts.

**Target Allocation Percentages (TAPs):** Mike publishes ranges by revenue tier in the book. They are STARTING benchmarks, not gospel. For a media-buying agency (DR), the gross-vs-net adaptation in the SKILL.md overrides the published TAPs — run percentages on NET revenue, after pass-through ad spend has been routed to a Trust account.

**Failure modes Mike warns about:**
- Keeping all money in one account and "tracking" via spreadsheet. Doesn't work. Humans don't have that discipline.
- Allocating quarterly instead of bi-monthly. Too long. Cash gets eaten.
- Setting aspirational percentages and missing them. Demoralizes. Start small. Win small. Grow.

## Framework 3 — Clockwork 4D Mix + Queen Bee Role (Clockwork, 2022 revised)

**The 4D Mix — what every role's time actually consists of:**
- **Doing** — execution. Tasks. The work.
- **Deciding** — judgment calls. Approvals. Direction.
- **Delegating** — assigning work AND outcomes to others.
- **Designing** — building the system, the offer, the strategy.

**The diagnostic rule:** time-track a week. Categorize every block into one of the four Ds. Most stuck owners are 70-90% in Doing. Healthy founders trend toward Designing-dominant (80%+), with Deciding and Delegating filling most of the rest, and Doing close to zero.

**The Queen Bee Role (QBR):**
Inside the 4D Mix sits a deeper insight. Every business has ONE function that is disproportionately responsible for the business's success. The Queen Bee Role. If the QBR runs excellently, the rest of the business is easier. If it stutters, everything else collapses.

**How to find the QBR:**
1. Ask: "What activity, if it ran at world-class level, would make every other function easier and more effective?"
2. Identify who currently performs it. (Usually the owner — that's the trap.)
3. Audit what % of that person's week is actually on QBR vs admin/firefighting.
4. **Protect the QBR ruthlessly.** Redistribute every non-QBR activity off that person.

**The four-week vacation test:**
The terminal test of Clockwork: can the owner disappear for four weeks with no contact and the business still runs, sells, delivers, and grows? If not, the system is owner-dependent. That's a design defect, not a workload issue. The fix is system design, not more effort.

**Failure modes:**
- "I'll delegate later, once things calm down." Things don't calm down. Delegate now.
- Designating a QBR the owner WISHES were the QBR vs the one that actually is.
- Treating delegation as task-handoff. Real delegation transfers the OUTCOME, not just the activity.

## Framework 4 — The Pumpkin Plan (The Pumpkin Plan, 2012)

**The parable:** prize-winning pumpkin farmers don't try to grow ten medium pumpkins. They plant many seeds, identify the single most promising vine, KILL every other vine on that plant, and pour every resource into the one. The result is a colossal pumpkin — bigger than ten medium ones combined.

**The business translation:**
Most service businesses serve too many client types badly. The fix isn't more marketing — it's surgical pruning.

**The protocol (5 steps):**
1. **List every client.** Revenue, gross profit, joy (subjective), fit (does their problem match the ICP).
2. **Identify the top.** High revenue, high joy, low support drag. The colossal-pumpkin candidates.
3. **Find the pattern.** What do top clients have in common — industry, size, stage, decision-maker, problem framing?
4. **Prune the bottom.** Fire the worst-fit, lowest-margin, highest-drama clients. Politely. Quickly. With a written script.
5. **Replicate the top pattern.** Re-tool offer, marketing, hiring, and delivery around the top-client profile. Refuse new business that doesn't match.

**Why owners resist:**
The bad-fit revenue feels like safety. It's not. It's the trap. Bad-fit clients consume disproportionate support capacity and block the team from serving good-fit clients well. Firing them is a revenue increase in disguise.

**The firing script template (Mike's tone):**
> "We've taken a hard look at where we can deliver the most value. The work you need is better served by [specific alternative]. We'll wind down by [date], hand off cleanly, and refer you to [name]. We've appreciated working together."

No apology. No discount-to-stay. No vague "we're going in a different direction." Specific, clean, fast.

## Framework 5 — DAD Marketing (Get Different, 2021)

**The three jobs of any marketing, in order:**
1. **Differentiate** — be UNMISTAKEABLY different from the competitive set. Not better. Different. Mike's metaphor: a green tractor in a field of red tractors. The green one gets remembered. The red one is invisible.
2. **Attract** — pull the specific avatar you want. Marketing that "speaks to everyone" speaks to no one. Pick the one person. Make them feel seen. Repel the rest.
3. **Direct** — tell them the next action in one sentence. No clever fade-out. No "learn more." A specific verb, a specific time-bound action.

**The DAD test (any piece of marketing must pass all three):**
- **D test:** would a stranger describe this differently than every competitor's? If no, it's beige.
- **A test:** does it speak to ONE specific person, not a category? If no, it's a brochure.
- **D test (Direct):** is the next step embarrassingly obvious? If no, it's decoration.

**Why most agency / professional services marketing fails:**
- Differentiation is borrowed from competitor templates ("results-driven, data-led, full-service" — every agency website).
- Attraction is targeted at "growing businesses" — meaningless. The ICP needs a name, a role, a problem.
- Direction is buried in a footer "contact us" form. No verb. No urgency.

**Mike's heuristic:** if your marketing would still be 90% recognizable as yours after you swap your logo for a competitor's, you don't have marketing. You have wallpaper.

## How these frameworks interact (the system, not the silos)

Mike's books look standalone but operate as a layered diagnostic:

1. **Fix This Next** tells you which problem to open.
2. If the floor is **Sales** → open Get Different + Pumpkin Plan (demand + targeting).
3. If the floor is **Profit** → open Profit First (cash discipline).
4. If the floor is **Order** → open Clockwork (owner extraction).
5. If the floor is **Impact** → open Pumpkin Plan again (fit) + Clockwork (consistency).

The Services Operating System emerges when Profit First (cash) + Clockwork (owner) + Pumpkin Plan (clients) + Get Different (demand) all run simultaneously, with Fix This Next as the steering wheel.
