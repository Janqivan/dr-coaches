## 2. Frameworks and methods
### Creator-scale process and the diagnosis (Denney)
- When: before any roadmap; the strategy leader's job, redone ~quarterly. The process runs personas → diagnosis (the "real meat") → creator formats that scale.
- Steps: 1. Research ("onion peeling"; first stone: awareness levels of all live ads). 2. Personas both ways (customers vs the ads' intended audience). 3. Awareness and sophistication of current creatives (she has a prompt). 4. Creative-diversity audit: no persona spread, fewer than 2–3 unique angles per persona, no awareness range, low creator diversity (lifestyles, genders, ages, jobs). 5. Write the 1–2 sentence diagnosis.
- Their example: Bed Threads: all winners solution-aware or below → "this business needs more top-of-funnel content" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo].
- Sources: [YT:4wM-i1cxtAM, YT:-E9sjb2msjo, YT:yRgPbqywUJ8] (2026-09, 2026-06, 2026-07) · mentions 12
### Research SOP, persona prompt and recon report (Denney)
- When: onboarding any brand; a "living and breathing document" [quote src: YT:yRgPbqywUJ8], manual first.
- Steps: 1. Manual reputation analysis retracing the customer's vetting: Google page-one red flags; founder story ("the big why" [quote src: YT:yRgPbqywUJ8]), best sellers, merchandising, offers; per bestseller promise, transformation, proof, features, benefits, bundles; the big friction (distrust of efficacy, not worth it, cost); brand social; press; YouTube comments. 2. Review sheet: auto-export all reviews, add standout Reddit/Amazon lines, rank products by count and rating. 3. AI review analysis (persona prompt in section 3); ask Amazon's AI for an Amazon persona split. 4. Competitors doc. 5. AI recon report ("read the room before you write the ad" [quote src: YT:Uh0TE6nZs5A]): executive POV, sentiment scorecard, biggest PR risk, reputation by source and product, personas with verbatim voice notes, JTBD/triggers/objections, ad idea teasers. No data of your own? Run it on competitors' 3-, 2-, 1-star reviews.
- Their example: Ridge: 47,000 reviews, 3,000 pulled by variant → personas in a Google Doc → slides.
- Sources: [YT:-E9sjb2msjo, YT:UGiN8aVy2l8, YT:Uh0TE6nZs5A] (2026-06, 2026-05, 2026-06) · mentions 12
### Frameworks 1 and 2: winners already in the account; viral content SOP (Denney)
- When: F1 at >$100K/month or $1M spent ("your next top performing creative already likely exists in your ad account" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg]); F2 (built by partner Miguel) for low spend or few learnings, now used in onboarding and weekly.
- Steps: F1: 1. Take the last year's top 20. 2. State each big idea (every ad has an "essence"). 3. Find recurrences (validation). 4. Move the idea into a more emotional format (creator content via partnership ads) and repeat it across formats and creators, since ads are seen seven times before purchase "in the best of cases" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg]. F2: 1. Search TikTok/Reels for what goes viral around your idea (e.g. "parents of picky eaters" [quote src: YT:p4yXXdnCgWg]), especially small accounts. 2. Log recurring phrases, visuals, settings, creator types.

### Creative testing roadmap and evidence ladder (Denney)
- When: monthly per client.
- Steps: 1. Creators you want ("even in the age of AI... we still need creators" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo]). 2. Icebox: every ungraded research idea in one central place (Sheet, Replit, any file), enforced by leaders. 3. Quarterly plan: holidays, calendar, launches, personas by season. 4. Monthly roadmap: per persona a 1–2 sentence idea + three messaging options. 5. Rank by evidence (high = pressure-tested formats, messaging, creators; low = competitor copies) and speed (statics, internal creator or founder content, iterations in weeks one–two), then likeliest to scale (creator/partnership ads); map big swings (new personas, production). "leave room for creativity and magic" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo]; promo requests get an MVP process.
- Their example: Seed's pooping angle = high evidence; It Just Works (from Joanna Wallace) held its hot-climate gym-goer persona until May/Q2.

### Persona process and trigger workflow (Cooper)
- When: any account; "I'm not saying this is the way" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU]. Docs run 20–30 pages with 3–5 tabs (primary, secondary, expansion), kept "living breathing".
- Steps: 1. List every persona, rank by VOC frequency, pick the core. 2. Per persona, a 10-step prompt as skeleton: Gary Halbert paragraph; five Fs (fears, frustrations, failures, fantasies, failed solutions); emotion and trigger moment; awareness and sophistication; buying journey; cognitive bias map; objection stack; competing solutions; VOC copy phrases; confidence check. 3. Creative consumption: have Parker/Claude "reverse engineer the search terms" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU], search TikTok/YouTube, note creator type, production level, ring light, location, background. 4. Score /10 on revenue, LTV, CAC, strategic priority, averaged. 5. Allocate creative, then audit the account for underserved personas. Triggers: ask in every post-purchase survey; one script per trigger.
- Their example: The Perfect Jean: ~40,000 reviews; big/plus-size guys = 15–18% of reviews; every persona or script needs evidence (e.g. 10 reviews).
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU, YT:vD8eLrjAxEM, YT:9O3-AdYpwyQ] (2026-06, 2026-02, 2026-08) · mentions 13
### Rut breakers: Post-it exercise, ideas library, back to the well (Cooper)
- When: no ideas, or a rut after a winner fades.
- Steps: Post-its (from an AdCrate event in LA with Hott): 1. Everyone writes the product/problem in four or five words as static headlines, 3–4 each. 2. ~60 Post-its → ~30 good headlines. 3. Build statics → hooks → scripts, plus research and "a lot of scrolling organic" [quote src: YT:_9f0rTKKIOY]. Ideas library: one async database where buyers, strategists, editors and the founder log ideas; reviewed weekly. Back to the well: comments, reviews, competitor reviews, organic, AnswerThePublic ("you could never run out of research" [quote src: YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g]).

### Layers, four levers and the idea matrix (Cottrell)
- When: building any ad unit or idea batch.
- Steps: Layers: angle/concept + message → persona → format. Levers: persona; messaging (a different reason to buy); hook (first 3 s: visual, audible, copy, bold claim, demo); format. Idea matrix: angle → idea → hook → format, in any order. Ad unit: fix persona + messaging, pick format from account history, else "throw a load of stuff at the wall and see what sticks" [quote src: YT:CPWoW09WCCo]. Hunt hidden sub-personas.
- Their example: Grun's: "why suffer downing green juices when you can have this one simple gummy" [quote src: YT:RExPkxyRB6o] → AG1 convert (subscribed for ages, hates the taste) → us-vs-them UGC or VSL. Soap for mucky people: builders (obvious) vs untouched dirt bikers → "suddenly you're scaling like crazy" [quote src: YT:nUTzR9ilTts].
- Sources: [YT:RExPkxyRB6o, YT:CPWoW09WCCo, YT:gP-68LmgAQ4] (2026-10, 2026-02, 2025-11) · mentions 5

### Fraggle's process, 10 mistakes, $1M vs $10M (Cottrell)
- Steps: audit → research (AI deep research, reviews, social, competitors) → messaging/angles → formats → find, cast, brief UGC creators → launch with forced spend → read data, iterate.
- Mistakes: not knowing why an ad wins; too many ads; no conversation-starting ads; AI slop; one format; no footage reuse; too many offers; not knowing personas; playing safe ("your competitors have more balls than you" [quote src: YT:PuQ2TvXrWg4]); creative that doesn't match the landing page.
- $1M vs $10M: invest before ROI is obvious; assets compound; play long; trust hires; $1M brands want proof at signing, $10M brands commit, then build proof.
- Sources: [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k, YT:-q2lqfNysUs, YT:PuQ2TvXrWg4] (2026-07, 2025-11, 2026-04) · DTC · mentions 6
### Competitor and ad-library analysis done right (Denney, Cooper, Cottrell, Hott)
- When: monthly, and as one evidence input, never the sole test reason.
- Steps: 1. Ask why they run it; some teams chase misleading metrics (low CPCs, high hook rate), but repetition is "often a good bet" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg] if you trust the team (Hott). 2. One slide per competitor per month: repeated messaging = their learning (yours if same audience, made your own), launches (Denney). 3. Tag competitors, inspiration and "affinity brands" (same audience, other product); query their launches this week, 30-day tests and top ads by impressions (Cooper). 4. Estimate spend where impressions are public (UK/EU: 23.8M reach ≈ 214k via CPM); filter active 30+ days, niche, media, country, growth "rising"; rank by ad rank top 10%, growth rate, duplicates; Q4 scaling flags gifting (Cottrell, Trend Track segments). 5. Discount offer skew: big brands' top-impression ads lean to offers (Dr. Squatch: four of top five) because they are "in every Walmart in America" [quote src: YT:KSm0pvevV9k] (Cooper).
- Their example: Pura d'or Face's top ad "I'm never buying Pura d'or Face again..." [quote src: YT:y7TFcfgwhP8] ran 239 days, #1 of 2,000: "have a think before you just copy." [quote src: YT:Mqj05tYuDI0]
- Sources: [YT:2jVcLu3Earg, YT:UGiN8aVy2l8, YT:vD8eLrjAxEM] (2025-05, 2026-05, 2026-02) · mentions 24
### Three realities and research cadence (Levinger)
- When: before any tool, ask "What decision will this research help me make?" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; no answer, close the browser.
- Steps: Customer reality as a "cake": generational insights (small teams skip it) → micro moments (Reddit/forums for frictions; 5-, 3-, 1-star reviews for emotional language; quiz data, CS tickets, surveys for the "why now") → emotional mapping (strongest emotion; private or social; acute or chronic). Brand reality: health (account, capacity, cost, time) and alignment (this month's priority, non-goals, success measure); monthly ask founder, C-suite, buyer, designer, videographer what single thing to optimize for, verbatim, hunting disagreement, plus a one-sentence 30/60/90-day goal; stage sets the goal (pre-PMF learning over volume, scaling efficiency, mature hold and expand). Presentation reality: format by psychology, not trends. Cadence: customer "big dump" first Monday monthly; brand research first Monday quarterly and monthly; presentation research Mondays, 30–60 min; all feed the brief.
- Sources: [YT:pV0tM1gSJiU, YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06) · mentions 24
### The 12-question system (Levinger)
- When: weekly; covers ~80% of what you need, because humans skip phases under pressure.
- Steps: Research: WHEN (the moment the pain gets real; "moments of newness" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]; best hook source), WHY (the emotion beneath; wrong, and ads "feel off"), WHO (identity only: what all avatars share). Ideation: WHICH problem (relevant, underserved, "right now in this particular period of history" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]), WHAT ad (delivery fit, never trend), WHERE (trigger, exploration, evaluation, purchase). Concept development sets format + platform. Production: WHO makes it (falls out of format + platform), HOW long (fixed parameters, e.g. UGC and b-roll back in exactly 4 days). Keep one source-of-truth doc, every cell filled before production, updated ~monthly; stuck teams burn 1–2 hours on unanswerable questions.

### TEAP: Trigger → Exploration → Evaluation → Purchase (Levinger)
- When: instead of TOFU/MOFU/BOFU, which is media-buying logic yielding "ads that reach the right person just at the wrong moment" [quote src: YT:po85BKDTG-Y].

Re-brief toward the missing stage. 4. Run all four all year, each with its own CAC; evaluation buyers cut CAC fast and fund TOF losses. 5. Tag ideas by stage to swap dying concepts fast. For 2026, "All of your te ads now need to be wrapped around story" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY].
