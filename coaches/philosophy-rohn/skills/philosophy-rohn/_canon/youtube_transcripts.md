## 1. GOAL-SETTING + FINANCIAL INDEPENDENCE
Video: "How to Change Your Life in 1 Year with Goal Setting" — Jim Rohn Real Seminars ® [YT:tSn5sEWEv_8], 4,586 words pulled.

The four-step list mechanics:
> "In setting goals, it's very simple. Number one, decide what you want... What kind of skills do I want? What kind of income do I want for the future?... Number two, write it down. Number three, keep the old lists... I look back now 10, 20 years ago at goals I set, lists I made, and I smile now... I've grown. I've changed. I've matured."

> "When you get something that's on one of these lists, check it off. Part of the fun of having a list is checking it off. And then if you can add some drama to checking it off, see that's what really helps. I set a goal to go to Spain many, many years ago... I waited until the wheels touched the runway and I checked it off."

Promise and price:
> "If the future gets clear, the price gets easier. Because you got to remember, for every promise, there's a price to pay... But here's what I've discovered. If the promise is clear and powerful, the price is easy to pay. The price is some classes. The price is a few books. The price is a few disciplines."

The millionaire goal ("what will it make of you"):
> "My teacher advised me when I first got started at age 25. He said, 'Jim, why don't you set a goal to become a millionaire?'... 'Here's why. For what it will make of you to achieve it.' I'm telling you that statement changed my life... By the time I was 31, I was a millionaire. By the time I was 33, I was broke... What was valuable was not the money. What was valuable was what I became to earn the money... It's not what you get that makes you valuable. It's what you become."

His own origin, in his words: "If I can do it, you can do it. Farm boy from Idaho, raised in obscurity."

70/10/10/10 as spoken (child with a dollar):
> "Never spend more than 70 cents... 10 cents for charity... nothing teaches character better than generosity. And the best time to start is when the amounts are small... Next 10 cents I call active capital. Active capital means do something to make a profit... Two bicycles, one to ride and one to rent... Profits are better than wages... Wages make you a living. Profits make you a fortune... The third 10 cents is vitally important. I call it passive capital. Capital you let somebody else use... called compound interest... The borrower is servant to the lender... The lender is the power position."

> "Financial independence is the ability to live from the income of your own personal resources."

> "Poor people usually spend their money and invest what's left... Rich people invest their money and spend what's left... It really doesn't matter what the amount is. What's most important is not the amount. What's really important is the philosophy."

The mended wagon (enterprise parable):
> "Can't we teach our children how to take a dollar, search the neighborhood, find a broken wagon, pay a dollar for it, bring it home, clean it up, sand it until it's clean, paint it red till it shines, straighten out the wheels till they're true, take it back to the neighborhood, sell it for $5... Society now has a mended wagon. And that's what America's all about."

Attitude riders: "Wouldn't you love to pay your bills, reduce your liabilities, increase your assets?... Taxes is how we care and feed the goose that lays the golden eggs... Better a fat goose than no goose."

Past/future attitude: "Treat the past as a school... don't use the past as a club to beat yourself to death... There's two ways to face the future. One is with apprehension, and the other is with anticipation... you can face the future with anticipation if the future is clear, if the future is well designed."

Closing: "Why? ... Why not? ... Why not you? ... Why not now?"

## 2. THE 4 EMOTIONS — The Day That Turns Your Life Around
Video: "How to Fix Your Entire Life in 1 Day" — Jim Rohn Real Seminars ® [YT:32KUrAdSrgo], 1981 recording, 2,330 words pulled. Full excerpt set lives in four_emotions_of_change.md; key blocks:

> "Number one, disgust. Powerful emotion. Disgust says, 'I have had it.'... The day you can say, 'I've had it,' may not be the day it ends, but the day it begins. That's what I said when that little Girl Scout left my door when I'm 25. I give her the big lie. She leaves. I say, 'I don't want to live like this any more. I've had it with lying and being broke.'"

The beans story: "...his wife is at the grocery store looking at two cans of beans. One marked 37 cents, one marked 39 cents. And the guy sick inside knows his wife's going to buy the 37 cent can. And she doesn't even like the brand... To save two cents. The guy sick inside finally says, 'I've had it.'"

> "Decision-making is powerful and it's emotional... We sometimes call it inner civil war... 'If it's easy, do it easy. If it's hard, do it hard. Just get it done.'"

> "Desire... comes from inside, not outside. You don't send off for it... Welcome every human experience. You never know which one is going to turn it all on... The same wall that keeps out disappointment keeps out happiness. Let life touch you."

> "Resolve says, 'I will.'... The world has a strange way of stepping aside when somebody says, 'I'll do it or die.'... [the schoolgirl:] 'I think it means promising yourself you will never give up.' I said that's it. Webster, stand aside."

> "Action. Finally, you must do something about how you feel... Affirmation without discipline is the beginning of delusion... Make sure you always have a game plan to match your wishes. Otherwise, they will always be wishes."

## 3. DISEASES OF ATTITUDE (the verified "killers" framework)
Videos: "Diseases of Attitude" — Inspiring Change Everday [YT:0sG31mTbHJU], 3,757 words; "Avoid These 7 Habits at All Costs" — GrindBuddy ® [YT:l4Sc2QwQ-nc], 2,030 words. Same seminar material, two cuts.

The setup:
> "These attitude diseases are like weeds that grow in the garden... Negative is normal. It's not successful, but it's normal... You must learn to handle the negative. Don't ignore it. Handle it... I know some people teach just turn your head real quick and say there's no weeds. There's no weeds. They'll take your garden."

The war frame:
> "If democracy sleeps, guess who never sleeps? Tyranny. In the absence of light, guess what's automatic? Darkness... Evil is no match for good. But good must be active. Weeds are no match for human activity. But if you stand still, how far in will they come? All the way... If you rest too long, the jungle overtakes the village."

> "A week of neglect could cost you a year of repair."

THE SEVEN, in order, his words:
1. **Indifference** — "The shrug of the shoulder. The guy's not even concerned. He's just drifting... You cannot drift to the top of the mountain... Here's the key to the good life. Learn to put everything you've got into everything you do. Whatever you are doing, pour it on."
2. **Indecision** — "Mental paralysis... 'I know I'm on the fence, but what if I get off on the wrong side?' Listen, after a while, it doesn't matter. Just get off. Any side will do... Don't see how many decisions you can get out of. See how many you can get into."
3. **Doubt** — "Doubt's like a plague. And one of the worst is self-doubt... Turn this coin over and become a believer... The understanding of self-worth is the beginning of progress."
4. **Worry** — "That's a devastating disease... Worry long enough, it'll drop you to your knees... My advice to you is do what I finally did on worry. Give it up. Who needs it? I'm not saying it's easy. I'm saying it's worth it. It took me almost one year to kick the worry habit."
5. **Over-caution** — "Some people never will have much. They're too cautious... It's all risky. The minute you were born, it got risky. If you think trying is risky, wait till they hand you the bill for not trying... You're not going to get out alive. That's risky... Don't ask for security, ask for adventure. Better to live 30 years full of adventure than 100 years safe in the corner."
6. **Pessimism** — "The deadly disease of always looking on the bad side... He doesn't look for virtue. He looks for faults. And when he finds them, he's delighted... This is the poor guy who looks through the window, doesn't see the sunset. He sees the specks on the window... Our lives are mostly affected by the way we think things are, not the way they are."
7. **Complaining** — "Crying, whining, griping. A Bible word called murmuring... Spend five minutes complaining, and you have wasted five, and you may have begun what's known as economic cancer of the bone. Surely they will soon haul you off into a financial desert and there let you choke on the dust of your own regret." Plus the Children of Israel story: "They griped about the water... whined and cried for years. Finally, God said, 'I've had it. Trip cancelled.'... Even God himself can only take so much."

Shoaff on thinking (same seminar):
> "Poor thinking habits keeps most people poor. Not poor working habits. Most people work hard, but they don't think hard... The mind is like a factory, a mental factory... 'Jim, every day stand guard at the door of your mind.'"

The coffee lesson: "Life is both sugar and strychnine. You got to be careful... It doesn't matter who hands you the bad stuff... Lesson two: watch your coffee."

Strong feeling (the Saul of Tarsus segment lands here): "Somebody asked me one time what quality would I pick if I wanted to work with somebody... Number one, strong feeling. Please, number one, give me somebody that feels strong about most anything... I'd rather do that than try to resurrect people from the dead. Pump them up every month. I pass."

**CANON NOTE:** This is the verified framework, and SKILL.md framework #9 now teaches it (corrected Aug 28 2026). The "7 Silent Killers of Success" list that circulates online (indecision, doubt, worry, over-caution, lack of urgency, failing to plan, disconnect from lessons of failure) does NOT appear in any primary source — it's an AI-era YouTube construction. If it surfaces anywhere, refuse it and teach these seven diseases instead. The book-essay parallel is "Facing the Enemies Within" [LAIL]: indifference, indecision, doubt, worry, over-caution.

## 4. WEALTH & HAPPINESS / PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT (1981)
Video: "If You Want to be Wealthy & Happy..." — GrindBuddy ® [YT:OyKIbaSk2s4], 1,921 words pulled.

The thesis:
> "If you want to be wealthy and happy the rest of your life, just learn this lesson. Learn to work harder on yourself than you do on your job. I have found in my experience that income does not far exceed personal development. Now, sometimes income takes a lucky jump, but sure enough, unless you grow out where it is, it'll usually come back where you are."

> "If somebody hands you a million dollars, best you become a millionaire quickly. So you get to keep the money... 'If you took all the money in the world, divided it up equally among everybody, it would soon all be back in the same pockets.'"

> "Success is something you attract, not something you pursue... True happiness is not contained in what you get. Happiness is contained in what you become."

Ideas over money (Shoaff):
> "I said to Mr. Shoaff, 'I don't have any money.' He said, 'That's not a problem... The problem is lack of an idea on how to create money and wealth. It isn't lack of money, it's lack of ideas.'"

The three ways to find out (read / listen / observe):
> "Number one is to read... They just read, read, read, read... One book might save you five years... 30 minutes a day. Read something positive, something challenging, something inspirational, something instructional."
> "Find out what poor people read and don't read it."
> "Poor people ought to take rich people out to dinner... If you get a successful person to eat and talk for two hours, they're liable to drop ideas in your lap."
> "Success leaves clues. Watch how the man shakes hands... If a guy's making $10,000 a month, I'd watch how he walks. Maybe that's it. Copy his funny little walk... I haven't got the money yet, but I got the walk. It's bound to start somewhere."
> "There's two ways to see. One is called sight... The other one is called insight... See with your eyes, you'll see things. See with your mind, you'll see answers... Wherever you are, be there."

Go to work + the journal:
> "On any given day, you can massively change the direction of your life... Start with the little disciplines... You can't handle the big challenges in life unless you take on the little ones."
> "Don't use your head for a filing cabinet. Put it in your journal... Repetition, repetition, repetition... someday, some mysterious day, the idea takes root, starts to grow, and shows up in your bank account and your dress and your personality and your lifestyle."
> "Don't let your learning lead to knowledge, you'll become a fool. Let your learning lead to action, you can become wealthy."
> "Self-motivation... I don't know why we call it self-motivation. It's really the only kind there is... The guy says, 'Boy, if somebody just come by and turn me on' — what if they don't show up?"

Don't beat the system:
> "Don't try to beat the system. Find out how it works. Work it, but don't try to beat it. Some people learn just enough to start slicing it, shading it, thinning it, cutting corners, and looking for cheap answers... you'll wind up with a cheap life... Don't compromise with right."

The five-year mirror:
> "If you don't do something starting tomorrow that'll make a difference, guess what? It's going to be the same... Look at the last five cuz the next five are going to be like the last five unless you change... It's choice time... It's nice to know any day you wish you can change your whole life."

## 5. HOW TO GET WHAT YOU WANT
Video: "How To Get What You Want In Life" — Jim Rohn Real Seminars ® [YT:siw7Jqlc3ak], 2,983 words pulled.

> "The promise of the future is an awesome force. We look back for experience, but we have to look forward now for inspiration."

> "Reasons make the difference in your appetite and zest for taking on the challenge... You can overcome the most unbelievable challenges if you have enough reasons."

The Shoaff goals exchange (his fullest spoken telling):
> "'Let me see your current list of goals'... And I said to him, 'I don't have a list.' He said, 'Well, if you don't have a list of your goals, I can guess your bank balance within a few hundred dollars.' Which he did... 'You mean if I had a list of goals, it would change my bank balance?' He said, 'Drastically.'"

Why → how:
> "When the why gets stronger, the how gets easier... If you had strong and powerful enough goals, you'd figure out how to manage your time... You can get up any hour, read any book, take any class, make any change, develop any skill, do any discipline."

Purpose over object:
> "Purpose is stronger than object. The object would be the house... But here's a stronger goal, the purpose for the million-dollar home... 'That'll be the centerpiece of all the family's activity'... See, now we're getting somewhere."

Turned on / turned off lists:
> "What's got you turned on?... What's got you turned off?... Once I got that settled... my life has never been the same. It was like a revolution. A personal revolution. A 180° turn."

The become question:
> "What kind of person must I become to achieve all I want?... What you become helps you to achieve, and what you achieve helps you to become... From milking cows to sitting on this stool. Incredible. What a journey."

List hygiene:
> "The key is to take it out of your head and put it on paper... If something's not that important to you, take it off your list... If you had a hundred items on your list and you accomplished eighty, who cares about the other twenty?"

Behold and beware:
> "Here's the positive word from antiquity. Behold... Behold the possibilities. Behold the opportunity... Now, here's the negative word. Beware... Beware of what you become in pursuit of what you want... Some things I went for in the very beginning cost me too much... Don't be so obsessed with something that you compromise your virtues and your values."

Habits and self-worth (the toothpick story):
> "Mama said... 'Just put it in your pocket. And then when you get home, you put it in the trash.'... Here's what makes the difference: the habit... Give you the incredible feeling of self-worth because you have good habits."

> "The greatest source of unhappiness is self-unhappiness."

> "Always be sincere and always do your best, no matter who you talk to, because you just don't know who's in the audience."
