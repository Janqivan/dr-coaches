# B2B Should Act Like B2C — Frameworks & Tactics

Source body of work: Drift-era category creation (2016-2020), Conversational
Marketing book (Cancel + Gerhardt, Wiley 2019), Exit Five community playbook
(2022-2026), Founder Brand book, Exit Five podcast catalog, davegerhardt.com
essays, HubSpot author archive.

This is the synthetic-Gerhardt position on why modern B2B marketing only
works when you stop pretending B2B and B2C are different worlds.

---

## 1. The Core Thesis — B2B is Just P2P

"B2B" is a category we made up to justify boring marketing.

There is no such thing as a "business" that buys software. A business is a
group of humans, one of whom makes the call, and four of whom influence it.
All of them are consumers in their day-to-day life. They listen to Spotify,
they scroll TikTok, they buy from brands that have a personality. Then they
walk into a meeting and we expect them to suddenly prefer beige.

They don't.

The buyers of B2B software in 2026 grew up on internet culture. They want:
- Brands with a voice, not brands with a deck
- Founders who post, not founders who hide
- Communities of peers, not "channels of communication"
- Real customers on camera, not stock-photo case studies
- A point of view, not a feature list

If you sound like every other vendor in your category, you ARE every other
vendor in your category. Sameness is the actual competitor.

The B2B brands that have won in the last decade — HubSpot, Drift, Gong,
Notion, Linear, Vercel, Ramp — all act like consumer brands. They have
voices. They have memes. They have communities. They have aesthetics.
Their CEOs post. Their employees post. Their customers post.

The B2B brands that lost — you've already forgotten their names.

## 2. What "Acting Like B2C" Actually Means

It does NOT mean:
- Slapping a mascot on everything
- Making "fun" ads with stock comedy
- Writing in lowercase like a DTC brand
- Performative cringe-marketing

It DOES mean:

### a) A real, consistent voice
Not "brand guidelines say we should be friendly and authoritative." A voice
your customer can identify in three sentences with the logo removed. The
HubSpot voice. The Notion voice. The Linear voice. These are recognizable
because they're tight and consistent.

### b) A face (or several)
B2C brands have founders, athletes, characters, influencers. B2B brands
need the same thing — usually the founder, sometimes a CMO or category
evangelist. Anonymous logos don't build relationships. Faces do.

### c) Real customers as the brand
The best marketing in B2B is proof. Customer screenshots, customer videos,
customer wins on LinkedIn. Not "case studies" — those are too polished to
trust. Real, ugly-but-true customer content beats a $50k case study every
time.

### d) A community, not an audience
Audience = people consume your content.
Community = people talk to each other because of your content.
The shift from audience to community is what unlocks 10x leverage. Audience
is rented. Community is owned.

### e) An opinion
A B2C brand has a vibe. A B2B brand needs an opinion. What is broken in
your category? What does the industry get wrong? What should the customer
expect that they don't get today? Write that down. Say it publicly.
Repeat it. That's a position.

### f) Distribution that doesn't cost CPM
B2C brands invest in owned audiences (email lists, communities, creator
deals) because paid is unaffordable at scale. B2B brands are 5 years behind
on this. The B2B brands that build owned audiences — newsletter, podcast,
community — own their CAC. Everyone else rents it from LinkedIn Ads.

## 3. Voice & Tone Calibration

The single fastest test for B2B voice: read your homepage out loud, then
explain what you do to a friend at a wedding. If they don't match, your
homepage is wrong.

### Sentence-level rewrites — corporate → human

| Corporate-B2B (wrong) | Human-B2B (right) |
|---|---|
| "Leverage best-in-class solutions" | "Use the best tool" |
| "Empower your team to drive outcomes" | "Help your team get more done" |
| "Holistic platform for enterprise growth" | "One tool to run your sales team" |
| "Synergize cross-functional workflows" | "Stop emailing four departments" |
| "Mission-critical SaaS infrastructure" | "Software your business runs on" |
| "Unlock unprecedented value" | "Make more money" |
| "We're excited to announce" | "Here's what we built" |
| "Reimagining the future of work" | (delete entirely) |

The rule: if a human wouldn't say it at lunch, don't put it on the website.

### Voice tests for B2B writing

Before any piece of copy ships, ask:

1. Could this have been written by 50 other companies in our category?
   → If yes, kill it.
2. Does it have an opinion?
   → If no, add one.
3. Does it sound like a person, or like a press release?
   → If press release, rewrite.
4. Would my best customer enjoy reading this, or just tolerate it?
   → Tolerated content is worse than no content. Cut it.
5. Is there a specific number, name, or story in it?
   → Specifics build trust. Abstractions don't.

## 4. The Drift Playbook — What They Actually Did

Drift's category-creation play between 2016-2020 is the modern textbook for
B2B-acting-like-B2C. Specific tactics worth copying:

- **They named the category.** "Conversational Marketing." Not "live chat."
  The name positioned chat as a revenue tool, not a support tool. Words
  matter. Pick yours.
- **They wrote the book.** *Conversational Marketing* (Wiley, Jan 2019) by
  David Cancel + Dave Gerhardt. The book was the category bible. Every
  competitor had to either play along or fight the framework.
- **They gave the category away.** Free Conversational Marketing
  Certification through Drift Insider. Thousands of marketers got trained
  on Drift's worldview, with Drift's vocabulary, for free.
- **They built in public.** The Drift blog, the SeekingWisdom podcast (the
  Cancel-Gerhardt one), the LinkedIn-native content, the keynotes — all
  pushed the same narrative. Repetition without sounding repetitive.
- **They put the founder out front.** Cancel posted. Gerhardt posted.
  Every employee was empowered to post. Drift was a place to follow
  people, not a place to read a careers page.
- **They had a personality.** Pink. Robots. Conversational tone in every
  asset. Memes. Anti-form-fill positioning. They picked a fight (forms
  are bad) and ran at it for 4 years straight.
- **They built community before they had the product to back it.**
  Hypergrowth Conference. The Drift community. Local meetups. The
  community came before scale, not after.

Result: $1B exit. Category they named is now table stakes. Their playbook
is the modern B2B playbook.

## 5. The Exit Five Playbook — Community as the Product

Exit Five (the Gerhardt-founded community, 5,700+ paying B2B marketers,
on track for ~$3M revenue) is the proof that community-as-product works
in B2B, not just B2C.

Operating principles worth stealing:

- **Treat the community like a product.** Onboarding, weekly value drops,
  active moderation, member feedback loops. Not "set it and forget it."
- **Charge for it.** Free communities die because nobody has skin. Paid
  ($49/mo or $449/yr standard; $99/mo Marketing Leaders Club tier)
  filters for serious people and funds the engine.
- **Multi-format, one brand.** Podcast (340+ episodes), newsletter (38k+
  subscribers), Circle community, in-person Drive conference, local
  chapters in 30+ cities. Different surfaces, same voice.
- **Founder is the face.** Gerhardt is Exit Five. The Delphi clone at
  delphi.ai/davegerhardt extends that face to 24/7 availability without
  diluting it.
- **No ads model.** Membership funds the content. No sponsored episodes
  diluting the trust. Owned audience, owned monetization.
- **Build in public.** Gerhardt posts revenue numbers, member counts,
  what's working and what isn't. The community is the case study for
  the community.

For a B2B company that wants to act like B2C: the community + content +
founder-brand stack is the modern operating system. Not optional.

## 6. Brand vs. Demand — The Real Frame

The "brand vs demand" debate is mostly fake. You need both. The real
question is order and ratio.

- **Demand** captures intent that already exists. Paid search, outbound
  SDRs, retargeting. Cash in, leads out. Stops the day you stop spending.
- **Brand** creates preference BEFORE intent shows up. Founder content,
  podcast, community, newsletter, opinion-led PR. Builds slow, compounds
  fast after 12-18 months.

The CFO-friendly version Gerhardt teaches:

- Demand without brand = your CAC creeps up every quarter forever
- Brand without demand = great podcast, no pipeline
- Brand + demand = brand lowers CAC over time, demand cashes the check today

The right ratio depends on stage. Early stage: 80/20 demand to brand (you
need cash). Growth stage: 60/40 demand to brand (you need a moat). Scale
stage: 50/50 (you ARE the brand now, defend it).

The mistake 95% of B2B companies make: 100% demand, 0% brand, then wonder
why ad costs keep going up and pipeline keeps getting harder. The answer
isn't more demand. The answer is the brand you didn't build 3 years ago.

## 7. The Anti-Generic Filters

Before you ship any B2B asset — homepage, deck, ad, post, email — run it
through these:

1. **The category test.** Remove your logo. Could this be any of your top
   5 competitors? If yes — kill it.
2. **The mom test.** Could you explain what you do to your mom in one
   sentence? If not — your positioning is too dense.
3. **The proof test.** Is there a real customer name, number, or story
   somewhere in this asset? If not — add one or kill it.
4. **The voice test.** Does this sound like you talk, or does it sound
   like a brochure? If brochure — rewrite.
5. **The opinion test.** Is there a take in here that someone could
   disagree with? If not — add one. Beige content gets beige results.
6. **The "who reads this" test.** Is this written for the customer, or
   for marketing's internal review? Most B2B copy is the second one.
   Always write for the customer.
7. **The 10-second test.** A smart visitor lands on your homepage. In 10
   seconds, can they tell: who it's for, what it does, and why they
   should care? If not — fix the hero.

If a piece of copy can't pass 5 of these 7, don't ship it. Rewrite.

## 8. The Operational Move for Most B2B Companies

If you're a B2B company sitting at "we have no real brand" and want to
move toward acting-like-B2C, the order is:

1. **Pick the ONE narrative.** What's the phrase you want to be known for.
   Write it on the wall. Make every asset reinforce it.
2. **Put the founder out front.** LinkedIn first, podcast second, newsletter
   third, stage fourth. Year one is founder brand.
3. **Hire a Promoter as your first marketing person.** Not a demand-gen
   manager. A Promoter — someone who writes, posts, evangelizes, gets
   you on the map.
4. **Build owned distribution.** Newsletter is the cheapest, fastest
   defensible asset in B2B. Start it this month.
5. **Build a community.** Small first. Even 100 customers in a Circle/
   Slack is worth more than 10,000 LinkedIn followers you can't email.
6. **Add personality to every asset.** Voice, opinion, story. Edit
   everything for "would a real person say this."
7. **Then layer demand on top.** Ads, outbound, SEO. They all work
   3-5x harder when the brand exists than when it doesn't.

This is the order. Skipping straight to demand without a brand layer is
the most expensive mistake in B2B. And it's the one almost everyone makes.
