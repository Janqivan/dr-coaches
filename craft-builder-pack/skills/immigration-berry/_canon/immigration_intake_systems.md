# Immigration Intake Systems: How Elite Immigration Firms Convert Inquiries Into Signed Retainers

The single biggest leverage point in an immigration law firm — bigger than
ad creative, bigger than SEO, bigger than the website — is what happens in
the 24 hours between when a prospect submits a form and when they sign a
retainer. Two firms running identical ad budgets on identical creative will
produce 3x different cost-per-signed-case numbers if one of them has elite
intake and the other has whoever-picks-up-the-phone intake.

I learned this living inside an immigration firm in Dallas for two years.
I have watched firms triple revenue without spending an extra dollar on
ads — by fixing intake. I have also watched firms double ad spend with no
revenue lift — because intake was broken.

This is the playbook.

## The Five Non-Negotiables of Immigration Intake

### 1. Speed-to-Lead Under 5 Minutes

The single most expensive metric in immigration marketing is the gap between
form submission and first human callback. The industry-wide data is clear:
contact within 5 minutes vs. 30 minutes is roughly a 21x difference in
conversion to consult booked. Within 5 minutes vs. 24 hours, the difference
is approximately a 60x decline.

Inside an immigration firm, this is even more brutal than in other practice
areas, because the prospect is anxious. They are calling 3-5 firms in
parallel. The first competent voice they hear wins the consult booking. The
prospect who submits a form at 9:14am and gets called back at 9:17am is a
12% close on signed retainer. The same prospect called at 11:30am is a 2%
close.

How to actually deliver this:

- Dedicated intake person, or rotation of dedicated intake people. Not
  "whoever has time." Not the paralegal handling cases. Not the attorney.
- Form submissions trigger immediate alerts to intake's phone AND desktop.
- Call attempt within 90 seconds of submission during business hours. Three
  attempts in the first hour if no answer. Voicemail script ready.
- After-hours form submissions: automated SMS within 60 seconds
  acknowledging receipt and confirming first-call time the next morning,
  THEN actual human call first thing.
- Hold the intake person to a daily metric: % of leads contacted within 5
  minutes of submission. Goal: 80%+ during business hours.

This metric alone, if you fix nothing else, will lift retainer revenue 20-40%
within 60 days. The math is brutal.

### 2. Bilingual Intake From Day One

Most U.S. immigration firms serve a Spanish-dominant client base for at
least 40-70% of their volume. If your intake person doesn't speak Spanish
fluently — meaning conversationally, comfortably, able to discuss legal
fees and case timelines, not "I took two years in high school" — you are
hemorrhaging the Spanish-speaking prospect on call one.

The minimum bar:
- One intake person fluent in Spanish, ideally native speaker, ideally
  someone the Latino community in your city recognizes as "one of us"
  rather than a translator.
- For firms working Asian markets (NIW, H-1B, EB-5): Mandarin, Hindi,
  Punjabi, Tagalog as appropriate to the market.
- Spanish-language voicemail greeting available, not just an English one.
- Intake forms available in both languages on the website. Spanish version
  is NOT Google-translated.
- Intake person is briefed on cultural context. Spanish-speaking clients
  often discuss family decisions multi-generationally — be patient with
  "let me talk to my husband / mother / sister" responses. That's not a
  brush-off, that's the decision-making process.

If you can't hire bilingual yet, partner with a bilingual VA service that
handles initial intake and warm-transfers to your English-only attorney
team. Better than losing the call entirely.

### 3. Qualification Before Consult Booking

The most common immigration intake mistake is booking every caller for a
paid consult regardless of fit. This burns the attorney's calendar with
unqualified consults, drops show-up rates, and trains the firm to chase
unqualified leads.

Three things to qualify on the call BEFORE booking:

**Case type.** "Walk me through your situation — what brings you to us
today?" This is open-ended. Let them talk. Most prospects can describe
their case in 60 seconds. From there, intake should categorize: marriage-
based green card? Family petition? Work visa? Asylum? Removal proceedings?
EB-5 investment? Match the case to the right attorney on the team.

**Basic facts that determine viability.** Is the spouse a US citizen or
green card holder? Has the prospect ever been deported? Have they had a
prior visa denied? Are they in removal proceedings? These five facts cover
80% of the basic viability check.

**Ability to pay the retainer band.** Don't quote exact pricing — that's
the attorney's job — but anchor expectations. "Most of our marriage-based
cases run between $5,000 and $8,000, with payment plans available. Is that
the range you're prepared for?" If the prospect says they have $500 and
no payment-plan ability, the case is either pro-bono territory or a
referral, not a consult. Save the attorney's time.

Qualification questions to USE:
- "Can you tell me a little about what's going on?"
- "Is anyone in your family already in the United States legally?"
- "Have you spoken to any other immigration attorneys about this?"
- "What's your timeline — when do you need this resolved by?"
- "Have you had any past immigration issues — visa denials, prior
  deportations, anything that came up at a port of entry?"

Qualification questions to AVOID:
- "What's your income?" (Too direct, feels invasive)
- "Are you here legally?" (Status questions belong with the attorney, not
  intake. And the answer doesn't determine whether they can be helped.)
- "Have you been arrested?" (Wait for the attorney consult.)

### 4. Warm Transfer When Possible, Same-Day Callback Otherwise

If a qualified prospect is on the phone with intake AND the assigned
attorney is available — warm-transfer the call. Right now. Mid-sentence if
you have to. "Maria, I'm going to put you on a very brief hold and bring
in Attorney Davis right now — she handles exactly this type of case. Stay
with me."

The warm transfer:
- Cuts the gap between qualification and consult from 24-48 hours to zero.
- Eliminates no-shows on the consult — the prospect is already on the
  phone with the attorney.
- Compresses the deal cycle from days to a single call.
- Converts at 2-3x the rate of a "we'll book you for a consult next
  Thursday" flow.

When warm-transfer isn't possible (attorney in court, on another consult,
out of office):
- Schedule the consult within 24 hours, never more than 48. Anything past
  48 hours doubles the no-show rate.
- Confirm with TWO channels: voice call within 2 hours of booking, SMS
  confirmation 24 hours before, email reminder 2 hours before.
- Pre-consult intake form sent immediately so the attorney walks in
  prepared.

### 5. Confirmation Call Within 24 Hours

Show-up rate on the first consult is the single most overlooked metric in
immigration intake. The industry average is around 50-60% with no
confirmation discipline. Elite firms run 85-92%.

The mechanic:
- 24 hours before the consult: live phone call from intake (not a text,
  not an email) to confirm the appointment. "Hi Maria, this is Sarah from
  Davis Law. I'm calling to confirm your consultation tomorrow at 2pm
  with Attorney Davis. Anything we can do to help you prepare?"
- 2 hours before the consult: SMS reminder with the meeting link or
  office address.
- If they don't answer the day-before call: leave a voicemail AND send a
  text AND email. Three channels.

This sounds like overkill. It isn't. The prospect who is going to show up
doesn't mind the confirmation. The prospect who was going to ghost gets
prompted to reschedule, which gives the firm a chance to actually close
them. No-shows are not lost cases — they are cases where you didn't
confirm hard enough.

## The Consult-to-Retainer Pricing Conversation

This is where most immigration firms fumble the close. The attorney walks
through the case beautifully, the prospect is engaged, and then the
attorney says "okay, my paralegal will send you a fee proposal" — and
the case dies in email follow-up.

The right structure:

1. **Quote the retainer in the consult.** Always. Don't punt it to email.
   The prospect has emotional momentum on the call. Lose the moment and
   you lose the case.

2. **Quote a range tied to scope.** "Most marriage-based cases like
   yours run $5,500 to $7,500 depending on whether we need to address
   the prior visa denial. Let me explain the difference."

3. **Present the engagement structure clearly.** "Here's how we work: we
   require $X to begin the case today, with the balance over four
   monthly payments. We can have the I-130 ready to file within 14 days
   of engagement."

4. **Ask for the engagement on the call.** "Are you ready to move
   forward?" If yes, send the engagement letter via DocuSign while the
   prospect is still on the call. Get the signature today.

5. **If hesitation, surface it.** "What's making you pause?" Common
   answers: cost, timing, need to discuss with spouse. For each, have a
   prepared response. Cost: walk through payment plan. Timing: clarify
   what waiting costs. Spouse: schedule a follow-up call WITH the spouse
   within 48 hours, not "let me know when you've talked."

What kills retainer conversion:
- "Here's the fee, take your time, let us know."
- Email-only follow-up with no scheduled call.
- Vague engagement terms.
- No payment plan offered.
- No "ready to start today" close attempt.

## How Intake Quality Multiplies Marketing ROI

If your firm is running $20,000/month in immigration ads and getting 200
qualified leads, here's how intake quality changes the result:

**Mediocre intake (industry average):**
- 50% callback rate within 24 hours = 100 contacted
- 60% book a consult = 60 booked
- 60% show up = 36 consults held
- 25% sign retainer = 9 signed
- CPSC = $2,222

**Elite intake (the playbook above):**
- 90% callback within 5 minutes = 180 contacted
- 75% book a consult = 135 booked
- 88% show up = 119 consults held
- 40% sign retainer = 48 signed
- CPSC = $417

Same ad spend. Same leads. 5x more signed cases. This is why I tell
operators: before you raise the ad budget, fix the intake. Doubling ad
spend in a broken intake firm produces 1.4x revenue. Fixing intake in a
firm with stable ad spend produces 3-5x revenue.

## Staffing The Intake Function

For firms under $2M revenue: one dedicated intake person, bilingual if
the market requires it, paid $50k-$70k base with a small per-retainer-
signed bonus ($25-$50 per signed retainer creates the right incentive
without breaking ethical rules — confirm with state bar guidance).

For firms $2M-$5M: two intake people on rotating coverage, bilingual on
at least one, with overflow to an answering service for after-hours.

For firms $5M+: a dedicated intake team of 3-5 with a team lead, KPIs
tracked weekly, CRM (Lawmatics, Clio Grow, or equivalent) as the source
of truth, all calls recorded and reviewed.

Do not have the receptionist do intake. Receptionist is a different job.
Receptionist greets walk-ins, routes calls, handles administrative
inbound. Intake is a sales role with legal-fluency training. They are
different humans.

Do not have the attorney do intake on inbound web leads. Attorneys do
consultations, not intake calls. The most expensive billable hour in the
firm should not be answering "do you handle marriage-based green cards?"
for the fortieth time today.

## Tracking The Intake Funnel End-to-End

You cannot improve what you do not measure. The minimum dashboard:

1. Lead submitted (form, phone, walk-in)
2. Lead contacted within 5 minutes
3. Lead qualified
4. Consult booked
5. Consult confirmed (24-hour confirmation call)
6. Consult held (no-show rate)
7. Retainer offered on the consult
8. Retainer signed
9. Retainer signed within 7 days of consult
10. Case engaged (first work product delivered)

Tools that work for this in immigration: Lawmatics (purpose-built for law
firm intake), Clio Grow, Lead Docket, CallRail for call attribution into
the CRM. Pick one. Connect it to the ad platforms. Look at the dashboard
weekly with intake, monthly with the founder.

If a firm tells me "we have an Excel spreadsheet" for intake tracking,
that's the first thing we fix.

## The Intake Audit (Do This Once A Quarter)

Pull the last 100 web leads. Trace each one through the funnel above.
Write down where they fell out. The pattern will tell you exactly what to
fix:

- Big drop at step 2 (contact within 5 minutes)? Intake staffing or
  notification problem.
- Big drop at step 4 (booked the consult)? Qualification skill or
  pricing-anchor problem.
- Big drop at step 6 (showed up)? Confirmation discipline problem.
- Big drop at step 8 (signed retainer)? Attorney consult-close skill or
  fee-presentation problem.

Most firms have ONE primary bottleneck. Fix that one. The other numbers
move with it.
