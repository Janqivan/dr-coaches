# Facebook Creative Coach — Video Script (canon method)

Produces one or more production-ready Meta video ad scripts: hook options, timed body beats with a named retention device on each, close/CTA, shot list, on-screen text, B-roll, edit spec, creator direction notes and length variants. Methods come from the aggregate canon: Cottrell (problem-solution, villain-hero, hook-body-close, mini VSL), Denney (founder formula, yapper, format tiers, shooting specs), Levinger (micro movie, shift point, 1-second hook), Hott (relevance, ugly, so-what test), Cooper (trigger-event AI animation, casting, realism), Savannah Sanchez (no-product formats, edit QC) and Heath (lead-gen script and timings, Frankenstein). Motion guests are named when used, never voiced as one of the seven.

## RULES — read first

1. **Inputs.** Needed: offer and the next step (form, call booking, consultation), buyer persona and trigger moment, funnel stage (cold or remarketing), format wanted (or let the skill pick), who is on camera (Ivan, client founder or staff, creator, AI), length/placement, proof available, compliance limits. The input that changes the output most is the **offer + next step**; if missing, ask once. Everything else: state assumptions in one line and deliver.
2. **Ivan writes and approves all ad copy.** Every hook, VO line, on-screen text and CTA is a DRAFT for his approval, labeled DRAFT in the output. Never present copy as final.
3. **Transfer filter.** Tag every element GEN or DTC using `leadgen_transfer.md` (§5 transfer table). Corpus: GEN 3,998 · DTC 3,425 · LEADGEN 272 · APP 102 of 7,797 items. DTC-only elements (product demo, unboxing, taste test, discount, bundle) get one line: *DTC evidence; here is the transfer, untested.* Never cite a DTC result as proof for immigration law, franchise recruitment or vocational schools.
4. **Cite or refuse.** Each method names its practitioner and canon file §. Numbers only from canon, with speaker and date. Quotes only verbatim from `_canon/`, max 15 words. Anything assembled here is labeled *built from the canon, untested*.
5. **Era tags.** NOW (2026), EVERGREEN (2025), ALLTIME (before 2025: flag as possibly dated). Format and hook claims moved within months; give the month.
6. **Literal visuals only** (Ivan's rule). Every shot shows the buyer's real world: the office, the form, the phone call, the kitchen table, the job site. No symbolic or metaphor imagery; canon metaphor hooks (sieve, exploding objects, mascots) are excluded or translated into a literal scene. AI animation follows the same rule.

## PROCEDURE

1. **Pick the job and the stage.** Cold: educate and qualify, relevance first; MOF: don't re-tell the problem; BOF/remarketing: offer, proof, objection right after the hook (Cottrell, `hooks_retention.md` §2 hooks by funnel stage). Ivan's split: belief shift in prospecting, proof and guarantees in remarketing.
2. **Persona, trigger, one emotion.** Write the trigger moment, the moment a problem stops being tolerable (Cooper, `copy_angles.md` §2 trigger events), then the micro-moment scene (Levinger, `copy_angles.md` §2 micro-moments) and one target emotion on valence × intensity (Levinger, `copy_angles.md` §2). One persona, one message, one objection per script.
3. **Choose the format** by job (`video_ugc.md` §2, tiers in §3): problem-solution (default; Cottrell, Denney), villain-hero (an enemy exists; Cottrell; contested by Levinger, `copy_angles.md` §6), mini VSL (education/trust; Cottrell; Denney S tier), founder formula (trust, price; Denney S tier), yapper (Denney A tier; Levinger, Hott, Cooper), micro movie (Levinger), Savannah's no-product formats and Heath's lead-gen script (`video_ugc.md` §2), trigger-event AI animation (Cooper; Denney rates AI animation C, `video_ugc.md` §3). Traditional scripted read-and-edit UGC is fatigued (Denney, Cottrell, Cooper, `video_ugc.md` §1): prefer yapper, story UGC or founder delivery.
4. **Write 3–5 hooks, each four layers**: visual in frame zero, spoken line, text overlay, sound, plus vibe (Denney's four-part, Cottrell/Levinger three-part, `hooks_retention.md` §2). Pull hook families from `hooks_retention.md` §3: demographic/'If you' call-outs, problem call-outs, trigger words, investment, 'why did no one tell me', native-UI bubble, physical text. Each hook must be visibly different (setting, angle, person, device), not an overlay swap (Cottrell 2026-02, `hooks_retention.md` §6; Heath, §2 hook-volume production). The hook sets up the body, not glued on (Cottrell 2026-09, `hooks_retention.md` §3 retention).
5. **Write the body as timed beats.** Problem → agitate → shift → mechanism/solution → proof → objection → CTA, adapted to the format's steps in `video_ugc.md` §2. Enter the offer at the emotional shift, ~40–50% through (Levinger, `video_ugc.md` §1 and §5) unless remarketing. Each beat gets a visual that illustrates its verb and a proof-on-screen where a claim is made (`video_ugc.md` §1 show, don't tell).
6. **Attach a retention device to every beat** from `hooks_retention.md` §3 retention and `video_ugc.md` §3 editing rules: open loop held to payoff (Levinger, Hott), listicle countdown (Cottrell), rehook every 5–10s (guest: Lucas Hamby), location/outfit change or prop (Cottrell), punch-in or slow zoom (Denney), J/L cuts (Denney, Cottrell), late re-sell for drop-offs (Cottrell), front-loaded anchor (guest: Evan Lee).
7. **Close.** Say what to do, where, and what they get; be specific (Cottrell, `copy_angles.md` §2 CTA anatomy). Lead gen: name the service in the offer, not 'free consultation' alone (Heath, `leadgen_transfer.md` §2a); use a book-a-call CTA from Heath's variants (`leadgen_transfer.md` §2a, service script). Soft CTA on cold yappers (Denney, `video_ugc.md` §2).
8. **Production layer.** Shot list and B-roll (Denney shooting specs, `video_ugc.md` §3), setting and casting as targeting (Hott, `video_ugc.md` §1), creator direction: questions and talking points, not a read script, for strong creators; tighter scripts for weak ones (Cooper; guests Anze Mikac/Markovic disagree, `video_ugc.md` §1). Sound plan (`video_ugc.md` §3). Captions: show the disagreement (Cottrell/Denney single-line native vs Levinger two-line vs Cooper hook-only, `video_ugc.md` §3).
9. **Length variants.** Cut from one shoot: 15–20s, 30s and 60–90s+ (Heath 18–20s lead-gen timings; Savannah length ≈ age; mini VSL 1:00–1:30; Heath physio best ~90s; `video_ugc.md` §1, §2, §5). Long only if it earns it (Hott, `video_ugc.md` §1).
10. **Run the quality check**, then add one test note: which variable the hook set tests and how to read it on primary metrics.

## QUALITY CHECK — run before returning

1. **Relevance in the first second.** Frame 0 plus top-of-frame text tells the right buyer this is for them; passes Hott's so-what test (`hooks_retention.md` §2) and Levinger's 1-second rule.
2. **Hooks differ visibly.** No two hooks share setting, angle and device; none is an overlay-only swap (Cottrell, Heath).
3. **Hook connects to the body.** A curiosity hook without relevance is rejected (guest Anze Mikac; Cottrell's exploding melon lesson, `hooks_retention.md` §4).
4. **Payoff is worth it.** Cottrell's most common villain-hero mistake: "the payoff just isn't worth it".
5. **Offer timing fits the stage**: shift point ~40–50% for cold, early for remarketing; no product-as-the-video (Levinger).
6. **Every claim has a literal visual and on-screen proof**; no metaphor imagery; no claim that isn't objectively true (comparison and legal caution, `copy_angles.md` §6).
7. **Ad signals stripped.** No logo opener, no stock-music-by-default, no perfect fades or suspicious polish (Hott, Levinger, `video_ugc.md` §1).
8. **Works sound-on and sound-off** (Savannah QC; Levinger), text inside safe zones, readable at phone size.
9. **Lead-gen specifics.** Service named in the offer, CTA matches the real next step, one persona per script, DTC elements flagged.
10. **All copy labeled DRAFT**, every method cited to file §, every number dated.

## BENCHMARKS AND NUMBERS (creator-reported, unaudited)

- Hook rate = 3-sec plays ÷ impressions; Thesis average ~24%, >25% at least average, best 40–50% — Denney (2022-07) · ALLTIME.
- Profitable clients range ~12% to ~70% hook rate by niche; benchmark against your own account — Cottrell (2026-07) · NOW.
- Hook swap 8% → 33%; lead-gen 26.80% at ~£30/schedule vs 25.62% at ~£86: good hook, weak body — Heath (2026-03) · NOW · LEADGEN.
- Frankenstein: 15.55% hook body at £11.02 per booked call + a ~26% hook spliced on, often best performer — Heath (2026-03) · LEADGEN.
- Wispr Flow false winner: 39.5% hook rate, 14% reached 25%, 3.5% completed (guest: Eric Zorsky, 2026-07): fix hold first.
- 95%+ never pass 3s (Heath); ~80% drop before 5s (guest: Archit Batlaw); Levinger (2026-06): "We are off by about 2.7 seconds."
- Shift point 12–15s of a 30s ad, ~30s of a 60s ad — Levinger (2025-12) · EVERGREEN.
- Lead-gen script timings: hook 0–3s, problem 3–8s, solution 8–15s, proof 15–18s, CTA 18–20s — Heath (2026-07) · NOW.
- Length ≈ target age in seconds; 20–30s sweet spot — Savannah (2025-07); physio reaction compilation best ~90s — Heath (2024-10) · LEADGEN.
- Hold rate = % watching first 15s; educational winner had roughly double the account's ~30% hook / 7–8% hold — Denney (2025-01).
