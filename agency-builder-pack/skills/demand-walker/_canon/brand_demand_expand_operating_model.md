# Brand → Demand → Expand (B→D→E) — The Operating Model

> Source corpus: Chris Walker founder-era Refine Labs material. B→D→E was
> formulated by Walker during his tenure and is now Refine Labs' explicit
> public operating framework under CEO Megan Bowen (post-July 2025).
> This file documents the founder-era version as Walker taught it.

---

## What B→D→E Is

B→D→E is the operating model that operationalizes the
demand-creation-vs-capture distinction. It splits the GTM motion into
three pillars, each with its own audience, content, channels, metrics,
and ownership:

1. **Brand** — build preference with future buyers (the 95% out-of-market)
2. **Demand** — capture buyers in-market right now (the 5%)
3. **Expand** — drive net revenue retention and expansion post-sale

If you collapse these into one team called "marketing" with one metric
called "MQL," you will under-resource Brand, over-resource Demand, and
ignore Expand. That is the default state of most B2B firms. B→D→E is
the org-design fix.

---

## Pillar 1: Brand

**Audience:** the 95% of your TAM that is NOT in-market. They have the
problem your product solves, but they aren't shopping yet — maybe never
have been, maybe won't be for 12-24 months.

**Job:** make the company name, the founders, the point of view, and
the category position familiar and trusted by the time those buyers
enter the buying window.

**Content:** native, educational, on-platform, ungated.
- Founder LinkedIn presence (daily, point-of-view-driven, not corporate)
- Podcast — owned, weekly cadence, focused on category insight not
  product pitches
- YouTube — long-form demand-creation content, again focused on the
  category and the buyer's world
- Newsletter — broad-distribution (not gated)
- Executive speaking, paid social distribution of native posts/video
- Communities, partnerships with peer creators

**Channels:** LinkedIn organic + LinkedIn paid for distribution of
native content, YouTube, Spotify / Apple Podcasts, owned newsletter,
events, podcast guest tour.

**Metrics:**
- Self-Reported Attribution share — % of inbound demos that cite a
  Brand channel ("how did you hear about us?")
- Branded search volume growth
- Direct traffic growth
- Podcast subscribers / YouTube subscribers growth
- LinkedIn follower growth + engagement on POV content
- Audience survey: unaided brand awareness in your ICP segment
- DO NOT measure with MQLs. There are no MQLs in Brand.

**Ownership:** typically the CMO or a head-of-brand role plus a small
content production unit. At early stage this is the founder. The trap
is letting "demand gen" eat Brand because Demand has a dashboard and
Brand doesn't.

**Budget share:** 50-70% of total marketing program spend in most
B2B SaaS firms Walker analyzed.

## Pillar 2: Demand

**Audience:** the ~5% who are in-market right now — they typed a query,
they clicked a retargeting ad because they remember the brand from the
Brand work, they came in from a peer referral, they hit your pricing
page.

**Job:** intercept them efficiently, qualify them quickly, route them
to sales without friction, and convert them to pipeline at high
win-rates.

**Content:** bottom-of-funnel.
- Pricing pages, comparison pages, ROI calculators, demo CTAs
- Solution / use-case pages with clear next steps
- Sales enablement: case studies, customer reference videos
- Retargeting creative

**Channels:** Google Search (branded + non-branded), Bing, retargeting
across Meta/LinkedIn/display, review-site sponsorships (G2, Capterra,
TrustRadius), BDR/SDR outbound to in-market accounts, partner referrals
at decision stage.

**Metrics:**
- **Pipeline Created** by source (not MQLs, not leads — actual pipeline
  with declared high intent)
- **HIRO Pipeline** — High Intent, high win-rate (>25% from this cohort),
  the only pipeline number Walker considers worth optimizing against
- CAC payback by channel
- Win rate by source
- Sales cycle length by source

**Ownership:** demand-gen lead, RevOps, sales-aligned. This is where
attribution software actually works — the user took an action you
can trace.

**Budget share:** 20-40% of total marketing program spend.

---

## Pillar 3: Expand

**Audience:** existing customers.

**Job:** drive net revenue retention (>110% is the benchmark for healthy
SaaS), reduce churn, expand seats / modules / use-cases, generate
referrals and case studies that feed back into the Brand pillar.

**Content:**
- Customer onboarding journeys
- In-app education
- Customer community / user groups
- Case study production (which then becomes Brand fuel)
- Customer marketing — events, advocacy programs, reference programs
- Expansion plays: cross-sell sequences, upgrade triggers

**Channels:** in-product, email lifecycle, customer community, CSM
outbound, customer events.

**Metrics:**
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR) %
- Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) %
- Expansion ARR by cohort
- Referral pipeline generated
- Customer advocacy participation rate (NPS promoters who agree to
  case studies, references, podcast guest spots)

**Ownership:** typically a customer marketing function reporting either
into marketing or into the CCO. The trap: this gets handed to CS-only
with no marketing involvement, and the brand benefit of customer
storytelling is lost.

**Budget share:** 5-20% of marketing spend depending on maturity. At
mature SaaS this is the highest-ROI pillar because the customer base
is the largest leverage point.

## Why B→D→E Beats the Old Marketing Org

The old org: one "demand gen" team, one "field marketing" team, one
"product marketing" team, one "content" team — all reporting up under
a CMO who reports to the CRO. Every team is measured on MQLs or
contribution-to-pipeline through MQLs.

What goes wrong:
- Brand work loses out because it doesn't generate MQLs in-quarter
- Demand work over-invests in lead-gen channels (gated content,
  content syndication) because those are easiest to MQL-count
- Expand work is invisible — the marketing team doesn't even own it
- Sales blames marketing for low-quality MQLs (correctly)
- Marketing blames sales for not working the MQLs (incorrectly)
- The CFO sees MQL volume go up, revenue go flat, and assumes
  marketing isn't working

B→D→E rebuilds around the actual job. Brand is funded as its own
pillar with its own metrics — not bullied into MQL contribution.
Demand becomes ruthlessly efficient because it stops trying to also
do creation. Expand gets a real budget and accountability.

## Org Structure Implications

**Minimum viable B→D→E team (Series A-B, $1-10M ARR):**
- Founder runs Brand (LinkedIn presence, podcast, POV)
- 1 demand-gen lead runs Demand (paid search, retargeting, web,
  inbound routing)
- Customer success owns Expand for now, marketing supports with
  case study production

**Scale-stage B→D→E team (Series C-D, $20-100M ARR):**
- Head of Brand (or VP Marketing) + content producers, podcast
  producer, video editor, paid social distribution lead
- Head of Demand + paid search lead, ABM lead, RevOps partner
- Head of Customer Marketing + case study producer, community lead,
  advocacy program manager
- All three report to CMO. CMO reports to CEO (not CRO — that's a
  red flag in B→D→E thinking).

## Sales Alignment: The Pipeline Council

B→D→E only works if marketing and sales agree on the scoreboard. The
mechanism is the **Pipeline Council** — weekly forum, CEO + CMO + CRO +
head of sales + head of demand. Agenda:

1. Pipeline Created last week, by source (declared high-intent only).
2. HIRO Pipeline conversion rate by source.
3. Self-Reported Attribution data from inbound demos last week.
4. What's working — double down.
5. What's not — kill it.

No MQLs. No SQLs. No "we sent sales 200 leads" framing. The unit of
discussion is pipeline created with declared intent that converts
above the win-rate threshold.

This forum is the cultural fix that B→D→E requires. Without it the
old MQL-fight resumes inside the first quarter.

## How to Diagnose a B→D→E Gap

For any B2B firm, ask:
1. Can the marketing team name their Brand metrics separately from
   their Demand metrics? Most can't.
2. Does the firm have a podcast, YouTube channel, or executive
   LinkedIn presence with weekly cadence? If no — Brand pillar is
   missing.
3. Does the firm have a "How did you hear about us?" question on
   every demo form, with reporting reviewed weekly? If no — they
   have no Brand attribution data.
4. Does customer marketing have a budget line item separate from
   CS? If no — Expand pillar is missing.
5. Is there a weekly Pipeline Council with sales? If no — alignment
   is broken regardless of the model.

A "no" on three or more = missing operating model. The fix is to
declare B→D→E publicly, assign owners per pillar, set pillar-specific
metrics, and start the Pipeline Council before Monday.

## The Walker Bottom Line

You don't have a marketing problem. You have an operating-model
problem. Marketing in B2B has been collapsed into a single MQL-
optimizing function for two decades and the result is universal:
flat pipeline, rising CAC, sales-marketing dysfunction. B→D→E is
the org redesign. Demand-creation-vs-capture is the strategy.
Self-Reported Attribution is the measurement. Pipeline Council is
the cultural enforcement.

Pick all four. Half-implementing one of them doesn't work.
