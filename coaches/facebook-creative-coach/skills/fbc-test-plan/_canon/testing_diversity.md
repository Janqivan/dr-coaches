# Testing, creative diversity, volume, metrics and Meta's algorithm (post-Andromeda)
> Scope: how the practitioners decide what is winning, how they structure tests, how much creative to make, what "diversity" means to Meta's 2025–26 retrieval system, and which metrics they trust. ## 1. Core positions (ranked by mentions × specificity)

- **Spend is the winner signal; sort by spend, not hook rate or lowest CPA** — In an optimized account the ad Meta spends most on is "Meta's indicator that this is a winning ad" (Cottrell). Hott: "I just look at spend to determine whether or not something's working"; the goal is "the most scale that is efficient", not lowest CPA. Denney sorts by spend then cross-checks purchases; Cooper judges creative sources by spend share and trains his AI "brain" to prioritise spend over ROAS/CPA. Condition (Hott): holds when there is plenty of conversion data, sane media buying, and an optimization event that mirrors real business results. Counter (guest: Charles Tichenor): an ad capped at $500/day when you want $5K is not crushing. Who: Hott, Denney, Cottrell, Cooper (consensus). [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:RSa4wUuw_2k, YT:wRA2VgYIDVs, YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2026-04, 2026-07, 2025-01, 2025-04) · NOW/EVERGREEN · GEN · mentions 36

- **Don't turn off the top spender — the breakdown effect** — Meta optimizes at the level where budget is set (ad set, or campaign under CBO), not per ad; the ad clicked before purchase is often not the one that brought the buyer in. A 5-ROAS ad on low spend vs a 2-ROAS ad on high spend means Meta predicts more spend on the 5-ROAS ad wouldn't add incremental sales. Pausing worst-CPA ads shifts spend to ads predicted to be worse — "a race to the bottom" (Hott). Stories: an agency paused Hott's top "Banger" ad on 7-day CPA and account spend shriveled for weeks; Denney's team killed a top performer and the account "fell over" within days; Cooper says clients still do it in 2026. Exceptions: a 2-day-old ad that sucks (Hott); in a test that's only spent ~$1,000, turn off a poor top ad (Denney); if a top ad taking 30–50% of budget suddenly doubles CPA, check the ad's comments and the website first (Hott). Denney tested throttling spend toward better-CPP ads "multiple times" — they typically underperform. Who: Hott, Denney, Cooper, Cottrell (consensus). [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4, YT:RVRRGf746Bw, YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g, YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2026-04, 2025-04, 2025-01, 2025-01) · BOTH · GEN · mentions 38

- **Hook, hold, CTR, CPM, CPC are "storytelling"/"soft" metrics; only spend, results, CPA/ROAS call a winner** — Denney: primary KPIs show "if your campaign is working"; "everything else is going to tell you why." Cottrell: hook and hold are "soft metrics"; "Sometimes a 20% hook can perform better than a 70% hook" because relevance matters. Cooper: "Stop glorifying hook rates and hold rates." [quote src: YT:Be9YLcOory4] Hott: hook rate, CTR, CPC, CPM "almost completely useless" for knowing what works; ~90% of what he cares about is cost per conversion, ~10% whether it limits reach; hook rates between unrelated videos are meaningless. Heath: physio reaction ads had high hook, low watch time, great ROAS — "who cares about average watch time" [quote src: YT:z3LiJ0PbY-0]. Dissent: Denney (2022) found highest-hook ads also had highest back-end CVR at Thesis; guest Evan Lee (Motion) puts ~80% of analysis on hooks; guest Sophia Beauvoir (2026-06) says hook/hold matter more now because scroll-past signals fatigue and limits delivery. Who: Denney, Cottrell, Hott, Cooper, Heath (consensus). - **Post-Andromeda, near-identical ads collapse into one entity — diversity must be real** — Every ad gets a similarity score; same image with a new headline, a hook-text change, copy tweak or "a different vibe" is grouped as one asset/"one retrieval ticket" [quote src: YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms], and the other variants "won't get a fair crack" (Heath). Savannah's threshold: Meta treats creatives as distinct when the visual differs in the first 3–5 seconds; text on top alone doesn't. Softener: Heath (2026-02) says Meta sometimes (not always) merges ads differing only in the opening — keep hook testing via the testing tool. - **Creative is the targeting; go broad** — Meta reads the creative (transcript, visuals, hook, language, creator, setting) on upload to choose the seed audience before your settings. - **Volume vs quality: "guided volume" — better beats more, diversity beats raw count** — Cottrell (2026): "Andromeda doesn't reward more ads", it "rewards intentional variety"; "better pretty much always beats more" [quote src: YT:qZhOnITrN0Q]; more ads when nothing works is "a more expensive version of the same problem". Hott: "Blind volume is useless. You need guided volume." — but "it's all random" is "a lie". Levinger: no magic number; 25/week at quality standard is great; volume only works after strategy exists. Cooper: quality × quantity × diversity; friends went $10M→$50M with 10x creative helping 5x ARR in 18 months. Denney rejects "arbitrarily putting out like 150 ads" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]; 2025 buzz term was "creative volume". Heath: one campaign alone starves new ads, leaving no bank of proven ads when winners fatigue; split up to ~80% to scaling when results are good. ## 2. Frameworks and methods

### Andromeda → GEM → Lattice (Denney)
- When they use it: explaining why account structure matters less and diversity matters more (2025-12).
- Steps: 1. Andromeda (launched December 2024) = retrieval only, narrows millions of ads to a pool per user. 2. GEM (generative ads recommendation model) narrows further — a Meta employee's "super brain". 3. Lattice = library of Meta's AI models where GEM lives; makes the final ad-to-user match. 4. (2026-06) Meta calls Andromeda "more of a persona-based algorithm" [quote src: YT:-E9sjb2msjo] — ask of every creative "who are we targeting with this?" [quote src: YT:Lhl4o7yyuDQ]
- Related: Cottrell — Meta used to ask "who should I show this ad to" [quote src: YT:CPWoW09WCCo], now "which version of this ad should I show to this person" [quote src: YT:CPWoW09WCCo]; AI views each asset, sends it to a test pool, expands only if they're interested (2026-10). Guest Danil Saliukov: a broad bucket (US males 35–45) is now split into persona auctions (truck driver vs Wall Street office manager).
- Sources: [YT:x1b4h42HTG8] (2025-12), [YT:-E9sjb2msjo] (2026-06), [YT:CPWoW09WCCo] (2026-02), [YT:RExPkxyRB6o] (2026-10), [YT:m8dbcF407H8] (2026-03)

### "Two doors, not one" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — the FYP prediction layer (Levinger)
- When: briefing creative for 2026 feeds.
- Steps: 1. Door one: the same prediction layer as TikTok's FYP asks whether this content will hit this person at this moment (e.g. Saturday 11 a.m.). 2. Only then door two: the auction vs competitors. 3. The layer reads "the big four" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] micro-behaviors — hover, swipe speed, rewatch, hesitation — plus day and hour. 4. So ask "does it behave like content people respond to?" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] — content mechanism first, ad mechanism second.
- Their example: her feed serves holiday-cookie baking only on Saturday mornings.
- Sources: [YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] (2025-12), [YT:cJlBabiKX9k] (2026-04)

### Primary vs storytelling metrics + column setup (Denney)
- When: every performance review.
- Steps: 1. Far-left primary KPIs: amount spent, purchases/results (leads/CPL for lead gen), cost per result, ROAS — the only winner-callers. 2. Reach block: frequency, reach, impressions, cost per reach, CPM. 3. Storytelling: unique outbound CTR ("the purest form of clickthrough rate" [quote src: YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]), cost per link click, hook rate, hold rate, average play time, post shares. 4. Ask only two questions: what is working (spend + purchases) and why (comparison). 5. Analyze first, then tweak.
- Variants: Cottrell's always-on columns lead with spend, purchases, CPA, CPM, ROAS, thumb-stop, hold, outbound CTR, click-to-purchase. Hott opens consults with "why is your column view so stupid?" [quote src: YT:2jVcLu3Earg]; adds cost per share in every account, cost per 3-second view and cost per ThruPlay.
- Sources: [YT:CCsty8R0UaA] (2025-01), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:VtEoAOfQsCU] (2025-05), [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01)

### Three testing setups by budget (Denney)
- When: choosing structure; Testing at $3M/month is vastly different.
- Steps: 1. Lean (≤~$5–15K/mo): one Advantage+ Shopping campaign that is all testing; launch ~10 creatives, add 1–2/week. 2. Growth (~$1,000/day, $30–50K/mo): lean ASC becomes scaling; add a manual testing campaign, each concept its own ad set, 3–6 creatives, $100–200/ad set/day, much bigger messaging swings. 3. Scale (>$1M/mo): same, 10–20+ tests/week, often cost caps, ABO, $200/day, ~7 days or ~$1,000 spent; bigger-swing creative types (celebrities, high-production founder ad if lo-fi founder worked).
- Note: "nine times out of 10 you likely have a creative problem" [quote src: YT:7knQyPYLmfo].
- Sources: [YT:7knQyPYLmfo] (2025-03) · DTC

### Testing loop and scaling steps (Denney, Cooper, Heath)
- Steps (Denney): 1. Wait ≥3 days. 2. Ad level first: kill any ad at ~2x account average CPA. 3. No winners after 5–7 days → turn the ad set off. 4. Winner → scale the test ad set ~20% every three days (sometimes 50%+), 2–3 times, then duplicate into the core/ASC; never turn the test ad set off after duplicating.
- Cooper: ad set daily budget = 50 × target CPA / 7 (CPA $40 → $286/day); launch Monday/Tuesday, check every 3–4 days: kill, scale to ASC, or leave.
- Heath (2026-07, via Claude): sales objective, $30/day, 5 days, broad, 5 ads; after 5–7 days pick the best, generate 5 more (same actor, different scripts).
- Sources: [YT:KFFcno59vQ8] (2021-09), [YT:CmkWzdgl6iI] (2024-02), [YT:M-zOOiG5GLw] (2026-07), [YT:TpZrPY6jTE8] (2026-07)

### Hott's test campaign
- Steps: 1. One testing campaign (ABO or CBO). 2. Each batch its own ad set with one variable (only creative changes). 3. 4–6 ads per ad set. 4. Exclude customers, recent visitors, 1/7-day engagers so creative learns cold. 5. Launch best-known ads first as the "goal cost to beat" [quote src: YT:XIn0vQdErsM]. 6. Optional cost cap at ~2x expected CPA only to stop runaways; periodically rerun spend-restricted ads with a higher cap. 7. ASC does retargeting and other prospecting — "with ASC, the rules are different" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (50+ ads fine).
- Sources: [YT:Oj-KGCw7K1g] (2025-01), [YT:XIn0vQdErsM] (2023-08), [YT:R0k0rVzv_yQ] (2024-08), [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04)

### Read metric combinations to locate the failure (Cottrell, Denney, Heath, Savannah, Levinger)
- Order (Cottrell): CPA → CTR → hook rate → hold rate. Guest Evan Lee: spending? → thumb-stop → hold → outbound CTR → conversion.
- Combos: low hook + strong hold/CTR → new hook; great hold, poor hook → change hook; 50%+ hook but poor CTR/click-to-purchase → good at educating, weak at selling → stronger CTA (Cottrell). Low hook + low hold = not resonating; high hook/hold, low CTR = wrong audience (Denney). Good hook + poor CPA = body fails; low hook + great CPA = strong body (Heath). Strong hook and CTR, no conversions → look beyond the ad: LP speed, checkout, offer, price (Savannah). Good CTR and hold, no sales → "you're not asking for a sale" [quote src: YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]; UGC drop at ~50% = "you told people about the video way too soon" [quote src: YT:sBS1vy-5Be0] (Levinger).
- Sources: [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:-q2lqfNysUs] (2025-11), [YT:bAo6SICFyIE] (2026-03), [YT:KJ3Q5bha6WA] (2024-03), [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo] (2026-04)

### Four-step creative analysis (Denney) and three-step audit (Levinger)
- Denney: 1. Metrics (least important). 2. Content — messaging, imagery, talent, setting, pacing; categorize by sub-format (us-vs-them, features point-out, founder, statistic). 3. Comparison — vs top performers, competitors, platform trends. 4. Feedback beyond comments. Bad analysis = Version B won with 1.2 ROAS… so make more variants of B.
- Levinger: 1. Top 10 ads of the last 12 months by efficiency, not spend ("This is not the same list" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4]). 2. Per ad: closest of five formats, psychological mechanism, customer mental state. 3. Gaps become briefs — brands typically have "about four ads that all do the same psychological thing" [quote src: YT:6ZBccBr5AV4].
- Cottrell audit: read the account before research, make no changes, watch the top ~5 ads, note commonalities (e.g. several at 34% hook; product in a car → highest hook rate).
- Sources: [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01), [YT:yNTlVKvuX0o] (2025-03), [YT:6ZBccBr5AV4] (2026-05), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07)

### Creative diversity levels and the squint test (Denney)
- Levels 0–5 (2025): L0 statics vs video; L1 format; L2 awareness; L3 desire/motivation; L4 audience/use-case personas; L5 true creative diversity. Earlier (2024): beginner / intermediate (sub-formats) / advanced (creator demographics, celebrities, awareness levels, 50+ tests/month).
- Squint test: put top 10–20 creatives in a deck, squint for repeated colors, visuals, formats, UGC/static over-reliance.
- Cottrell's definition: diversity = format + messaging + persona; UGC after UGC "is not creative diversity" [quote src: YT:RSa4wUuw_2k]. Cooper: 90-day diversity chart by format, net-new vs iteration, product, LP, creator vs brand page.
- Sources: [YT:U7T3_-NnHHg] (2024-11), [YT:ZydGeio1DGQ] (2025-12), [YT:RSa4wUuw_2k] (2026-07), [YT:EOZp34uDIc4] (2025-04)

### Persona spend vs voice audit (Cooper)
- Steps: 1. Tag every ad by persona with a hypothesis column. 2. Compare each persona's share of spend vs share of customer voice (surveys, reviews, comments), e.g. 30% of voice but 18% of spend. 3. Ask: too few ads, failed ads, or over-weighted super fans? 4. Allocate the next batch top-down (e.g. 45/40/15). 5. Monthly sprint review, weekly sprints, recurring VOC re-rank.
- Example: a persona with 50% of reviews but 10% of spend got many more ads — now "three of the top five ads" [quote src: YT:79doYBhLLFU].
- Sources: [YT:79doYBhLLFU] (2026-06), [YT:_9f0rTKKIOY] (2025-01) · GEN

### True iterations and when to iterate (Denney; Levinger dissent)
- Denney: true iteration = (1) swap the hook on a winner, (2) put a winning hook on an under-spent creative, (3) swap messaging only — only on "Banger" winners; 9 of 10 times don't iterate losers. ~90% net-new / ~10% iterations. "Iteration paralysis": swapping hooks/headlines until everything looks the same — brands at a plateau iterated themselves to oblivion.
- Savannah: ~80% new / 20% iterative. Small iterations still pay only on a scaling winner showing fatigue (Hott; guest Viti: when an ad is "almost there").
- Levinger: "I don't understand the concept of iterating on something that's producing cash" [quote src: YT:cJlBabiKX9k]; "That's a tactic. It's not a strategy." [quote src: YT:cJlBabiKX9k] Her iteration = same concept, new vehicle; raise the budget on a winner instead of trying to beat it (Babe Ruth analogy).
- Sources: [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs] (2025-01), [YT:U7T3_-NnHHg] (2024-11), [YT:cJlBabiKX9k] (2026-04), [YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms] (2026-06)

### TEAP funnel phases and goal-weighted mix (Levinger)
- Steps: 1. One campaign (ideally per avatar) with four ad sets: trigger, exploration, evaluation, purchase; trigger optimizes for link click/traffic, rest for conversion. 2. Monthly goal sets the mix: TOF eyeballs → trigger formats (short video, pattern interrupts); more sales → evaluation/purchase formats with proof, "not really a place for short hooks" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]; cost reduction → formats your team makes at high quality ("Quality is a cost reducer" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU]). 3. Define per-ad success metrics before launch — "a test you can't read is always going to tell you nothing" [quote src: YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o]. 4. Before killing a message, check you didn't build "an exploration ad" [quote src: YT:pV0tM1gSJiU] and run it as a purchase ad.
- Later view (2026-05): "trigger and exploration should be outside of the ad account" [quote src: YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0] — move them to organic.
- Sources: [YT:gRQ1y0fLpWY] (2026-02), [YT:OiKpEOXY4zc] (2026-07), [YT:vrBYS4-Gr4o] (2026-06), [YT:ZMXFvjYZoE0] (2026-05)

### Attribution triangulation (Hott)
- Steps: 1. Define existing-customer and engaged audiences at account level; use the audience-segments breakdown (new/engaged/existing). 2. Compare windows (1d view, 1d click, 7d click, incremental). 3. Optimize to 7-day click (or + 1-day engaged view); almost never 1-day view. 4. Build a multiplier vs real results (reported $104K ≈ actual $150K). 5. Triangulate platform, third-party, post-purchase survey — no single source of truth. 6. Since late 2025/2026, incremental attribution (now usable with cost caps) is his default for most advertisers.
- Sources: [YT:u4B0GGBBDXY] (2025-05), [YT:RVRRGf746Bw] (2025-04), [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4] (2026-04)

## 5. Numbers and benchmarks

**Volume by spend**
- Cottrell (2026-08, DTC net-new/month): $10–20k → 3–4; $20–50k → 5–6 ("The focus here is quality. ## 6. What changed (2025 → 2026) and where they disagree

**What changed**
- **Hook-swap iterations stopped working.** Pre-Andromeda, 4 new hooks on a winner "almost guaranteed" a new winner (guest Sophia Beauvoir); now Meta merges them. Cottrell (2026-02): "simply won't be a thing this year"; Denney (2025-12): statics now go into one ad set 1–2x/month, each video test aims for the widest visual range. [YT:CVWHkhUA5Ms, YT:CPWoW09WCCo, YT:ZydGeio1DGQ]
- **Diversity went from nice-to-have to required.** Cottrell (2026-02): he preached it for years; only now have the algorithms caught up. Denney: buzz terms 2023 creative strategy → 2024 creative diversity → 2025 creative volume; late 2025 = "the age of creative strategy after Andromeda". [YT:CPWoW09WCCo, YT:ZydGeio1DGQ]
- **Media buying automated; creative strategy is the lever.** Cottrell (2025-11): "Media buying is completely automated at this point." Denney (2025-12): interests, lookalikes, prospecting-vs-retargeting splits "pretty much useless". Hott keeps a role for exclusions and attribution rules — "the system will play by the rules that you allow it to" [quote src: YT:R0k0rVzv_yQ]. [YT:-q2lqfNysUs, YT:x1b4h42HTG8, YT:u4B0GGBBDXY]
- **Creators/partnership ads.** Denney (2026-05/06): Meta's summit said selling is done by creators; AI statics will "struggle" without real creators "in the coming months". [YT:9UBSGSS2Yuo, YT:eFyvp0mu7UQ]
- **Attribution.** From end of March 2026 click attribution counts link clicks only (Hott) — expect clicky, comment-heavy, tiny-text ads to lose spend; incremental attribution became usable with cost caps (December, unannounced) and is now Hott's default. [YT:mpj0A4Prxu4 (2026-04)]
- **Settings trap.** Levinger (early April 2026): Meta auto-selected "Reach new and existing customers" and it "trashed everybody's performance" for brands excluding purchasers — check it. [YT:RCrvd8pTUzo]
- **Testing structure.** Seed moved from forced-spend "sandbox testing" to ASC creative sprints (guest Tiffany); Hott: forcing spend to every ad in 2026 "probably unnecessary"; Heath (2026-02/03) now uses Meta's creative testing tool for fine detail. [YT:Hh4zkUdRVzg, YT:onFwSud9C2Y]
- **Metric weight shifted.** Denney (2025-01) weights hook/hold/CTR "less and less" [quote src: YT:RVRRGf746Bw] as Meta automates; CTR is lower on broad delivery, so low CTR can mean the right people. Hott used to compare hook rates across ads; now says look at spend. [YT:wRA2VgYIDVs]
- **Faster burn.** Cottrell (2026-08): ads fatigue "a bit faster" [quote src: YT:KH7FZfCUFn4] post-Andromeda; guest Danil Saliukov: persona auctions burn creative faster. Counter: Hott wants ads that last 2–5 years and calls "feed the beast" [quote src: YT:sdEV9rZ70BY] fatigue overstated. [YT:nUTzR9ilTts, YT:m8dbcF407H8, YT:6jvIXIjieyA]
