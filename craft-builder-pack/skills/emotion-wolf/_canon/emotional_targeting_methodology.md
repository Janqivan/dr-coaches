# The Emotional Targeting Methodology — Full Framework

Talia Wolf's central system. Built across 15 years of CRO work at Conversioner
and GetUplift. The premise: every buying decision is emotional, and the brands
that win are the ones who do the research to find the right emotion and let the
customer write the copy. Everything else — headlines, hero images, CTAs, ads,
emails, onboarding — is downstream of that research.

The methodology is a 3-step loop: **Research → Hypothesis → Test**. Repeat
forever. The research never stops, because the customer never stops changing.

This document is the operator's playbook for running the loop end-to-end.

---

## WHY THE METHODOLOGY EXISTS

Most CRO is element-level optimization. Change the button color. Shorten the
form. Move the hero image. The operator sees a 2% lift, calls it a win, and
moves to the next element. Twelve months later they have made the page slightly
faster, slightly cleaner, slightly more "best practice" — and conversion has
moved nowhere meaningful.

This is the deadly hamster wheel of optimization. The reason it doesn't work is
that the operator does not actually know why people buy. They are testing
random variables against an unknown function.

Emotional targeting fixes the root cause. Instead of testing "what color
button," it asks: what is the customer feeling right now, what do they want to
feel instead, and what would I have to say to bridge that gap. Now every test
has a hypothesis tied to a real emotional driver. Tests move the needle by
10-100% lifts, not 2%, because they are addressing the actual reason people are
or are not converting.

## STEP 1 — RESEARCH (the most important step, almost always skipped)

Research is where 80% of the leverage lives. Skip it and the rest of the loop
is theater. The research has six methods, used in combination.

### 1.1 — Customer Interviews

The gold standard. Five to ten interviews, 30-45 minutes each, recorded with
permission, transcribed. Talk to existing paying customers, not prospects.
You want the people who already bought, because they can tell you what
actually pushed them across the line.

The interview is not a feature interview. It is an emotional archaeology dig.
Structured around the buying journey, with emphasis on the emotional state at
each stage.

**Interview question stack (use 8-12 of these per call):**
1. Take me back to the moment you first realized you needed [solution]. Where
   were you, what triggered it, what were you feeling?
2. What was the situation in your life/business at that moment? What was at
   stake?
3. Before you found us, what had you already tried? Why did those not work?
4. When you tried other things and they didn't work, how did that make you
   feel about yourself?
5. What were you most worried about when looking for a solution?
6. What almost stopped you from buying from us?
7. Walk me through the moment you decided to buy. What tipped you over?
8. What did you expect would be different after you bought?
9. What IS different now? How do you feel now compared to before?
10. How would your life/business be different if this hadn't worked?
11. If a friend was in your old situation, what would you tell them?
12. What words would you use to describe us to that friend?
13. What's the one thing you wish we had said on our website that we didn't?

The last three are gold. Q11-13 produce the exact phrasing you put on the
landing page. Customers literally write the copy.

### 1.2 — Visitor Surveys (on-page, pre-exit)

A two-question survey that fires when a visitor is about to leave or has been
on the page 30+ seconds without converting. Questions:
1. What brought you to our site today?
2. What's stopping you from [signing up / buying / booking] right now?

This catches the emotional friction in real time, from people who did NOT
convert. That is harder data than post-purchase surveys, because converters
have a survivorship bias.

### 1.3 — Customer Surveys (post-purchase)

Sent 7-14 days after purchase. Questions:
1. Why did you decide to buy?
2. What were you trying to achieve / solve / avoid?
3. What was your biggest hesitation before buying?
4. What convinced you to buy from us specifically?
5. How would you describe us in one sentence to a colleague?
6. How do you feel now that you've solved [problem]?
7. What was the situation that made you start looking for a solution?

Aim for 40-100 responses. Group answers by emotion, not by demographic.

### 1.4 — Review Mining

Mine your own reviews and your competitors' reviews. Sources: G2, Capterra,
Trustpilot, Google Reviews, App Store, Reddit threads, niche forums. For
immigration: Avvo, Google Reviews, immigration subreddits, Facebook groups
for visa applicants. For franchise: franchise forums, FranchiseTimes, Reddit
r/Franchises, Indeed reviews of the franchise. For vocational: school review
sites, Reddit r/Welding / r/HVAC / r/Trades, Trustpilot.

Tag every review by the emotion it expresses. Build a frequency map. The top
3 emotions are your targets. The exact phrases are your headlines.

### 1.5 — Social Listening

Reddit, Facebook groups, Twitter/X, niche forums. What are people complaining
about? What are they celebrating? What language do they use when nobody is
selling them anything? This is unfiltered voice-of-customer. It is the rawest
emotional data you can get.

### 1.6 — Emotional Competitor Analysis

Not a feature competitor analysis. An EMOTIONAL one. For each top-3
competitor, answer:
- What emotion is their homepage selling?
- What is the implied current-pain state?
- What is the implied desired-outcome state?
- What trust signals do they use?
- Where is the white space — what emotion is NOBODY in this market selling?

The white space is your opportunity. If five immigration lawyers all sell
fear ("don't get deported"), the sixth who sells dignity and family reunion
will own the segment.

## STEP 2 — HYPOTHESIS (mapping emotion to page elements)

Research gives you raw data. The hypothesis turns it into a testable claim.

### 2.1 — The Hypothesis Statement Format

"For [audience segment], the current page fails because it speaks to [wrong
emotion / no emotion / feature list]. If we instead address [current-pain
emotion] and bridge to [desired-outcome emotion] using [customer's exact
language], we will lift [primary KPI] by [estimated lift %], because
[research evidence]."

This format forces every test to be tied to research. No more "let's try a
green button."

### 2.2 — Mapping Emotion to Page Elements

Every element on the page carries an emotional load. The mapping looks like:

| Element | Emotional Job |
|---|---|
| Hero headline | Name the current-pain emotion in customer language. Make them feel "this is for me, they get it." |
| Sub-headline | Name the desired-outcome emotion. Make them feel "this is where I want to be." |
| Hero image / video | Visual proof of the desired-outcome emotional state. Faces, situations, body language that matches the after-state. |
| Social proof above the fold | Trust trigger. Quick. Logos, star rating, customer count. |
| First body section | Acknowledge the deeper pain. Show you understand the situation beyond the surface. |
| Second body section | The bridge. How does the product/service produce the desired outcome? Stay in feelings, not features. |
| Testimonials | Customer-written copy. Pick testimonials where customers describe the BEFORE and AFTER emotional state, not feature lists. |
| Risk reversal | Address the deepest fear of buying. Guarantee, free trial, transparent pricing, no-contract. |
| CTA copy | Outcome-led, not action-led. "Get my visa approved" beats "Submit form." "Start my new career" beats "Apply now." |
| Footer trust | Authority, accreditations, press, awards. Final social-image reassurance. |

### 2.3 — Pain-Led vs Outcome-Led Decision

A core hypothesis question: should the page lead with the current pain, or
with the desired outcome?

- **Lead with desired outcome** for: B2B SaaS, professional services where
  buyers want to feel smart, franchise resales (self-image), most lifestyle
  purchases, transformation-led verticals.
- **Lead with pain acknowledgement** for: immigration (fear is real and must
  be validated before hope), medical, debt, divorce, anything where the
  buyer is in a crisis state and feeling unseen.
- **Test both** when unsure. Run a 48-hour ad test before touching the page.
  Two ads, one pain-led, one outcome-led, same audience, same budget. Whichever
  has the lower CPL wins the hypothesis for the page.

### 2.4 — The Customer-Written Copy Rule

The copy on the page is not written by the marketer. It is assembled from the
exact phrasing customers used in interviews, surveys, and reviews. The
marketer's job is curation, not composition.

If the operator finds themselves writing original copy, they are guessing.
Send them back to the research transcripts. The line they need is in there.

## STEP 3 — TEST (what to A/B test that actually moves conversion)

Now you test. The test is a referee between hypotheses, not a fishing
expedition for lifts.

### 3.1 — Test Hierarchy (from highest to lowest leverage)

1. **Audience / offer** — wrong audience or wrong offer = no test fixes it.
   Verify these are right before touching the page.
2. **Hero section (headline, sub, image)** — 60% of the emotional contract is
   here. Highest-leverage element-level test.
3. **First body section + testimonials** — emotional acknowledgement +
   transformation proof.
4. **CTA copy** — outcome-led vs action-led. Often 10-20% lift just from this.
5. **Page structure / order** — does the page tell the emotional story in the
   right sequence?
6. **Risk reversal / guarantee placement** — addresses the deepest fear.

Element-level tests below this line (button color, form field count, font
size) are hamster wheel territory unless you have a research-backed
hypothesis that points to them.

### 3.2 — Test Design Rules

- One hypothesis per test. Not "change everything and see what happens."
- Run to statistical significance. 95% confidence, minimum 1,000 conversions
  per variant for B2B, 250+ for B2C.
- Track segments, not just averages. The winner often loses in one segment
  and wins in another. That tells you who to target.
- Document the LEARNING, not just the WINNER. Even losing tests teach you
  about your audience's emotion.

### 3.3 — Test Cadence

For most operators: one well-designed test per month beats four bad tests
per week. Quality of hypothesis > quantity of tests.

### 3.4 — When to Test in Ads First (the 48-hour shortcut)

Before redesigning a landing page, test the hypothesis in ad copy. Two ads,
pain-led vs outcome-led, same image, same audience, same $50-200 budget per
ad. 48 hours of data tells you which emotional direction has pull. Then build
the page around the winner. This saves weeks of redesign work.

---

## THE LOOP

Research → Hypothesis → Test → back to Research (because the test taught you
something new). This is not a one-time project. It is the operating system of
a CRO program. Most operators do it once and stop. The ones who do it
continuously compound 10-100x over 24-36 months.

## DR APPLICATION — How to Use This for Ivan's Three Verticals

**Immigration law** — Interview 10 signed clients per firm. Ask Q1-Q13. The
emotional gold is in Q4 ("when other things didn't work, how did that make
you feel about yourself") — most applicants feel powerless, ashamed, scared
of being judged. The desired outcome is dignity + family safety + control over
their life. Build the page around that, not "we file your I-130 form
correctly."

**Franchise resales** — Buyers are escaping something (corporate, a boss, a
ceiling). Self-image trigger dominates. Interview 5 successful franchisees.
The desired-outcome language is almost always "be my own boss" + "build
something for my family" + "prove I could do it." Pages that lead with
"proven system, $X EBITDA, 12-month ramp" are feature-led. Pages that lead
with "the day you stop answering to a boss" are emotion-led.

**Vocational training** — Students are buying a new identity. Transformation
trigger + belonging trigger. Interview recent graduates 6-12 months post-
program. The before-state is often "stuck in retail/warehouse/dead-end."
The after-state is "I'm a [welder/electrician/HVAC tech]." That identity
shift IS the product. Sell the identity, not the curriculum.

## COMMON MISTAKES

- Skipping research because "we already know our customer." You don't. Do the
  10 interviews anyway. You will be wrong about at least 3 things.
- Testing without a hypothesis. This is the hamster wheel.
- Writing original copy instead of mining customer language. Your job is
  curation, not composition.
- Defaulting to fear-based emotion because it's loud. Fear only works in
  crisis verticals. For most B2B and considered purchases, self-image and
  social image are stronger and more durable.
- Stopping the loop after one cycle. The research is continuous.
