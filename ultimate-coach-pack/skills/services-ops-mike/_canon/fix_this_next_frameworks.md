## What Fix This Next is for

Fix This Next is the DIAGNOSTIC LAYER of Mike's Services Operating System. It is the entry point. Before opening Profit First (cash), Clockwork (owner), or Pumpkin Plan (clients), the operator runs Fix This Next to determine WHICH of those books to open at all.

The single insight that makes the book work: **operators almost always try to fix the level ABOVE their actual floor.** They want to talk about scaling delivery (Order) while their margins are negative (Profit). They want to talk about purpose and legacy while their leads have dried up (Sales). The wrong fix at the wrong level is the most common cause of stalled growth — not lack of effort, not lack of strategy, just diagnosis failure.

Fix This Next is the operator's version of medical triage. Stabilize the lowest bleeding level first.

## Framework 1 — The Business Hierarchy of Needs (BHN)

**The structure:** five levels, stacked. Modeled deliberately and loosely on Maslow. Lower levels are foundational. Higher levels are aspirational. You don't ascend the stack until the level below is solid.

**The five levels (bottom up):**

### Level 1 — SALES (the foundation)
The question: *is cash actually coming in the door?* Without inflow, nothing else matters. Sales is the breathing apparatus of the business. Health checks at this level:
- Lead generation is consistent and predictable.
- Conversion rate is known and stable.
- Cash inflow covers fixed costs at minimum.
- Demand exists for the offer (not "we just need to market more").
- The business serves a specific customer with a specific problem.

### Level 2 — PROFIT (cash retention)
The question: *is cash being kept?* Sales without profit is theatre. A business with revenue but no profit is bleeding while looking healthy. Health checks:
- Profit is allocated systematically (Profit First).
- Owner is paid a market salary, not "whatever's left."
- Debt is being serviced or eliminated.
- Reserves exist to absorb shock.
- Margins are improving, not eroding.

### Level 3 — ORDER (organizational health)
The question: *does the business run without the owner being the daily hero?* If Sales and Profit are stable but the owner is still firefighting, the business has a structural defect. Health checks:
- Roles are documented and accountable.
- Processes survive without the owner.
- The team can decide and act without permission gates.
- The owner can take time off without revenue impact.
- The Queen Bee Role is protected.

### Level 4 — IMPACT (client transformation)
The question: *are clients getting transformation, not just transaction?* Health checks:
- Client outcomes are measured, not just deliverables shipped.
- Repeat purchase and referral are happening organically.
- The team is mission-aligned, not just task-aligned.
- The business says "no" to bad-fit clients.
- Reputation is compounding (case studies, testimonials, recognition).

### Level 5 — LEGACY (permanence)
The question: *does the business outlast and outgrow the owner?* Health checks:
- The brand has identity independent of the founder.
- Leadership succession is named.
- The business is sellable (a buyer would want it).
- Adaptability is built in (the business can pivot).
- Mission is institutionalized, not founder-bound.

**The hard rule Mike enforces:** find the LOWEST level with unmet needs. That's the floor. Fix at the floor. Do not ascend until the floor is solid.

## Framework 2 — The Vital Need (diagnosing the single fix)

**What it is:** within the lowest broken level, there are multiple possible problems. The Vital Need is the SINGLE problem at that level that, if fixed, will unblock the level. Not all problems at a level are equal weight. One is the keystone.

**Why the "single fix" discipline matters:**
- Operators try to fix five things at once and finish none.
- Resources are scarce; concentration beats distribution.
- The Vital Need is usually obvious once correctly identified — but it's masked by louder, lower-leverage problems.

**The Vital Need test:**
1. Within the lowest broken level, list every visible problem.
2. For each: ask "if this were resolved, would the level become healthy?"
3. The problem that returns the strongest yes is the Vital Need.
4. Everything else goes on a backlog. Address it later.

**Operator failure mode:** treating the loudest problem as the Vital Need. Loud problems are usually symptoms, not roots. The Vital Need is upstream of the noise.

## Framework 3 — The OMEN Cycle (the implementation loop)

**What it is:** Mike's four-step protocol for actually solving the Vital Need once identified. Treats the fix as a hypothesis to be tested, not a project to be completed.

**The four steps:**

1. **O — Objective** (the destination)
   State the outcome. Quantified. Time-bound. Tied to a level of the Hierarchy.
   Example: "Increase signed-retainer conversion from 18% to 30% within 90 days" — Sales-level objective.

2. **M — Measurement** (the leading indicator)
   What number, observed weekly, will tell you whether the fix is working before the outcome arrives? Not the same as the objective.
   Example: "Track number of qualified consultations booked per week" — leading indicator for the conversion objective.

3. **E — Evaluation** (the cadence)
   When do you check the measurement? Weekly is Mike's default. Set the meeting. Set the metric review. Set the standard for "on track" vs "off track."

4. **N — Nurture** (the response)
   If the leading indicator is off track, do what? Course-correct, not abandon. The Vital Need was correctly diagnosed; the implementation is what's failing. Adjust the implementation, not the diagnosis.

**The cycle:** O → M → E → N → repeat for the next Vital Need, on a 90-day rhythm.

## Framework 4 — Why Operators Fix the Wrong Level

**Mike's diagnostic taxonomy of the three most common diagnosis failures:**

### Failure 1: Ascending too fast
The operator gets a taste of Level-2 (Profit) success and immediately tries to operate at Level-4 (Impact). They start hiring for purpose-aligned culture before they've solved Order. The business stalls because it's been re-platformed at a level it can't yet support.

### Failure 2: Loud-problem fixation
The operator fixes the problem that's screaming in their face. The screaming problem is often Order-level (a team conflict, a delivery miss) — but the actual broken level is Profit (margins are negative; the team is overstretched because the business can't afford enough people). Fixing the screaming Order problem doesn't help. The Profit fix would.

### Failure 3: Vanity-level fixation
The operator wants to talk about Impact and Legacy because those are the satisfying levels. They are also the LAST levels to solve. Operating from vanity-level when the floor is broken creates a hollow business — purposeful messaging, broken cash flow.

**Mike's reframe:** the right diagnosis FEELS unglamorous. If your prescription for yourself sounds like "I need to find my deeper purpose and articulate my mission," check whether your floor is actually Sales or Profit. The unsexy diagnosis is usually the correct one.

## Framework 5 — The Five Levels, Asymmetric Energy Allocation

**What it is:** Mike's framing for how much of the business's total energy should sit at each level at any given time.

**The healthy allocation pattern:**
- Most energy concentrated at the LOWEST broken level until it's healthy.
- A maintenance trickle at every level above (so they don't degrade).
- No energy allocated to levels above the broken one (until the broken one is fixed).

**The unhealthy patterns Mike flags:**
- Energy evenly distributed across all five levels → no level gets the concentration needed to solve.
- Energy concentrated at the level the founder *enjoys* (often Impact or Legacy) while a lower level decays → eventual collapse.
- Energy concentrated at the level the founder *fears* (often Order, because it requires people decisions) while Sales or Profit are the actual broken levels → wasted effort.

**The discipline:** the BHN tells you where to put the energy. The OMEN tells you how. Don't allocate by preference. Allocate by diagnosis.

## Framework 6 — The 25-Question Diagnostic

**What it is:** the assessment instrument Mike published in *Fix This Next* and on fixthisnext.com. Five questions per level, twenty-five total. Each is yes/no. The level with the most "no" answers (or the lowest with ANY "no" answers, depending on severity) is the floor.

**How the five questions per level work:**
- They are sequenced. The first question at each level is the most foundational; the fifth is the most advanced.
- Two or more "no" answers at a level indicates the level is unhealthy.
- The lowest level with two or more "no"s is the diagnostic floor.

**Why Mike built it as 25 questions rather than asking for an overall score:**
- Operators self-report too optimistically on overall health.
- Binary yes/no on specific evidence is harder to fake than "rate your sales on a scale of 1-10."
- The granularity reveals which Vital Need at the level is closest to the surface.

**Operator use:**
1. Take the diagnostic in 20-30 minutes.
2. Score honestly (no's are good — they're information, not judgment).
3. Identify the lowest level with 2+ no's.
4. From those no's, name the Vital Need.
5. Run OMEN on the Vital Need.
6. Re-diagnose in 90 days.

## Framework 7 — The 90-Day Re-Diagnosis Rhythm

**What it is:** Fix This Next is not a one-time exercise. It's a quarterly ritual.

**The rhythm:**
- Diagnose at the start of each quarter.
- Pick one Vital Need at the diagnosed floor.
- Run OMEN for 90 days.
- Re-diagnose at quarter end.
- The floor may have moved up (Sales is solid now, Profit is the new floor). Or it may have moved DOWN (something at a lower level decayed because attention shifted).
- Either way, re-allocate energy to the new floor.

**Why 90 days:**
- Too short and the OMEN cycle hasn't had time to produce data.
- Too long and the business has drifted before the next diagnosis catches it.
- 90 days aligns to the quarterly Profit First distribution cadence, the quarterly Rocks cadence in EOS, and most natural review rhythms.

**The compound effect Mike claims:** over 4-8 quarters of disciplined diagnosis and Vital-Need fixing, the business graduates through the hierarchy. Most operators never finish Level 2 (Profit) because they keep skipping back up to vanity levels.

## Framework 8 — The Three Diagnoses for Three Stages

**Mike's stage-overlay for the BHN:**

**Stage 1 — Startup (pre-product-market-fit / pre-revenue stability)**
- Almost always at Sales floor.
- Vital Need is typically demand validation or repeatable customer acquisition.
- Profit, Order, Impact, Legacy are aspirational — leave them.

**Stage 2 — Scaling (revenue real, owner-dependent, margins thin)**
- Floor oscillates between Profit and Order.
- Vital Need is either margin discipline (Profit First) or owner extraction (Clockwork).
- Diagnose carefully — operators here often misdiagnose.

**Stage 3 — Established (cash healthy, team functional, owner partially extracted)**
- Floor moves to Order, Impact, or Legacy.
- Vital Need shifts to systemization, transformation, or institutionalization.
- Risk: complacency. Lower levels can decay without attention.

The BHN routes the operator to the right book:

| Diagnosed floor | Open this book |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Sales | *Get Different* + *The Pumpkin Plan* |
| Profit | *Profit First* |

Operators read Mike's books in the wrong order — they open Clockwork because they're tired, but their actual floor is Profit. The redesign fails because the Profit problem starves the redesign of resources.
