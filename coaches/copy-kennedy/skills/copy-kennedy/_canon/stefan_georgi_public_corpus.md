## Framework 1 — The VSL Structural Framework

**What it is:** Georgi's standard architecture for a video sales letter. Used across health, financial, e-com, and info-product verticals. The structure works for written long-form copy as well — Georgi treats the VSL as the master format that other formats are derived from.

**The structure (the four blocks):**

**Block 1 — HOOK (0:00 — 0:30).**
A specific, polarizing, curiosity-driven opening that does ONE job: stop the scroll and earn the next 30 seconds. The hook is NOT the headline of the offer — the hook is the first piece of audacity that earns the read.

**Block 2 — LEAD (0:30 — 5:00 in a typical VSL; first 10-25% of any long-form asset).**
The lead bridges from the hook to the body. It establishes the avatar, the problem, the false solutions the avatar has tried, the operator's qualification to speak on the problem, and the foreshadowing of the mechanism. The lead's job is to keep the prospect watching through the moment where they would normally drop off.

**Block 3 — BODY (5:00 — 25:00 in a typical VSL; middle 60-70% of any long-form asset).**
The body delivers:
- The mechanism reveal (why YOUR thing works).
- The proof stack (case studies, data, named results, third-party verification).
- The story integration (the operator's origin story, the discovery story, customer transformation stories).
- The objection pre-handling (every reason the avatar would say no, answered before they raise it).
- The transition into the offer.

**Block 4 — CLOSE (25:00 — end; last 15-25% of any long-form asset).**
The close delivers:
- The offer stack (core + bonuses, named and priced).
- The price anchor (what it would cost to solve the problem any other way).
- The price reveal.
- The risk reversal (guarantee).
- The scarcity / deadline.
- The CTA, repeated 3-5 times with the same exact wording.

**Georgi's rule:** the four blocks are non-negotiable. Cutting any one of them produces a weaker asset. The temptation to "shorten" a VSL is almost always a research / mechanism failure manifesting as length anxiety.

## Framework 2 — Hook Architecture

**Georgi's claim:** the first 10-15 seconds of any VSL — or the first 50 words of any sales letter — determines whether the rest of the asset gets a chance.

**The hook test (3 questions, all must be YES):**

1. Does it earn attention in a saturated environment?
2. Does it speak directly to the avatar's existing thought stream — what they were already worrying about?
3. Does it foreshadow a payoff worth staying for?

**The hook types (Georgi's working set):**

- **The Big Promise Hook.** Bold, specific, contrarian claim that contradicts the avatar's expectation. Works when the avatar is tired of conventional advice.
- **The Big Secret Hook.** A piece of insider information the avatar doesn't have. Works when the avatar feels stuck and is looking for the missing piece.
- **The Story Hook.** A specific scene or moment — "I was sitting in my car at 3am when I realized…" Works when the avatar needs to drop their guard before they'll hear an offer.
- **The Pattern Interrupt Hook.** Something that contradicts what the avatar expected the video to be about. Works in cold paid traffic where the avatar has no pre-context.
- **The Question Hook.** A direct question to the avatar that forces a mental answer. Works when the avatar is uncertain and the question taps an open loop in their mind.
- **The Curiosity Loop Hook.** Opens a question or mystery and explicitly defers the answer. "Stay with me — by the end of this, you'll know exactly why this works when nothing else has."

**Operator rule:** write 10-20 hooks for any VSL. The hook test eliminates 80%. The remaining 20% get tested in market.

## Framework 3 — Lead Types (Georgi's set)

**What it is:** Georgi's lead types overlap with the Kennedy lead types but are framed for the modern paid-traffic VSL environment. The core difference: Georgi's leads are tuned for cold, low-attention, paid-media audiences where the prospect arrived via a single ad click.

**The set:**

- **Story Lead.** A narrative — the operator's discovery, a customer's transformation, a specific scene. Strong for cold paid traffic. Lowers the prospect's commercial defenses.
- **Promise / Big Idea Lead.** The dominant benefit, stated as a specific contrarian promise. "What if [the conventional wisdom in your niche] is exactly wrong?"
- **Secret Lead.** Inside information the avatar doesn't have. Strong when the niche feels saturated and the avatar is hunting for the missing piece.
- **Problem-Agitation Lead.** Restate the avatar's pain in their own language, then deepen it. Strong when the avatar is in active distress.
- **News / Trend Lead.** Tie the offer to a current event, a recent study, a regulatory change, a market shift. Strong when the avatar perceives the world has just changed and old solutions might not work anymore.
- **Confession / Vulnerability Lead.** The operator admits a failure, a mistake, or a previous misunderstanding. Strong because it bypasses the avatar's "this is a slick salesperson" filter.

**Selection rule:** match the lead type to (a) traffic temperature (cold vs warm), (b) avatar emotional state (curious vs frustrated vs hopeful), and (c) niche saturation (fresh vs burned-out).

## Framework 4 — Body + Proof Architecture

**What it is:** Georgi's discipline for the middle of the asset — where the mechanism is revealed, proof is stacked, and objections are pre-handled.

**The body sub-architecture:**

1. **Establish the avatar's predicament.** Restate the problem, the failed attempts, and the cost of not solving it. Anchor the avatar in the pain.
2. **Introduce the operator's qualification.** Why does THIS person have the right to teach on this problem? Credentials, results, story, named clients, third-party verification.
3. **Reveal the mechanism.** The specific reason your offer works. Named, explained, defended. The mechanism is the centerpiece of the body — without it, the rest is just claims.
4. **Stack proof for the mechanism.** Case studies, data, before/after, named results. Each proof point ties back to the mechanism, not to a generic "we're good".
5. **Pre-handle objections.** Every objection the avatar would raise, placed inline at the point the avatar would mentally raise it. Price, time, skepticism, prior bad experience, "it won't work for me", "I need to ask my spouse".
6. **Story-integrate.** Wrap the mechanism and proof inside a story — the discovery story, a customer transformation story, the operator's origin story. Stories carry more recall than facts.
7. **Bridge to the offer.** Transition from "here's why this works" to "here's what you get if you want this". Smooth bridge, not a hard pivot.

**Georgi's rule:** the body is the part most copywriters get wrong. They write claims without mechanism, proof without story, and pre-handle objections too late. Fix the body by ensuring every paragraph either deepens the mechanism, proves the mechanism, or pre-handles an objection.

## Framework 5 — Close Architecture

**What it is:** Georgi's discipline for the end of the asset — where the offer is stacked, price is anchored, risk is reversed, scarcity is invoked, and the CTA is asked.

**The close architecture:**

1. **Offer summary.** Restate the core deliverable in the avatar's own language.
2. **Bonus stack.** Each bonus named, briefly described, and individually priced. The total of the bonuses should exceed the price of the core offer.
3. **Price anchor.** What it would cost to solve the problem any other way (competitor prices, DIY cost, the cost of NOT solving it). Anchor high.
4. **Price reveal.** The actual price, dropped after the anchor has done its work.
5. **Payment options.** If the price is high, present payment terms — instalments, financing, monthly.
6. **Risk reversal.** A specific, bold, time-bound guarantee. The bolder the guarantee, the more it sells (within reason — the operator must actually be able to deliver).
7. **Scarcity / deadline.** A specific reason to act now. Time-bound, quantity-bound, or status-bound (closing the doors, limited spots, expiring bonus).
8. **CTA.** Exact action, exact outcome, exact next step. Repeated 3-5 times with the same wording.
9. **P.S. / final hook (in written copy).** Restate the offer, the deadline, and the biggest objection answered.

**Georgi's failure mode:** rushing the close. Most copywriters spend 80% of their effort on the hook and lead, then collapse at the close. The close is where revenue is decided. Spend proportional effort on it.
