## Section 1 — Building a StoryBrand (the SB7 spine)

### Framework 1 — The SB7 Framework (master frame, Ch 1-9 spine)

**What it is:** Seven story elements applied to marketing. Miller's claim — every story that has ever worked uses these seven beats. Brains are wired for story, so brands that speak story get processed; brands that speak noise get filtered.

**The seven:**
1. A CHARACTER — the customer is the hero
2. has a PROBLEM — external, internal, philosophical
3. and meets a GUIDE — you, the brand, positioned with empathy + authority
4. who gives them a PLAN — typically a 3-step path
5. and CALLS THEM TO ACTION — direct CTA + transitional CTA
6. that ends in SUCCESS — the transformation made concrete
7. or helps them avoid FAILURE — the stakes, the loss

**Order matters.** A brand that calls itself the hero (#1 in the wrong seat) is the most common failure. A brand that skips stakes (#7) is the second most common.

**Failure mode:** treating SB7 as a checklist. It's a narrative. Order, weight, and concreteness all matter. A filled-in SB7 with vague entries beats nothing — but only barely.

### Framework 2 — The BrandScript (Ch 10)

**What it is:** A one-page worksheet that fills all seven SB7 boxes for a single offer to a single audience.

**Rule:** one BrandScript per offer, per audience segment. DR running Case Flow to immigration firms = one BrandScript. DR running Case Flow to vocational training schools = a different BrandScript. The hero changes, the problem layers change, success changes.

**Order of fill:**
1. Character (the customer, specific to the avatar)
2. What they want (the desire, in their words)
3. Their three problem layers (external / internal / philosophical)
4. The Guide statement (empathy line + authority proof)
5. The Plan (3 steps, customer-facing language)
6. The Call (direct + transitional)
7. Avoiding failure (3 things they avoid)
8. Achieving success (3 things they gain)

**Failure mode:** filling the BrandScript with founder-language instead of customer-language. The customer's problem must be stated in the customer's words, not yours.

---

### Framework 3 — The Three Problem Layers (Ch 4-5)

**What it is:** The depth axis of the Problem box. Miller's biggest contribution to B2B copy.

- **External:** the practical, observable problem. "I'm not getting enough qualified EB-5 leads."
- **Internal:** how the external problem makes the customer FEEL about themselves. "I feel invisible compared to the bigger firm with the slick website."
- **Philosophical:** why the situation is wrong on a moral level. "Attorneys who actually win cases shouldn't lose to attorneys who just market harder."

**Mechanism:**
- External is the hook (the headline).
- Internal is the close (the body — the line that makes them buy).
- Philosophical is the evangelism (the line that gets screenshotted and texted to a friend).

**Failure mode:** B2B brands stopping at external. The sale happens at internal. Loyalty happens at philosophical. Skipping either is leaving real money on the table.

---

### Framework 4 — The Guide Position (Ch 6)

**What it is:** The brand's correct role in the customer's story. NOT the hero. The Guide. Yoda to Luke. Gandalf to Frodo. Haymitch to Katniss.

**Two ingredients of a Guide:**
1. **Empathy** — proof the brand understands the customer's struggle. Empathy comes first. Without it, authority feels like bragging.
2. **Authority** — proof the brand can actually deliver. Testimonials, results data, case studies, awards, credentials. Light touch — too much authority without empathy reads as ego.

**The empathy line template:** "We know how frustrating it is when [the customer's actual experience]."

**The authority surface:** numbers > names > logos. "$5.19M revenue at 5.91x ROAS for Ashoori Law" beats "we work with great clients."

**Failure mode:** leading with founder backstory. Customer doesn't care about your story until you've earned the right by demonstrating you see theirs.

---

### Framework 5 — The Plan (Ch 7)

**What it is:** A clear, low-friction path the customer can follow to get what they want. Usually 3 steps. Sometimes labeled steps; sometimes promises (an "agreement plan" that covers their fears).

**Two flavors:**
1. **Process plan** — sequential steps. "1) Book the audit. 2) We install Case Flow. 3) You sign more cases."
2. **Agreement plan** — promises that defuse fear. "We won't lock you in for 12 months. We won't bill for ad waste. We won't disappear after kickoff."

**Mechanism:** uncertainty kills purchase. A visible plan reduces uncertainty. 3 steps because brains overload past 3.

**Failure mode:** plans full of internal jargon ("Discovery → Strategy → Activation"). Use customer-facing verbs.

---

### Framework 6 — Call to Action (Ch 8)

**What it is:** The exact ask. Two types, always both.

- **Direct CTA** — the hard ask. "Book the call." "Buy now." "Start the audit."
- **Transitional CTA** — the soft ask. "Download the guide." "Take the quiz." "Read the case study." Low-risk path for the not-yet-ready customer.

**Mechanism:** most prospects aren't ready to buy on first touch. Without a transitional CTA, they leave and don't come back. With one, they enter the email list and the nurture sequence does the work.

**Failure mode:** websites with only one type. A direct-only homepage filters out 95% of traffic. A transitional-only homepage doesn't convert ready buyers.

---

### Framework 7 — Success Vision (Ch 9, first half)

**What it is:** A vivid, concrete picture of the customer after they've bought. Not "grow your business" — "fully booked through Q3 with EB-5 retainers and a referral pipeline you didn't have to chase."

**Three flavors of success to ladder:**
1. Status gain (power, recognition, belonging)
2. Completion (becoming whole, finishing what was started)
3. Self-realization (the better version of themselves)

**Mechanism:** people buy futures, not products. Make the future visible.

---

### Framework 8 — Failure / Stakes (Ch 9, second half)

**What it is:** What the customer LOSES if they don't act. Loss aversion is real — losses move buyers 2x more than equivalent gains.

**The stakes section** in a wireframe is where Miller does this work. List 3 specific things the customer continues to suffer if they don't act.

**Failure mode:** brands that pitch only gain. Stakeless copy reads as "nice to have." Stakes copy reads as "need to fix."

---

### Framework 9 — The Grunt Test (Ch 11, the homepage gate)

**What it is:** A 5-second test for any homepage. Show it to someone who's never seen the brand. Five seconds later, can they grunt back three answers:

1. **What do you offer?**
2. **How will it make my life better?**
3. **What do I need to do to buy it?**

**The pass bar:** all three answers must be visible above the fold, in plain language. Not implied. Not in a video. Not three scrolls down. Above the fold, in words a caveman can grunt.

**Mechanism:** working memory is brutal. The brain decides in seconds whether to keep reading or close the tab. If those three answers aren't immediate, the brain files the brand as noise and stops processing.

**Where DR uses it:** every client landing page ship gate. The DR agency site itself. The NO BS Ads site. Non-negotiable.

---

### Framework 10 — The One-Liner (Ch 12)

**What it is:** A single sentence that compresses the BrandScript for use in any conversation.

**Template:**
> "We help [CHARACTER] who want [DESIRE] but face [PROBLEM]. We do this by [PLAN] so they can [SUCCESS]."

**Where it lives:**
- Email signatures
- Sales-call opens
- Networking events
- Above-the-fold subheads
- LinkedIn bios
- Pitch decks (slide one)

**Failure mode:** one-liners that name what the brand DOES (process language) instead of what the customer GETS (outcome language).

## Section 2 — Marketing Made Simple (the deployment chain)

### Framework 11 — The 5-Part Marketing Funnel

**What it is:** The asset chain that takes a customer from curiosity to commitment. Maps to the customer's psychological journey, not your sales stages.

1. **The one-liner** (used in every touchpoint — curiosity)
2. **The website / wireframe** (Grunt Test pass — curiosity → enlightenment)
3. **The lead generator** (PDF, quiz, checklist — earns the email at low risk)
4. **The nurture email sequence** (6+ emails, weekly, value-only, no hard pitch — builds authority through enlightenment)
5. **The sales email sequence** (5 emails — problem, agitation, testimonial, vision, direct CTA — drives commitment)

**Mechanism:** customers move through curiosity → enlightenment → commitment. Skipping a stage is why most funnels leak.

**Wireframe section order (from Marketing Made Simple):**
- Header (one-liner + CTA)
- Stakes
- Value proposition (3 bullets)
- Guide section (empathy + authority)
- Plan (3 steps)
- Explanatory paragraph
- Video (optional)
- Price (anchored)
- Junk drawer (FAQs, fine print)

This is the canonical StoryBrand wireframe. Use it for every long-form landing page.

## Section 3 — How to Grow Your Small Business (the operating layer)

### Framework 12 — The 6-Part Flight Plan

**What it is:** A 6-part operating doc that clarifies the underlying business. The BrandScript answers "what do we say?" The Flight Plan answers "what do we do?"

The six parts:
1. **Mission statement** — definite, time-bound, measurable. Not vague aspiration. A specific outcome by a specific date.
2. **Key characters** — the team, the customer avatar, the strategic partners. Named.
3. **Goals** — three priorities only. More than three = none.
4. **Themes** — the recurring drumbeats. The cultural through-lines.
5. **Three economic objectives** — the levers that move the P&L.
6. **Income streams** — every revenue source, mapped.

**Where DR uses it:** the DR Flight Plan (the agency itself) and the NO BS Ads Flight Plan (the media brand). Two separate Flight Plans for two separate businesses. Different missions, different characters, different income streams.
